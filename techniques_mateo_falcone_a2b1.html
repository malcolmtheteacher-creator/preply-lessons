A boy alone at home. A man who needs to hide. A silver watch that is too beautiful.

Today: tell the story of a boy who hides a man — and then sells his secret for a silver watch. Grammar: if + will — the words we use for promises and warnings ("If you hide me, I will give you money").

Key words — learn them, then make your guess

First, let's learn eight words. Read each word and its sentence, guess the meaning, then click Reveal to check. Say each word out loud.

1. When you hide something, you put it where nobody can see it. You can hide a thing — or a person. (Past: hid.)
to hide (hid, hidden) (verb) — to put something (or someone) where nobody can see it.
2. Hay is dry grass for animals. On a farm it stands in a big pile — a haystack. A haystack is a good place to hide!
hay (noun) — dry grass for animals. A haystack is a big pile of hay.
3. A soldier works in an army. He wears a uniform and carries a gun. Soldiers sometimes look for people who run from the law.
a soldier (noun) — a person in an army, with a uniform and a gun.
4. A coin is a small round piece of money, made of metal — for example, a silver coin.
a coin (noun) — a small round piece of metal money.
5. A watch is a small clock. Two hundred years ago, a silver watch was very expensive — a dream for a poor boy.
a watch (noun) — a small clock. A silver watch was a very expensive thing.
6. When you promise, you say: "I will do it — for sure." A promise is your word, and good people keep their word.
to promise (verb) — to say that you will surely do something. (noun: a promise)
7. When you betray a person, they trust you — but you help their enemy. It is a terrible thing to do. A person who betrays is a traitor.
to betray (verb) — to help the enemy of a person who trusts you. (a person: a traitor)
8. Honour means: people respect you because you are good and you keep your word. In this story, honour is the most important thing of all.
honour (noun) — the respect people give you because you are good and keep your word.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — Alone at home

This story happens on the island of Corsica, two hundred years ago. Near the village there is the maquis — a wild land of thick bushes. Men who run from the law hide there.

Mateo Falcone lives near the maquis with his wife Giuseppa and his son Fortunato. Mateo is famous for two things: he never misses with his gun, and he always keeps his word. Fortunato is ten years old.

One morning, Mateo and Giuseppa go out to see their animals. Fortunato stays at home alone. Suddenly he hears guns. A man comes up the path. His leg is hurt, and he can hardly walk. It is Gianetto, a bandit. Soldiers are hunting him.

"Hide me!" says Gianetto. "The soldiers are coming!" Fortunato is not afraid. "What will you give me if I hide you?" he asks. Gianetto gives him a silver coin — five francs. Then Fortunato hides him in the haystack. He puts a cat on top of the hay. Now it looks like nobody is there.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who is Gianetto, and why does he come to the house?

2. What does Fortunato ask for before he hides him?

1. Gianetto is a bandit. His leg is hurt, and soldiers are hunting him. He needs a place to hide.

2. He asks, "What will you give me?" Gianetto gives him a silver coin — five francs — and then the boy hides him in the haystack.

Talk about it:
  • Fortunato helps the man — but only for money. Is that clever, or is it wrong? Why?

Part 2 — The silver watch

Soon six soldiers arrive. Their leader is Sergeant Gamba — a cousin of the Falcone family. "Did a man come this way?" he asks. "I saw nobody," says Fortunato. The soldiers look in the house. They find nothing.

Gamba gets angry. "If you don't tell me the truth, you will go to prison!" he says. Fortunato laughs. "My father is Mateo Falcone!" He is not afraid of threats.

Then Gamba has an idea. He takes out a beautiful silver watch. "Do you want it?" he says. "If you tell me where the man is, I will give you this watch." Fortunato looks at the watch. He wants it very, very much. His hand comes up slowly… He takes the watch — and points at the haystack.

The soldiers pull Gianetto out of the hay and tie him. Gianetto looks at the boy with cold eyes. Fortunato throws the five-franc coin back to him. Gianetto spits at the door of the house. "House of a traitor!" he says.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why is Fortunato not afraid of Gamba's threats?

2. How does Gamba get the truth from the boy?

1. Because his father is Mateo Falcone. Nobody can touch the son of such a famous man — and the boy knows it.

2. Not with threats — with a promise. He shows the beautiful silver watch and says: "If you tell me where the man is, I will give you this watch." Fortunato takes it and points at the haystack.

Talk about it:
  • The threats do not work, but the watch works. Why is a beautiful thing sometimes stronger than fear?

Part 3 — The father comes home

Then Mateo and Giuseppa come home. Gamba tells them everything: "We caught Gianetto — thanks to your son! He showed us the hiding place." Mateo says nothing. His face is like stone.

The soldiers leave. Mateo looks at the watch in his son's hand. "Who gave you that?" — "My cousin, the sergeant." Mateo takes the watch and smashes it on a stone. "Wife," he says, "is this child mine? He is the first of our family to betray."

Fortunato cries. Giuseppa begs: "He is your son!" — "Leave me. I am his father."

Mateo walks with his son to a small valley. "Say your prayers," he says. Fortunato says his prayers and begs: "Father, forgive me! I will never do it again!" Mateo raises his gun. "May God forgive you," he says. He shoots. Fortunato falls dead.

Giuseppa runs to them. "What have you done?" — "Justice," says Mateo. "He died a Christian. I will have a mass sung for him."

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What does Mateo do with the watch?

2. What are Mateo's last words in the story?

1. He smashes it on a stone. For him, the watch is the price of betrayal.

2. "He died a Christian. I will have a mass sung for him." Before that, when Giuseppa asks what he has done, he says one word: "Justice."

Talk about it:
  • Mateo says one word: "Justice." Do you think it is justice? What word would you use?

Grammar: if + will (promises & warnings)

This story is full of promises and warnings. In English, we make them with if + will.

A promise: "If you hide me, I will give you money." (Gianetto)
A warning: "If you don't tell the truth, you will go to prison." (Gamba)
The form: If + present simple, + will + verb. → "If you tell me where the man is, I will give you this watch."
Careful! After if, use the present simple — not will. Say "If you help me…" (not if you will help me). The two halves can change places: "I will give you the watch if you tell me." Use a comma only when the if-half comes first. For a negative result, use won't (= will not): "If you say nothing, they won't find him."

1. Put the verbs in the right form

One gap needs the present simple, the other needs will + verb. Verb pairs, in order: hide → give · tell → get · say → find · take → be.

1. "If you me, I you five francs," says Gianetto.

2. "If you me the truth, you this watch," says Gamba.

3. "If I nothing, the soldiers nothing," thinks Fortunato.

4. "If I the watch, I the happiest boy in Corsica," he thinks.

1. If you hide me, I will give you five francs.

2. If you tell me the truth, you will get this watch.

3. If I say nothing, the soldiers will find nothing.

4. If I take the watch, I will be the happiest boy in Corsica.

2. Say it with if

Finish the second sentence so it means the same as the first.

1. "Hide me, and I will pay you." → If you

2. "Tell me the truth, and the watch is yours." → If you

3. "Lie to me, and you will go to prison." → If you

1. If you hide me, I will pay you.

2. If you tell me the truth, I will give you the watch.

3. If you lie to me, you will go to prison.

3. Tell a short part of the story

Put each verb in the right form — present simple after if, will + verb in the other half. Verbs, in order: hide · give · tell · give · betray · lose. Then read the little story out loud.

Gianetto is hurt. He says: "If you me, I you a silver coin." The boy agrees. Then the sergeant comes with a watch: "If you me where the man is, I you this watch." Fortunato takes it. But he learns a hard lesson: if you a person who trusts you, you much more than a watch.

hide · will give · tell · will give · betray · will lose

Every promise in this story is kept — and that is the tragedy.

Going deeper — talk about the big ideas

Now take the story's ideas into the real world. Talk about each one out loud. There are no right answers — say what you think, and say why.

1 · A promise is a promise?

Gianetto keeps his promise: he pays the coin. Gamba keeps his promise: he gives the watch. But both promises lead to something bad. Can keeping a promise be wrong? When is it better to break a promise?

2 · Hard to say no

The watch was too beautiful for Fortunato. Imagine this: someone offers a child a wonderful present — but the child must break an important rule to get it. What can the child do? What helps people say no to things they really want?

3 · The village and the police

In the story, the people of Corsica think it is shameful to help the police against a neighbour. Why do they think this? Is it the same in the world today? Who should people help — the law, or the person who is running from it?

4 · Then and now

The story is from 1829. In it, a father thinks his family's honour is more important than anything — even his son. Could this story happen today? What has changed in two hundred years, and why?
