🎰 What Are the Enhanced Games?

The Enhanced Games is a new multi-sport event launching in May 2026 in Las Vegas. Unlike the Olympics, athletes are allowed to use performance-enhancing drugs under medical supervision.

  • 🏊 Swimming: 50m & 100m freestyle, 50m & 100m butterfly
  • 🏃 Track: 100m sprint, 100m/110m hurdles
  • 🏋️ Weightlifting: Snatch, clean and jerk

Venue: Resorts World Las Vegas — because where else would you launch the most controversial sports event in history?

59% of US Olympic athletes earn less than $25,000 in Olympic years. For many, the Enhanced Games represents their first chance at real money.

💰 The Money

  • $250,000 — Prize money per event
  • $1,000,000 — Bonus for breaking a world record
  • $500,000 — Maximum an athlete can earn per event

Compare that to the Olympics: Gold medallists receive $0 from the IOC. At the 2025 World Swimming Championships, first place earned just $20,000.

Key Expressions (Part 1)

👆 Click each card to reveal meaning, examples, and practice prompts

1. "Push the boundaries"
Neutral — works in casual & formal contexts
Meaning: To go beyond what is currently accepted, known, or considered possible. To test limits — physical, ethical, creative, or social. Can be positive (innovation) or concerning (recklessness).
Example: "The Enhanced Games claims to push the boundaries of human performance, but critics say it's really pushing the boundaries of what's ethical."
General usage: "SpaceX has consistently pushed the boundaries of what private companies can achieve in space exploration."
NOW YOU TRY: What's an area of life where you think we SHOULD push the boundaries? And an area where we've pushed them too far?
2. "Level the playing field"
Neutral — common in business, sports, and politics
Meaning: To make a situation fair by giving everyone the same advantages and opportunities. Originally from sports (a literal flat field), now used metaphorically in any competitive context.
Example: "Ironically, Enhanced Games supporters argue they're levelling the playing field — since many athletes already dope in secret, making it legal just makes it fair."
Business context: "The new regulations aim to level the playing field between large corporations and small startups."
NOW YOU TRY: Think of a situation in sport, business, or life where the playing field is NOT level. What would it take to level it?
💡 Notice the irony: Both expressions can be used positively or critically depending on your perspective. Enhanced Games supporters use "pushing boundaries" and "levelling the field" to justify their event — while critics use the same phrases sarcastically.

Key Expressions (Part 2)

👆 Click each card to reveal meaning, examples, and practice prompts

3. "Blur the line between X and Y"
Neutral — common in analysis and debate
Meaning: To make the distinction between two things unclear or confused. When categories that used to be separate become mixed or indistinguishable.
Example: "The Enhanced Games blurs the line between sport and entertainment — is it athletic competition or just a spectacle for billionaires?"
Tech context: "Social media has blurred the line between public and private life."
Work context: "Remote work has blurred the line between professional and personal time."
NOW YOU TRY: What's a line that modern life has blurred? (e.g., news/entertainment, work/life, natural/artificial) Is this good or bad?
4. "Weigh the risks against the benefits"
Neutral to formal — common in decision-making contexts
Meaning: To carefully consider both the potential dangers and the potential advantages before making a decision. To do a cost-benefit analysis.
Example: "Athletes joining the Enhanced Games have to weigh the risks against the benefits: potential health damage and a lifetime ban from traditional sport versus $1 million and the chance to see how fast they can really go."
Career context: "Before quitting my job to start a business, I had to weigh the risks against the benefits of leaving a stable income."
NOW YOU TRY: Describe a decision you made where you had to weigh the risks against the benefits. What did you decide? Would you decide differently now?
5. "Have a vested interest in"
Formal — common in business, politics, and media
Meaning: To have a personal stake in something — usually financial, but also reputational or emotional. Often implies bias: if someone has a vested interest, their opinion may not be objective.
Example: "The billionaire investors behind the Enhanced Games have a vested interest in making it succeed — they're not doing this for the love of sport."
Critical thinking: "When a pharmaceutical company publishes research saying their drug is safe, remember: they have a vested interest in the results."
NOW YOU TRY: In any debate or controversy, ask: who has a vested interest? Pick a current topic and identify who benefits from each side of the argument.
💡 Quick Review — All 5 Expressions:
  1. Push the boundaries — Go beyond current limits
  2. Level the playing field — Make competition fair
  3. Blur the line between X and Y — Make distinctions unclear
  4. Weigh the risks against the benefits — Consider pros and cons
  5. Have a vested interest in — Have a personal stake (possible bias)
