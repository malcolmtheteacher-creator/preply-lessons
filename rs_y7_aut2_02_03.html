Encounter

Let's meet the core Islamic understanding of God: absolute oneness, divine names, and how Muslims encounter the divine.

KEY TERM

Tawhid (تَوْحِيد)

Arabic: "to make one." The absolute belief that God is one and unique, with no partners, no equals, and nothing comparable to God. This is the most fundamental concept in Islam.

Core idea: "There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is His messenger" — this is the Shahada, the statement all Muslims must believe.

The Heart of Tawhid: Surah Al-Ikhlas (112)

This surah is the clearest statement of Islamic monotheism. It appears in the Quran and is recited by Muslims daily:

"Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal Absolute. He begets not, nor was He begotten. And there is none comparable to Him."

Quran 112:1–4 (Yusuf Ali translation)

Breaking it down:

"He is Allah, the One": God is absolutely unique and alone.
"The Eternal Absolute": God exists beyond time, depending on nothing, everything depending on God.
"He begets not, nor was He begotten": God has no parents, no children, no family. (This directly rejects the Christian belief in Jesus as God's son.)
"There is none comparable to Him": Nothing in creation is like God; God is utterly transcendent.

KEY TERM

Shirk (شِرْك)

Arabic: "to associate" or "to share." Associating partners with God, or believing anything is equal to God. This is the gravest sin in Islam because it violates Tawhid.

Examples of shirk: Worshipping idols, believing Jesus is God's son, praying to saints or angels as if they are gods, believing in multiple gods (polytheism).

The 99 Names of Allah (Al-Asma al-Husna)

Muslims believe God has 99 "most beautiful names" (Al-Asma al-Husna) that describe God's qualities and attributes. These are not 99 different gods — they are 99 ways to understand the one God.

Muslims sometimes use prayer beads (Misbaha) with 99 beads to remember the 99 Names while praying. Some Muslims say that reciting the 99 Names with sincere intent brings blessings and spiritual closeness to God.

How Muslims Experience God

Through the Quran

For Muslims, the Quran is God's own words, recited in Arabic. Reading or listening to it is direct contact with the divine message. The beauty of Quranic language is seen as a sign of its divine origin.

Through Prayer (Salat)

Muslims pray five times daily, facing Makkah. Prayer is not just asking God for things — it is an act of submission and remembrance. Each prayer renews the connection between the worshipper and God.

Through Creation (Ayat)

Muslims see the beauty, order, and complexity of the universe as signs (ayat) of God's wisdom and power. Reflecting on nature is a form of worship.
