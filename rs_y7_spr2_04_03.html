The Bible

Recall: Explain one way Christians DISAGREE about the Bible. Name the two main parts of the Bible.

One disagreement: Catholics and Protestants disagree on which books belong in the Bible. Catholics accept additional books called the Apocrypha that Protestants reject.

Two main parts:

  • Old Testament: 39 books (for Protestants) written before Jesus, containing history, law, poetry, and prophecy.
  • New Testament: 27 books written after Jesus, containing Gospels (stories of Jesus), letters from apostles, and Revelation.

The Huge Diversity of Christian Worship

Christianity has 45,000+ denominations worldwide. This means there's radical diversity in how Christians worship. Some traditions are highly formal and structured. Others are spontaneous and emotional. Some sit in complete silence. Others sing, dance, and speak in tongues. There is no single "Christian worship experience."

Four Major Worship Styles

Liturgical Worship

Catholic, Anglican, Orthodox
What you would see/hear:

Formal robes, incense, candles, bells. Set prayers read from a book. Hymns sung. Priest or minister leads the service in a prescribed order.

What you would feel:

Reverent, solemn, ancient, structured. The ritual creates a sense of continuity with centuries of Christian tradition.

Key element:

The Eucharist (or Holy Communion) is central. Bread and wine are blessed and shared to commemorate the Last Supper of Jesus.

Non-Liturgical Worship

Baptist, Methodist, many Evangelical
What you would see/hear:

Simple space, no robes or special ritual objects. Sermon-centered. Hymns and worship songs sung. Prayer is spontaneous and personal.

What you would feel:

Accessible, personal, flexible. The emphasis is on individual connection with God rather than formal ceremony.

Key element:

The sermon (a talk about the Bible) is central. The preacher interprets Scripture and applies it to modern life.

Charismatic Worship

Pentecostal, some Evangelical
What you would see/hear:

Lively music (often with a band). Hands raised in worship. Emotional expressions—clapping, shouting, crying. Some people speak in tongues (glossolalia—words that sound like an unknown language). Prophecy and healing prayer.

What you would feel:

Energetic, emotional, alive. People seek direct experience of the Holy Spirit—not just intellectual belief.

Key element:

Belief in the gifts of the Holy Spirit: healing, prophecy, speaking in tongues. God is active and present NOW, not just remembered from the past.

Quaker Worship

Society of Friends
What you would see/hear:

Complete silence. No leader, no set prayers, no music, no sermon. People sit together in silence until someone feels moved by the Spirit to speak.

What you would feel:

Peaceful, meditative, equal. No hierarchy. No need for priests or liturgy—God speaks directly to each person.

Key element:

Radical simplicity and equality. Silence allows people to listen for God's inner light. Only those truly "moved by the Spirit" speak.

The Eucharist (Holy Communion / Mass)

What is it?

A central ritual in Christian worship where bread and wine are blessed and consumed. It commemorates the Last Supper of Jesus with his disciples, where he said: "Take and eat; this is my body... Drink from this cup; this is my blood."

The Theological Debate
Catholic View (Transubstantiation): The bread and wine literally become the body and blood of Christ. The substance changes, even though it looks and tastes the same. This is a mystical transformation.
Protestant View (Symbolic): The bread and wine remain bread and wine. They are symbolic reminders of Christ's sacrifice. They represent his body and blood, but are not literally transformed.

Does it matter? Yes—it speaks to how different churches understand God's presence in the world. Is God mystically present in the Eucharist, or is it a remembered symbol?

Key Question: Does HOW You Worship Matter?

If two people worship God in completely different ways—one in silent meditation, one in ecstatic dance—are both valid? Does worship style matter, or does it only matter that you worship with sincerity?

Fact: There are over 45,000 Christian denominations worldwide. Worship styles range from complete silence (Quakers) to ecstatic dancing and speaking in tongues (Pentecostals). Christians cannot easily agree on what "proper" worship looks like.

Both Sides

"There is no 'correct' way to worship God."

Arguments FOR this statement
  • God cares about the sincerity of your heart, not the form of your worship.
  • Different people connect with God in different ways. Silence moves some; music moves others.
  • Jesus criticized religious leaders who focused on ritual form rather than genuine faith.
  • The existence of 45,000 denominations shows that Christians themselves can't agree on one way.
  • Imposing one "correct" style excludes people who worship differently.
Arguments AGAINST this statement
  • Jesus gave specific instructions (the Eucharist, baptism) that suggest form matters.
  • Without some agreed-upon practices, Christian community dissolves—people need shared rituals.
  • Some forms of worship are theologically incorrect. Not all beliefs about God are equally valid.
  • Allowing complete freedom in worship can lead to confusion and inconsistency.
  • Centuries of Christian tradition and theology should guide worship, not individual preference.
