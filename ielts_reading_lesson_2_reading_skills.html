The Three Essential Reading Skills

Key Insight: IELTS Reading is NOT about reading every word. Success comes from using THREE different reading speeds strategically. Most students fail because they read everything at the same slow pace.

1. Skimming (Fast Reading)

~500 wpm

Purpose: Get the general idea quickly

  • Read first/last sentences of paragraphs
  • Notice headings, subheadings, bold text
  • Understand the main topic and structure
  • Takes 2-3 minutes per passage

Use for: First look at passage, Matching Headings

2. Scanning (Search Reading)

~100 wpm

Purpose: Find specific information

  • Look for keywords from questions
  • Search for names, dates, numbers
  • Do not read - hunt for targets
  • Eyes move quickly across text

Use for: Locating answers, Matching Information

3. Detailed Reading (Slow Reading)

~50 wpm

Purpose: Understand meaning precisely

  • Read every word carefully
  • Understand relationships between ideas
  • Compare text meaning with question
  • Used only for relevant sections

Use for: T/F/NG, Multiple Choice, understanding

Common Mistake: Reading the entire passage in detail before looking at questions wastes 10-15 minutes. Instead: Skim first (2-3 min), then scan for specific answers.

Skimming Practice

Skimming Technique: Read ONLY the first sentence of each paragraph, plus any headings or bold text. Your goal is to understand the MAIN IDEA in under 60 seconds.

Timed Skimming Exercise

You have 45 seconds to skim this passage. Focus on first sentences only. Then answer what the passage is mainly about.

The Rise of Urban Farming

Urban farming has emerged as a significant response to food security concerns in major cities worldwide. As populations continue to grow and traditional agricultural land becomes scarcer, city dwellers are finding innovative ways to grow food in unexpected places. From rooftop gardens in New York to vertical farms in Singapore, the movement is gaining momentum.

The environmental benefits of urban agriculture are substantial and well-documented. By growing food closer to consumers, transportation emissions are dramatically reduced. Additionally, urban farms often employ sustainable practices such as composting, rainwater harvesting, and organic pest control methods that further minimize environmental impact.

Economic advantages also drive the urban farming movement forward. Local food production creates jobs within communities and reduces dependence on distant supply chains. Studies indicate that urban farms can generate significant income while providing fresh produce at competitive prices to local residents.

However, urban farming faces several significant challenges that must be addressed. Space limitations, soil contamination in former industrial areas, and high initial investment costs present ongoing obstacles. Furthermore, zoning regulations in many cities still do not accommodate agricultural activities, creating legal barriers for potential urban farmers.

Despite these challenges, the future of urban farming appears promising. Technological innovations such as hydroponics, LED growing lights, and automated systems are making urban agriculture more efficient and cost-effective. As cities continue to expand and climate change threatens traditional farming regions, urban food production may become not just beneficial but essential.

What is this passage mainly about?

A) The history of farming techniques throughout human civilization
B) Urban farming - its benefits, challenges, and future potential
C) Environmental problems caused by traditional agriculture
D) Government regulations affecting food production
Skimming Tips:
  • Focus on underlined/first sentences - they usually contain main ideas
  • Notice paragraph structure: introduction, body points, conclusion
  • Do not try to understand details - just get the big picture
  • Practice regularly to increase your skimming speed

Scanning Practice

Scanning Technique: Your eyes move quickly across the text looking for specific keywords. Do not read - HUNT for your target words like searching for a name in a phone book.

Keyword Scanning Exercise

Scan the passage below to find specific information. Race against the timer!

The Great Barrier Reef

The Great Barrier Reef, located off the coast of Queensland, Australia, is the world's largest coral reef system. Stretching over 2,300 kilometers, it comprises approximately 2,900 individual reefs and 900 islands. The reef was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1981 due to its outstanding universal value.

Marine biologist Dr. Sarah Chen has studied the reef since 1995 and estimates that it supports over 1,500 species of fish, 400 types of coral, and 4,000 species of mollusk. Her research team, based at James Cook University, has documented a 27% decline in coral cover since 2016 due to rising ocean temperatures.

Tourism contributes approximately $6.4 billion annually to the Australian economy, with over 2 million visitors exploring the reef each year. The Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority, established in 1975, manages the protected area which spans 344,400 square kilometers.

Conservation efforts have intensified following severe bleaching events in 2016, 2017, and 2020. The Australian government committed $500 million in 2022 to reef protection programs, focusing on water quality improvement and crown-of-thorns starfish control. Professor James Mitchell from the University of Sydney predicts that without significant climate action, up to 90% of the reef could be lost by 2050.

Find the answers by scanning (do not read the whole passage!):

Question 1: How long is the Great Barrier Reef?

Question 2: When did Dr. Sarah Chen start studying the reef?

Question 3: How much money did the Australian government commit in 2022?

Question 4: How many species of fish does the reef support?

Scanning Tips:
  • Look for CAPITAL LETTERS (names, places)
  • Look for NUMBERS (dates, statistics, measurements)
  • Use keywords from the question as your search target
  • Move your eyes in a Z-pattern or straight down the page
  • Do not get distracted by interesting information - stay focused on your target

Detailed Reading Practice

Detailed Reading: Used ONLY after you have located the relevant section. Read every word carefully to understand the exact meaning. This is where you determine if something is TRUE, FALSE, or NOT GIVEN.

Understanding Precise Meaning

Read this paragraph carefully, then decide if each statement is TRUE, FALSE, or NOT GIVEN based on the information.

"The Mediterranean diet, characterized by high consumption of olive oil, fruits, vegetables, and fish, has been associated with numerous health benefits. A 2018 study conducted by researchers at Harvard University followed 25,000 women over 12 years and found that those who closely adhered to the Mediterranean diet had a 25% lower risk of cardiovascular disease compared to those who did not. However, the researchers noted that the benefits were most pronounced in women over 55 years of age. The study also observed that participants who combined the diet with moderate exercise showed even greater improvements in health markers, though the diet alone was sufficient to produce significant benefits."

Key Difference - FALSE vs NOT GIVEN:
  • FALSE: The passage says the OPPOSITE of the statement
  • NOT GIVEN: The passage does not mention this information at all

Ask yourself: "Does the text CONTRADICT this, or simply NOT MENTION it?"

Using Context Clues for Unknown Vocabulary

Reality Check: You WILL encounter unknown words in IELTS Reading. Do not panic! Use context clues to understand the meaning without needing a dictionary.

Types of Context Clues

1. Definition Clues

The meaning is given directly in the sentence.

"The archipelago, a chain of islands, stretches across the Pacific Ocean."

2. Example Clues

Examples help explain the unknown word.

"Arthropods, such as insects, spiders, and crabs, have exoskeletons."

3. Contrast Clues

An opposite meaning shows what the word means.

"Unlike the sparse vegetation of the desert, the rainforest has lush plant growth."

4. Logic Clues

Use common sense and the overall meaning.

"After the long drought destroyed their crops, farmers faced destitution."
