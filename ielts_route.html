The IELTS Route

One path from your first diagnostic to your last mock. Follow the stages in order — every lesson is chosen and placed for you.

How it works. The route has five stages. Stage 1 finds your starting point. Stages 2 to 5 build, test and finish your skills, with a different mix each week. Every step below is a lesson or practice page — click it, do it, tick it off.

A good week is one writing step, one speaking step, one reading or listening step, and ten minutes on the side lessons at the bottom. Steps marked ★ Band 7+ are for stretching beyond the core; everything else is for every student.

The whole route fits twelve weeks, but it moves at your pace. If your test is sooner, do Stage 1, then spend your time on the skills where your diagnostic was lowest.

Build the skills

Weeks 3–8

Six tracks run side by side. Each week, take the next step in two or three of them — the order inside each track matters, the order between tracks does not.

Writing Task 2 — the five essay types

  1. Opinion essays (agree / disagree) Take a clear position and keep it for the whole essay.
  2. Discussion essays (both views) Present both sides, then give your own view.
  3. Advantages and disadvantages Weigh both sides and answer the exact question asked.
  4. Problem and solution Causes, effects and realistic solutions.
  5. Two-part questions Answer both parts equally and recognise mixed types.
  6. Conclusions and linking End the essay properly and link ideas smoothly.
  7. Essay practice: guided, all five types A band-8 model, then your own essay paragraph by paragraph.

Writing Task 1 — describing visuals

  1. Task 1 overview All the question types, the marking criteria and the structure.
  2. Line graphs Describe trends and change over time.
  3. Bar charts and pie charts Compare categories and describe proportions.
  4. Process diagrams and maps A different style of report, in the right order.
  5. Task 1 language toolkit ★ Band 7+Trends, comparisons and overviews — nine short lessons of phrases that score.

Speaking — Parts 1, 2 and 3

  1. Part 1: sounding natural Short answers that score higher: no echoing, tenses that move, the precise word.
  2. Part 1: topics in depth The common Part 1 topics, with ideas ready to use.
  3. Part 2: the long turn One minute to plan, two to talk — with cue cards and a real timer.
  4. Part 3: the discussion Opinions, reasons and the wider question.
  5. Speaking grammar toolkit ★ Band 7+The structures that lift a speaking score to Band 7 and above.

Reading — practice and training

  2. Read Your Way to Band 7 ★ Band 7+Reading questions built from a story, an essay and a piece of science — each carries into writing and speaking.

Listening

Language — vocabulary and accuracy
