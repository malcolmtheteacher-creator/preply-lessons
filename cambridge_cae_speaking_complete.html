Cambridge CAE Speaking

Complete Guide to Parts 1-4

C1 Advanced Exam Strategy

The CAE Speaking Test: What You Need to Know

The speaking test lasts approximately 15 minutes and is taken with a partner. You will be assessed by two examiners: one asks questions (the interlocutor), while the other only listens and takes notes (the assessor).

The 4 Parts at a Glance

Part 1: The Interview

2 minutes

The examiner asks you and your partner questions about yourselves - work, studies, hobbies, daily life, future plans. You answer individually, not together.

Part 2: Long Turn (Comparing Photos)

4 minutes (1 min each + 30 sec response)

You each receive two photos. You speak for 1 minute comparing them and answering a question. Your partner then gives a brief 30-second response to a related question about your photos.

Part 3: Collaborative Task

4 minutes (2 min discussion + 1 min decision)

You and your partner discuss a diagram with several options, then try to reach a decision together. This tests your ability to interact, negotiate, and collaborate.

Part 4: Discussion

5 minutes

The examiner asks broader questions related to the Part 3 topic. You discuss together, sharing opinions, agreeing, disagreeing, and developing ideas.

What Examiners Assess

You are marked on 5 criteria, each worth up to 5 marks:

Grammar

Range and accuracy of grammatical structures

Vocabulary

Range and appropriate use of lexical items

Discourse Management

Coherence, relevance, and ability to extend turns

Pronunciation

Stress, rhythm, intonation, and individual sounds

Interactive Communication

Initiating, responding, and turn-taking with your partner

Key Insight

The difference between a Band 3 and Band 5 is rarely about "knowing more English." It's about how you use what you know: extending answers, using a range of structures, and interacting naturally with your partner.

Part 1: The Interview (2 minutes)

This is your warm-up. The examiner asks you and your partner simple questions about your life, interests, and experiences. You answer individually.

Typical questions:

  • What do you enjoy most about your work?
  • Would you like to change your career in the future?
  • What skills are important in your profession?

Typical questions:

  • What are you studying? / What did you study?
  • Why did you choose this subject?
  • What do you hope to do after you finish?
  • How important is education in your country?

Typical questions:

  • What do you enjoy doing in your free time?
  • Have you taken up any new hobbies recently?
  • Do you prefer indoor or outdoor activities?
  • How do you usually spend your weekends?

Typical questions:

  • Where are you from?
  • What do you like most about your hometown?
  • Has your area changed much over the years?

Typical questions:

The Golden Rule: Expand Your Answers

Don't Do This

One-word or very short answers waste your opportunity to show your English ability. "Yes," "No," or "I like reading" won't impress anyone.

Do This Instead

Use the ANSWER + REASON + EXAMPLE/DETAIL structure. Give your answer, explain why, and add a specific detail or example.

Sample Questions with Model Answers

What do you enjoy doing in your free time?
I like reading.
I'm really into reading, especially historical fiction. I find it fascinating to learn about different time periods while being entertained by a good story. Recently, I've been working my way through a series set in Tudor England, and I've actually learned quite a lot about that era.
Do you think it's important to learn foreign languages?
Yes, it's important.
Absolutely, I think it's essential in today's globalized world. Not only does it open up career opportunities, but it also gives you a deeper understanding of different cultures. Personally, learning English has completely changed my life - it's allowed me to travel more confidently and connect with people from all over the world.
What's the best thing about your hometown?
It's nice and quiet.
What I appreciate most is the sense of community. It's a fairly small town where everyone knows each other, and there's a real feeling of belonging. We have a weekly market in the main square where local farmers sell their produce, and it's become a social event where people catch up and chat. It's something I really miss when I'm away.

Practice: Expand These Short Answers

Transform these weak answers into strong, extended responses.

Activity: Expand the Answer

Question: Do you prefer working alone or in a team?

Weak answer: "I prefer working in a team."

Model answer: "I'd say I generally prefer working in a team, although it depends on the task. When it comes to creative projects, I find that bouncing ideas off others leads to much better results than working in isolation. For instance, in my current job, we often brainstorm solutions together, and it's amazing how one person's idea can spark something completely different in someone else's mind. That said, for tasks that require deep concentration, I do appreciate having some quiet time alone."

Question: Has your country changed much in recent years?

Weak answer: "Yes, a lot."

Model answer: "Oh, enormously. The pace of change has been quite remarkable, particularly in terms of technology and infrastructure. When I was growing up, internet access was limited to a few places, but now it's everywhere, and that's transformed how people work, shop, and communicate. The cities have expanded dramatically too - my hometown, which used to be fairly quiet, now has shopping centers, international restaurants, and a much more cosmopolitan feel. It's exciting in many ways, though sometimes I do feel nostalgic for the simpler times."

Part 2: Comparing Photos (1 minute each)

You receive two photos and must complete THREE tasks in just one minute:

The 3 Tasks

  1. Compare the two photos (similarities and differences)
  2. Speculate about the situation/people in the photos
  3. Answer the question printed above the photos
Time Management

Suggested split: 20 seconds comparing, 20 seconds speculating, 20 seconds answering the question. Don't spend too long describing - get to all three tasks!

Practice: Sample Photo Task

Photo A

Imagine: A person working alone at home, laptop on kitchen table, casual clothes, coffee cup, quiet environment

Photo B

Imagine: A busy open-plan office, multiple people at desks, professional attire, lots of activity and interaction

Question: How might the people be feeling about their work environment?

Practice speaking for 1 minute covering all three tasks: compare, speculate, and answer the question.

Model response:

"Both photos show people at work, but the environments couldn't be more different. Whereas the first photo shows someone working alone at home in quite a relaxed setting, the second one depicts a typical open-plan office with lots of colleagues around.

The person working from home appears to be quite comfortable and focused - judging by the casual clothes and the fact that they're in their kitchen, they might be enjoying the flexibility and peace. On the other hand, the office workers seem to be quite busy, and it looks as if there's a lot of collaboration happening, though it's possible some of them find the noise distracting.

In my view, the person at home probably feels more relaxed and in control of their environment, while the office workers might appreciate the social aspect but could sometimes find it harder to concentrate. I think the home worker would feel more satisfied with their setup, especially if they're introverted."

Responding to Your Partner (30 seconds)

After your partner finishes their long turn, the examiner will ask you a short question about their photos. Keep your response brief (about 30 seconds).

Which work environment would you prefer?
Personally, I'd prefer the home office setup. I find I'm much more productive when I have peace and quiet, and I love the flexibility of being able to take breaks whenever I need to. That said, I do think I'd miss the social interaction of an office sometimes.
Common Mistake

Don't give a one-word answer ("The first one") or speak for too long. Aim for 2-3 sentences with a reason.

Part 3: Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

You and your partner receive a diagram with a central question and several options around it. You must:

Two-Phase Structure

  1. Discussion (2 minutes): Discuss ALL the options with your partner
  2. Decision (1 minute): Try to reach an agreement together
Critical Strategy

You MUST discuss ALL options, not just your favorites. The examiner is testing your ability to consider different perspectives and interact with your partner. Skipping options loses you marks.

Two Things to Avoid

1. Dominating: Don't talk constantly without letting your partner speak. Ask for their opinion regularly.

2. Being Silent: Don't wait passively for your partner to lead. Take initiative, share ideas, and build on what they say.

STARTING Shall we start with...? / Where would you like to begin?
MOVING ON What about...? Shall we move on to...?
MOVING ON Let's consider... now. What do you think about that?
MOVING ON We haven't talked about... yet.
INVITING What do you think? / How do you feel about that?
INVITING Do you agree? / Would you say the same?
INVITING What's your view on this?
INVITING I'd be interested to hear what you think.
AGREEING I completely agree. And I'd add that...
AGREEING That's a really good point. Building on that...
AGREEING Exactly. And another thing to consider is...
PARTIAL I see what you mean, and I think...
DISAGREEING I see your point, but I'm not sure I agree because...
DISAGREEING That's an interesting perspective, but have you considered...?
DISAGREEING I understand where you're coming from, although I tend to think...
DISAGREEING I'm not entirely convinced. What about...?

Sample Task with Strategy Notes

Central Question: What makes a city a good place to live?

Discuss these factors and decide which TWO are most important:

Public transport Green spaces Job opportunities Cultural events Healthcare facilities

A: "Shall we start with public transport? I think this is quite fundamental because it affects everything else - getting to work, accessing services, even social life."

B: "I completely agree. Good transport makes a city much more accessible, especially for people who can't afford cars. What do you think about green spaces?"

A: "That's also important. I'd say it's crucial for mental health and wellbeing, especially in big cities where life can be quite stressful. Parks give people a place to relax and exercise."

B: "True. And they're good for families with children. What about job opportunities? I think without those, people can't really settle somewhere."

A: "That's a really good point. After all, most people need to work to afford living somewhere. I see your point, but I'm not sure it's the most important factor - some people work remotely nowadays."

B: "Fair enough. Let's consider cultural events. I think they make life more interesting, but would you say they're essential?"

A: "Not essential, perhaps, but they contribute to quality of life. What about healthcare?"

B: "I'd say healthcare is absolutely vital - it's something you can't do without when you need it."

A: "So if we had to choose two, I'd say healthcare and... perhaps public transport? What do you think?"

B: "I'd agree with healthcare. But I might choose job opportunities over transport. Can we compromise on healthcare and job opportunities?"

A: "Yes, I can agree to that. Healthcare and job opportunities it is."

Part 4: Discussion (5 minutes)

The examiner asks broader questions that develop the theme from Part 3. You discuss together with your partner - this is NOT an interview where you take turns.

What Examiners Want to See
  • Well-developed opinions with reasons and examples
  • Natural interaction - responding to what your partner says
  • Range of vocabulary and grammatical structures
  • Ability to discuss abstract ideas

Giving Opinions with Reasons

OPINION In my opinion... / From my point of view...
OPINION I firmly believe that... / I'm convinced that...
OPINION The way I see it... / As far as I'm concerned...
OPINION I'd argue that... / I would say that...
REASON The main reason for this is... / This is largely because...
EXAMPLE For instance... / Take... for example...
EXAMPLE A good example of this would be...
EVIDENCE I read somewhere that... / Research suggests that...
AGREEING I couldn't agree more. In fact, I'd go even further and say...
BUILDING That's a good point, and it relates to what you said about...
CONTRASTING That's interesting. I see it slightly differently, though...
QUESTIONING That's an interesting point. But don't you think...?
BALANCE On the one hand... but on the other hand...
BALANCE While it's true that..., we shouldn't forget that...
BALANCE There are certainly advantages, but there are also drawbacks...
CONCESSION I take your point about..., however...

Sample Part 4 Questions

Following the "What makes a city a good place to live?" theme:

Do you think cities are becoming too crowded?
How important is it for cities to have good public services?
Some people prefer to live in the countryside. Why do you think that is?
How might cities change in the future?

Practice: Extended Response

Record yourself answering this question for 45-60 seconds:

"Do you think young people today want different things from a city compared to older generations?"

Model answer: "I think there are definitely some differences, yes. Young people today seem to prioritize things like vibrant nightlife, networking opportunities, and access to fast internet - things that support both their social lives and careers in the digital economy. They're often drawn to cities with a strong startup culture or creative industries.

Older generations, on the other hand, might place more emphasis on factors like healthcare quality, safety, and perhaps proximity to nature. Though having said that, I think some priorities are universal - everyone wants affordable housing and good public transport, regardless of age.

What I find interesting is that with remote work becoming more common, even young people are starting to reconsider whether they need to live in expensive city centers at all. So these preferences might be shifting again."

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Giving one-word answers in Part 1 Always expand: Answer + Reason + Example
  • Only describing photos without comparing Use comparison phrases: "whereas", "unlike", "on the other hand"
  • Forgetting to speculate in Part 2 Use modal verbs: "might", "could", "appears to"
  • Not discussing ALL options in Part 3 Track which options you've covered - mention each one
  • Dominating the conversation Ask your partner: "What do you think?" regularly
  • Being too passive Take initiative - don't wait for your partner to lead everything
  • Disagreeing too directly Soften: "I see your point, but I tend to think..."
  • Running out of time in Part 2 Practice the 20-20-20 split: Compare, Speculate, Answer

Confidence Tips

1
Mistakes Are OK

Native speakers make mistakes too. Self-correct naturally and keep going.

2
It's Not a Competition

Help your partner succeed. Good interaction benefits both of you.

3
Be Yourself

Authentic answers are better than memorized responses. Your personality can shine.

4
Use Fillers Wisely

"Well...", "Let me think...", "That's an interesting question..." give you thinking time.

5
Eye Contact

Look at your partner and the examiner. It shows confidence and engagement.

6
Practice Out Loud

Speaking practice must be spoken! Record yourself and listen back.

  • Using a range of vocabulary and grammar
  • Interacting genuinely with your partner
  • Completing all tasks in each part
  • Staying calm and communicating clearly
