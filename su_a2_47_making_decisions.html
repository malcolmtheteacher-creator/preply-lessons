Asking for Opinions

Click each phrase to see when we use it.

"What's your opinion?"
Formal, in meetings and professional discussions
"What about you?"
Casual and friendly, in everyday conversation
"What's your position on...?"
Very formal, for serious or political topics
"What do you think?"
Neutral and most common in all situations

Giving Your Opinion

Click each phrase to see how to use it.

"I believe that..."
Strong and formal, shows conviction
"I feel that..."
Personal and emotional view
"Personally, I think..."
Emphasises that it's YOUR view, not a fact
"In my opinion..."
Classic opinion phrase, formal and clear

How to Agree

Click each phrase to see how strong the agreement is.

"Absolutely!"
Strong agreement, very positive
"Exactly!"
Total agreement, the person said it perfectly
"I couldn't agree more."
Very strong agreement, emphatic
"That's a very good point."
Polite agreement, shows respect for their idea
"You're absolutely right."
Strong and direct agreement

How to Disagree

Click each phrase to see how to disagree politely.

"I'm not so sure."
Polite and gentle disagreement
"I see your point, but..."
Acknowledges their idea, then disagrees
"That's not quite how I see it."
Diplomatic disagreement, less direct
"I disagree because..."
Direct but gives a reason (not rude)
Notice: In English, we often soften disagreement. "I'm not so sure" is more polite than "No, you're wrong."
