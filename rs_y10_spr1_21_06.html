Assessment: Christianity Practices Part 1

YEAR 10 · UNIT 21: CHRISTIANITY — PRACTICES (PART 1) · LESSON 6

What You'll Learn

Review and assess your understanding of Christian worship, prayer, baptism, the Eucharist, and sacraments

What is worship?

Answer: Showing love, respect, and devotion to God

Name two types of worship.

Answer: Liturgical, non-liturgical, and/or private

What does the Lord's Prayer teach us?

Answer: How to pray—praise God, ask for His will, ask for needs, forgive others, seek protection

What is baptism?

Answer: A ceremony using water as a sign of joining the Christian faith and being cleansed of sin

What is the Eucharist?

Answer: Communion—consuming bread and wine to remember Jesus's death and resurrection

Unit Review: Key Concepts

Lesson 1: Worship

  • Liturgical: Formal, set prayers (Catholic, Anglican, Orthodox)
  • Non-liturgical: Spontaneous, led by the Holy Spirit (Baptist, Pentecostal)
  • Private: Personal prayer and study alone
  • Key verse: Matthew 18:20

Lesson 2: Prayer

  • ACTS: Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving, Supplication
  • The Lord's Prayer: Jesus's model for how to pray
  • Set vs. spontaneous: Both valued; different traditions emphasize different types
  • Key verse: Philippians 4:6

Lesson 3: Baptism

  • Infant baptism: Removes original sin, makes child part of Church (Catholic, Anglican, Orthodox)
  • Believers' baptism: Full immersion; conscious personal choice (Baptist, Pentecostal)
  • Significance: Forgiveness, new life, Holy Spirit, Church membership
  • Key verses: Matthew 28:19, Acts 2:38, Romans 6:3–4

Lesson 4: The Eucharist

  • Catholic: Transubstantiation—bread and wine become body and blood
  • Protestant: Symbolic—bread and wine represent body and blood
  • Orthodox: Mystery—transformation happens but how is beyond explanation
  • Key verse: Luke 22:19–20, 1 Corinthians 11:23–26

Lesson 5: Sacraments

  • Catholic/Orthodox: Seven sacraments; means of grace
  • Protestant: Two sacraments (Baptism and Eucharist); signs of grace
  • Quaker/Salvation Army: No formal sacraments; all life can be sacramental
  • Purpose: Convey grace, mark transitions, build community, connect to Jesus

Quick Test

Answer these in 1–2 sentences each.

1. What is the difference between liturgical and non-liturgical worship?

Liturgical worship is formal with set prayers; non-liturgical is spontaneous and led by the Holy Spirit.

2. What do Catholics believe happens to the bread and wine in the Eucharist?

Transubstantiation—they literally become the body and blood of Christ.

3. Why do Baptists practice believers' baptism instead of infant baptism?

They believe baptism requires a conscious personal choice to follow Jesus, which only older children or adults can make.

4. What does ACTS stand for in prayer types?

Adoration, Confession, Thanksgiving, Supplication.

5. How many sacraments do Catholics recognize, and what is a sacrament?

Seven sacraments. A sacrament is a sacred action using physical signs (water, bread, oil) to convey God's grace.

12-Mark Evaluation Question

Question: "Sacraments are central to Christian faith and practice." Evaluate this statement with reference to different Christian traditions.

What to include in your answer:

For the statement (Catholic/Orthodox perspective):

  • Sacraments are means of grace—they deliver grace through their performance
  • Jesus instituted sacraments (baptism, Eucharist, others)
  • Seven sacraments cover all of Christian life—birth, maturity, marriage, sickness, death
  • The priest's authority to perform sacraments is central to Church structure
  • Without sacraments, believers can't access grace in the same way

Against the statement (Protestant perspective):

  • Only faith saves, not rituals or practices
  • Sacraments are good practices but not essential for salvation
  • The Reformation rejected the idea that priests control grace through sacraments
  • Only two sacraments were clearly commanded by Jesus (Baptism and Eucharist)
  • Grace comes through faith, not through external rituals

Alternative view (Quaker/Salvation Army):

  • They don't practice traditional sacraments
  • All of life can be sacramental if lived spiritually
  • Inner transformation and social action matter more than rituals
  • Proof that Christianity doesn't require formal sacraments to be authentic

Conclusion:

Different Christian traditions have fundamentally different views. For Catholics and Orthodox, sacraments are central. For Protestants, they're important but not essential. For Quakers and the Salvation Army, they're not part of their faith at all. Rather than being "central" to all Christianity, sacraments are central to some Christian traditions.

Unit 21 Summary: Christianity — Practices (Part 1)

This unit covered five key Christian practices:

Practice What It Is Why It Matters
Worship Showing love and respect to God in church or privately The central act of Christian community and faith
Prayer Speaking to God and listening for His guidance Direct communication with God; changes the person praying
Baptism Water ceremony marking entry into the faith Signifies cleansing from sin and new life in Christ
Eucharist/Communion Consuming bread and wine in remembrance of Jesus Connects believers to Jesus's sacrifice and to each other
Sacraments Sacred actions using physical signs to convey grace Mark important life transitions; connect believers to God's grace

Key Learning Points

  • Christian practice varies by tradition — Catholics, Anglicans, Orthodox, Protestants, Quakers, and others have different approaches
  • Physical and spiritual are connected — God works through material signs (water, bread, oil) to reach spiritual reality
  • Community and individual both matter — These practices are usually communal but also deeply personal
  • Scripture guides practice — Matthew 18:20, Matthew 6:9–13, Matthew 28:19, Luke 22:19–20 are all foundational
  • Tradition matters in some churches — Catholics and Orthodox value centuries of liturgical tradition; Protestants prioritize direct scripture and personal faith
