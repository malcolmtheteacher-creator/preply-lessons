2

Phrases for the table

Four jobs you'll need to do in a negotiation. Read them aloud once.

Proposing

  • What if we were to …?
  • How would you feel about …?
  • We'd like to suggest …
  • One option would be …

Trading

  • If you could …, we'd be willing to …
  • We could agree to that, provided that …
  • As long as …, we can …
  • In return, we'd need …

Holding firm

  • I'm afraid that's a bit beyond our budget.
  • That would be difficult for us.
  • I'm not in a position to go any lower.
  • That's a deal-breaker for us.

Summing up

  • So, just to summarise: …
  • Can we agree on …?
  • Where does that leave us?
  • I think we've got a deal.

Listen to how it sounds

AHow would you feel about a two-year cleaning contract at nine hundred a month? BI'm afraid that's a bit beyond our budget. We were thinking seven hundred. AWe could come down to eight hundred, provided that you sign for three years. BThree years is a long time. But if you could include the windows, we'd be willing to consider it. AAs long as it's once a month, we can do that. BSo, just to summarise: three years, eight hundred a month, windows once a month. I think we've got a deal.

Which job is the phrase doing?

These are new phrases. Say which group each one belongs to.

Soften the demand

Say each one again so it keeps its meaning but sounds professional. Then compare.

Key words

concession (noun) — something you give up or agree to in order to reach an agreement. Example: "We made a small concession on the start date."
counter-offer (noun) — a different offer you make after rejecting the first one. Example: "They asked for £10,000, so we made a counter-offer of £7,000."
bottom line (noun) — the lowest or highest point you will accept. Example: "£500 a month is our bottom line; we can't go any lower."
deal-breaker (noun) — one condition that would make you refuse the whole agreement. Example: "Working weekends is a deal-breaker for me."
non-negotiable (adjective) — fixed; you will not discuss changing it. Example: "The delivery date is non-negotiable."
trade-off (noun) — a balance where you accept less of one thing to get more of another. Example: "A lower price in return for a longer contract is a fair trade-off."
leverage (noun) — power to influence the other side because they need something from you. Example: "They need the band more than we need the festival, so we have leverage."
flexible (adjective) — willing to change your position. Example: "We're flexible on the dates, but not on the fee."
walk away (phrasal verb) — leave a negotiation without agreeing. Example: "If they don't improve the offer, we'll walk away."
