Words for Frequency
Never 0% — not at any time
Hardly ever about 5% — very rarely
Rarely about 30% — not often
Sometimes about 50% — at some times but not all
Usually about 60% — most of the time
Often about 85% — many times
Always 100% — all of the time

Adverbs of Frequency

Learn how to place these adverbs in sentences. The position changes the meaning!

Word Order Rules

With "always", "often", "usually", "rarely", "hardly ever":

Place after the subject and before the main verb

I never work on Sundays.

She rarely takes a lunch break.

They always arrive early.

With "sometimes", "usually":

Can go at the beginning, middle, or end of the sentence

Sometimes I work from home.

I sometimes work from home.

I work from home sometimes.

Real Workplace Problems

Read about these common issues. Which ones are familiar to you? Which are most serious?

Desk Eating

People eat snacks or lunch at their desks during work.

Impact: Can be untidy, smelly, or distracting for others.

Possible solution: Eat in a designated break room.

Broken Equipment

Machines or equipment stop working (water cooler, printer, photocopier).

Impact: Slows down work and frustrates everyone.

Possible solution: Have a maintenance schedule.

Noise Issues

The office is too loud. People talk, phones ring, or there is background noise.

Impact: Hard to concentrate on focused work.

Possible solution: Create quiet zones or use headphones.

Room Booking

Meeting rooms are always booked. No space available when you need it.

Impact: Hard to hold private conversations or meetings.

Possible solution: Share a booking system (online or paper).

Temperature Control

The office is sometimes too hot and sometimes too cold.

Impact: Uncomfortable working conditions.

Possible solution: Adjust settings by season or allow layers.

Lighting

Few windows means little natural light in the office.

Impact: Dark workspace affects mood and energy.

Possible solution: Add brighter lights or time outside.

Role-Play Task

You are going to have a conversation about a workplace problem. One person is the employee, the other is the boss or manager.

What you need to do:

  • Explain the problem clearly
  • Say how often it happens (use adverbs)
  • Suggest at least one solution
  • Listen and agree or ask questions

Remember to use:

  • Starting phrases: "Do you have a minute?", "I have a problem I want to discuss…"
  • Adverbs: "It always happens…", "It is usually…", "I rarely…"
  • Suggestions: "What do you suggest?", "We can…"
  • Agreement: "I think you are right."
