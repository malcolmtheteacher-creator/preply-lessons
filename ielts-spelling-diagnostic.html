The Uncomfortable Truth About IELTS Spelling

Let's start with the reality that most students don't want to hear:

Spelling errors directly affect your Lexical Resource score.

The IELTS band descriptors say Band 7 requires "occasional errors in spelling" while Band 6 allows "some errors in spelling." The difference between "occasional" and "some" can be the difference between getting into your university or not.

Spelling problems are fixable.

Your Five Error Patterns

Click each pattern to understand it better:

🔴 Pattern 1: Phonetic Spelling — Your biggest problem ▼

You're spelling words how they SOUND, not how they're written. This is the #1 sign of someone who learned English through listening.

  • defineatly → definitely (you hear "eat" but it's "ite")
  • becouse → because (you hear "ow" but it's "au")
  • threw → through (you wrote the wrong homophone)
  • sceared → scared (you hear "ear" but there's no 'e')
  • acheve → achieve (missing the 'i')
🟠 Pattern 2: Truncated Words — Missing endings ▼

You're dropping final letters—probably because you're writing fast and your hand can't keep up with your brain, or you don't hear the endings clearly.

  • dor → door
  • glas → glass
  • wil → will
  • pocet → pocket
  • breez → breeze
🟡 Pattern 3: Vowel Confusion — Unstressed syllables ▼

English has a "schwa" sound (ə) that can be spelled with ANY vowel. You're guessing—and guessing wrong.

  • rout → route
  • exibit → exhibit
  • mater → matter
  • retriving → retrieving
  • relised → realised
🟢 Pattern 4: Double Consonants — When to double? ▼

You're inconsistent with doubled letters—sometimes adding them when you shouldn't, sometimes missing them.

  • priceles → priceless
  • controll → control
  • dissapear → disappear
  • carfuly → carefully
🔵 Pattern 5: Homophones — Sound the same, spelled differently ▼

Words that sound identical but have different spellings and meanings.

  • threw → through (threw = past of throw)
  • write → right (write = to put words on paper)

How To Fix Each Pattern

Different error types need different fixes. Here's what works for YOUR patterns:

🔴 For Phonetic Spelling: Visual Anchoring

Since you're spelling by ear, you need to train your EYES instead. Try these:

  • Look-Cover-Write-Check: Look at the word for 10 seconds. Cover it. Write it. Check. Repeat until perfect.
  • Word Shapes: Notice the shape—definitely has a "tall-short-tall" pattern (d-e-f). Draw the outline.
  • Etymology Hooks: "Definitely" comes from "finite" (meaning limited/defined). If you can spell "finite," you can spell "definitely."
  • Read More: Even 10 minutes of reading daily builds visual word memory automatically.

🟠 For Truncated Words: Slow Down + Proofread

This is likely a speed problem. Your brain knows "door" but your hand writes "dor."

  • Finger Tracking: When proofreading, point at each word with your finger. This forces slower reading.
  • Read Backwards: Read your essay backwards (last word first). This breaks meaning and forces you to see each word.
  • Whisper Test: Whisper each word as you proofread. You'll hear missing sounds.

🟡 For Vowel Confusion: Morpheme Awareness

Learn word PARTS, not whole words. The vowels often come from Latin/Greek roots.

  • Learn Roots: "exhibit" = ex (out) + hibit (hold) → "hold out for display"
  • Word Families: heart → hearty → heartfelt → sweetheart (the 'ea' stays constant)
  • Related Words: Can't spell "definitely"? Think "finite" or "definition"—the 'i' is clearer there.

🟢 For Double Consonants: Learn The Rules

There ARE rules for doubling. Here are the main ones:

  • 1-1-1 Rule: One syllable + one vowel + one consonant = double when adding suffix (run → running)
  • Stress Rule: If stress is on final syllable, double (beGIN → beginning). If not, don't (OPEN → opening)
  • -less/-ness/-ly: These suffixes DON'T change the base word (price + less = priceless, NOT priceles)

🔵 For Homophones: Meaning Links

Connect the spelling to the meaning:

  • through/threw: "Through" has "rough" in it—going through something can be rough.
  • right/write: "Write" has 'w' like 'word'—you write words.
  • Make Sentences: "I threw the ball through the window" — use both in one sentence.
