A Few Famous Heists

Throughout history, certain crimes have captured the public imagination. Three of the most famous robberies are:

The Millennium Dome Diamond Heist (2000)

Thieves targeted the world-famous Millennium Dome in London, attempting to steal eleven diamonds on display for the millennium celebrations. The robbery was dramatic — involving a speedboat getaway on the Thames, a disguised entry, and ultimately, a police ambush. The gang was caught, and the diamonds were recovered.
The Antwerp Diamond Heist (2003)

One of the largest diamond heists ever, this robbery targeted the Antwerp Diamond Centre in Belgium. Thieves made off with nearly £5 million in diamonds and jewellery in what some call the "Heist of the Century."
The Hatton Garden Safe Deposit Burglary (2015)

Over the Easter weekend, a gang of criminals — mostly in their 60s and 70s — broke into the Hatton Garden Safe Deposit Centre in London, one of the world's most secure vaults. Using heavy drill equipment, they breached the vault and made off with approximately £14 million in jewellery and valuables. CCTV footage eventually led to arrests and convictions.

In this lesson, you'll build your own accounts of two of these real-life crimes.

Narrative Tenses in Crime Stories

To tell a compelling crime story, you need to show what happened clearly. You do this using three narrative tenses:

Which tense, when?

Past Simple For the main events of the story ("The thieves broke into the vault and took the diamonds.")
Past Continuous For what was happening in the background or at the same time ("The security guard was sleeping when they arrived.")
Past Perfect For things that happened before the main story ("They had planned the robbery for six months.")

Compare with the Real Story

Click to reveal: The Real Millennium Dome Heist

What Actually Happened (2000):

On November 10, 2000, a gang of thieves attempted to break into the Millennium Dome in Greenwich, London. They had planned a heist to steal eleven diamonds from the De Beers Diamond Exhibition. The criminals used a variety of methods: they arrived in a van, used a digger (excavating equipment) to breach the walls, and had even arranged a speedboat getaway on the Thames to escape. However, the security at the Dome was exceptional. CCTV cameras captured every movement, and the police had been tipped off. Armed officers ambushed the gang before they could complete the robbery. The diamonds were never stolen, and the gang was arrested. Most were later convicted.

How close was your story? Did you predict the CCTV failure? The getaway plan? The police trap?

Click to reveal: The Real Hatton Garden Burglary

What Actually Happened (2015):

Over the Easter weekend (April 2-3, 2015), a gang of burglars — mostly retired men in their 60s and 70s — broke into the Hatton Garden Safe Deposit Centre in London. The basement vault, which had housed valuable jewellery and gems for decades, was targeted. Using industrial drills, the gang penetrated the vault's wall, accessing deposit boxes containing approximately £14 million in jewellery. Despite having removed CCTV footage before the break-in, the thieves were eventually identified through other CCTV cameras and arrested. In 2016, members of the gang received sentences ranging from years to decades in prison.

How close was your story? Did you guess the team's age? The drill method? The amount stolen?
