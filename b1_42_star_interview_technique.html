STAR Interview Technique

B1 Level · Work & Interview Focus

What is the STAR Technique?

STAR is a method to answer interview questions using a clear structure. It helps you give complete, organized answers that show your skills and experience.

STAR stands for:

S Situation: Describe the background and context. Where were you? What was happening?

T Task: Explain what you needed to do. What was the problem or goal?

A Action: Say what you did to solve the problem. What specific steps did you take?

R Result: Tell the outcome. What happened? What did you learn?

Why Use STAR?

Organized Your answer has a clear beginning, middle, and end
Evidence You show real examples of what you can do
Confident You sound prepared and sure of what you say
Complete You answer the whole question, not just part of it

Interview Vocabulary

responsibility A duty or task that you are expected to do
challenge A difficult problem or task
teamwork Working together with other people
achievement Something you did successfully
outcome The final result of something
skill An ability you have learned and can do well

What are three situations from your own life that you could use as STAR examples? (School, work, family, hobbies, etc.)

Example 1: Working in a Team

Interview Question: "Tell me about a time you worked in a team."

S Situation: I was in a school project group.

T Task: We needed to make a presentation.

A Action: I helped make the slides and gave ideas.

R Result: We finished on time and got a good grade.

Example 2: Solving a Problem

Interview Question: "Describe a time you solved a problem."

S Situation: My printer didn't work before class.

T Task: I needed to give my homework to the teacher.

A Action: I emailed my work and explained the problem.

R Result: The teacher said thank you and gave full marks.

Example 3: Helping Others

Interview Question: "Have you ever helped a friend?"

S Situation: My friend didn't understand the homework.

T Task: I wanted to help him learn.

A Action: I explained the questions slowly.

R Result: He did better in the next class.

Example 4: Learning Something New

Interview Question: "Tell me about a time you learned something new."

S Situation: I started using Excel for the first time.

T Task: I had to make a list for class.

A Action: I watched a video and practiced.

R Result: Now I can use it easily.

Example 5: Being Responsible

Interview Question: "Describe a time you were responsible."

S Situation: I had to take care of my little brother.

T Task: My parents went out.

A Action: I cooked for him and helped with homework.

R Result: He was happy and my parents were proud.

Look at all 5 examples:

Notice how each answer has the same structure. Each is short (4-5 sentences), clear, and complete. The result shows what you learned or achieved.

Create Your Own STAR Answer

Think of a real situation from your life and prepare a STAR answer. You will practice speaking it aloud.

Step 1: Choose Your Question

Pick one question, or use your own:

• Tell me about a time you worked in a team

• Describe a time you solved a problem

• Have you ever helped someone? Tell me about it

• Tell me about a time you learned something new

• Describe a time you were responsible

Step 2: Plan Your STAR Answer

Write short notes for each part. Be specific!

Step 4: Example - Putting It Together

Here is how your STAR answer sounds when spoken:

"I was in a school project group where we had to make a presentation on climate change. The challenge was that we only had two weeks and our group had very different ideas. I suggested we have a planning meeting, and I organized the research tasks. Each person took one topic, and I helped coordinate everything. We finished on time, our presentation was well-organized, and we got a really good grade. From this, I learned that communication and planning are important for successful teamwork."

Practice tip:

Say your STAR answer out loud several times before the real interview. This helps you feel confident and natural. You don't need to memorize it word-for-word—just practice the key points!
