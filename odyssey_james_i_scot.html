The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. He spent 18 years in captivity.
captivity (n) — being a prisoner.
2. The Kingis Quair is a long poem.
a poem (n) — a piece of writing in verse.
3. His ransom was 60,000 merks.
a ransom (n) — money paid for release.
4. Back home he was ruthless.
ruthless (adj) — without pity.
5. He executed Albany's family.
to execute (v) — to kill as punishment.
6. Assassins broke into his rooms.
an assassin (n) — a person who kills an important person.
7. He hid in a drain.
a drain (n) — a pipe or channel for waste water.
8. The door had no bolt.
a bolt (n) — a bar to lock a door.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — A prisoner in England

Captured at sea in 1406, James was held in England for eighteen years. He was educated at the English court and even fought for Henry V in France. He fell in love with Joan Beaufort, a cousin of the English king, and is believed to have written The Kingis Quair, a poem about seeing her from his prison window.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How long was he a prisoner?

2. What is The Kingis Quair?

1. Eighteen years.

2. A poem, probably by James, about falling in love with Joan.

Discuss:
  • What can captivity teach a future leader?

Part 2 — Home and revenge

In 1424 James was released for a ransom and returned with Joan. His uncle Albany had died, but Albany's son Murdoch had ruled in his absence. In 1425 James had Murdoch and two sons executed. He reformed laws, raised taxes, and held parliaments often.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did he do to Murdoch?

2. Name one reform.

1. Had him and two sons executed in 1425.

2. New laws, taxes or regular parliaments.

Discuss:
  • Was his revenge justice or cruelty?

Part 3 — Perth

On the night of 20–21 February 1437, conspirators broke into the Blackfriars in Perth. James escaped into a drain under the floor, but its outlet had been blocked — reportedly so tennis balls would not be lost — and he was killed. A later legend says Catherine Douglas barred the door with her arm, earning the name "Kate Barlass".

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why couldn't James escape?

2. Who was "Kate Barlass"?

1. The drain's outlet had been blocked.

2. In legend, Catherine Douglas, who used her arm as a door bar.

Discuss:
  • What does the drain story say about chance in history?

Grammar — Past Perfect & Sequencing

History is a chain of causes. To show that one thing happened before another in the past, English uses the past perfect.

How it works:
• Past simple tells the main line: "The Danes attacked at Christmas."
• Past perfect (had + past participle) reaches further back: "By then, Alfred had already escaped into the marshes."
• Signal words: by the time, already, before, after, when, until.

1. Past simple or past perfect?

Complete each sentence about James I. Use the word in brackets.

1. By 1424, James (spend) 18 years in England.

2. Before he returned, Albany (die).

3. He knew Murdoch (rule) in his absence.

1. had spent

2. had died

3. had ruled

2. Put the earlier event in the past perfect

1. The drain (be) blocked days before.

2. By the time guards arrived, the assassins (escape).

3. He (write) a poem before he became a ruler.

1. had been

2. had escaped

3. had written

Going deeper

James I returned determined to make the crown strong. His harshness made enemies; his reforms made a stronger state.

Worth noticing: Medieval "tennis" was real tennis, an indoor game played by royals.
Learning from the past — for today: Use the waiting time. James turned captivity into an education. When life puts you on pause, learn the skills you'll need when it starts again.
