Gruffydd ap Llywelyn

The only king who ever ruled all of Wales — until his own men killed him.

The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. Legend says he was idle as a youth.
idle (adj) — lazy, not working.
2. He became ambitious.
ambitious (adj) — wanting success and power.
3. He unified Wales.
to unify (v) — to make into one.
4. He burned Hereford.
to burn (v) — to destroy by fire.
5. Harold led a campaign into Wales.
a campaign (n) — a series of military actions.
6. His own men betrayed him.
to betray (v) — to be disloyal.
7. His widow later married Harold.
a widow (n) — a woman whose husband has died.
8. He was ruthless with rivals.
ruthless (adj) — without pity.

  • Using any four of these words, predict: what kind of reign do you think this will be — and how might it end?
  • Which words belong to power and which to danger? Sort them out loud and say why.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — From idle to king

A story told by Walter Map, writing over a century later, says Gruffydd was lazy as a young man until a remark made him determined to succeed. It is probably a legend. What is certain is that in 1039 he became king of Gwynedd and Powys and defeated an English army at Rhyd-y-groes on the Severn.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who tells the "idle youth" story?

2. What happened in 1039?

1. Walter Map, writing over a century later.

2. He became king and defeated an English army.

Discuss:
  • Do people really change suddenly?

Part 2 — All Wales

Gruffydd fought his Welsh rivals ruthlessly. By about 1055 he controlled the south as well, becoming the only king to rule the whole of Wales. In 1055, with English allies, he burned Hereford. He married Ealdgyth, daughter of the Earl of Mercia.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did he achieve by 1055?

2. What happened to Hereford?

1. He ruled all of Wales.

2. He burned it.

Discuss:
  • Is it worth being ruthless to unite a country?

Part 3 — Betrayed

In 1063 Harold Godwinson and his brother Tostig attacked Wales by land and sea. Gruffydd was hunted, and in August 1063 he was killed by his own men. His head was sent to Harold. Ealdgyth later married Harold — and three years later, Harold died at Hastings.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who attacked in 1063?

2. How did Gruffydd die?

1. Harold Godwinson and Tostig.

2. Killed by his own men.

Discuss:
  • Why might his followers turn against him?

Grammar — Third Conditional — What If?

History invites the great question: what if? The third conditional imagines a different past and its different result.

How it works:
• Form: If + past perfect, would have + past participle.
• "If the wind had changed a day earlier, William would have landed first."
• Use might have / could have for less certainty.

1. Complete the conditional

Complete each sentence about Gruffydd ap Llywelyn. Use the word in brackets.

1. If he (not fight) his rivals, he wouldn't have ruled all Wales.

2. If Harold (not attack), Gruffydd might have lived longer.

3. If his men (stay) loyal, he could have escaped.

1. hadn't fought

2. hadn't attacked

3. had stayed

2. Mixed practice — what if?

1. Wales (remain) united if he had had a strong heir.

2. If he (not burn) Hereford, the English might have left him alone.

3. If Ealdgyth (not marry) Harold, she wouldn't have lost two kings.

1. might have remained

2. hadn't burned

3. hadn't married

Going deeper

Gruffydd's unity was a one-man achievement. After his death, Wales split again, just before the Normans arrived.

Worth noticing: His wife Ealdgyth was the widow of two kings who died violently within three years.
Learning from the past — for today: Loyalty must be earned, not forced. Gruffydd won by fear, and fear disappears in a crisis. Build a team that wants to stay with you when things get hard.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Was Gruffydd a hero?
  • Why did unity not last?
  • What makes people loyal?
