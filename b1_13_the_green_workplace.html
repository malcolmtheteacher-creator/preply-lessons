How Green Is Your Workplace?

True or False Quiz

How much do you know about being green at work? Read each statement and decide if it is true or false. Discuss your answers.

1. Approximately 40% of electricity in the average office is used for lighting.

True. Lighting uses a lot of energy in most offices.

2. In the UK, the recommended workplace temperature in winter is 23 degrees centigrade.

False. The recommended temperature is 19 degrees centigrade.

3. In the average office, each person uses about 500 sheets of paper a month.

False. The estimate is 1,000 sheets per person per month.

4. Low energy light bulbs cost more to buy than normal ones, but they also use more energy.

False. They may cost more to buy, but they use 20% of the energy of normal bulbs and last much longer.

5. It uses a lot of energy to turn your computer on and off, so you should leave it on all day.

False. Starting your computer uses about the same energy as leaving it running for 3 minutes.

6. You should turn fluorescent lights off when you leave the room for more than 15 minutes.

True. Turning lights off saves energy when no one is in the room.

7. A one-degree change in the thermostat setting can save 10% on the office energy bill.

True. Small temperature changes make a big difference to energy costs.

Expressing Opinions About Being Green

Click on each phrase to see how it is used. These expressions help you talk about what you think about green issues at work.

GIVING ADVICE
It is better to give old computers to charity rather than throw them away.
Use "It is better to..." to recommend a good action
STATING OPINIONS
You should turn your computer off when you are not using it.
Use "should" to say what you think people ought to do
CONDITIONAL ADVICE
If you wear warm clothes, you can turn the heating down a few degrees.
Use "if...can" to show how one action makes another possible
BENEFITS
You can save money if you walk to work rather than drive.
Show the positive result of a green action
WHAT NOT TO DO
You should not leave the lights on when the room is empty.
Use "should not" to say something is a bad idea
RECOMMENDATIONS
It is a good idea to recycle paper at work.
Use "It is a good idea to..." for positive suggestions
PERSONAL OPINION
As far as I am concerned, managers should set an example to their employees.
Use "As far as I am concerned" to give a personal viewpoint

How to Be Green at Work

Read about different ways to create a green workplace. Which ideas are already part of your work or school?

Paper and Office Supplies
Recycling Bins
Put separate recycling bins for paper, cardboard, and plastic in the office. Most offices throw away too much waste that could be recycled.
Recycled Paper
Use recycled paper and ask employees to print on both sides of the paper. This saves trees and reduces costs.
Machines and Equipment
Low Energy Light Bulbs
Replace normal light bulbs with low energy bulbs. They cost more at first but use much less electricity and last longer.
Computer Settings
Turn off computers at the end of the day. Use sleep mode on screens. Computers use energy even when they are not being used.
Energy and Transport
Adjust the Temperature
Set the heating to 19 degrees in winter. Small changes in temperature save a lot of energy and money.
Encourage Walking and Cycling
Create a safe bicycle parking area. Offer incentives for employees who walk or cycle to work rather than drive.
Video Conferencing
Use video meetings instead of travelling for business meetings. This saves fuel and reduces travel time.
Food and Drink
Water Dispensers
Install water dispensers so employees can use reusable cups instead of plastic bottles. This reduces plastic waste.
Reusable Cups
Provide reusable cups for coffee and tea. Many offices throw away thousands of plastic cups every year.
Facilities
Open Plan Offices
Open plan offices with fewer walls need less heating and cooling. They also help teams communicate better.
Natural Light
Use more windows and natural light instead of electric light. This saves energy and helps people feel healthier.

Create a Green Workplace Plan

Think about what makes a workplace green. Prepare to discuss your ideas about personal green actions and company green policies.

What you need to think about:

  • What can you personally do to be green at work?
  • What can your company or school do to create a green workplace?
  • Which green ideas are most important?
  • What are the benefits of being green?

Remember to use:

  • Giving advice: "It is better to...", "You should...", "You should not..."
  • Conditional: "If you..., you can..."
  • Personal opinion: "As far as I am concerned...", "I think..."
