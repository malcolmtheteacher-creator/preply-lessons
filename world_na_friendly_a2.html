Friendly Americans, Fake Smiles?

Americans smile and say “How are you?” but do not wait for an answer. Are they fake?

Today: you will learn why Americans smile and ask “How are you?”, and use questions with how and what.
Talk first:
  • Do you smile at strangers?
  • Do you say “How are you?” to people?
  • Is a smile always real?

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. She gave me a big smile.
smile (n) — a happy look on your face. Example: “She gave me a big smile.”
2. I made small talk in the lift.
small talk (n) — light, polite conversation. Example: “I made small talk in the lift.”
3. His smile is fake.
fake (adj) — not real. Example: “His smile is fake.”
4. The shop assistant was very friendly.
friendly (adj) — kind and open with people. Example: “The shop assistant was very friendly.”
5. Americans often smile at a stranger.
stranger (n) — a person you do not know. Example: “Americans often smile at a stranger.”
6. He did not wait for my answer.
answer (n) — what you say when someone asks a question. Example: “He did not wait for my answer.”

The stereotype: “Americans are friendly but fake. Their smiles mean nothing.”

The story

Part 1 — “How are you?”

In the USA, “How are you?” is often just a greeting. A shop assistant says it and expects “Good, thanks. You?” They do not want a long answer. Visitors from some countries find it strange. For many Americans, it is a friendly way to say hello.

Quick check:

1. What does the shop assistant expect?

2. Is “How are you?” a real question in the USA?

1. “Good, thanks. You?”

2. Often it is only a greeting.

Discuss:
  • What do you say when someone asks “How are you?”

Part 2 — Peach and coconut

Trainers use a picture. Americans are “peaches”: soft and friendly on the outside, but the “stone” of close friendship is hard to reach. People in Germany or Russia are “coconuts”: hard on the outside, but friendships are deep.

Quick check:

1. What is an American in this picture?

2. Who are the coconuts?

1. A peach.

2. People in countries like Germany or Russia.

Discuss:
  • Are you a peach or a coconut?

Part 3 — Why they smile

A 2015 study said that countries built by immigrants, like the USA and Canada, value smiling more. When people do not share a language, a smile is a fast, clear sign: “I am friendly.” The American smile is about building trust.

Quick check:

1. Which countries value smiling more?

2. What does a smile show?

1. Countries built by immigrants, like the USA and Canada.

2. That you are friendly.

Discuss:
  • When is a smile useful?

Grammar — questions with how and what

How + adjective or verb: “How are you?” “How do you feel?”
What + noun: “What do you do?” “What time is it?”
Answer short: “Good, thanks. And you?”
