Renting a Flat

You want a new home. Today you look at a flat. Time to walk around, ask some questions, and talk about the rent — in simple, polite English.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

You want to rent a new flat. You looked at many photos online, and this one looks nice. It has one bedroom, a small kitchen, and a little balcony. It is above a bakery, so it smells like fresh bread!

Now you are here to see it. A person from the office — the agent — opens the door. You have some time to look around. You want to see if the flat is good, ask about the money, and check for any problems.

The rent is a little high for you. So, if you like the flat, you can ask a small question: "Can the rent be a little lower?" It is OK to ask. Many people ask. The worst answer is only "no".

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you rent your home, or does your family own it? What do you like about the place where you live?
  2. When you look for a new home, what is most important for you — the price, the rooms, or the area? Why?

Who's who

You
The Renter 🔑

Friendly and polite. You want a nice home. You look around, ask about the money, say what is wrong, and ask about the rent.

The other person
The Agent 🏠

Kind and busy. They show you the flat and answer your questions. If you are polite, they will help you — and maybe ask the owner about the rent.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the polite words: please, thank you, can I, I would like. Small words, big help.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the polite words.

AgentHello! Come in, please. So, this is the flat. It has one bedroom, a kitchen, and a small balcony. What do you think?
YouIt's nice! I like it. It's bigger than the photos. Can I look around, please?
AgentOf course! Look everywhere. The kitchen is new, and the flat is warm in winter.
YouGood, thank you. Can I ask a question? How much are the bills? I want to know the real price every month.
AgentSure. The bills are about eighty pounds a month — that is gas, electric, and water.
YouOK, thank you. And is the internet included?
AgentNo, the internet is not included. You pay for that. It is about twenty-five pounds a month.
YouI see. Hmm, there is a problem with the window. It doesn't close well, and the room is a little cold.
AgentAh, yes. I'm sorry about that. The owner can fix the window next week. I will write it down.
YouThank you. Can you fix it before I move in? I would like that.
AgentYes, no problem. We can fix it first, before you move in.
YouThat's very kind. I like the flat a lot. But the rent is a little high for me. Can the rent be a little lower?
AgentHmm. The price is good for this area… but I can ask the owner. Maybe we can meet in the middle.
YouThank you so much. Can I take your number? I will think about it tonight and call you tomorrow.

From memory — don't look up!

1. The renter asks about two costs on top of the rent. What are they?

2. What is the problem in the flat? What does the agent say they will do about it?

3. The rent is a little high. What does the renter ask, and what does the agent say they will do?

1. The bills (gas, electric, and water — about eighty pounds a month), and the internet (not included — about twenty-five pounds a month).
2. The window doesn't close well, so the room is a little cold. The agent says the owner can fix it next week, and they will fix it before the renter moves in.
3. The renter asks if the rent can be a little lower. The agent says the price is good, but they will ask the owner, and maybe they can meet in the middle.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use at any flat viewing. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Ask, don't demand. It is OK to ask about money and problems — but ask in a soft, friendly way. Use please, can I, and thank you. A calm voice and a smile help a lot. You do not need long sentences — short and polite is perfect.

🚪 Looking around

Phrase 1

You are at the door. You want to walk around the flat and see all the rooms. Complete it: "Can I ___ around, please?"

"Can I look around, please?"
"Look around" means walk about and see everything. "Can I…, please?" is a polite way to ask. You can use it for lots of things: "Can I sit here, please?", "Can I open the window, please?"
Phrase 2

You walk in and you like the flat. You want to say something nice and friendly first. Which one is warm and polite?

A. "It's OK, I suppose."
B. "It's nice! I like it."
C. "Is this really the flat?"
B — "It's nice! I like it."
A warm start makes the agent happy to help you. A sounds cold, and C sounds like you don't believe them. A little kindness at the start makes everything easier.

💷 Asking about money

Phrase 3

The rent is not the only cost. Gas, electric, and water cost money too. You want to know how much. Complete it: "How much are the ___?"

"How much are the bills?"
The bills are the money you pay for gas, electric, and water. Ask this before you say yes — then you know the real price every month, not just the rent.
Phrase 4

You want to know if the internet comes with the flat, or if you pay for it. Put the words in order:

the internetisincluded?
"Is the internet included?"
"Included" means it comes with the flat — you do not pay extra. Use it for many things: "Is breakfast included?", "Is parking included?"

🔧 A problem

Phrase 5

You see that the window does not close well. You want to tell the agent, in a calm and polite way. Complete it: "There is a ___ with the window."

"There is a problem with the window."
"There is a problem with…" is the calm, friendly way to say something is wrong. You are not angry — you just want them to know. Use it for anything: "There is a problem with the door", "There is a problem with the shower".
Phrase 6

The agent says the owner can fix the window. You want it done before you move in. Complete it: "Can you ___ it before I move in?"

"Can you fix it before I move in?"
"Fix" means make it work again. "Before I move in" is important — you want the problem gone first, not later. Asking now, in a polite way, saves you trouble later.

🤝 Talking about the rent

Phrase 7

You like the flat, but the rent is a bit too much for you. You want to say this without being rude about the flat. Which one is polite?

A. "This flat is way too expensive."
B. "No, that's too much money."
C. "I like the flat, but the rent is a little high for me."
C — "I like the flat, but the rent is a little high for me."
Say something nice first ("I like the flat"), then the small "but". "A little high for me" is soft and about your money, not their price. A and B sound like a fight — and then people don't want to help you.
Phrase 8

Now, ask the big question in a soft, polite way: can the rent go down a little? (Say it out loud first, then check.)

"Can the rent be a little lower?"
"A little lower" is soft — you are not asking for a lot, just a small change. You can also say: "Is a lower rent possible?" It is always OK to ask nicely. The agent can only say yes or no.

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work at any flat, and in shops and cafés too. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"How much are the ___?" — What price do you want to ask about next time? (the bills, the tickets, the shoes…)

"There is a problem with ___." — Think of a thing that can go wrong at home. What is it?

"Can the ___ be a little lower?" — What price would you like to ask about? (the rent, the price…)

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — look around → ask about the bills → say the problem → ask about the rent → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your flat viewing. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the renter 🔑

The agent speaks — you answer. Be polite: use please, thank you, and can I.

AgentOf course, look everywhere. The kitchen is new and the flat is warm.
AgentAbout eighty pounds a month for gas, electric, and water.
AgentNo, the internet is extra — about twenty-five pounds a month.
AgentAh, sorry about that. The owner can fix it next week.
AgentYes, no problem, we can do that first. So — do you like the flat?
AgentHmm, I can ask the owner. Maybe we can meet in the middle.

Round 2 — now YOU are the agent 🏠

Change places. Now you show the flat. Be friendly and helpful. Say "Welcome", "Of course", "No problem", "I'm sorry".

The Twist

🚗 There is no parking

One more time — no help this round. You love the flat, but there is a problem: there is no place to park your car. The street is very busy. You ask the agent: "Is there parking for the flat?" and they say: "No, sorry, there is no parking here." What do you say? Stay polite and friendly. Maybe ask about parking near the flat, or ask if the rent can be lower because of it. Try to leave happy. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said something nice about the flat first.
  • I asked about the bills and the internet.
  • I told them the problem in a calm, polite way.
  • I asked about the rent nicely, without a fight.
  • I said "thank you" and stayed friendly the whole time.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

Your flat is above a bakery — a shop that makes bread. That is a nice thought. But bakeries have a long story in London, and not always a happy one.

In 1666, a small fire started in a baker's shop in London. The houses then were made of wood, and they were very close together. The fire grew and grew. In four days, it burned most of the old city — thousands of homes were gone.

After the fire, people built a new city. This time they used brick, not wood, and they made wider streets. When you stand in a flat and talk about the rent, you are part of a very old London story — of homes, owners, and people looking for a place to live.

Two questions to talk about

  1. The Great Fire started small, in one shop, and grew very big. Have you ever seen a small problem grow into a big one? What happened?
  2. After the fire, London built a new and better city. When something old is gone, is it a chance to build something better? Or do we miss the old thing too much?
