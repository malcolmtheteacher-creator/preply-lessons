The butterfly effect

A small event happens. What happens next? And then what? And then what? You take turns building a chain of consequences — each one caused by the previous one.

The rules: Person A says what happens because of the event. Person B says what happens because of THAT. Keep alternating. Each link must be a logical (or creative) consequence of the previous one. 20 seconds per link. How long can you keep the chain going?

Example

Starting Event
"You oversleep by 30 minutes."
1
Person A
"So I skip breakfast and run to the bus stop."
2
Person B
"But because you ran, you drop your phone on the pavement and crack the screen."
3
Person A
"So now I can't check my emails, and I miss a message from my boss saying the meeting was moved to 8am."
4
Person B
"You arrive at the empty meeting room. Your colleagues are all in a different room. You look terrible."

...and so on. See how one small event spirals?

Cause & effect language

"So because of that..." "Which means that..." "As a result..." "That leads to..." "The consequence of that is..." "And then naturally..." "But the problem is..." "Which makes things worse because..."

Reflection

Which chain went the most unexpected direction? Was there a moment where one link completely changed where the story was heading?
Did the chains stay realistic or become absurd? Is it harder to keep things realistic, or is the fun in letting them spiral into chaos?
Think about real life: Can you think of a time when one small decision led to a chain of unexpected consequences? Tell the story.
