Check your idea CLICK
English sentences follow a fixed shape: subject, verb, object — and when there's a time word too, it usually goes right at the end, after everything else. Questions have their own shape too: with ordinary verbs like "swim", you need a helper word ("do") to build the question, because the main verb can't jump in front of the subject on its own.
Today: put your sentences in the right order — subject before verb, place before time — and get comfortable asking questions the natural way, so you can tell a clear story about your day and ask people things without sounding odd.

Saying It Straight

Every English sentence needs a subject — even when nothing seems to be "doing" anything.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday sentences:

  • "My brother cooks dinner every Friday."
  • "It rains a lot in this city."
  • "We need a bigger table."

Before you check: what comes first in every sentence, and what comes right after the verb?

Check the pattern CLICK
subject + verb + object. Even sentence two, about the weather — there's no real "rainer", but English still needs a subject word, so it uses it. A subject is never left out, not even for weather, dates, or times.
subject + verb + object
(weather / time / dates: it + verb — never left out)

Never leave the subject out

"Raining."
→
"It's raining."

In some languages you can drop the subject when it's obvious. In English, you can't — even a short comment needs "it".

Place, Then Time

When a sentence has both a place and a time, place comes first — time waits until the very end.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday sentences:

  • "I went to the gym yesterday."
  • "She arrived at the airport at six o'clock."
  • "We had dinner at a new restaurant last night."

Which comes first after the verb — the place or the time? Say it before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
Place comes right after the verb; time comes last, right at the end of the sentence. It's the opposite order from many other languages, so it's worth checking every time you tell a story.
subject + verb + place + time

Fixing the order

"I yesterday went to the gym."
→
"I went to the gym yesterday."

The time word ("yesterday") moves out of the middle and goes to the end, after the place.

Asking Naturally

Two systems for questions: one for "be", one for ordinary verbs.

Spot the pattern

  • "Are you free tonight?"
  • "Do you live near here?"
  • "Where does she work?"

Why does the first question not need "do", but the second and third ones do? Say your idea before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
"Are" is already a form of be, so it simply swaps places with the subject: you are → are you. "Live" and "work" are ordinary verbs — they can't jump in front of the subject on their own, so English adds a helper word, do/does/did, to build the question instead.
am / is / are + subject ...?
question word + am / is / are + subject ...?
do / does / did + subject + plain verb ...?
question word + do / does / did + subject + plain verb ...?

Swap, or add "do"?

"You are ready."
→
"Are you ready?"

With "be", just swap the subject and the verb. With every other verb, you need do/does/did — the main verb never moves.
