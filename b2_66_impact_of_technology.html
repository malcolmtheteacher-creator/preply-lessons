Great Technological Inventions

Which inventions have most significantly shaped human history?

Key Inventions to Consider

The Clock Enabled timekeeping, scheduling, and coordination of work
The Telephone Revolutionized long-distance communication
The Light Bulb Extended productive hours beyond daylight
The Refrigerator Changed food storage and preservation dramatically
The Airplane Made global travel and commerce possible
The Car Transformed transportation and urban development
The Internet Connected the world and created instant global communication

Reflection Questions

Which three have had the most significant impact on your life?

Has their impact been mainly positive or negative? Think about benefits and drawbacks.

Are there any you wish had never been invented? Why?

Essential Debate Language

Learn to participate effectively in structured debates by mastering these language functions:

Asking for Opinion

  • "What do you think about...?"
  • "What's your view on...?"
  • "Don't you agree that...?"
  • "How do you feel about...?"

Supporting Your Opinion

  • "In my opinion,..."
  • "I believe that..."
  • "I would argue that..."
  • "The main reason is..."
  • "It seems to me that..."
  • "I'd like to point out that..."

Getting Involved in the Discussion

  • "Can I say something?"
  • "I'd like to add..."
  • "That's a good point, and I'd also like to mention..."
  • "If I could just add..."
  • "I think it's important to consider..."

Dealing with Interruptions

  • "Sorry, I haven't finished yet."
  • "If I could just finish my point..."
  • "I appreciate your comment, but I'd like to complete my thought."
  • "Can I get back to what I was saying?"

Controlling the Speakers (for Chair/Moderator)

  • "Let's hear from someone else now."
  • "We need to move on."
  • "Can we hear another perspective?"
  • "Let me give others a chance to speak."

Debate Topic: "The World Would Be Better Without the Internet"

This is the motion for structured debate. Some will argue for this position, others against.

Arguments FOR (The World Would Be Better Without Internet)

Click to see supporting arguments:

  • Less social media addiction and mental health problems
  • More face-to-face human interaction
  • Reduced cyberbullying and online harassment
  • Less time wasted on distraction
  • Greater privacy protection
  • Less misinformation and fake news

Arguments AGAINST (The World Needs the Internet)

Click to see opposing arguments:

  • Essential access to information and education
  • Enables remote work and global business
  • Connects people across distances and cultures
  • Provides emergency services and critical infrastructure
  • Facilitates innovation and scientific research
  • Democratizes access to knowledge

Useful Phrases for This Debate

For the motion: "I would argue that the internet has created more problems than it has solved..."

Against the motion: "While I acknowledge those concerns, the reality is that modern society simply cannot function without the internet..."

Nuanced view: "I can see both sides of the argument. The internet is powerful, but its impact depends on how we use it."

Prepare and Deliver Your Debate

Take a position on the motion: "The world would be better without the internet."

Preparation Steps

Step 1: Choose Your Position

FOR: Focus on problems the internet has created (privacy, addiction, misinformation)

AGAINST: Focus on benefits (education, connection, innovation)

Step 2: Gather Your Arguments

Select 2-3 strong arguments from the lists above or add your own. For each:

  • State the argument clearly
  • Provide a specific example or reason
  • Explain the impact

Step 3: Anticipate Counter-Arguments

Think about what the opposite side might say. How will you respond?

Use phrases like: "I understand that perspective, however..." or "That's a valid point, but we also need to consider..."

Debate Checklist

Opening: "The motion is 'The world would be better without the internet.' I am [FOR/AGAINST] this motion because..."

Main arguments: Use debate language to present 2-3 points clearly

Address opposition: Reference opposing views and explain why your view is stronger

Conclusion: "In summary, I believe the internet [is/is not] beneficial because..."

After the Debate

Reflect on your performance:

  • Did you clearly state your position?
  • Were your arguments logical and well-supported?
  • Did you use debate language effectively?
  • Did you listen to and respond to opposing views?
  • What would you improve for next time?

Remember: Good debate is about clear reasoning and listening to others, not about winning at all costs.
