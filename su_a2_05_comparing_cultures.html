Grammar Reference: Comparing Things

Comparing two things (Comparatives)

  • Short adjectives: Add -er → "The weather is colder than before."
  • Long adjectives: Use more/less → "It's more expensive than I thought."
  • Use "than" after the comparative → "This city is bigger than that one."
  • Modify with intensity: "a lot more", "a little less", "much bigger" → "France is a lot warmer than Scotland."
  • Use "not as… as" instead of "less" → "Winter here is not as cold as in Canada."

Comparing with everything (Superlatives)

  • Short adjectives: Add -est → "The coldest day of winter."
  • Long adjectives: Use most/least → "the most important festival"
  • Always use "the" before the superlative → "This is the best restaurant in town."

Your Speaking Task

Imagine a business colleague from the UK is visiting your country for the first time. They have asked you to give them a 5-minute talk about your country's culture.

What to include in your talk:

  • Use ideas from the lead-in about what culture means to you
  • Compare your country's culture with UK culture
  • Explain reasons why cultural differences exist
  • Use comparatives and superlatives naturally
  • Speak for about 5 minutes

Useful language to remember:

Comparatives: "is more… than", "is less… than", "is not as… as", "is a little… than", "a lot more…"
Superlatives: "the most…", "the least…", "the… -est"
Giving reasons: "because", "due to", "as a result of", "thanks to"
