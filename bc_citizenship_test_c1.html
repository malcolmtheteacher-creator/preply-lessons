British Life and Culture

Sample Citizenship Test Questions

To become a British citizen, applicants must pass the "Life in the UK" test. Here are some real examples:

What do most British people have for breakfast?

a) Tea, cereal and toast
b) Tea, sausages, bacon and eggs
c) Just a cup of tea

Answer: a) Tea, cereal and toast
Although a "full English breakfast" (sausages, bacon, eggs, beans, mushrooms and toast) is traditional, it's mainly eaten as a weekend brunch, not as a daily breakfast. Most British people eat cereal or toast with tea or coffee on weekdays.

What is the most popular sport in the UK?

a) Cricket
b) Golf
c) Tennis
d) Football

Answer: d) Football
Football is by far the most popular sport in the UK, with a long history. The English Premier League attracts international players and millions of fans worldwide. England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland each have separate football leagues.

What time do most pubs open?

a) 11.00 a.m.
b) 10.30 a.m.
c) Whenever they want

Answer: a) 11.00 a.m.
Most UK pubs open around 11 a.m. It is customary for groups to take turns buying drinks for everyone — this is called buying a "round."

What percentage of the UK population has a parent or grandparent born outside the UK?

a) 5%
b) 10%
c) 25%

Answer: b) 10%
The UK today is much more diverse than 100 years ago. Post-war immigration means nearly 10% of the population has a parent or grandparent born outside the UK. The UK is a multinational and multiracial society.

Making Generalisations

When describing a culture, we often make generalisations — statements about groups of people. But good generalisations are "soft" — they avoid sounding absolute or judgmental.

Softening Language

Quantifiers (to avoid "all"):
"Most British people...", "The vast majority of people...", "A significant portion of..."

Frequency words (to avoid always/never):
"As a rule,", "Generally speaking,", "By and large,", "More often than not,", "There's a tendency to..."

Traditional/Customary phrases:
"It is customary to...", "It is traditional to...", "Typically..."

Practice: Soften These Statements

Original: "British people drink tea with milk."

Softened: "Most British people traditionally drink tea with milk." or "By and large, British people prefer tea with milk."

Original: "British people have a fantastic sense of humour."

Softened: "The British are generally known for their sense of humour." or "It is often said that British people have a distinctive sense of humour."

Key Vocabulary

Government and Society

  • Constitutional monarchy: A system where a king or queen's power is limited by law
  • Parliament: The legislative body that makes laws in the UK
  • Devolution: Giving power to regional governments (Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland)
  • Census: An official count of the population

Culture and Society

  • Multicultural: Including people from many different cultures
  • Borough: A town or district with its own local government
  • Constituency: An area represented by one member of parliament

British Culture and Customs

  • Traditions: Customs passed down through generations
  • Bonfire Night: November 5th — celebrates the failure of the 1605 Gunpowder Plot
  • Citizenship test: Exam on British culture and law for people seeking citizenship
