Here's something strange...

If you described an old man with a white beard sitting in the sky, you're like most people. But almost no religion actually teaches that. Not Christianity, not Islam, not Judaism, not Hinduism. Most theological traditions would say that image is childish and misleading.

So what DO religions actually mean when they talk about 'God'? That's what we're about to find out — and the answer is much stranger than the old man in the sky.

Four types of belief about God

🔒 Hidden for retrieval — try from memory first!
KEY TERM

Monotheism

Belief in one God. God is singular, unique, all-powerful, eternal. Examples: Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Sikhism.

KEY TERM

Polytheism

Belief in many gods. Different gods have different powers and personalities. Examples: Hinduism (though complicated), ancient Greek religion, ancient Egyptian religion.

KEY TERM

Pantheism

Belief that God is everything and everything is God. God and the universe are the same thing. Examples: some forms of Buddhism, some mystical branches of other traditions.

KEY TERM

Atheism

Belief that there is no God or gods. Reality is purely physical and material. Examples: Humanism, strict scientific materialism.

KEY TERM

Agnosticism

The view that we cannot know whether God exists or not. It's not about disbelief — it's about the limits of knowledge. An agnostic might say 'God's existence is unknowable.'

The "omni" attributes of God

When theologians talk about God, they often use three special words. These are claims about what God can do and what God knows:

⚡

Omnipotent

All-powerful. God can do anything. Nothing limits God's power. (Omni- = all, potent = powerful)

💡

Omniscient

All-knowing. God knows everything that has happened, is happening, and will happen. (Omni- = all, scient = knowing)

❤

Omnibenevolent

All-loving. God is perfectly good and loves all creation unconditionally. (Omni- = all, benevolent = loving/good)

These "omni" attributes don't appear in the same way in every religion. Christians, Muslims, and Jews typically emphasize all three. Hindus might emphasize omnipotence differently. Buddhists don't believe in a creator God at all. And this creates theological problems — if God is all-good and all-powerful, why does evil exist? That's called "the Problem of Evil" and it's been debated for 2,000 years.

Transcendent vs Immanent

Different traditions understand God's location and relationship to the world differently:

Transcendent God

God is beyond and separate from the world. God exists outside space and time. God is "wholly other" — totally different from creation. Muslims emphasize this heavily: Allah is unlike anything we know.

Immanent God

God is present and active within the world. God is here now, in creation, in nature, in people's hearts. Christians emphasize this when they say "God with us" (Emmanuel). Hindus emphasize this through the presence of divinity within all things (Brahman).

How different traditions understand "God"

✝ Christianity: The Trinity

One God in three persons: Father, Son (Jesus), and Holy Spirit. This is both transcendent (God the Father as creator) and immanent (God the Son present in flesh, God the Holy Spirit in believers' hearts). Monotheistic but complicated — a mystery, not a contradiction (they say).

☪ Islam: Tawhid (Divine Oneness)

Absolute monotheism. "There is no God but Allah, and Muhammad is His messenger." God (Allah) is one, unique, eternal, transcendent, utterly unlike creation. Not a Trinity. Muslims say the Christian Trinity is a form of polytheism — a serious misunderstanding.

☬ Hinduism: Brahman and Gods

Brahman is the ultimate reality — one infinite consciousness underlying everything. But Brahman manifests through many gods and goddesses (Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Durga, etc.). So it's both: one ultimate reality, many divine forms. Some call it "monotheism with polytheistic expression."

☸ Buddhism: No Creator God

Buddhism doesn't have a creator God. Instead, it focuses on Enlightenment (Nirvana) and the Buddha's teachings about suffering. Some Buddhists are atheists. Some forms of Buddhism do have gods, but they're not creators or ultimate beings — they're beings who also suffer and seek enlightenment.

☬ Judaism: One God, Many Interpretations

Monotheistic: "Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one" (Shema). God is called Yahweh. Some emphasize God's transcendence (God is above and beyond). Others emphasize covenant — God's personal relationship with the Jewish people.

☬ Sikhism: Ik Onkar

"Ik Onkar" — "One God." God is formless, timeless, and beyond human comprehension. A strict monotheism. Sikhs reject both the Hindu concept of multiple gods and the Christian concept of the Trinity.

⚱ Humanism: No God

Humanists don't believe in God. Meaning, morality, and purpose come from human reason, compassion, and shared values — not from divine revelation. Many Humanists respect religion but see it as unnecessary for a good life.

1. "God is the energy that flows through all living things. The universe is divine, and divinity is everywhere."

Pantheism. This describes God and nature as one.

2. "Zeus is the king of the gods, Athena is the goddess of wisdom, and Ares is the god of war. Each has different powers."

Polytheism. This describes multiple gods with different roles.

3. "There is no scientific evidence for God. Morality comes from human evolution and social cooperation, not divine command."

Atheism. This denies God's existence.

4. "There is only one God, eternal and all-powerful, who spoke to the Prophet Muhammad."

Monotheism. This describes one God.

Scripture Analysis: What concept of God does each teach?

Read these three sacred texts. For each, identify whether it emphasizes transcendence, immanence, or both.

"In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth. The earth was formless and empty, darkness was over the surface of the deep, and the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters." Genesis 1:1-2 (Hebrew Bible/Old Testament)

What does this tell us about God?

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. Through him all things were made... The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us." John 1:1-3, 14 (New Testament)

What does this tell us about God?

"Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah is the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, and there is none like unto Him." Quran 112:1-4 (Islam)

What does this tell us about God?

Genesis 1:1-2: Both. God creates (transcendent action) but God's Spirit hovers present (immanent).

John 1:1-14: Both. The Word is with God (transcendent) but becomes flesh (immanent) — Jesus as the bridge between heaven and earth.

Quran 112:1-4: Transcendent. Allah is utterly unique and unlike creation. "None like unto Him" emphasizes God's transcendence and otherness.
