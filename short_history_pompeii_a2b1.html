Pompeii

A Roman city that disappeared in one day.

Today: tell the story of Pompeii, a Roman city. Grammar: past simple and past continuous.

Key words — learn them, then put them in order

First, let's learn eight words. Read each word and the sentence. Try to guess the meaning. Then click Reveal and check. Say each word out loud.

1. A volcano is a mountain that can open and throw out fire, rock, and ash.
volcano (noun) — a mountain that can throw out hot rock and ash.
2. The volcano started to erupt — it suddenly opened and threw rock and ash into the sky.
to erupt (verb) — when a volcano explodes and throws out hot rock and ash. (noun: an eruption)
3. Ash is the soft grey powder that comes out of a fire or a volcano.
ash (noun) — the soft grey powder from a fire or a volcano.
4. The volcano made a huge cloud of smoke and ash, high in the sky.
cloud / smoke (noun) — the grey shape of ash and gas in the sky. Smoke comes from a fire.
5. Many people tried to escape — to run away from the danger and get to a safe place.
to escape (verb) — to run away from danger to a safe place.
6. The ash fell and fell, and it started to bury the city — to cover it completely.
to bury (verb) — to cover something completely, so you cannot see it.
7. Many years later, people came to dig up the city — to take away the ash and find the houses below.
to dig up (verb) — to take away earth or ash and find something below the ground.
8. Today we can walk in the ruins — the old broken houses and streets that are still there.
ruins (noun) — the old, broken parts of buildings that are left after a long time.

Now put the words in order — out loud

Before we read:
  • Put these six things in the order you think they happened. Say why for each one: the city → the volcano → the ash → the gas → buried → found again.
  • For example: "First, there was the city. People lived there. Then…" Use the words from above.
  • Quick question: do you know another old city or place that was lost or broken a long time ago? What happened to it?

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A busy town and a quiet mountain

Pompeii was a busy Roman town. It was near a big mountain called Vesuvius. The town had streets, houses, shops, and many people. Life was good.

Every day, the people looked up at the mountain. But they did not know one important thing. They did not know that the mountain was a volcano. They did not know it was dangerous.

Then, in the year 79 AD, something terrible happened. The volcano erupted. It opened with a huge noise. Hot rock and ash came out. The people were afraid. They did not understand what was happening.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What kind of place was Pompeii? What was near it?

2. What did the people not know about the mountain?

1. Pompeii was a busy Roman town with streets, houses, and shops. There was a big mountain called Vesuvius near it.

2. They did not know that the mountain was a volcano. They did not know it was dangerous.

Talk about it:
  • The people lived next to a danger, but they did not know it. Do you think there are dangers near us today that we do not see?

Part 2 — The ash falls

The volcano sent a huge cloud of ash and stone high into the sky. The cloud went up and up, very high. People could see it from far away.

Then the ash started to fall. It fell on the town like grey rain. It fell for hours. Slowly, the ash covered the houses and the streets.

Many people ran away to escape. They took what they could and ran to the sea or the roads. But many other people stayed. Maybe they thought the ash would stop soon. A famous Roman man called Pliny watched everything from across the bay. Later, he wrote about it, so we know the story today.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What came out of the volcano, and what did it do to the town?

2. What did the people do? Who was Pliny?

1. A huge cloud of ash and stone went high into the sky. The ash fell on the town and slowly covered the houses and streets.

2. Many people ran away to escape, but many others stayed. Pliny was a famous Roman man who watched from across the bay and wrote about it.

Talk about it:
  • Some people ran away. Some people stayed. What would you do? Is it easy to know the right thing in a moment like that?

Part 3 — Lost, then found

On the second day, the volcano became more dangerous. It sent hot gas down the mountain, very fast. The gas reached the town. The people who stayed could not escape. The hot gas killed them. About 13,000 to 16,000 people died.

After this, the town was quiet. The ash buried Pompeii completely. The city stayed under the ash for about 1,700 years. People forgot it was there.

Then, in 1748, people started to dig up the town. Slowly, they found the streets, the houses, and even the shapes of the people. Today, you can walk in the ruins of Pompeii. It is a famous place, and many people go there to visit.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened on the second day? How many people died?

2. How long was the city under the ash? When did people dig it up?

1. On the second day, hot gas came down the mountain and killed the people who stayed. About 13,000 to 16,000 people died.

2. The city was under the ash for about 1,700 years. People started to dig it up in 1748.

Talk about it:
  • The ash killed the town, but it also kept it safe for 1,700 years. Now we can see it. Is this a good thing or a sad thing? Maybe both?

Grammar: past simple & past continuous

To tell a story, we use two past tenses together.

Past simple = a finished action: "the volcano erupted," "many people died."
Past continuous (was/were + -ing) = a longer action, happening at that time: "while the ash was falling…"
We often use them together with while and when: a long action (continuous) and a short action inside it (simple).

1. Choose the right tense

1. While the people (live) in the town, the volcano suddenly (erupt).

2. The ash (fall) when many people (run) away.

3. While Pliny (watch) from across the bay, the cloud (go) higher and higher.

1. were living / erupted (long background action + sudden event)

2. was falling / ran (long action + short action inside it)

3. was watching / went (or: was going) (both fit the ongoing scene)

2. Tell a short part of the story

Put the verbs in the past simple or past continuous. Then read it out loud.

In the year 79 AD, the people of Pompeii (live) a normal day. The mountain (not look) dangerous. Then the volcano (erupt). While the ash (fall), many people (escape) to the sea. Many years later, people (find) the lost city.

were living · did not look · erupted · was falling · escaped · found

The long "background" actions are in the past continuous; the finished actions are in the past simple.
