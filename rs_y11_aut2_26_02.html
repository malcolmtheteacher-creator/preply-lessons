Define Hajj.

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The pilgrimage to Makkah, the fifth pillar of Islam, obligatory once in the lifetime of able Muslims.

Who is obligated to perform Hajj?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Adult, physically able, mentally capable Muslims with sufficient financial means.

When does Hajj occur in the Islamic calendar?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: In Dhu al-Hijjah (the 12th month), from the 8th to 13th.

Name two spiritual reasons Hajj is important.

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Submission to Allah, seeking forgiveness, spiritual purification, following Prophet Abraham's example.

What three types of preparation do Muslims undertake before Hajj?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Spiritual (prayer, study), physical (fitness, health checks), financial (saving money).

The Five Main Rituals

The Hajj ritual follows a precise sequence that takes place over five days. Each ritual has deep spiritual significance and commemorates events from the life of Prophet Abraham.

Qur'an 22:28 states: "That they may witness the benefits for themselves, and mention the name of Allah on known days over the livestock He has provided for them."

Ihram: The Sacred State

Before entering the sacred precinct (Haram) of Makkah, pilgrims must declare their intention (Niyyah) and enter the state of Ihram.

What is Ihram?

  • Ihram is a sacred state of ritual purity and devotion
  • Male pilgrims wear two simple white, seamless garments (Ihram cloth)
  • Female pilgrims wear modest clothing (typically an abaya and headscarf)
  • The white garments symbolise equality before Allah — no class distinctions

Restrictions During Ihram

While in Ihram, pilgrims must abstain from:

  • Cutting hair or nails
  • Wearing perfume or cosmetics
  • Sexual relations
  • Hunting or harming animals
  • Arguing or speaking angrily

These restrictions emphasise spiritual focus and transformation. The pilgrim becomes focused entirely on worship.

Tawaf: Circumambulation of the Ka'bah

Upon arriving in Makkah, pilgrims perform Tawaf — walking counter-clockwise around the Ka'bah seven times.

  • The Ka'bah is a cubic black-covered structure at the heart of the Grand Mosque
  • Pilgrims walk as close to it as possible, often kissing or touching the Black Stone (Hajar al-Aswad)
  • Each circuit represents increasing closeness to Allah
  • Pilgrims recite prayers and Qur'anic verses during Tawaf

The Black Stone is believed to have been given by the Angel Gabriel to Prophet Abraham and has been kissed by millions of pilgrims over centuries.

Sa'i: Running Between Safa and Marwa

Sa'i (meaning "to walk with purpose") involves running between two hills, Safa and Marwa, seven times.

  • This ritual commemorates Hagar's (Ibrahim's wife) desperate search for water in the desert
  • Pilgrims walk/run the 450-meter distance, starting at Safa and ending at Marwa
  • The action demonstrates determination and trust in Allah
  • In modern times, the route is covered and air-conditioned for comfort

Qur'an 2:158: "Indeed, al-Safa and al-Marwa are among the symbols of Allah."

Standing at Mount Arafat (Yawm al-Arafat)

The Day of Arafat (9th Dhu al-Hijjah) is the spiritual climax of Hajj. Pilgrims gather on the plain of Arafat, about 20 km east of Makkah.

  • From dawn until sunset, pilgrims stand in prayer and supplication
  • Many weep as they ask Allah for forgiveness (Wuquf)
  • This moment represents standing before Allah on the Day of Judgment
  • The Qur'an emphasises: "There is no Hajj without Arafat"

If a pilgrim misses standing at Arafat, their Hajj is invalid. This is the most essential ritual.

The Night at Muzdalifah

After sunset at Arafat, pilgrims move to Muzdalifah, between Arafat and Mina, where they:

  • Collect small pebbles for the stoning ritual
  • Spend the night under the open sky in prayer
  • Perform Maghrib and Isha prayers together

The night at Muzdalifah represents submission and humility before Allah.

Stoning the Jamarat at Mina

Jamarat al-Sughra (stoning the pillars) occurs at Mina on the 10th-13th Dhu al-Hijjah.

  • Pilgrims throw seven pebbles at three pillars representing Satan (Iblis)
  • This commemorates Prophet Abraham's rejection of Satan's whispers
  • The first pillar receives 7 stones, the second receives 7, the third receives 7
  • The stoning represents humanity's struggle against evil (symbolic of lesser jihad)

Tragically, stoning is the most dangerous part of Hajj. In 2015, over 2,000 pilgrims died in a stampede at Mina.

Animal Sacrifice (Qurbani)

Qurbani (animal sacrifice) on the 10th Dhu al-Hijjah commemorates Prophet Abraham's willingness to sacrifice his son Ismail in obedience to Allah.

  • Each pilgrim (or a group of 7) sacrifices a sheep, goat, or cow
  • The meat is distributed: one-third to family, one-third to friends, one-third to the poor
  • This sacrifice demonstrates submission to Allah and charity to others
  • Qurbani is not unique to Hajj — it's also celebrated at Eid al-Adha (see Lesson 4)

Qur'an 22:37: "Neither their meat nor their blood reaches Allah, but what reaches Him is the piety from you."

The Symbolism of Hajj Rituals

Each Hajj ritual carries profound spiritual meaning:

  • Ihram: Stripping away ego and worldly status
  • Tawaf: Orbiting around God's house, symbolising devotion
  • Sa'i: Perseverance and trust in Allah despite hardship
  • Arafat: Confronting one's sins and seeking divine mercy
  • Mina: Patience and resilience
  • Stoning: Rejecting Satan and evil within oneself
  • Sacrifice: Obedience to Allah and sharing with the needy

Key Terms

  • Ihram: Sacred state of ritual purity; simple white garments worn by pilgrims
  • Tawaf: Walking counter-clockwise around the Ka'bah seven times
  • Sa'i: Running between Safa and Marwa seven times
  • Wuquf: Standing in prayer at Mount Arafat during Hajj
  • Jamarat: Pillars at Mina that are stoned during Hajj
  • Qurbani: Animal sacrifice performed during Hajj
  • Muzdalifah: Place between Arafat and Mina where pilgrims collect pebbles

How many times do pilgrims walk around the Ka'bah during Tawaf?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Seven times (counter-clockwise).

Why is standing at Mount Arafat considered the most essential part of Hajj?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Because the Prophet said "There is no Hajj without Arafat" — missing it invalidates the entire pilgrimage.

What does Qurbani commemorate?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Prophet Abraham's willingness to sacrifice his son Ismail in obedience to Allah.
