What's It Like? Describing before a noun

A describing word can sit right in front of a noun — "a small flat".

Spot the pattern

Three everyday sentences:

  • "I live in a small flat near the station."
  • "We ate at a new restaurant last night."
  • "She has a friendly dog."

Where does the describing word go — before the noun or after it? And does it change for a plural noun (two flats)? Say your idea before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
The describing word (adjective) goes before the noun, after "a" or "an". It never changes — not even for plural nouns: "two small flats", never "two smalls flats".
a / an + adjective + singular noun
adjective + plural noun (no extra -s on the adjective)

Same meaning, different structure

"This café is quiet."
→
"It's a quiet café."

Same meaning, two positions. We'll look at the "is" version next.

Big or Small? Describing after "be"

The same describing word also works after am / is / are — no noun needed straight after it.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday sentences:

  • "My flat is small, but it's warm."
  • "The bread at that bakery is fresh."
  • "My parents are busy this week."

What comes right before the describing word this time? Does the word itself change? Say it before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
am / is / are + adjective. The verb "be" changes with the subject (is / are), but the adjective itself still never changes — no "busys", no "smalls".
subject + am / is / are + adjective

How Often? Words like always and never

Always, usually, sometimes, never — they say how often, and they have their own special place in the sentence.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday sentences:

  • "I usually walk to work."
  • "She is never late."
  • "We sometimes eat pizza on Fridays."

Look closely — where does the frequency word go with a normal verb (walk, eat)? Where does it go with "be" (is)? Say it before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
Before the main verb ("usually walk", "sometimes eat"), but after "be" ("is never" — never "never is").
subject + always / usually / sometimes / never + main verb
subject + am / is / are + always / usually / sometimes / never + adjective

Adding how often

"I drink tea every morning."
→
"I always drink tea in the morning."
