Does the Afterlife Matter?

This final lesson brings together everything you've learned about afterlife beliefs across Christianity, Islam, Buddhism, and Humanism. Test your knowledge, reflect deeply, and consider how belief in the afterlife shapes how people live.

Spaced Repetition: Recall Everything

You've learned about afterlife beliefs across six lessons. Test your memory on all of them:

From L25 — Where Did Everything Come From?

Q: What does ex nihilo mean, and which religions use this concept?
Reveal answer
A: Ex nihilo means "from nothing." Christianity and Islam teach that God/Allah created the universe from nothing (not from pre-existing materials).

From L26 — What Happens When We Die?

Q: How is Hindu reincarnation different from Buddhist rebirth?
Reveal answer
A: Hindu reincarnation involves an eternal atman (soul) transmigrating into new bodies. Buddhist rebirth has no permanent soul (anatta)—consciousness continues due to karma, but there's no unchanging self being reborn.

From L27 — Christian Views of the Afterlife

Q: What is purgatory, and which Christian tradition emphasizes it?
Reveal answer
A: Purgatory is a state of purification before entering heaven. It's emphasized in Catholic theology but not in most Protestant traditions.

From L28 — Islamic Views of the Afterlife

Q: Name the eight stages of the Islamic afterlife journey.
Reveal answer
A: (1) Death and Azrael, (2) Barzakh, (3) Questioning in grave, (4) Yawm al-Din (Day of Judgment), (5) The Scales, (6) The Bridge (As-Sirat), (7) Jannah (paradise), (8) Jahannam (hell).

From L29 — Buddhist Rebirth and Humanist Views

Q: What are the six realms of Buddhist rebirth?
Reveal answer
A: Deva (gods), Asura (demigods), Human, Hungry Ghosts (Preta), Animals, Hell Beings. Rebirth into each realm depends on karma.

Quick-Fire Knowledge Test

Click each question to reveal the answer. Self-mark as correct.

Q1: In Christianity, what does "resurrection" mean?
A: The belief that the body and soul are reunited and raised to eternal life, not just the soul surviving.
Q2: What is Barzakh in Islam?
A: The intermediate state between death and the Day of Judgment; a period of waiting where the soul experiences comfort or difficulty based on earthly deeds.
Q3: What does Nirvana mean in Buddhism?
A: Extinguishing; the cessation of greed, hatred, and delusion; liberation from the cycle of rebirth and suffering.
Q4: In Islamic belief, what are the Scales (al-Mizan)?
A: A metaphorical weighing of good and bad deeds on Judgment Day to determine a person's final fate.
Q5: What is Anatta in Buddhist philosophy?
A: The doctrine of "no-self"—the belief that there is no permanent, unchanging soul (in contrast to Hindu atman).
Q6: What do Humanists believe about the afterlife?
A: Humanists do not believe in an afterlife. Death is the end of consciousness. This life is all we have.
Q7: In Islam, what is Yawm al-Din?
A: The Day of Judgment; the day when all souls are resurrected and brought before Allah for final judgment.
Q8: What are the six realms of Buddhist rebirth?
A: Deva (gods), Asura (demigods), Human, Hungry Ghosts, Animals, and Hell Beings.
Q9: In Christianity, what is Purgatory?
A: In Catholic theology, a state of purification where souls are cleansed before entering heaven. Not universally believed in Protestant Christianity.
Q10: What determines rebirth in Buddhism?
A: Karma—the moral quality of one's actions, thoughts, and intentions in previous lives.

Critical Evaluation

Consider this statement carefully:

"Belief in an afterlife makes people live better lives."

Arguments FOR
  • Belief in judgment or karma creates accountability; people think twice before wrongdoing.
  • Religious rituals and practices (prayer, meditation) reduce stress and increase compassion.
  • Many surveys show religious people report higher life satisfaction.
  • Belief in purpose (serving God, achieving enlightenment) motivates ethical behavior.
Arguments AGAINST
  • Non-believers (Humanists, atheists) are often highly ethical without supernatural belief.
  • Religious people commit terrible acts too—belief doesn't guarantee moral behavior.
  • Some believers use afterlife belief to justify inaction in this world (e.g., ignoring injustice).
  • Secular ethics (based on reducing suffering and human flourishing) work without God.
