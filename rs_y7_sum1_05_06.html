From L25 — Where Did Everything Come From?

Q: What does ex nihilo mean, and which religions use this concept?
A: Ex nihilo means "from nothing." Christianity and Islam teach that God/Allah created the universe from nothing (not from pre-existing materials).

From L26 — What Happens When We Die?

Q: How is Hindu reincarnation different from Buddhist rebirth?
A: Hindu reincarnation involves an eternal atman (soul) transmigrating into new bodies. Buddhist rebirth has no permanent soul (anatta)—consciousness continues due to karma, but there's no unchanging self being reborn.

From L27 — Christian Views of the Afterlife

Q: What is purgatory, and which Christian tradition emphasizes it?
A: Purgatory is a state of purification before entering heaven. It's emphasized in Catholic theology but not in most Protestant traditions.

From L28 — Islamic Views of the Afterlife

Q: Name the eight stages of the Islamic afterlife journey.
A: (1) Death and Azrael, (2) Barzakh, (3) Questioning in grave, (4) Yawm al-Din (Day of Judgment), (5) The Scales, (6) The Bridge (As-Sirat), (7) Jannah (paradise), (8) Jahannam (hell).

From L29 — Buddhist Rebirth and Humanist Views

Q: What are the six realms of Buddhist rebirth?
A: Deva (gods), Asura (demigods), Human, Hungry Ghosts (Preta), Animals, Hell Beings. Rebirth into each realm depends on karma.

Key Patterns to Notice

  • Eternal vs. Cyclic: Monotheistic religions (Christianity, Islam, Judaism) believe in eternity; Eastern religions (Hinduism, Buddhism) emphasize cycles.
  • Judgment vs. Karma: Western religions feature judgment by a deity; Eastern religions rely on impersonal cosmic law (karma).
  • Soul vs. No Soul: Most traditions affirm some continuity of self; Buddhism uniquely denies an eternal, unchanging self (anatta).
  • This Life vs. Next: How you live now determines your afterlife (or rebirth) fate.

Unit Summary: Does the Afterlife Matter?

What We've Learned

Over six lessons, we've explored how six major worldviews answer the question of the afterlife:

Christianity

The afterlife matters because God judges souls. Resurrection of body and soul; heaven or hell based on faith and works.

Islam

The afterlife matters because Allah judges on the Day of Judgment. Deeds are weighed; paradise (Jannah) or hell (Jahannam) awaits.

Hinduism

The afterlife matters because karma determines rebirth. Moksha (liberation) is the ultimate goal, achieved through dharma and spiritual practice.

Buddhism

The afterlife matters because karma shapes rebirth. Nirvana (the end of suffering) is the ultimate goal, not judgment or heaven.

Judaism

The afterlife matters because righteous living fulfills the covenant. Views vary: World to Come, resurrection, or immortal soul.

Humanism

There is no afterlife, but this life matters profoundly. Meaning comes from relationships, learning, and contributing to human flourishing.

Why This Matters to You

  • Understanding others: People's beliefs about the afterlife shape how they live, grieve, celebrate, and make ethical decisions.
  • Finding your own answer: Whether you believe in an afterlife is deeply personal. This unit equips you to think critically about it.
  • Recognizing shared values: Despite different afterlife beliefs, all six traditions emphasize: how you live now matters, treat others with respect, and seek meaning beyond yourself.
