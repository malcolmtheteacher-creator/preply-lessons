Introduction to Population Change

Consider these statements about world population:

Statement A: The world's population is growing by approximately 78 million people per year at the moment due to the difference between birth rates and death rates.

Statement B: The world's population is growing by approximately 80 million per year.

Statement C: Most of the world's population growth is happening in developing countries.

Statement D: The majority of babies are now being born in developing countries rather than developed ones.

Which statements are true according to the data?

Consider whether these statements are True, False, or Not Given based on the reading passage above.

Statement A - TRUE: The passage states the growth is approximately 78 million per year due to the difference between birth and death rates.

Statement B - FALSE: The passage specifically says "78 million," not "80 million." The difference may seem small, but precision matters in academic reading.

Statement C - NOT GIVEN: While this may be true in the real world, the passage does not directly address where growth is occurring.

Statement D - NOT GIVEN: The passage does not provide information about where babies are being born.

Key Reading Tip:

In IELTS True/False/Not Given tasks, you must distinguish between:

  • True: The statement matches or is supported by information in the passage
  • False: The statement contradicts information in the passage
  • Not Given: The passage does not provide enough information to determine the statement's accuracy

IELTS Reading Skills: Comparing Statements

The key to success is careful comparison between the statement and the passage.

Strategy: How to approach True/False/Not Given questions

  1. Read the statement carefully: Identify the key claim or proposition
  2. Scan the passage for relevant information: Look for key words from the statement
  3. Compare precisely: Check if every element of the statement is supported
  4. Watch for qualifying words: "Most," "some," "all," "approximately"—these matter!
  5. Check for contradictions: If the passage says the opposite, mark it False
  6. Be confident saying "Not Given": If information is not in the passage, say so

Practice: Analyse this statement

Statement: "Nowadays more babies are born in developing countries than in developed countries."

Key words to find: "babies," "born," "developing countries," "developed countries"

Potential issues: The statement uses "now" and "more," which require specific comparative data. The original passage discusses growth rates and general fertility trends, but may not directly compare birth locations by development level.

Classification: Likely "Not Given" unless the passage explicitly addresses this comparison.

Common pitfalls to avoid:

  • Using outside knowledge (only rely on the passage)
  • Confusing "False" with "Not Given"
  • Missing small qualifying words like "most," "some," "approximately"
  • Reading the statement too quickly and missing nuance
  • Assuming similarity when the passage uses different terminology

Timed Speaking Task

Task: Discuss how population change affects your country and global society. Address demographic trends, challenges, and implications.

Time remaining: 10:00

Your presentation framework (10 minutes):

  1. Opening (1 min): "Population change is significant in my country because..."
  2. Current situation (2 mins): Describe demographic trends (birth rates, aging, migration)
  3. Challenges (3 mins): Discuss 2-3 consequences:
    • Healthcare and aging populations
    • Labour market changes
    • Resource allocation and sustainability
    • Education system demands
    • Social security systems
  4. Global perspective (2 mins): Connect to worldwide population trends
  5. Closing (2 mins): What solutions or adaptations are needed?

Useful expressions:

  • "The population in my country is growing/declining at a rate of..."
  • "As a result of these demographic changes, we are facing..."
  • "One significant challenge is the aging population..."
  • "This trend has implications for healthcare, education, and employment..."
  • "To address this, the government is implementing..."
  • "Globally, we're seeing similar patterns in developing nations..."

Discussion and Reflection

Question 2: Global trends

What are the most significant population trends globally? How do they differ between developed and developing nations?

Key Takeaways

  • Precision and accuracy are essential in reading population data
  • IELTS True/False/Not Given questions require careful statement analysis
  • Population trends have far-reaching social, economic, and environmental impacts
  • Global population distribution is shifting toward developing nations
  • Understanding demographic change is crucial for policy and planning
