Population Change: Comparing Statements

Analyse and compare population data and demographic trends

Understanding Population Data

Population statistics tell us about birth rates, death rates, life expectancy, migration, and demographic changes. To understand population data, you need to read carefully and compare statements.

Key Demographic Terms

  • Birth rate: The number of babies born per 1,000 people per year
  • Death rate: The number of deaths per 1,000 people per year
  • Life expectancy: The average number of years a person is expected to live
  • Migration: Movement of people from one country/region to another
  • Urbanisation: The movement of people from rural to urban areas
  • Population density: The number of people per square kilometre
  • Fertility rate: The average number of children per woman
  • Ageing population: A demographic where the average age increases

Read the Data

The world's population is growing by 78 million per year at the moment due to high birth rates and increasing life expectancy (currently 68 years for men and 73 years for women). Growth has been so great that about seven per cent of the humans that have existed over the last 200,000 years are alive today. In the future most new arrivals will live in the world's cities, whose populations now outnumber rural areas. Few will live in high-income countries. If current trends continue, three quarters of people will be born in Asia or Africa. However, they will be born into families that are smaller than they used to be and they are likely to only have 1.9 brothers or sisters on average.

Which of these statements is TRUE according to the passage?

A) The world's population is growing by approximately 80 million per year
B) More women live longer than men
C) More people live in the countryside than in towns
D) Most new population growth will be in high-income countries

Answer: B) More women live longer than men
The passage states: "life expectancy (currently 68 years for men and 73 years for women)." This shows women live longer. Option A is close but says 80 million (not 78). Option C is false (cities outnumber rural areas). Option D is false (few will live in high-income countries).

Language for Comparing Statements

When you compare population statistics, use these phrases to show similarities and differences clearly.

Comparing: Showing Contrasts

  • "In contrast to...", "Whereas X has..., Y has..."
  • "Compared with...", "Compared to..."
  • "While X shows..., Y shows..."
  • "The most significant difference is..."
  • "Unlike..."

Comparing: Showing Similarities

  • "Both X and Y share...", "Similarly..."
  • "Like X, Y also...", "Both countries have..."
  • "The key similarity is..."

Describing Trends

  • has risen sharply — increased quickly
  • has declined gradually — decreased slowly
  • remained stable — stayed the same
  • has fluctuated — gone up and down
  • peaked at — reached its highest point
  • reached a low of — hit its lowest point

Practice: Compare Two Countries

Country A: Birth rate 18/1000, Life expectancy 76 years, Urbanisation 75%
Country B: Birth rate 32/1000, Life expectancy 61 years, Urbanisation 35%

Write a comparison sentence using "whereas" or "in contrast to":

Example: "Whereas Country A has a low birth rate and high life expectancy, Country B has a significantly higher birth rate and lower life expectancy. In contrast to Country A's urban population of 75%, Country B remains largely rural at 35% urbanisation."

True / False / Not Given (IELTS Reading Skill)

This is an important IELTS reading skill. You must decide if a statement is True, False, or Not Given based on the text.

Understanding the Three Options

  • TRUE: The statement is supported by information in the passage
  • FALSE: The passage states the opposite of the statement
  • NOT GIVEN: The passage does not mention this, even if you think it's true in real life

Practice with Text

Background: By 1805 the world's population had reached one billion for the first time in the 200,000 year history of our species. It has now reached eight billion. The reason why growth has accelerated so rapidly can be summed up in one word: technology. If we look back over the last 10,000 years we can see that the processes of population change and technological development have always taken place side by side, each creating the conditions for the other to occur.

Statement 1: The world's population reached one billion in 1805.

FALSE. The passage says "by 1805" the population "had reached" one billion, meaning it reached one billion by that year, not necessarily in that exact year.

Statement 2: Technology is the main reason for rapid population growth.

TRUE. The passage states: "The reason why growth has accelerated so rapidly can be summed up in one word: technology."

Statement 3: Technology was invented to solve population problems.

NOT GIVEN. The passage discusses the relationship between population and technology but does not say technology was invented to solve population problems.

Your Analysis Task

Choose a country you are familiar with or interested in. Analyse its population situation and challenges.

What to Include:

Guide Questions:

  • Is your country's population growing or shrinking? How fast?
  • What is the life expectancy in your country?
  • Are more people moving to cities or staying in rural areas?
  • What is the average age of the population? Is it ageing?
  • What are the main challenges (housing, employment, ageing population, migration)?

Write Your Analysis:

Success Criteria

  • Clearly described the country's current population situation
  • Used at least 3 comparison phrases correctly
  • Used trend vocabulary (rising, declining, fluctuating, etc.) appropriately
  • Identified and explained a major population-related challenge
  • Wrote in clear, academic English suitable for IELTS preparation
