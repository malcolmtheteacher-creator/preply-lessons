📚 Let's Learn: How to Say Your Job

To say what you do for work, here are the patterns you use every day:

Pattern: "I am a [job]" OR "I'm a [job]"

Most common way: I + am (I'm) + a + [job name]

Examples:

  • "I'm a student." 🎓
  • "I'm a teacher." 🏫
  • "I'm a software developer." 💻
  • "I'm a doctor." ⚕️
  • "I'm a designer." 🎨
  • "I'm a lawyer." 👨‍⚖️

Note: We use "a" before the job! "I'm a doctor" (correct) ✅ NOT "I'm doctor" ❌

🇮🇹 In Italian: In Italian you say Sono insegnante (no "a"), but in English we always add "a": "I'm a teacher." An easy one to forget — you've got this!

🗣️ How to Say It

Doctor = DOCK-tur (stress first syllable, second syllable sounds like "ur")

Teacher = TEECH-ur (stress first syllable, long E sound)

Do you = duh YOO (not "do you"—sounds like "duh-yuh" when speaking)

Work = WORK (rhymes with "lurk", not "walk")

Engineer = en-jin-EER (three syllables, stress on last one)

Doctor = DOCK-tur (stress first syllable, second syllable sounds like "ur")

Teacher = TEECH-ur (stress first syllable, long E sound)

Do you = duh YOO (not "do you"—sounds like "duh-yuh" when speaking)

Work = WORK (rhymes with "lurk", not "walk")

Engineer = en-jin-EER (three syllables, stress on last one)

Alternative: "I work as a [job]"

Slightly more detailed: I + work as a + [job name]

Examples:

  • "I work as a teacher." 🏫
  • "I work as a software developer." 💻

Same meaning! Both are correct. "I'm a teacher" = "I work as a teacher"

Are you a student? Say: "I'm a student" 🎓

Being a student is a great answer to "What do you do?" — it's exactly what you do!

  • "I'm a student." 🎓
  • "I'm a student at university." 🎓
  • "I'm a student and I study English." 📚 (or say another subject: medicine, engineering, languages...)

Other answers you might give

You can also say:

  • "I'm retired." 🏖️ (used to work but don't anymore)
  • "I'm looking for a job." 🔍 (searching at the moment)
  • "I'm a parent." 👨‍👩‍👧 (taking care of children at home)
⚠️ Important Rule: These patterns describe what you DO REGULARLY NOW. It's your current job. Not what you did yesterday, not what you will do tomorrow — what you do right now.
