My methodology choices — notes

Presenting your methodology — key phrases

I chose PPP because it provides a clear scaffold from form-focused input to communicative output, which is particularly effective for introducing new grammar structures at this level.
This approach aligns with Krashen's Input Hypothesis — students are exposed to language slightly beyond their current competence (i+1) before being asked to produce it.
The controlled practice stage follows Swain's Output Hypothesis — producing language forces students to notice gaps in their knowledge.
The communicative stage reflects CLT principles — meaning-focused interaction in a personalised context that mirrors authentic language use.
To address the needs of students with DSA, I have provided a graphic organiser / word bank / extended processing time during the practice activity.
The lesson follows the CEFR action-oriented approach, treating students as social agents performing communicative tasks rather than passive recipients of grammar instruction.
ICT is integrated through [tool], which enhances engagement, provides authentic input, and allows for immediate formative feedback.

The panel will probe your choices. These are the most common questions — have your answers ready before you walk in.
Why did you choose this particular approach?
Name the method (PPP/TBL/CLT), then link it to the topic, level, and learners.
How does this lesson reflect the CEFR action-oriented approach?
Students as social agents, can-do descriptors, tasks that mirror real communication.
How have you catered for students with DSA or learning differences?
Name specific strategies: graphic organisers, extra time, simplified handouts, multisensory input, UDL framework.
How would you assess whether the lesson aims were achieved?
Both during the lesson (CCQs, observation, exit task) and after (homework, next lesson review).
How does your lesson link to the national curriculum (Indicazioni Nazionali)?
Reference to competenze chiave europee, development of autonomous learners, cross-curricular links.
Why did you choose this particular text / material?
Authentic input, level-appropriate, engaging, culturally relevant, supports the target language in context.
How would you handle errors during the speaking activity?
Distinguish fluency tasks (delayed/selective correction) from accuracy tasks (immediate). Recast vs. explicit correction.
