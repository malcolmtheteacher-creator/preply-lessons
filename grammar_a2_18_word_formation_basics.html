Job Words: Teacher, Driver, Actor

Turn an action into the person who does it — for jobs and everyday roles.

Spot the pattern

Three people and what they do:

  • "My aunt teaches at the primary school. She's a teacher."
  • "My cousin sings in a wedding band. She's a singer."
  • "My brother-in-law directs adverts for TV. He's a director."

Before you check: what do you add to the verb to name the person?

Check the pattern CLICK
Most of the time, add -er: teach → teacher, sing → singer. A smaller group adds -or instead: direct → director, act → actor. There's no rule for which one — you just learn them as you meet them.
verb + -er → the person who does it (most jobs)
verb + -or → the person who does it (a smaller group of jobs)
"She acts in films."
→
"She's an actor."
Watch out: a person who cooks is a cook — no ending at all! A "cooker" is the machine in the kitchen, not a person.

Opposites: Happy → Unhappy

Add a small piece to the front of an adjective to get its opposite.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday complaints:

  • "It rained all week on our holiday — so unlucky!"
  • "He never says thank you — quite unkind, really."
  • "I felt really unhappy when the trip was cancelled."

What do all three bold words have in common? Say your idea before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
un- + adjective = the opposite meaning. lucky → unlucky, kind → unkind, happy → unhappy. It's one of the most common ways to make an opposite in English.
un- + adjective → the opposite meaning (most adjectives)
Careful: not every adjective uses un-. "Possible" is irregular — the opposite is impossible (im-, not un-). There's no way to guess this one; you just have to learn it.

Feelings: Bored or Boring?

Two words from the same verb — one for how you feel, one for the thing that causes it.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday comments:

  • "That meeting was so boring — I nearly fell asleep."
  • "I felt really bored in that meeting — nothing happened."
  • "The news last night was surprising — nobody expected it."

Which word describes the THING, and which describes the PERSON? Say your idea before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
-ing describes the thing or situation that causes the feeling: a boring meeting, surprising news. -ed describes the person who feels it: I was bored, we were surprised.
the thing/situation that causes the feeling → adjective + -ing
the person who feels it → adjective + -ed
"This job bores me."
→
"I'm bored. The job is boring."
Big trap: "I am boring" does NOT mean you feel no interest — it means you make other people feel that way! If you mean YOU feel no interest, say "I am bored." And remember: you're interested in something, never "interesting in" it.
