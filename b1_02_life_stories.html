Past Simple Review

Use the Past Simple to tell stories about accidents and incidents that happened in the past.

Complete These Sentences

1. I _____ had _____ an accident.

2. We _____ ran _____ out of petrol.

3. I _____ got stung _____ by a bee.

4. He _____ lost _____ control of his car.

5. Sorry, I _____ got stuck _____ in a traffic jam.

6. She _____ fell _____ and _____ sprained _____ her ankle.

Past Simple Formation

Regular verbs: verb + -ed

opened, twisted, bruised, damaged, rushed

Irregular verbs: change form (no -ed)

fell, broke, caught, ran, lost, had, got, sprained

Sound Linking

When you tell a story, link words together smoothly. Notice how words blend:

climbed out (climbed + out sounds like "climbed-aut")

I damaged it (damaged + it sounds like "damaged-it")

She tripped on the rug (tripped + on sounds like "tripped-on")

He slipped on the ice (slipped + on sounds like "slipped-on")

It crashed into the tree (crashed + into sounds like "crashed-int-o")

Narrative Linkers

Use these words and phrases to connect ideas in your story. They help your audience follow what happened.

Beginning the Story

Once upon a time...
Used for fairy tales or stories from long ago
One day...
Perfect for personal stories
It all started when...
Explains how the story began

What Happened Next

Then
Shows the next event in order
After that
Moves to the next part of the story
Suddenly
Something unexpected happened
Meanwhile
Something else was happening at the same time

Showing Results

As a result
This happened because of what came before
So
Shows cause and effect
Therefore
More formal way to show results

How the Story Ended

In the end
Final part of the story
Finally
The last thing that happened

Example: Using Linkers in a Story

One day I was riding my bike down the hill. Suddenly I hit a big bump. As a result, I crashed into a bin and hurt my leg. In the end, I learned to ride more carefully!

Plan Your Story

Think of a story about something that happened to you. Choose one of these scenarios:

A funny or annoying incident at school

What happened? When? Who was there? What was the result?

A bad weekend

Did something go wrong? What did you do about it?

A problem on holiday

Travel problems? Lost luggage? Weather? How did you solve it?

A party or family celebration

What made it special? Was anything unexpected?

A time when you did something naughty

What happened? Did you get caught? What did you learn?

Story Planning Checklist

✓ What is the main event? (the problem or the interesting thing that happened)

✓ When did it happen? (age, time, season)

✓ Where were you? (school, home, on holiday)

✓ What did you do? (use Past Simple verbs)

✓ What was the result? (did you learn something? was it funny? was it embarrassing?)

Language Reference

Use this section to review key vocabulary and grammar from the lesson.

Past Simple Verbs for Accidents & Stories

Regular verbs (add -ed):

  • collide → collided
  • slip → slipped
  • catch fire → caught fire
  • get stuck → got stuck
  • open → opened
  • twist → twisted
  • bruise → bruised
  • damage → damaged
  • rush → rushed

Irregular verbs (change completely):

  • have → had
  • run → ran
  • lose control → lost control
  • break down → broke down
  • get stung by a bee → got stung by a bee
  • fall → fell
  • sprain → sprained
  • climb → climbed

Narrative Linkers Chart

Beginning Once upon a time, One day, It all started when
What Happened Next Then, After that, Suddenly, Meanwhile
Results So, As a result, Therefore, Because of this
How It Ended In the end, Finally, Eventually

Story Framework

One day, [describe the situation]. Suddenly, [something unexpected happened]. As a result, [what you did]. In the end, [how the story finished].

Key Grammar Points

Past Simple Affirmative:

I / You / He / She / It / We / They + verb in past form

Example: I crashed into a bin. She fell off her bike. They got stuck in traffic.

Sound Linking:

When a word ends with a consonant sound and the next word starts with a consonant, the sounds blend together smoothly.

climbed out → "climbed-aut" | tripped on → "tripped-on"
