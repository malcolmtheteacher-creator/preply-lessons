Section 4 — two summaries, about 7 minutes

The last section of the test is the one closest to real airport work: something happens, and you tell someone else, clearly and in order, what happened. It has two parts. Both are recorded, and both are marked by the two raters on all six skills — pronunciation, structure, vocabulary, fluency, comprehension and interaction.
PartWhat happensThe clock
Part 1 · Report + key wordsA short written report appears on the screen. Read it and take notes. Then the report disappears and five key words appear. Summarise the report aloud, using all five words.90 seconds to read and take notes · 90 seconds to speak.
Part 2 · Radio summaryListen to a radio exchange between pilots and controllers. It starts as a routine situation, then something unexpected happens. Take notes, then summarise what happened in your own words.About 2 minutes of audio · 45 seconds to speak. You may ask for one replay.
The rules that cost marks:
  • All five key words, exactly as written. If the word is showed, you cannot say shows or showing. Build your sentence around the word.
  • Any order. The words do not have to appear in the order they are shown — use them where they fit the story.
  • Fill the time. 90 seconds is longer than it feels. Stopping after 40 seconds loses fluency marks.
  • Part 2 is about the turn. A summary that describes only the routine part misses the point. The raters want the unexpected event, what was done about it, and how it ended.
Five moves for any summary
  1. The scene — who, where, when.
  2. The problem — what went wrong, or what changed.
  3. The action — what people did about it.
  4. The result — how it ended.
  5. What next — a decision, a recommendation, a check.
Five moves, one or two sentences each, and you have a clear, complete summary — with no need to remember every detail.
Warm up — the five moves on your own day (45 seconds)

Think of a recent shift when something did not go as planned — the weather changed, a system failed, an observation was late. Summarise it in five moves: the scene, the problem, the action, the result, what next.

The language of a summary

A summary retells what other people said and did, so it runs on reporting verbs in the past, a few linking words to show the turn, and one hard rule about the key words.

1 · Reporting verbs

VerbPatternExample
reportedreported + that + clauseThe crew reported that the left brake was overheating.
requestedrequested + noun · requested + to + verbThe pilot requested a longer runway. · The pilot requested to hold over the sea.
instructed / toldinstructed + someone + to + verbGround told the vehicle to leave the taxiway.
advisedadvised + someone + of + noun · advised + someone + that + clauseThe tower advised the crew that the ILS was out of service.
confirmedconfirmed + noun · confirmed + that + clauseThe engineer confirmed that the tyre was undamaged.
recommendedrecommended + -ing · recommended + that + clauseThe team recommended replacing the lights. · The team recommended that the lights should be replaced.
Tense: the summary is in the past, so what people said moves back one step. The pilot says “We have a hydraulic problem” → The pilot reported that they had a hydraulic problem. Be careful not to change the meaning: are losing (it is still happening) is not the same as have lost (it is finished).

Choose the correct sentence

1. The pilot wanted permission for a priority landing.
2. The controller gave an instruction: climb to flight level 120.
3. The report said: “The runway should be inspected more often.”
4. The pilot said: “We are losing fuel pressure.”
5. The tower told the pilot about strong gusts on final.

2 · Linking words for the turn

Contrast (the turn): however, but, although, despite this  ·  Result: as a result, so, therefore  ·  Time: meanwhile, shortly afterwards, eventually, in the end.
Example: The inspection found nothing unusual. However, twenty minutes later a second pilot reported debris near the same spot. As a result, the runway was closed again.
6. The approach was normal. ___, at 500 feet the pilot reported a bird strike.
7. The fog did not lift. ___, the aircraft diverted to its alternate.
8. ___ the engine readings were normal, the crew decided to return.
9. The fire crew attended the aircraft on the runway. ___, the other arrivals were told to hold.

Part 1 — the report and the five key words

Three reports, on the real clocks. For each: start the 90-second clock, read and make notes in the S-P-A-R-N frame, then hide the report. The five key words appear — start the second clock and summarise aloud, using every word exactly as written. Then compare with the model.
A good habit: when the key words appear, tick each one on your notes as you use it. Five ticks before the clock ends = no lost marks for missing words.

Part 2 — the radio summary

In the test you hear one radio exchange of about two minutes. It begins as routine work, then something unexpected happens. You may take notes, and you may ask for one replay. Then you have 45 seconds to say what happened. The two exchanges here are shorter than the real one, but they work the same way. Press Listen, make notes without looking at the script, then summarise.
45 seconds = four sentences.
  1. The routine: An aircraft was on a normal approach to runway…
  2. The turn: Suddenly, the pilot reported that…
  3. The response: The controller asked… / told… / arranged…
  4. The ending: In the end, the aircraft…
Call signs and frequencies are not needed in the summary — say the aircraft, the pilot, the controller. Keep the numbers that matter to the story: a height, a distance, a runway.

Exchange 1 — on final approach

APP: Serra 7 1 2, descend to 3,000 feet, QNH 1 0 1 4, cleared ILS approach runway 3 4.
P: Descending 3,000 feet, QNH 1 0 1 4, cleared ILS runway 3 4, Serra 7 1 2.
APP: Serra 7 1 2, contact Tower 1 1 8 decimal 7, good day.
P: Tower 1 1 8 decimal 7, Serra 7 1 2.
P: Tower, Serra 7 1 2, established ILS runway 3 4, 8 miles.
TWR: Serra 7 1 2, Tower, continue approach, wind 330 degrees 9 knots, you are number one.
P: Continue approach, Serra 7 1 2.
P: Tower, Serra 7 1 2, we just saw a drone, very close, on our left side, at about 1,500 feet, 5 miles from touchdown.
TWR: Serra 7 1 2, roger, can you describe it?
P: Small and dark, it passed about 100 metres from our left wing, moving east.
TWR: Serra 7 1 2, thank you, runway 3 4, cleared to land, wind 330 degrees 8 knots. All stations, a drone has been reported 5 miles south of the airport at 1,500 feet, the police have been informed.
P: Cleared to land runway 3 4, Serra 7 1 2.
Summarise it aloud — 45 seconds
00:45
An aircraft was making a normal ILS approach to runway 34. About five miles from touchdown, the pilot suddenly reported a drone that passed very close to the left wing, at around 1,500 feet. The controller asked for a description and then cleared the aircraft to land. The controller also warned all other aircraft about the drone and said that the police had been informed.

Exchange 2 — just after take-off

TWR: Lumo 4 3 8, runway 1 6, cleared for take-off, wind 150 degrees 12 knots.
P: Cleared for take-off runway 1 6, Lumo 4 3 8.
TWR: Lumo 4 3 8, contact Radar 1 2 6 decimal 2, good day.
P: 1 2 6 decimal 2, Lumo 4 3 8.
P: Radar, Lumo 4 3 8, passing 2,500 feet, climbing to flight level 90.
RAD: Lumo 4 3 8, Radar, identified, continue climb to flight level 90.
P: Radar, Lumo 4 3 8, we have a warning light for the rear cargo door. We would like to stop our climb at 6,000 feet and return to the airport.
RAD: Lumo 4 3 8, roger, stop climb at 6,000 feet, turn right heading 340. Do you require any assistance?
P: Stopping climb at 6,000 feet, right heading 340. Negative assistance at this time, but we would like the fire services to check the door after landing, Lumo 4 3 8.
RAD: Lumo 4 3 8, understood, expect ILS approach runway 1 6. The fire services will meet you at the end of the runway.
P: Expect ILS runway 1 6, thank you, Lumo 4 3 8.
Summarise it aloud — 45 seconds
00:45
An aircraft took off normally from runway 16 and was climbing to flight level 90. Shortly afterwards, the pilot reported a warning light for the rear cargo door and asked to stop the climb and return to the airport. The controller stopped the aircraft at 6,000 feet, turned it back and offered help. The pilot did not declare an emergency, but asked for the fire services to check the door after landing, and the controller arranged it.

Part 2 — the radio exchange

GND: Pavo 2 6 1, taxi to holding point runway 2 5 via taxiway Bravo, hold short of taxiway Delta.
P: Taxi to holding point runway 2 5 via Bravo, hold short of Delta, Pavo 2 6 1.
GND: Pavo 2 6 1, stop immediately, you have passed Delta.
P: Stopping, Pavo 2 6 1. Sorry, we missed the sign in the rain.
GND: Pavo 2 6 1, roger. An aircraft is pushing back onto Delta behind you. Hold position.
P: Holding position, Pavo 2 6 1.
GND: Pavo 2 6 1, the pushback is complete. Continue on Bravo to holding point runway 2 5. For your information, a report will be filed, and the airport safety office may contact your company.
P: Continue Bravo to holding point runway 2 5, copied the report, Pavo 2 6 1.
Summarise it aloud — 45 seconds
00:45
An aircraft was taxiing to runway 25 and had been told to stop before taxiway Delta. However, the pilot missed the sign in the rain and went past Delta, so the ground controller told them to stop immediately, because another aircraft was pushing back behind them. When the pushback was finished, the aircraft continued to the runway, and the controller said that a report would be filed.

Self-check

Summarising is what a good controller does at every handover: the scene, the problem, the action, the result, what next. Practise it on your own shifts — every unusual event is a Section 4 question.
