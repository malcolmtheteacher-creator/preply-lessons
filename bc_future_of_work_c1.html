The Future of Work

C1 Advanced · Discussing Workplace Transformation

The World of Work is Changing

Consider the current trends in workplaces and the possibilities emerging for the future.

Current Trends

  • Remote working is becoming the norm
  • Traditional offices may disappear
  • AI will replace some jobs
  • Freelance work is growing rapidly

Future Possibilities

  • 4-day work weeks gain popularity
  • Digital literacy becomes essential
  • Physical labor steadily decreases
  • Soft skills become more valuable

Quick Reflection

Which of these trends have you already experienced in your own work or studies? Which do you think will have the biggest impact?

Key Discussion Topics

Disappearing Jobs

Which careers are becoming obsolete? Why are they vanishing from the workplace?

Think about jobs that have changed dramatically in the last 10 years:

  • Travel agents → online booking
  • Retail cashiers → self-checkout and e-commerce
  • Customer service → chatbots and AI
  • Manufacturing jobs → automation

New Work Patterns

How are people working differently now? What changes have you noticed?

Consider:

  • Location flexibility and remote options
  • Gig economy and freelance work
  • Collaboration across time zones
  • Focus on results rather than hours
  • Multiple roles and portfolio careers

Future Predictions

How will work transform in the next decade? What skills will be essential?

Think about:

  • Technical skills that will matter (coding, data analysis, AI literacy)
  • Human skills that machines can't replace (creativity, emotional intelligence, complex problem-solving)
  • Learning and adaptation as continuous requirements
  • Work-life integration vs. work-life balance

Grammar: Talking About the Future

These structures help you discuss workplace changes and make predictions with appropriate levels of certainty.

will / won't

Form: will + verb Example: "AI will transform healthcare."

Function: Predictions about what you expect to happen. Expresses confidence in the prediction.

Use this when you have a strong belief about a future outcome.

be going to

Form: am/is/are + going to + verb Example: "Companies are going to cut costs."

Function: Intentions or plans that seem evident now. Something already in motion or planned.

Use this when you see clear signs something will happen or is already planned.

might / may / could

Form: might/may/could + verb Example: "We might see more hybrid jobs."

Function: Possibility or uncertainty. Less confident than "will."

Use this for possibilities you're less sure about or for acknowledging multiple options.

is/are + V-ing (Present Continuous for Future)

Form: is/are + verb-ing Example: "Tech is reshaping recruitment."

Function: Current trends that show what's happening now. Often implies this trend continues.

Use this for trends you're observing right now that will likely continue.

Certainty Spectrum

Choose your grammar based on how certain you are:

Structure Certainty Level Best Use
will High certainty Clear predictions
is going to Signs visible now Plans, trends underway
might / may / could Low certainty Possibilities, options
is + V-ing Observable now Current trends

Timed Speaking Task

10:00

Instructions: Talk about your chosen job or industry with focus on these topics:

  1. Current situation (2-3 minutes): Describe how the job/industry works now. What are the main responsibilities? What's changing?
  2. Trends (2-3 minutes): Discuss the trends you've noticed. Use "is reshaping," "is becoming," "is growing" to describe current movements.
  3. Predictions (2-3 minutes): Make predictions for the future. Use "will," "might," "may," and "could" appropriately. What new skills will be essential?
  4. Personal reflection (1-2 minutes): How does this future affect you? What should you prepare for?

Try to speak continuously. Use future forms naturally to discuss possibilities, predictions, and changes ahead.
