Question Forms About Films

Click each question to see the explanation:

1. "How was your weekend?" / "What was the ending like?"

Similarity: Both questions ask for a general opinion or feeling. They want to know what you thought about the whole experience or event.

Example: "How was the film?" means "What did you think of it in general?"

2. "What did you see?" vs "What kind of films do you like?"

Difference:

  • "What did you see?" asks for the name of a specific film you watched (past tense)
  • "What kind of films do you like?" asks about your preference or type/genre (present habit)

Examples:

  • "What did you see?" → "I watched Mr Bean's Holiday"
  • "What kind of films do you like?" → "I like comedies and action films"
3. "Didn't you like it?" / "Don't you like Stallone?"

What are these? These are negative questions. We use them to respond to something negative someone said.

Use: When someone says something negative, we often respond with a negative question to show surprise or ask for more information.

Example:

  • A: "I hated the film!" (negative statement)
  • B: "Didn't you like the acting?" (negative question asking for clarification)

Practice Examples

Q1: What kind of films do you usually watch? A: Mostly serious movies, but I sometimes like watching comedies.
Q2: What movie did you watch recently? A: I watched Mr Bean's Holiday.
Q3a: How was it? A: I enjoyed it but my wife thought it was boring.
Q3b: What was the acting like? A: Oh, really good. Both main actors were wonderful.
Q4a: How was it? A: I hated the film!
Q4b: Didn't you like the acting? A: Oh, it was disappointing. The acting was bad and the story was boring.

Film Reviews Task

Let's practise describing films and recommending them.

Your Task

Choose 2–3 films you have seen. For each film, write notes about:

  • The genre
  • Key adjectives to describe it (use the vocabulary cards)
  • What you liked and didn't like

Then: Discuss your films and decide on one film to recommend together.

Speaking Guide

Use these sentence patterns:

  • "I recently watched [film name], which is a [genre] film."
  • "I found it very [adjective]."
  • "What I liked was [e.g., the acting/the story]."
  • "What I didn't like was [e.g., the ending/the violence]."
  • "I would / wouldn't recommend it because..."

Discussion Questions

Let's explore your thoughts on films more deeply:

Preferences and Habits

  • What is your all-time favourite movie? Why?
  • Do you prefer watching films at home or at the cinema? Why?
  • How often do you watch films? What do you usually watch?

Types of Films

  • Do you prefer fiction or non-fiction films?
  • Can films be educational? Give an example.
  • Do you like documentaries? What topics interest you?

Film Industry and Culture

  • What do you think about comic book movies and superhero films?
  • Are there any films from your country that you would recommend to someone learning about your culture?
  • How have films changed since you were younger?

Personal Experiences

  • Tell me about a film that made you laugh. What was funny about it?
  • Have you ever watched a film that made you think differently about something?
  • Is there a film you didn't understand? What confused you?
