Does Money Buy Happiness?

People win a lot of money. Are they happier a year later?

← All the Psychology Questions

Today: you'll talk about money and happiness and use the present perfect to ask and answer questions about life experience.
Does money buy happiness? Choose the answer that is closest to what you think.

The lottery study: real or made up?

In 1978, scientists asked people about their lives after they won a lottery prize. Here are six things about this study and the studies after it. Some are real and some are made up for this game. Which are real?

1. The winners enjoyed small things, like reading or talking to friends, more than before.
Made up. They enjoyed small things a little less.
2. A later study said people feel happier when they have enough money for daily life.
Real. Money can help when you worry about bills.
3. More research says money never helps at all.
Made up. Many studies say money helps, but not in every way.
4. The winners were much, much happier than everybody else.
Made up. The winners were not much happier.
5. The scientists also talked to people who did not win anything.
Real. They wanted to compare the two groups.
6. The scientists talked to 22 people who won a lottery prize.
Real. They also talked to 22 people who did not win.
Talk about it:
  • Why do you think winners did not enjoy small things as much?
  • What would you do first if you won a lot of money?

The evidence, in three steps

Before each part, make a prediction out loud. Then read, and check yourself.

Part 1 — The lottery winners

Before you read: You win a large prize in the lottery. How will you feel in one week? How will you feel in two years?

In 1978, three scientists did a famous study. They talked to 22 people who won a lottery prize in the USA. They also talked to 22 people who did not win anything, and to 29 people who had a very bad accident and could not walk.

The scientists asked everybody: 'How happy are you? How happy are you with small, everyday things, like talking with a friend or reading a magazine?'

New words in this part — say what you think each one means, then reveal.

She won the lottery and became very rich.
lottery (n) — a game where you buy a ticket and can win a lot of money by luck.
In one study, lottery winners were not much happier than other people.
What does study mean here? (a) a place to read   (b) a piece of research   (c) a holiday
study (n) — answer (b). A careful piece of research. Study is also a verb: to learn.
Your call:
  • Would you be happier with a lot of money? Why or why not?

Part 2 — The surprise

Before you read: A later study says money helps when you do not have enough. Why do you think this is true?

The winners were not much happier than the other people. They also enjoyed small things a little less than before. Why? Maybe because everyday pleasures looked small after a big prize.

Scientists call this the 'hedonic treadmill'. Hedonic means 'about pleasure'. A treadmill is a machine that you run on, but you stay in the same place. People get used to a new car or a bigger house, and soon they want more. The study was small, but the idea became very famous.

New words in this part — say what you think each one means, then reveal.

After a few months, we get used to new things.
get used to — to feel that something new is normal after some time.
Which word means 'feeling good and pleased with life'? (h_____)
happy (adj) — Example: "She is very happy with her new job."
Your call:
  • What is the best thing that money can buy?

Part 3 — So does money help?

Before you read: Is it better to spend money on things, or on days out with people? What do you think?

More research says money can help. In 2010, scientists said that people feel happier when they have enough money for their daily life. They also said that above $75,000 a year in the USA, daily mood did not get better. But in 2021 and 2023, new research found that for most people happiness continued to go up with more money.

Other research says how you use money is important. People who spend money on other people, for example a gift for a friend, often feel happier than people who spend it on themselves. So money can help, but it is not magic.

New words in this part — say what you think each one means, then reveal.

Which word means 'the money you get from work every month'? (s_____)
salary (n) — Example: "His salary is not high."
Which word means 'a part of what you earn that you keep for later'? (s_____)
savings (n) — Example: "I have some savings for a holiday."
Your call:
  • Is a big house or more free time better for happiness?

Present perfect (experience)

Use the present perfect to talk about experiences in your life. We do not say when they happened.

The pattern:
• I have won a prize. (some time in my life)
• She has never been rich.
• Have you ever won a prize? — Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
Use ever in questions and never in negative sentences.
Past simple or present perfect?
• Present perfect: no time. I have visited Rome.
• Past simple: a time. I visited Rome last year.
Irregular participles: be → been, have → had, win → won, buy → bought, go → gone/been.

Build it

Join the two facts so they mean the same as the model will show. Type your sentence, say it, then reveal.

1. Make a question: you / ever / win / a prize
Have you ever won a prize?
2. Write a sentence: I / never / win / the lottery
I have never won the lottery.
3. Write a sentence: she / be / to Paris (two times)
She has been to Paris twice.

What would you choose?

Five choices. Choose, then say why in one sentence. If you can, use the present perfect for an experience: I have…. Then check an idea.

1. You can have a big house or more free time. Which do you choose?
Idea. Many people say free time is better for happiness. But it depends on your life.
2. You can have a new phone or a day out with friends. Which one makes you happier for longer?
Idea. Experiences often last longer in the memory than things.
3. You win a lot of money. Do you tell your friends and family?
Idea. Some winners keep it a secret. Sometimes money changes friendships.
4. Is it better to earn a lot in a job you dislike, or less in a job you love?
Idea. Many people choose the job they love, but money worries are stressful too.
5. What is the happiest day you have had?
Idea. Often it is a simple day with other people, not an expensive day.
