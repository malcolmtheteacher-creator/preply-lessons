Key words

hold forth (verb) — to talk for a long time, like a speech, without letting anyone else speak. Example: "He held forth about politics from the soup to the pudding."
get a word in (idiom) — to manage to say something while someone else is talking too much, often with "edgeways". Example: "She talked so much that I couldn't get a word in edgeways."
bore (noun) — a person who makes conversation dull, often by saying only what everyone expects. Example: "He's a terrible bore: he agrees with everything and adds nothing."
show-off (noun) — a person who tries hard to make others admire how clever or rich they are. Example: "Only a show-off quotes Latin at a dinner party."
contrarian (noun) — a person who takes the opposite view on purpose, often just for effect. Example: "Whatever you say, a contrarian will say the reverse."
assumption (noun) — something you believe is true without checking or thinking about it. Example: "Her question made me notice an assumption I didn't know I had."
take for granted (verb) — to accept something as true or normal without ever questioning it. Example: "We take it for granted that forks go on the left."
attentive (adjective) — listening and watching carefully, with real interest. Example: "He was so attentive that I said more than I meant to."
far-fetched (adjective) — so unlikely or extreme that it is hard to believe. Example: "His idea was interesting, but the second part was a bit far-fetched."
wonder aloud (verb) — to say your questions and half-formed thoughts as they come to you. Example: "She wondered aloud why we shake hands at all."
