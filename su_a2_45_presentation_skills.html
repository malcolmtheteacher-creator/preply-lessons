The Power of Three

Good presentations often use groups of three. This makes ideas easier to remember.

Three Common Structures

Structure A

Problem → Solution → Outcome
"We have a problem with traffic. We propose building a new train line. This will reduce journey times by 30%."

Structure B

Past → Present → Future
"Last year we sold 100 units. This year we've sold 200. Next year we aim for 500."

Structure C

Pros → Cons → Best Choice
"Working from home saves time. But it can be lonely. The best option is a mix of both."

The Three-Part Framework

Every good presentation follows this pattern:

1 Tell them what you will tell them (Introduction)
2 Tell them (Main content — 3 key points)
3 Tell them what you told them (Summary)

Transition Signals

Transition signals help your audience follow your ideas. They show when you're moving to a new point.

Categories of Signals

Click each category to see examples:

STARTING: Beginning your presentation
"I'd like to start by..."
"First of all..."
"To begin with..."
MOVING ON: Changing to the next point
"Now let's look at..."
"Next, I'd like to talk about..."
"Moving on to..."
ADDING: Including more information
"In addition..."
"Also..."
"Another point is..."
CONTRASTING: Showing a different view
"However..."
"On the other hand..."
"But..."
CONCLUDING: Ending your presentation
"To sum up..."
"In conclusion..."
"Finally..."

How You Say Something Matters

HOW you say something is as important as WHAT you say.

The 3 Vs

1 Verbal — your words (clear, simple language)
2 Vocal — your voice (speed, volume, pauses)
3 Visual — your body language (eye contact, gestures, posture)

Tips for Each V

Verbal

Use short sentences. Repeat key words.

Vocal

Speak slowly. Pause between ideas. Change your tone to show importance.

Visual

Look at your audience. Stand/sit up straight. Use your hands to emphasise points.

Quick Practice

Read this sentence three different ways — fast, slow, and with pauses:

"We need to change. Our customers want better service. And we can give it to them."

Notice how:

  • Fast delivery feels rushed and unimportant.
  • Slow delivery sounds more serious and thoughtful.
  • With pauses, each sentence feels like a separate idea — very powerful.
