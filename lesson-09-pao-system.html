The Dominic System Reference

0 = O, 1 = A, 2 = B, 3 = C, 4 = D, 5 = E, 6 = S, 7 = G, 8 = H, 9 = N

Two-digit numbers are converted using these letters. For example:

  • 23 = BC (B from 2, C from 3)
  • 47 = DG (D from 4, G from 7)
  • 89 = HN (H from 8, N from 9)

Why We're Learning PAO Next

The Dominic System converts two-digit numbers into letters, which you then turn into person names. But the PAO system takes this further: it encodes six digits at once into a single, vivid image. This is what separates good memory athletes from champions.

What is the PAO System?

PAO stands for Person-Action-Object. It's the evolution of the Dominic System and the standard encoding method used by world memory champions. Instead of converting two-digit numbers into one person, PAO converts six-digit numbers into one vivid image combining a person, an action, and an object.

Key Insight: Six digits encoded as three 2-digit pairs become Person (pair 1) + Action (pair 2) + Object (pair 3) = ONE image in ONE location. This is 3x more efficient than the Dominic System.

How PAO Works

Imagine you need to remember the number 341526:

  • Break it into three pairs: 34 | 15 | 26
  • 34 = your Person entry for 34 (e.g., "Al Capone")
  • 15 = your Action entry for 15 (e.g., "Counting money")
  • 26 = your Object entry for 26 (e.g., "A briefcase")
  • The Image: Al Capone counting money into a briefcase

Three Approaches to Building Your PAO System

You have complete freedom in how you build your 100-entry PAO system (00-99). Here are the three primary approaches:

1. Dominic Letter Approach

Use the Dominic System letters (0=O, 1=A, 2=B, etc.) to determine initials, then find a famous person with those initials.

Example: 23 = BC → Choose "Benjamin Franklin" for the Person. The action and object are whatever you associate with Franklin.

2. Major System Approach

Use the Major System consonant codes to convert digits to sounds, then build names from those sounds. (If you learned the Major System in Lesson 7)

Example: 23 = "name" (using Major codes 2=N, 3=M) → Choose a person who sounds like "name"

3. Personal Association

Pick whoever you personally associate with each number—friends, family, celebrities, anyone meaningful to you.

Example: 23 might be your friend Michael born in 1923, or Michael Jordan because you associate him with excellence.

What Makes a Good PAO Entry?

Person: Should be someone famous and easy to visualize (movies, music, history, sports). Avoid generic people; go for distinctive personalities.

Action: Must be specific to that person and visually distinctive. "Running" is generic; "breakdancing" is vivid. The action should be something that person is famous for.

Object: Associate it with the person's action or public image. It should make sense together with the person and action you've chosen.

The Power of PAO: 1 Million Combinations

Here's why PAO is revolutionary:

  • You create just 100 entries (00-99), one for each pair possibility
  • But when combining them, you get 100 × 100 × 100 = 1,000,000 possible images
  • This means you can encode ANY six-digit number with a single vivid memory palace location
  • Champions often use PAO for numbers but Dominic for cards (52 cards fit easily)

Complete PAO Example

Task: Remember 583749

Break into pairs: 58 | 37 | 49

Using a model PAO system:

  • 58 = "Schwarzenegger" (Person) — Action: "Flexing muscles"
  • 37 = "Audrey Hepburn" (Action) — "Twirling in her elegant dress"
  • 49 = "Andrea Bocelli" (Object) — "Microphone"

The Image: Schwarzenegger flexing his massive muscles while Audrey Hepburn twirls elegantly around a giant microphone he's holding.

Absurd? Absolutely. Memorable? Completely.
