The House Viewing

Twenty minutes inside the biggest purchase of your life, guided by a man whose job is to make you hurry. The photos were wide-angled, the description was poetry, and the word 'cosy' is doing a lot of work. You have a list of questions. Use all of them.

The Scene 🎬

Nobody buys a jacket without trying it on twice. Houses, people buy in twenty rushed minutes — because someone in a nice suit kept the tour moving.

You and your partner are viewing a house you might genuinely buy: three bedrooms, garden, close to the school. The listing called it "a charming period property with huge potential, in a sought-after pocket" — which, translated, could mean anything from a dream to a money pit with good light.

Showing you round is Julian, the estate agent. Julian is not a villain; he's a professional whose income depends on the sale, whose language has evolved over a century to make small sound snug and problems sound like character. Every question you ask, he's answered a thousand times — which is fine, because the questions still work. Asking them is the whole game.

The skills of this twenty minutes: translating the polish back into facts, asking the questions that reveal why this house is really for sale, escaping the guided tour long enough to actually look — and leaving with your decision still your own, whatever the "two other interested couples" are supposedly doing tomorrow.

  1. What's the biggest thing you've ever bought too quickly? What question would have saved you?
  2. "Cosy", "characterful", "up-and-coming" — does your language have estate-agent words too? What do they really mean?
YouSerious buyer, done the homework, knows the street prices. Has one rule: no decisions inside the house.
JulianThe agent. Charming, quick, professionally optimistic. Every flaw has a euphemism and every silence has a filler. Respects buyers who ask real questions — they're the ones who actually buy.
First: read the conversation and count the questions You asks. Then notice which single question changes Julian's voice — there's always one that does.

The Conversation 💬

Saturday, 11am. Julian is already at the door, keys jangling, smile on.

JulianCome in, come in! Isn't it charming? You can really feel the period character. This is the reception room — cosy, as you can see, wonderful light in the mornings.
YouIt's lovely. And "cosy" meaning small?
JulianHa! Compact, I'd say. Intimate. People pay a premium for intimate these days. Now, through here—
YouBefore we tour — how long has it been on the market?
JulianOh — a little while. It's a discerning market at this price point.
YouHow long is a little while?
Julian…Eleven weeks. Which is nothing for a property of this character, honestly.
YouEleven weeks. And why are they selling?
JulianThe owners are… relocating. Family reasons. All very amicable.
YouAmicable as in divorce?
Julian*a pause* …You didn't hear it from me. But yes — which means, between us, they'd both like it done quickly.
YouThat's useful, thank you. So — what's it actually going for, realistically? Not the asking price. The street says number 14 went for thirty under asking in March.
JulianYou HAVE done your homework. Look — the asking is ambitious, I'll grant you. Realistically? Somewhere nearer number 14's money. But I didn't say that either.
YouNoted and forgotten. The listing says "scope for updating". What does "scope for updating" mean when I'm standing in it?
JulianThe boiler's original. The wiring… has history. And you'll want to look at the bathroom with generous eyes.
YouHonest answer — appreciated. Would you mind if we had a proper look round on our own? Ten minutes. You've seen it; we haven't.
Julian…Most people don't ask that. Go on then — I'll be in the garden pretending to check my emails. Cupboards open, taps run, that's what they're there for.
You*ten minutes later, returning* The bedroom cupboard has damp in the corner, and the garden wall's leaning. Also — what are the neighbours like? That trampoline next door has seen action.
JulianYoung family that side, retired couple the other. The trampoline's louder than the retired couple, I'll say that. Look — I can feel you're serious. Full disclosure: there are two other couples viewing this weekend, so if you're interested—
YouThere are always two other couples, Julian. If we go anywhere near an offer, I'd want a survey before anything — damp corner included. That's not negotiable.
JulianCompletely standard. So… shall we talk numbers? The vendors are motivated, as I accidentally told you.
YouNot today. We'll sleep on it and call you tomorrow either way — you won't have to chase us. And Julian — if the price moved towards number 14's, we might talk.
Julian*writing his number down* You know, most viewings, I do all the talking. This was refreshing. Tomorrow, then — either way.

From memory — don't scroll back up.

  1. Three facts Julian didn't volunteer come out through questions. What are they?
  2. What does "scope for updating" turn out to mean?
  3. How does You leave it — what exactly has been promised, and what hasn't?
1. Eleven weeks on the market (asked twice — "a little while" wasn't an answer); the sellers are divorcing and want it done quickly; and the realistic price is near number 14's, well under asking.

2. An original boiler, wiring "with history", and a bathroom that needs generous eyes — real money, in other words.

3. A call tomorrow, either way — that's the whole promise. No offer, no interest declared beyond "if the price moved, we might talk", and any offer would come only after a survey. The decision left the house with them.

The Echo 🕰️

The most beautiful house ever built was never lived in. The Taj Mahal — white marble, twenty-two years, twenty thousand workers — was raised by the emperor Shah Jahan as a tomb for his wife Mumtaz, who died giving birth to their fourteenth child. It is the greatest property listing in history: perfect symmetry, river views, gardens of paradise. And every inch of it is grief, dressed in marble.

Which is the thing about beautiful properties: the polish always has a story underneath, and the story is where the truth lives. Shah Jahan spent so ruinously on perfection that his own son eventually deposed him — and the emperor ended his days locked in a fort across the river, able to see the beautiful thing he'd built only from a window, at a distance, like a buyer who never got the survey done.

By all means fall in love at the viewing — everyone does, that's what viewings are for. Just remember that "charming period property" is marble-language, and ask what it cost, why it's really there, and what's in the corner of the cupboard.
