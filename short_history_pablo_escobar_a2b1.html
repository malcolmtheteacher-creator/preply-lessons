The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A poor boy who wanted more

Pablo Escobar was born in 1949, near the city of Medellín, in Colombia. His family was not the poorest, but they did not have much money. Pablo wanted much more. Even as a boy, he said he did not want to be poor.

As a young man, he did small illegal things to make money. Then, in the 1970s, he found something new: cocaine. Many people in the United States wanted this drug, and they paid a lot of money for it. Escobar started to send cocaine to the United States in secret. He worked with other criminals, and together they made a big, powerful group. People called it the Medellín cartel.

Soon Escobar had more money than he could count. He became one of the richest men in the world. He gave some money to poor people in Medellín. He built houses and football fields for them. Many poor people liked him. But most of the world knew him as a very dangerous criminal.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did Escobar find in the 1970s that made him rich?

2. Why did some poor people in Medellín like him?

1. Cocaine. He sent it to the United States in secret and made a lot of money.

2. He gave them money and built houses and football fields for them.

Talk about it:
  • Escobar gave money to poor people, but he was a criminal. Can a bad person also do good things? What do you think?

Part 2 — "Money or a bullet"

Escobar was very rich, but he was also very dangerous. He had a simple, terrible rule for people who were in his way. In Spanish it was "plata o plomo" — "silver or lead". It meant: take my money, or take a bullet. Take the bribe, or die.

Many judges, police and politicians took his money. This is called corruption. But some brave people said no to him. Escobar often killed these people. His men used guns and bombs. In these years, Colombia was a very dangerous place, and many people died — police, politicians, and ordinary people too.

Escobar was afraid of one thing. He did not want to go to prison in the United States. So he fought against the government of Colombia to stop this. There were car bombs in the cities, and it was a very dark and violent time for the whole country.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did "plata o plomo" (money or a bullet) mean?

2. What was Escobar afraid of?

1. Take Escobar's money (a bribe), or die. People who said no to him were often killed.

2. He was afraid of going to prison in the United States. He fought the government of Colombia to stop this.

Talk about it:
  • Many people took Escobar's money. Why do you think it is so hard to say no to a powerful, dangerous person?

Part 3 — The end

In 1991, Escobar made a deal with the government. He went to prison — but it was a very strange prison. He built it himself, and he chose his own guards. It was more like a big house. He still ran his business from inside. In 1992, the government wanted to move him to a real prison. So Escobar just walked out and escaped.

Now the police looked for him everywhere. A special police team, with help from the United States, tried to find him. Escobar hid in different houses in the city. But he made one mistake: he loved his family, and he called them on the telephone. Each call helped the police find him.

On 2 December 1993 — one day after his birthday — the police found his house. Escobar tried to run away over the roofs, but the police shot him, and he died. He was 44 years old. The most famous criminal in the world was dead. But the drugs and the violence did not stop with him. For many years, Colombia remembered the terrible things that happened in those days.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was strange about Escobar's prison in 1991?

2. How did the police finally find him?

1. He built it himself and chose his own guards. It was more like a big house, and he still ran his business from inside. In 1992 he escaped.

2. He called his family on the telephone. Each call helped the police find his house. They found him on 2 December 1993.

Talk about it:
  • The police found Escobar because he called his family. What does this tell us about him?

Grammar: the past simple

We tell this story in the past. English uses the past simple for finished actions in the past. Let's look at how it works.

was / were = the past of to be: "He was very rich." "They were criminals." "Colombia was dangerous."
Regular verbs = add -ed: work → worked, want → wanted, call → called. "He wanted more money." "She called her family."
Irregular verbs = special forms you must learn: go → went, make → made, find → found, give → gave, have → had, become → became. "He went to prison." "He became rich."

1. was or were?

Complete each sentence with was or were.

1. Escobar born in 1949.

2. His family not rich.

3. The police looking for him.

4. Colombia a dangerous place in those years.

1. was (he — one person)

2. was (his family — one group, singular)

3. were (the police — plural)

4. was (Colombia — one place)

2. Put the verb in the past simple

Write the past simple form of the verb in brackets. Some are regular (-ed), some are irregular.

1. He (want) more money. (regular)

2. Escobar (become) very rich. (irregular)

3. He (go) to prison in 1991. (irregular)

4. The cartel (send) cocaine to the United States. (irregular)

1. wanted (want + ed)

2. became (become → became)

3. went (go → went)

4. sent (send → sent)

3. Tell a short part of the story

Fill each gap with the past simple of the verb in brackets. Read it aloud when you finish.

Escobar (be) born in Colombia. He (want) a lot of money, so he (sell) drugs. He (become) very rich and very dangerous. In 1993, the police (find) him, and he (die).

was (be → was) · wanted (want + ed) · sold (sell → sold) · became (become → became) · found (find → found) · died (die + d)

Remember: regular verbs add -ed; irregular verbs have special forms you learn.
