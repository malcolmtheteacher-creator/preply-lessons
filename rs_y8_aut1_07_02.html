Core Jewish Beliefs

Judaism is one of the oldest monotheistic religions in the world, with roots stretching back 4,000 years to Abraham. It has shaped the lives of millions of people and has profoundly influenced both Christianity and Islam. Today we'll explore what Jews believe about their relationship with God, the Torah (God's law), and what it means to be "chosen."

The Foundation: The Covenant (Brit)

At the heart of Judaism is the covenant — God's sacred promise to Abraham. This isn't a one-time event. It's a living relationship that continues through every generation of Jewish people. When God made the covenant with Abraham, He promised:

  • Abraham would become the father of a great nation
  • His descendants would inherit the land of Canaan
  • All the earth would be blessed through Abraham

In return, Abraham and his descendants had to:

  • Obey God's laws and commands
  • Be circumcised as a sign of the covenant
  • Keep God's covenant for all generations

🕎 Why This Matters Today

For Jewish people, the covenant is not ancient history. It's a living promise that connects them to God and to each other. Every act of following Jewish law is seen as fulfilling Abraham's covenant. This is why many Jews emphasize their special relationship with God and their responsibility to keep the commandments.

The Law: The Torah

If the covenant is God's promise to Jewish people, the Torah is their response — God's laws for how to live. The Torah is the first five books of the Hebrew Bible (the Jewish scriptures), also called the Pentateuch. It contains:

The Creation Story

How God created the world and humanity, and how humans are made in God's image.

The Patriarchs

Stories of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob and God's promises to them.

The Exodus

How God freed the Israelites from slavery in Egypt, led by Moses.

The Laws

613 commandments (mitzvot) covering every aspect of life, from food to relationships to prayer.

The Torah is sacred. Jews believe it was given directly by God to Moses on Mount Sinai. Every Torah scroll is handwritten with extreme care, and a Jewish scroll in a synagogue is treated with profound respect. The Torah is not just a historical or religious text — it is the word of God.

The Commandments: Mitzvot

The Torah contains 613 commandments (mitzvot). These aren't seen as burdensome rules, but as gifts from God — ways to live a holy life. They include:

Positive Commandments

Things you should do: keep the Sabbath, pray three times a day, honor your parents.

Negative Commandments

Things you should not do: don't steal, don't murder, don't eat certain foods.

Ceremonial Laws

Rules about rituals, festivals, and holy days that mark the Jewish year.

Ethical Laws

Rules about how to treat others fairly and justly, caring for the poor and stranger.

The Shema: The Heart of Judaism

"Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is One"

These words, from Deuteronomy 6:4, form the core statement of Jewish faith. The Shema is recited twice daily by observant Jews. It declares the oneness of God and the unity of Jewish faith. Everything else in Judaism flows from this simple but profound statement: God is one, God is unique, and God is our God.

The Meaning of "Chosen People" — What It Really Means

This phrase is often misunderstood. When Jews say they are God's "chosen people," it does not mean Jews are superior to other people. It means Jews are chosen for responsibility, not privilege. God chose the Jewish people to:

  • Keep the Torah and the commandments as an example to other nations
  • Serve as "a light unto the nations" — to show the world what a moral, just society looks like
  • Bear witness to God's oneness and justice
  • Work for justice, peace, and righteousness in the world

A Common Misunderstanding

Some people think "chosen people" means Jews believe they are better than others. This is wrong. The phrase means Jewish people have a covenant responsibility — they were chosen to fulfill God's law, not to be superior. It's a burden and an honor, not a claim of supremacy.

Debate: Is It Fair for God to Choose One Group?

This is one of the most important ethical questions in religion. If God made a special covenant with the Jewish people, does that mean God treats different groups of people differently? Is that fair? Let's explore both perspectives.

✓ It's Fair and Necessary

Arguments for:

  • God chose Abraham and his descendants to carry out a specific mission — to be a moral example
  • Being chosen is not a privilege, it's a burden of responsibility
  • Other religions also have special relationships with God (Christians claim Jesus is God's Son, Muslims believe Muhammad is the final prophet)
  • Throughout history, God has called different people for different purposes
  • Covenant creates identity and continuity for the Jewish people

✗ It's Problematic and Unfair

Arguments against:

  • If God created all humans equally, how can He choose one group over others?
  • The idea of a "chosen people" has been used to justify discrimination and violence
  • It creates an "us versus them" mentality that divides humanity
  • If Jews are chosen for responsibility, what about everyone else? Are they left out?
  • This concept has contributed to antisemitism throughout history

Your Judgment:

Which argument is more convincing to you? Write a response that includes:

  • Your position (the "chosen people" concept is fair / it's unfair / it's complicated)
  • At least 2 reasons to support your view
  • One point from the opposing view that you think is valid

Scholar's Note:

Academics, theologians, and believers themselves have strong views on this question. There's no single "right answer." What matters is that you think carefully about justice, equality, and what it means for people to have different relationships with God.
