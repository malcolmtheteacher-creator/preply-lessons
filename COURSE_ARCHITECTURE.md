# Speaking Pathway: A2 → C1
## Complete Course Architecture

---

## The Problem with Grammar-First Courses

Traditional courses follow a grammar syllabus: tenses → modals → conditionals → passive...
This teaches **knowledge ABOUT English**, not **ability TO SPEAK English**.

**Speaking requires different skills:**
- Fluency (maintaining flow, managing hesitation)
- Real-time processing (thinking AND speaking simultaneously)
- Interaction (turn-taking, responding, clarifying)
- Discourse management (organizing ideas coherently)
- Strategic competence (circumlocution, buying time, self-correction)

---

## Our Approach: Evidence-Based Speaking Development

### Core Principles (Based on Research)

| Principle | Implementation |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Active Recall** | Every lesson requires retrieval, not recognition |
| **Spaced Repetition** | Built-in spiraling: items return at optimal intervals |
| **Interleaving** | Mix skill types within lessons (not blocked practice) |
| **Dual Coding** | Visual + verbal: diagrams, timers, color-coding |
| **Rosenshine's Principles** | Small steps, guided practice, high success rate |
| **70% Production** | Majority of lesson time = student speaking |

## Course Structure: 75 Lessons

### Level Breakdown

| Level | Lessons | Focus | Hours |
|-------|---------|-------|-------|
| **A2** | 1-20 | Foundation Speaking | ~20 |
| **B1** | 21-40 | Independent Speaking | ~20 |
| **B2** | 41-60 | Advanced Speaking | ~20 |
| **C1** | 61-75 | Sophisticated Speaking | ~15 |

### Spiraling System

Every lesson includes a **Recall Zone** that retrieves 2-3 items from previous lessons:

```
Lesson 5:  Recalls items from Lessons 1, 2
Lesson 10: Recalls items from Lessons 4, 6, 7
Lesson 15: Recalls items from Lessons 8, 10, 12
Lesson 20: Recalls items from Lessons 10, 14, 17
```

**Optimal Spacing Intervals:** +3 → +7 → +14 → +21 lessons

## The Six Speaking Skill Domains

### 1. FLUENCY & FLOW
- Hesitation fillers (um, well, you know...)
- Thinking time strategies (That's a good question...)
- Self-correction phrases (What I mean is...)
- Chunking and rhythm

### 2. INTERACTION & RESPONSE
- Active listening signals (Right, I see, Really?)
- Clarification requests (Sorry, what do you mean by...?)
- Turn-taking management
- Building on what others say (That reminds me of...)

### 3. DISCOURSE & STRUCTURE
- Organizing monologues (Firstly... Moving on... Finally...)
- Sequencing and narrating
- Comparing and contrasting
- Cause and effect

### 4. OPINION & ARGUMENT
- Expressing views (I'd say... In my opinion...)
- Hedging certainty (It seems to me... I might be wrong, but...)
- Supporting with reasons
- Acknowledging other views (I see your point, however...)

### 5. DESCRIPTION & EXPLANATION
- Describing people, places, objects
- Explaining processes
- Giving definitions (It's a kind of... It's like... It's used for...)
- Paraphrasing when stuck

### 6. REGISTER & APPROPRIACY
- Formal vs informal
- Softening and politeness
- Idiomatic expression
- Cultural awareness

## Lesson Template: Speaking Pathway

### Standard 6-Tab Structure

| Tab | Name | Time | Purpose |
|-----|------|------|---------|
| 1 | **The Spark** | 3 min | Engaging hook that creates NEED to speak |
| 2 | **Micro-Skill** | 5 min | One speaking technique with examples |
| 3 | **Guided Practice** | 8 min | Scaffolded speaking with prompts |
| 4 | **Free Production** | 10 min | Extended speaking (timed, 70% student talk) |
| 5 | **Recall Zone** | 4 min | Spaced retrieval from previous lessons |
| 6 | **Self-Check** | 2 min | Reflection + "I can..." statement |

### Key Differences from Grammar Lessons

| Grammar Lesson | Speaking Pathway Lesson |
|----------------|------------------------|
| Rule explanation | Technique demonstration |
| Gap-fill exercises | Speaking prompts |
| Accuracy focus | Fluency focus |
| Written output | Spoken output |
| Controlled practice | Free production |
| Teacher-centered | Student-centered |

## Assessment: "I Can..." Statements

### A2 Exit Criteria
- I can describe my daily life and experiences
- I can give simple opinions with reasons
- I can tell short stories with a clear structure
- I can ask and answer questions in conversation
- I can use basic strategies when I don't know a word

### B1 Exit Criteria
- I can discuss familiar topics at length
- I can express and justify opinions clearly
- I can structure responses with introduction, body, conclusion
- I can handle unexpected questions with thinking time phrases
- I can agree and disagree politely

### B2 Exit Criteria
- I can argue a point of view with supporting evidence
- I can discuss abstract topics fluently
- I can adjust my register for different situations
- I can use sophisticated hedging and qualification
- I can handle challenging questions with confidence

### C1 Exit Criteria
- I can speak fluently on any topic without preparation
- I can use language precisely and nuancedly
- I can employ humor, irony, and cultural references
- I can lead and manage complex discussions
- I can adapt my style to any professional context

## Implementation Notes

### For Each Lesson:
1. **Spark must create authentic need** - not artificial "let's practice hedging"
2. **Micro-skill = ONE thing** - not 5 expressions, ONE technique
3. **Guided practice = scaffolded success** - high success rate builds confidence
4. **Free production = real communication** - meaningful, not mechanical
5. **Recall Zone = retrieval practice** - not review, RECALL (without looking)
6. **Self-check = metacognition** - awareness of own progress

### Technical Requirements:
- Working timers (3 min, 5 min options)
- Click-to-reveal for Recall Zone
- "Now You Speak" prompts throughout
- Mobile responsive
- Progress tracking integration
