The conversation, in three parts

Look at the picture. Say:

Anna and Leo have lunch together. Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and answer the last one about you.

Part 1 — Are you married?

Anna: Are you married, Leo?

Leo: Yes, I am. My wife's name is Carla. She's a doctor.

Leo: And you, Anna? Are you married?

Anna: No, I'm not. But I have a boyfriend. His name is Pedro.

Part 2 — Children

Anna: Do you have children?

Leo: Yes, I do. I have two children, a son and a daughter.

Anna: How old are they?

Leo: My son is fifteen and my daughter is ten.

Anna: Fifteen! What are their names?

Leo: Marco and Sofia.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How many children does Leo have?

2. How old is his daughter?

1. Two — a son and a daughter.

2. She is ten. Her name is Sofia.

Talk about it:
  • And you? Do you have children? How old are they?

Part 3 — Brothers and sisters

Leo: Do you have brothers and sisters?

Anna: Yes! I have three brothers. It's a big family.

Leo: Three brothers! Do they live in Brazil?

Anna: Two brothers live in Brazil. One brother lives in Portugal.

Leo: And your parents?

Anna: They live in São Paulo. I call them every Sunday.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How many brothers does Anna have?

2. Where do her parents live?

1. Three. Two live in Brazil and one lives in Portugal.

2. In São Paulo. She calls them every Sunday.

Talk about it:
  • And you? Do you have brothers and sisters? Where do they live?

Grammar: I have / his, her

To talk about family, you need two small things: have, and the words his and her.

I have… "I have one sister." "I have two children." No children? "I don't have children."
Do you have…? Answer Yes, I do or No, I don't: "Do you have brothers?" — "Yes, I do. Two."
he → his · she → her. "This is my father. His name is Paul. He's seventy." "This is my sister. Her name is Eva. She's a nurse."
How old…? "How old is he?" — "He's twenty." · "How old are they?" — "They're five and eight."
