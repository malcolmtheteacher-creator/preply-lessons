10 Essential Social Science Concepts

Economic Systems & Theory

1Click to reveal
Supply & Demand
1776 - Adam Smith
Definition: The economic principle where price is determined by supply (how much is available) and demand (how much people want it).
Key Figure: Adam Smith (The Wealth of Nations)
Why: Foundation of all market economies worldwide
2Click to reveal
GDP (Gross Domestic Product)
20th Century Development
Definition: Total monetary value of all goods and services produced within a country in a specific period.
Example: USA GDP ~$27 trillion (world's largest)
Why: Measures economic health and growth of nations
3Click to reveal
Capitalism vs. Communism
1867 - Marx's Das Kapital
Capitalism: Free markets, private ownership, profit motive
Communism: Central planning, collective ownership, no class system
Key Figure: Karl Marx (Communist theory)
Why: Defines 20th-century geopolitics and economics

Political Systems & Governance

4Click to reveal
Democracy
508 BC - Athens, Greece
Definition: Government by the people, for the people. Citizens vote on laws and leaders.
Origins: Ancient Athens first democracy
Why: The most widely claimed ideal of government today, but far from universal: the Economist Intelligence Unit's Democracy Index 2024 rated 71 of 167 countries as full or flawed democracies
5Click to reveal
Social Contract
1600s-1700s - Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau
Definition: Implicit agreement where citizens give up some freedoms to government in exchange for order and protection.
Key Philosophers: Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau
Why: Justifies all legitimate government authority
6Click to reveal
United Nations
1945 - 193 member states
Definition: International organisation promoting peace, security, and cooperation between nations.
Members: 193 countries (almost every nation)
Why: Prevents world wars; coordinates global issues

Global Systems & Rights

7Click to reveal
Human Rights
1948 - UN Declaration
Definition: Universal, inalienable rights belonging to every human (life, liberty, dignity).
Key Document: Universal Declaration of Human Rights (30 articles)
Why: Foundation of international law and justice
8Click to reveal
Inflation
Ongoing economic phenomenon
Definition: Sustained rise in general price levels of goods and services over time.
Causes: Increased money supply, rising demand, increased costs
Why: Affects purchasing power and living standards globally
9Click to reveal
Globalisation
1990s-Present
Definition: Interconnected world economy where goods, services, capital, and ideas flow across borders.
Features: Free trade, global supply chains, cultural exchange
Why: Defines 21st-century economics and politics
10Click to reveal
Sustainable Development
1987 (Brundtland Commission)
Definition: Development that meets present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs.
UN Goals: 17 Sustainable Development Goals (climate, poverty, health)
Why: Critical for environmental survival and equity

Elaboration: How These Concepts Connect

Understanding the relationships between concepts anchors them in deeper memory.

The Historical Flow

  • 1776: Adam Smith's Supply & Demand → Foundation of capitalism
  • 1867: Marx rejects capitalism → Communism born
  • 1945: WWII ends → UN created to prevent future wars
  • 1948: Post-war → Human Rights Declaration written
  • 1990s: Cold War ends → Globalisation accelerates
  • Today: Climate crisis → Sustainable Development goals critical

"An economy is not just about making money. It's about distributing resources justly while preserving the world for future generations."

— Modern economic philosophy

Real-World Impact

Supply & Demand: Why gas prices spike or drop. Why concert tickets cost hundreds.

GDP: Determines a country's power and influence. Affects interest rates and job creation.

Democracy: Gives citizens voice. Recent democratic movements in Hong Kong, Myanmar show its power.

Human Rights: Basis for prosecuting war crimes. Justifies interventions against dictators.

Globalisation: Why you wear clothes made in Bangladesh, eat fruit from Chile, use products from China.

Sustainable Development: Why companies must reduce carbon emissions. Why cities build solar farms and wind turbines.
