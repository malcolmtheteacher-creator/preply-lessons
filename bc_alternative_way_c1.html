How to Persuade & Justify Your Opinion

These expressions help you give opinions, justify them, and refer to evidence. Master these to sound credible when debating.

Giving Your Opinion:

As far as I'm concerned...

The way I see it is...

Well, I reckon that...

If you think about it...

Justifying Your Opinion:

After all...

No matter how unlikely it might seem...

I'm absolutely convinced that...

Referring to Evidence:

There is evidence that...

The theory is...

Research suggests that...

Experts have shown...

You can't deny...

Your Speaking Task: The Expert & Patient Debate

Scenario:

You are a stressed-out professional with back pain, wrist pain, and sleep problems. Your doctor prescribed pills, but you want to explore alternatives. You'll meet two experts who want to persuade you to try their therapies.

Your Role:

You are the patient. Your job is to:

  • Ask difficult and penetrating questions about each therapy
  • Show healthy skepticism
  • Listen carefully to the evidence experts present
  • Decide which therapy is best for you

Sample Questions to Ask:

1. "How long before I see results?"

2. "What does the research actually show?"

3. "Are there any risks or side effects?"

4. "Why is this better than conventional medicine?"

5. "Can you guarantee it will work for my specific problems?"

Speaking Time:

Time for your task: 10:00

During Your Discussion:

Use the persuasion language you learned. Try to:

  • Use at least 3 expressions from the language patterns section
  • Ask follow-up questions to understand the evidence
  • Explain why you choose one therapy over another
