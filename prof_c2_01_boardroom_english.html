Why this matters

The boardroom is high-stakes. The language you use signals competence, authority, and fiduciary awareness. A vague concern gets dismissed. A precise, framed intervention shapes board decisions worth millions. At C2 level, you understand the legal and governance context. You speak to power — not as a subordinate seeking approval, but as a peer with statutory duty.

Six moves for the boardroom

1 The boardroom register +
Register is formal, measured, legalistic. You're creating a record. Every word may be quoted in minutes. Avoid colloquialisms, hedging, or emotional language. Precision is power.
How it sounds

"Based on the materials provided, significant gaps remain in the commercial due diligence. Specifically, the valuation model does not account for regulatory headwinds in the EU market."

Not this

"I'm a bit concerned — I'm not sure the numbers really add up in Europe, you know?"

2 Raising concerns without undermining +
You're not attacking the CEO. You're fulfilling your duty. Start by acknowledging the strategic rationale, then move to governance concern. Use passive voice — the concern is structural, not personal.
The move

"The strategic case is compelling. However, as a board, our fiduciary duty requires us to be satisfied that commercial due diligence is complete. Currently, integration risk has not been quantified."

3 Fiduciary language +
Name your role. Invoke duty — to shareholders, to the company, to good governance. This frames your intervention as principled, not personal. It's not "I think", it's "our duty requires".
Key phrases

• In discharging my fiduciary duty... • The board's statutory responsibility is... • To satisfy our obligations to shareholders... • As part of our duty of care... • Our governance framework requires...

4 Questioning financial assumptions +
Don't say "your numbers are wrong." Instead, expose the assumption. Ask for the underlying logic. This forces the CFO or CEO to defend the model in real time — on record.
The move

"The model assumes a 12% CAGR in the combined entity. Given market headwinds in Q3, what scenarios have been stress-tested? What's the downside case?"

5 The motion and resolution +
If you're blocking or deferring, use formal motion language. It slows the process in a procedurally sound way. You're not stalling — you're requesting proper governance.
The language

"I move that we defer this resolution pending completion of a full cultural integration assessment. I seek a seconder."

6 Minutes and formal record +
Everything you say is recorded. Dissent noted in minutes is a matter of record. You may request that your position be formally minuted. This protects you and signals seriousness.
If needed

"I wish it recorded that I abstained, noting material gaps in commercial due diligence remain unaddressed."

High-stakes scenario

You're 20 minutes into the board meeting. The CEO has presented the €800m acquisition. The finance director has walked through synergies: €45m annually by Year 3. The chair is about to move to the resolution vote.

You've reviewed the materials. You've spotted it: the valuation assumes the target's US contracts will migrate to the combined entity. But your team's legal review suggests 60% of those contracts have change-of-control clauses. If triggered, they're lost. That's €28m of the projected €45m in synergies — gone.
