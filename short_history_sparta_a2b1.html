The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A city of soldiers

Sparta was a city in the south of ancient Greece. It was different from other Greek cities. Athens loved art, ideas and trade. But Sparta loved only one thing: war. In Sparta, every man was a soldier. That was the most important job.

How could the men train all day and never work? There were other people who did the work. They were called helots. The helots were slaves. They worked in the fields and grew the food. There were many, many helots — many more helots than Spartans. The Spartans were afraid of them, because there were so many.

Life in Sparta was very hard. When a baby boy was born, the old men looked at him. If the baby was weak or sick, they did not keep him. Only strong boys could stay. This was very cruel, but the Spartans wanted only strong people.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was the most important job in Sparta?

2. Who were the helots, and why were the Spartans afraid of them?

1. Being a soldier. Every man in Sparta was a soldier, and war was the most important thing.

2. The helots were slaves who did the work and grew the food. There were many more helots than Spartans, so the Spartans were afraid of them.

Talk about it:
  • The Spartans were free because slaves did all the work. Is it right to be strong or free when other people are not free? Why?

Part 2 — How a boy became a soldier

In Sparta, boys did not stay at home with their families. At seven years old, a boy left his mother. The city took him and started to train him. The training was very hard.

The boys got very little food. They were often hungry, so they learned to steal food. But if someone caught them, the boys got a hard punishment! The lesson was: be clever, and do not get caught. The boys had no shoes and only thin clothes, even in winter. They slept on hard beds. They learned to be strong and to say nothing when there was pain.

When the boys grew up, they became the best soldiers in Greece. In a battle, they stood close together, side by side. Each man had a big shield and a long spear. They made a wall of shields, and they did not run away. Other soldiers were afraid, but the Spartans were not. Their army was stronger than any other army in Greece.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened to a Spartan boy at seven years old?

2. Why did the boys learn to steal food?

1. He left his mother and family. The city took him and trained him to be a soldier.

2. The boys got very little food and were hungry, so they stole food. But if they were caught, the punishment was hard — so they learned to be clever.

Talk about it:
  • Spartan boys left home at seven years old. Is this a good way to make strong people, or is it too hard? Why?

Part 3 — The battle at the mountains

The most famous story about Sparta is a battle. In 480 BC, a very big army came to Greece from Persia. There were thousands and thousands of Persian soldiers. The Greeks were afraid, because the Persian army was much bigger than their own.

The Spartan king was called Leonidas. He took a small group of soldiers to a narrow pass in the mountains. There were only three hundred Spartans, and some other Greeks. In the narrow pass, the big Persian army could not use all its soldiers at one time. For two days, the small Greek group stopped the huge army. They were very brave.

Then a man showed the Persians a secret path around the mountains. Now the Persians could attack from behind. Leonidas and his three hundred Spartans stayed and fought. They all died. But people never forgot them. Even today, two thousand years later, Sparta is famous. When we say a place is "spartan", we mean it is simple and hard. And people still tell the story of the brave three hundred.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did the small Greek group go to the narrow pass in the mountains?

2. What happened to Leonidas and his three hundred Spartans?

1. The Persian army was much bigger. In the narrow pass, the Persians could not use all their soldiers, so a small group could stop them.

2. A man showed the Persians a secret path around the mountains. Leonidas and his three hundred Spartans stayed, fought, and all died — but people never forgot them.

Talk about it:
  • The three hundred Spartans lost the battle, but people still remember them. Why do we remember brave people who lose?

Grammar: comparatives & there was / there were

To tell this story, we compare things (bigger, stronger) and we say what existed in the past (there was, there were). Here are two easy tools.

Comparatives — comparing two things.
• Short words: add -er → strong → stronger, big → bigger, hard → harder. ("The Spartan army was stronger.")
• Long words: use more → famous → more famous, afraid → more afraid.
• After the comparative, use than: "Sparta was harder than Athens."
there was / there were — say what existed in the past.
• there was + one thing (singular): "There was a battle." "There was a king called Leonidas."
• there were + many things (plural): "There were many helots." "There were three hundred Spartans."
Remember:
• one → there was; many → there were.
• short word + -er; long word + more; then than.

Write there was or there were.

2. Make the comparative

Write the comparative of the word in brackets. Add than if you need it.

1. The Spartan army was the other armies. (strong)

2. The Persian army was much the Greek group. (big)

Fill each gap with there was, there were, or a comparative. Read it aloud when you finish.

there was (one war) · bigger than (short word + -er) · there were (three hundred — plural) · braver than (short word + -er)

Use there was for one thing, there were for many, and -er + than to compare.
