Formal Emails

B2 Level · Writing & Professional Communication

ODAC: Email Structure

Use this proven framework to structure your formal emails clearly and professionally.

O = Opening

Start the email on a positive note and explain why you are writing.

Examples:

  • "I hope this email finds you well."
  • "Thank you for your recent email about..."
  • "I'm writing to follow up on..."
  • "I appreciate your support in..."

D = Details

Provide information such as news, instructions, or facts. Keep it clear and organized.

Guidelines:

  • Break information into short paragraphs
  • Use bullet points if listing multiple items
  • Include relevant details and dates
  • Be specific and precise

A = Action

State what you will do next or what you would like the reader to do.

Examples:

  • "Could you please review the proposal?"
  • "I would like to schedule a meeting for..."
  • "Please let me know your thoughts by..."
  • "I will send you the revised document next week."

C = Closing

End on a polite and positive note. Thank the reader and provide contact information.

Examples:

  • "Thank you for your attention to this matter."
  • "I look forward to hearing from you."
  • "Please don't hesitate to contact me if you have questions."
  • "Best regards," or "Kind regards,"

CAR: Choosing Your Tone

Adjust the formality of your email based on three key factors using the CAR continuum.

C = Context

Questions to ask:

  • What situation are you in?
  • How much does the reader know about the context?
  • Is this a routine request or an important matter?

A = Audience

Questions to ask:

  • Is the reader a colleague, client, supplier, or senior leader?
  • How close is the relationship? How long have you known them?
  • Have you communicated before?

R = Request

Questions to ask:

  • How much effort will the reader need to complete the action?
  • Are you asking permission or approval?
  • Is this a small favor or a significant request?

Formality Scale

More Formal (for senior leaders, external clients, important requests):

"Would it be possible for you to review the proposal and approve the costs?"

Medium Formal (for colleagues, routine communication):

"I appreciate your support in identifying an appropriate tool. Could you please review the proposal and approve the costs?"

Less Formal (for well-known colleagues, familiar contexts):

"We've trialled a new tool and would like to purchase a subscription. It'll cost £X, and we'd get a 20% discount on renewals. Could you review the proposal and approve this cost?"

Vocabulary for Professional Emails

proposal A formal suggestion or plan presented for consideration
approve To agree to or authorize something officially
subscription A regular payment for access to a service or product
renewal To extend or continue something, usually a contract or membership
discount A reduction in price or cost
follow up To continue contact about a previous matter or request
clarify To make something clearer or easier to understand
at your earliest convenience As soon as possible; within your available time

Useful Email Phrases

Making Requests

  • "Would you be able to...?"
  • "Could you please...?"
  • "I would appreciate it if you could..."
  • "Would it be possible to...?"

Giving Information

  • "I'm writing to inform you that..."
  • "Please find attached..."
  • "As discussed, the deadline is..."
  • "The results show that..."

Closing Remarks

  • "Thank you for your time."
  • "I look forward to hearing from you."
  • "Please let me know if you have any questions."
  • "Best regards" / "Kind regards"
