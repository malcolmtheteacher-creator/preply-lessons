The Library of Alexandria

The biggest library in the ancient world — and how it was lost.

Today: tell the story of the Library of Alexandria, the biggest library in the ancient world, and talk about books, knowledge and loss. Grammar: the past simple (was, were, had, built, kept, lost).

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A library for every book

More than two thousand years ago, there was a city in Egypt called Alexandria. The kings of this city were rich and powerful. They wanted their city to be the best in the world. So they built a very big library. They had a big dream: they wanted to keep a copy of every book in the world in one place.

In those days, books were not like our books. They were long rolls of paper. We call them scrolls. The kings sent people to buy scrolls in many countries. When a ship came to the city, the guards looked for books on the ship. They took the books to the library and made a copy. Sometimes they kept the real book and gave back only the copy!

Nobody knows exactly how many scrolls were in the library. Some people say tens of thousands. Other people say hundreds of thousands. But everyone agrees: no library in the world had so many books.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What big dream did the kings of Alexandria have?

2. What did the guards do when a ship came to the city?

1. They wanted to keep a copy of every book in the world in one library.

2. They looked for books on the ship, took them to the library, and made copies. Sometimes they kept the real book and gave back only the copy.

Talk about it:
  • The kings kept the real books and gave back copies. Was this a good idea, or was it wrong?

Part 2 — The clever people

The library was not only books. It was also people. The kings gave money to clever people — scholars — so they could live and study there. The scholars did not need to work in the fields or the shops. They only needed to think, read and write. This was very special.

Some of these scholars did amazing things. One man, Euclid, wrote a book about shapes and numbers. People used his book for two thousand years! Another man, Eratosthenes, worked out the size of the whole Earth. He used only the sun, some shadows and clever thinking — and his answer was very close to the real size.

The scholars also looked after the old books. They read them, fixed the mistakes, and wrote notes to help other people understand them. For about three hundred years, Alexandria was the best place in the world to learn. Clever people came from far away to study there.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did scholars want to live and work at the library?

2. Who was Eratosthenes, and what did he do?

1. The kings gave them money, so they did not need other work. They only needed to think, read and write — and they had a huge library to use.

2. He was a scholar at Alexandria. He worked out the size of the whole Earth, using the sun and shadows, and his answer was very close to the real size.

Talk about it:
  • The kings paid people just to think and study. Is this a good way to spend money? Why, or why not?

Part 3 — How the library was lost

Many people think the library ended in one big fire. But the true story is slower and sadder. The library became weaker over hundreds of years. There were many problems, not just one.

One famous problem happened in 48 BC. A Roman leader, Julius Caesar, was in a war in the city. He set fire to some ships in the harbour. The fire moved to the land and burned many scrolls. But the library did not stop. Later, the kings lost their power, and there was no more money for the library. The scholars went away. And the old scrolls were made of paper, so they slowly broke and turned to dust when nobody copied them.

Little by little, the great library disappeared. We do not know all the books it had, because they are gone forever. But the idea of the library never died. People still believe that we must keep our books and our knowledge safe. Every big library today is a "child" of Alexandria.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Did the library end in one big fire? What really happened?

2. What idea from the library is still with us today?

1. No — it became weaker over hundreds of years. There was a fire in 48 BC, but the library also lost its money and its scholars, and the old scrolls slowly broke when nobody copied them.

2. The idea that we must keep our books and knowledge safe. Every big library today comes from this idea.

Talk about it:
  • Many people say "a big fire" burned the library, but the true story is slow and quiet. Why do people like the big, dramatic story more?

Grammar: past simple

This story happened a long time ago. So we use the past simple to tell it. The past simple is the verb form we use for finished actions in the past.

Regular verbs add -ed: work → worked, want → wanted, look → looked, copy → copied.
"The kings wanted every book." "The guards looked for scrolls."
Many common verbs are irregular (no -ed — you must learn them):
be → was / were · have → had · build → built · make → made · take → took · give → gave · write → wrote · lose → lost · come → came · go → went.
Remember:
• One thing in the past = was ("the library was big").
• More than one = were ("the kings were rich").

Regular verbs (want, disappear) add -ed. Irregular verbs (send → sent, come → came, lose → lost) change their form.
