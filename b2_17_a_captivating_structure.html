Four Powerful Speech Structures

Learn how to build a speech that audiences won't forget.

1. Heart / Head / Guts / Heart

What is it?

A framework that takes the audience on an emotional journey: warm emotions → logical information → tough truths → warm conclusion.

Example journey:

  • Heart: "I remember the day my daughter first rode a bicycle..."
  • Head: "Studies show that physical activity increases cognitive development by 23%..."
  • Guts: "But we're not giving children enough time to play. We're failing them."
  • Heart: "That's why I'm asking you to join me. Together, we can change this."

2. Mnemonic Phrase

What is it?

Use the initials of your key points to create a memorable word or phrase that audiences will remember.

Example:

A speech about effective leadership using the acronym LEAD:

  • L = Listen to your team
  • E = Empower them to make decisions
  • A = Adapt to change
  • D = Deliver results together

3. Power of Three

What is it?

Organize your speech around three main sub-topics or points. Three is psychologically satisfying to audiences.

Example:

A speech about social media includes three sections:

  • The benefits of social media
  • The challenges and dangers
  • How to use it responsibly

4. VISITT

What is it?

A formal structure especially for speeches at events. The acronym stands for: Venue, I, Staff, Importance, Thanks, Thought.

Example (speech at a graduation):

  • V: "What a beautiful venue. I love being here on campus..."
  • I: "I graduated from this university 25 years ago..."
  • S: "I want to thank the wonderful staff who made this day possible..."
  • I: "The importance of education cannot be overstated..."
  • T: "Thank you for inviting me to share this moment..."
  • T: "My final thought: your education is just beginning..."

Openings That Work (and Don't)

The first words of your speech are critical. They set the tone and capture attention.

"What if we were all blind?"
✓ GOOD: This is a provocative question that creates curiosity and engages the audience immediately.
"In this room, 90% of us struggle with public speaking."
✓ GOOD: A surprising statistic grabs attention and connects to the audience personally.
"My boss asked me to give this speech."
✗ BAD: This undermines your credibility and suggests you don't want to be there.
"I am SO nervous right now."
✗ BAD: This puts doubt in the audience's mind and makes you seem unprepared.

Closings That Stick

Your closing is the last thing the audience hears. Make it memorable.

"Before I go, one thing I forgot to mention..."
✗ BAD: This signals you didn't prepare properly and confuses your message.
"People forget what you said, forget what you did, but they never forget how you made them feel." – Maya Angelou
✓ GOOD: A powerful quote that's relevant and leaves the audience with something to remember.
"What choice will you make now?"
✓ GOOD: A closing question that empowers the audience and calls them to action.
