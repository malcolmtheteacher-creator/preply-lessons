Paragraph Structure

The Three Parts

Topic Sentence: The first sentence that states the main idea

Supporting Details: 2-3 sentences that explain and support the main idea

Concluding Sentence: The last sentence that summarises or connects ideas

Topic Sentences

Getting Started Right

A good topic sentence:

✓ States ONE main idea clearly

✓ Is specific, not too general

✓ Sets the direction for the paragraph

✓ Usually comes first in the paragraph

Examples:

"Social media has both advantages and disadvantages for students."

"Exercise improves mental health in three important ways."

Supporting Details

Building Your Argument

Each supporting sentence should:

✓ Give specific examples, reasons, or evidence

✓ Explain how it supports the topic sentence

✓ Be logically connected to other sentences

✓ Use transition words (For example, Additionally, In addition)

Concluding Sentences

Finishing Strong

Your concluding sentence should:

✓ Remind the reader of the main idea

✓ Not introduce new information

✓ Use transition words (Therefore, In conclusion, As shown)

✓ Be slightly different from the topic sentence

Essential Vocabulary

Coherence Ideas that connect logically and clearly
Unity All sentences focus on one main idea
Evidence Facts or examples that support your ideas
Transition Words that connect ideas (e.g., furthermore, however)
Elaborate Explain in more detail or at greater length
Thesis Main idea or argument of a piece of writing
Reiterate State something again or repeat it
Irrelevant Not directly related to the topic

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: Identify Parts

Which sentence is the topic sentence? "First, many students work part-time jobs. Second, they balance studying with their responsibilities. Third, they develop time-management skills. Overall, working while studying prepares students for adult life."

"Overall, working while studying prepares students for adult life." is the concluding sentence. A better topic sentence would introduce the main idea at the beginning.

Exercise 2: Write a Topic Sentence

Write a topic sentence about the importance of sleep for students.

Example: "Getting enough sleep is essential for academic success and overall well-being." (Specific, clear main idea)

Exercise 3: Spot the Irrelevant Sentence

Which sentence doesn't fit the paragraph about climate change? "Global temperatures are rising due to greenhouse gases. Cars and factories emit CO2. My cat loves sunny weather. Renewable energy can help reduce emissions."

"My cat loves sunny weather." This sentence is irrelevant to climate change discussion.

Complete Paragraph Example

Topic Sentence: "Technology has changed the way students learn in schools today."

Supporting Detail 1: "For example, students now use tablets and laptops instead of just textbooks."

Supporting Detail 2: "Additionally, teachers can access online resources and create interactive lessons."

Supporting Detail 3: "Furthermore, students can collaborate with classmates online and submit assignments digitally."

Concluding Sentence: "Therefore, technology has made education more flexible and interactive than ever before."

Key Takeaways

✓ Start with a clear topic sentence

✓ Support your main idea with 2-3 relevant details

✓ Use transition words to connect ideas

✓ Maintain unity — all sentences focus on one idea

✓ End with a strong concluding sentence

✓ Remove any irrelevant or off-topic sentences
