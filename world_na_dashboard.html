North America: Stereotype vs Reality

8 lessons on North American cultures and differences — each one opens with a stereotype and finds out what is really true.

Fake smiles, sorry-saying Canadians, giant portions, no holidays, confusing tips: Americans and Canadians attract plenty of stereotypes. Start with the Background lesson, then work through the topics. Each lesson asks three questions — what does the stereotype say, what is the reality, and why does the idea exist? — and finishes with practical tips for working, travelling and making friends across cultures.

BackgroundNorth America: Two Neighbours, One Continent
Background
To many outsiders, "North America" just means "America". In reality it is a continent where the USA and Canada share a long border, a lot of culture — and some strong differences.
The stereotype: "Americans and Canadians are basically the same people."

Culture, cultural differences and the stories we tell about each other.

1Friendly Americans, Fake Smiles?
Friendliness & small talk
Americans smile at strangers and ask "How are you?" without waiting for an answer. Are they fake — or is it a different idea of friendliness?
The stereotype: "Americans are friendly but fake — their smiles don't mean anything."
Generalising Carefully — Tend to, On the WholeOpen →
2Polite Canadians: Sorry, Not Sorry?
Politeness & identity
Canadians are said to apologise for everything — even when you step on their foot. Is "sorry" a national habit, a law, or a joke?
The stereotype: "Canadians are extremely polite and say sorry for everything."
Comparatives & SuperlativesOpen →
3Same Language, Different Words?
Language
Americans and Canadians speak English — but which English? Spelling, vocabulary and accents tell a story of independence, neighbours and immigrants.
The stereotype: "Americans and Canadians speak exactly the same English."
Reported SpeechOpen →
4Big Portions and Fast Food?
Food & daily life
Burgers, giant sodas and drive-throughs — American food has a big reputation. But North American eating is also regional, immigrant-made and changing fast.
The stereotype: "Americans only eat fast food, in huge portions."

5Live to Work? Vacation and the Hustle
Work & time off
Americans are said to live to work: few holidays, long hours and emails at midnight. What does the law say — and how is Canada different?
The stereotype: "Americans never take holidays — they live to work."

6Tipping: Generous or Obligatory?
Money & service
In the USA and Canada you tip 15 to 20% — or more. Visitors call it generous, confusing or unfair. Where did it come from, and why does it continue?
The stereotype: "Americans tip because they're generous."

Immigration & identity
The USA calls itself a melting pot; Canada prefers a mosaic. Do these images describe reality — or national stories each country tells itself?
