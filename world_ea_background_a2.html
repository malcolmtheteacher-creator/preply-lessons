East Asia: Three Countries, Three Cultures

People say “Asian culture” as if it were one thing. But China, Japan and Korea are very different.

Today: you will learn how China, Japan and Korea are different, and use who, which and where.
Talk first:
  • Which East Asian countries do you know?
  • Is Asia one place or many places?
  • What do people say about your country?

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. Japan has thousands of islands.
island (n) — land with water all around it. Example: “Japan has thousands of islands.”
2. Every country has its own language.
language (n) — the words people speak in a country. Example: “Every country has its own language.”
3. Korean writing looks very different from Chinese.
writing (n) — letters and words on paper. Example: “Korean writing looks very different from Chinese.”
4. Korea has its own alphabet.
alphabet (n) — a set of letters. Example: “Korea has its own alphabet.”
5. About 124 million people live in Japan.
million (n) — 1,000,000. Example: “About 124 million people live in Japan.”
6. The three countries are very different.
different (adj) — not the same. Example: “The three countries are very different.”
Talk it through:
  • Which word is new for you? Say a sentence with it.
The stereotype: “China, Japan and Korea are the same.”

The story

Part 1 — A big region

East Asia has about 1.6 billion people. That is about one person in five in the world. China has more than 1.4 billion people. Japan has about 124 million, and South Korea has about 51 million. Japan is a group of islands. Korea is a peninsula, and China is a very big country.

Quick check:

1. How many people live in China?

2. Is Japan one island or many islands?

1. More than 1.4 billion.

2. Many islands.

Discuss:
  • Which East Asian country would you like to visit?

Part 2 — Shared roots, different writing

China, Japan and Korea share some old traditions, for example tea and Buddhism. But they have different writing. In the 1440s, Korea made its own alphabet, Hangul. Japan uses Chinese characters and two other scripts. The three kinds of writing look very different.

Quick check:

1. Which country made the alphabet Hangul?

2. Do the three kinds of writing look the same?

1. Korea.

2. No, they look very different.

Discuss:
  • Is it easy to learn a new writing system?

Part 3 — Different today

The languages are different. In China, people speak Mandarin and Cantonese, and they cannot understand each other. The governments are different too. China has one party, Japan is a democracy with an emperor, and South Korea is a democracy with a president. “Asian culture” is not one thing.

Quick check:

1. Can people who speak Mandarin and Cantonese understand each other?

2. Is “Asian culture” one thing?

1. No.

2. No, it is many things.

Discuss:
  • What is different between your country and your neighbour?

Grammar — who, which and where

who = for people: “a person who speaks Cantonese”.
which = for things: “an alphabet which Korea made”.
where = for places: “a city where people speak Cantonese”.

1. Choose the word

1. Korea is a country made its own alphabet. (who / which)

2. Hong Kong is a city people speak Cantonese. (where / who)

3. Confucius was a man gave ideas about family and respect. (who / where)

1. which

2. where

3. who

2. Make your own

1. I know a person who ______.

2. I have a book which ______.

3. I know a city where ______.

1. ...speaks three languages. / ...lives in China.

2. ...is about Japan. / ...is very old.

3. ...people are very friendly. / ...there is a big park.
