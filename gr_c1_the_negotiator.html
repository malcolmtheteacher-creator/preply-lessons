The Negotiator

A speaking game for strategic persuasion

Warm-Up: Your First Negotiation

You want Friday off work. Your boss is reluctant. You have 60 seconds to convince them. Go!

60

What just happened?

Think about how you tried to convince your boss. Did you make demands? Ask questions? Use reasoning?

Round 2: Same Negotiation, Different Approach

Now do it again — but this time, don't make any demands. Only ask questions. You have 60 seconds.

60

Questions work better than demands. When you ask questions, your boss feels heard. They're more likely to think they came up with the solution. That's the power of negotiation: making the other person feel like they're winning too.

Today's goal: Learn how to negotiate through questions, compromise, and strategic language.

5 Power Moves for Negotiators

These are the language patterns that work. Learn them. Use them.

Move 1: The Anchor

Start with an ambitious position. This becomes the reference point for everything that follows.

"Ideally, what we're looking for is a 25% increase in budget, remote work 4 days a week, and access to your design team."

Even if they say no, you've set a high bar. Their counteroffer will likely be higher than if you'd opened low.

Move 2: The Bridge

Connect your need to theirs. Show you understand their position and find common ground.

"I can see that cost is really important to you, and I understand why. What would work for both of us is if we found a way to keep the budget down while still making the move work."

You've acknowledged their concern, then pivoted to problem-solving mode.

Move 3: The Question Trap

Ask questions that naturally lead them towards your desired answer.

"What would happen if we kept one meeting room instead of two? Would that really impact your efficiency? And if we moved the timeline back by a month, would that give us time to plan better?"

These aren't really questions — they're persuasion disguised as curiosity. The answers lead them where you want them to go.

Move 4: The Conditional

Never give without getting something in return. Trade strategically.

"I'd be willing to move the date forward to 6 weeks if you could guarantee us standing desks. Is that something you can do?"

Every concession is a bargaining chip. Use it to extract what you really want.

Move 5: The Summary Close

Repeat back what they've agreed to. This locks in the deal and prevents misunderstandings.

"So what we've agreed on is: we move in 8 weeks, you provide standing desks for the core team, we keep two meeting rooms, and we get a small budget for office improvements. Is that right?"

This protects you. Once they confirm, you both have the same understanding. No surprises later.

Negotiation 1: The Office Move

8 minutes

Your Role: Team Manager

Your team has been told to relocate to a smaller office on a different floor. Your team is unhappy, morale is dropping, and people are already talking about finding new jobs. You need to negotiate with the facilities director to either keep your current space or get significant upgrades to the new space.

Your Goals (pick at least 2):
  • Keep at least 2 of your 4 meeting rooms
  • Get standing desks for everyone
  • Keep the kitchen area in the new space
  • Push the move date back by 2 months (from month 1 to month 3)

Starting position: Your team needs at least 3 of these 4 things. If you can't get them, you'll have to tell your boss you can't keep the team together.

Their Role: Facilities Director

The company needs to cut costs. Moving the team to the smaller office saves money. But you're not heartless — you have a small budget for improvements and you want to keep morale high enough that people stay.

Their Goals:
  • Complete the move within 1 month
  • Only 1 meeting room in the new space (saves rent)
  • Standard desks only (standing desks cost €5k extra)
  • No kitchen in the new area (maintenance costs)

Starting position: Budget for 2-3 small improvements, but the core goals are non-negotiable.

Negotiation Timer: 8 minutes

480

Debrief

Reflect on what just happened:

What did you agree on? What did you get? What did you have to sacrifice?

Was this a win for both sides? Or did someone lose more than the other?

Negotiation 2: The Contract

8 minutes

Your Role: Freelance Consultant

A company wants to hire you for a 6-month project. It's good work, but you need to negotiate terms that protect you and let you work effectively.

Your Goals:
  • Daily rate: €800 (you want premium work)
  • Remote work: 3 days a week (the other 2 in-office)
  • Cancellation notice: 4 weeks minimum (you need planning time)

Starting position: You're in demand. You can walk away if they don't meet at least 2 of these goals.

Their Role: Hiring Manager

You like this consultant, but your budget is tight. You want good value for money.

Your Goals:
  • Daily rate: €550 (your budget maximum)
  • In-office: 4 days a week (you need them here to collaborate)
  • Cancellation notice: 1 week (you need flexibility if things change)

Secret: There's actually room in the budget for €650-€700/day. You can move on the rate if they budge on the other points. Don't reveal this. Make them work for it.

Negotiation Timer: 8 minutes

480

Debrief

Reflect on what you negotiated:

Did you use any of the 5 power moves? Which ones? Give an example.

What did you sacrifice? Was it worth it?

Looking back, what would you do differently?

Negotiation 3: The Partnership

10 minutes (this one is harder)

Your Role: Founder of Tech Company

You've built something innovative and a major corporation wants to partner with you. This could be your big break, but you need to protect what you've built.

Your Non-Negotiables:
  • Equal branding (your logo visible alongside theirs)
  • Access to their distribution network (this is why you want the deal)
  • You keep your intellectual property (they don't own your tech)

Starting position: You're the innovator. They need you. Don't let them push you around.

Their Role: Corporate Executive

You've seen this startup's technology. It's good. You want to own it, or at least control it.

Your Goals:
  • Your brand dominant (their logo small, yours barely visible)
  • Exclusive rights to the technology for 3 years (you own the development)
  • Majority control of the partnership (51%+ equity)

Starting position: You have the distribution power and the money. Use it.

Negotiation Timer: 10 minutes

600

Bonus Reflection

This is an unequal negotiation. Talk about it:

Was this negotiation fair? Why or why not?

The power imbalance: They have money and distribution. You have innovation. Did the imbalance make it easier or harder to negotiate?

In real business, how do you negotiate when you have less power?
