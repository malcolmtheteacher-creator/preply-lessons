Giving Your Opinion

English speakers use different phrases depending on whether they are being positive, negative, or neutral about a topic. Here are key opinion phrases for discussing medicine and health:

Positive Opinions

The good thing about [topic] is...
The big positive is...
One thing I really like about [topic] is...
I swear by [topic] because...

Negative Opinions

The problem with [topic] is...
The downside is...
One thing I worry about is...
My concern is...

Neutral or Summary Opinions

My question is...
Sounds like...to me
It seems to me that...
In my view...

Medicine: Old vs New

Explore the differences between traditional remedies and modern medicine. Some old beliefs are true, while others are just old wives' tales.

Common Health Beliefs

Feed a cold, starve a fever

An old saying suggesting you should eat more when you have a cold, but eat less when you have a fever.

Old wives' tale. Modern doctors say your body needs proper nutrition during both colds and fevers to fight infection. You should eat healthy food and stay hydrated regardless.

Honey helps soothe a cough

A traditional remedy suggesting honey can calm throat irritation and suppress coughing.

Backed by science. Modern studies show honey can be effective at soothing coughs. It is especially safe for adults and children over one year old.

Vitamin C prevents colds

A popular belief that consuming high amounts of vitamin C will keep you from catching a cold.

Mostly false. Modern research shows vitamin C does not prevent colds in most people. However, it may slightly reduce how long a cold lasts if taken at the first sign of symptoms.

Ginger settles indigestion

An ancient remedy from Asia suggesting ginger root can ease stomach discomfort and nausea.

Backed by science. Modern studies confirm ginger can reduce nausea and aid digestion. It is used in modern medicine and recommended by many doctors.

You should exercise when you feel drowsy

The belief that physical activity will cure fatigue and sleepiness.

Partially true. Light exercise can boost energy temporarily. However, if you are very tired or ill, rest is more important. Modern doctors recommend rest first, then gentle movement.

Vocabulary in Context

Match each phrase to its correct definition:

"Swear by" something: Believe strongly that it works, often based on personal experience

"Old wives' tale": A traditional belief that is probably not true or not scientifically proven

"Consume" medicine: To take or use medicine (more formal than "take")

"Alternative treatment": A medical approach that differs from standard modern medicine

The Health Debate

You are part of a government health advisory board. Your task: Should alternative medicine be covered by public healthcare?

Arguments For Including Alternative Medicine

  • Many people swear by alternative treatments, and denying them limits patient choice.
  • Some alternative medicines (like acupuncture and ginger) are backed by modern scientific research.
  • Alternative treatments often have fewer side effects than pharmaceuticals.
  • Public healthcare should provide comprehensive, holistic care.
  • Offering alternatives may reduce demand for expensive medications.

Arguments Against Including Alternative Medicine

  • The problem with alternative medicine is that not all treatments are scientifically proven.
  • Public healthcare funds are limited; resources should go to evidence-based treatments.
  • Some alternative remedies are old wives' tales with no real benefit.
  • One thing we worry about is patients delaying proven treatments to try alternatives.
  • The downside is that including unproven treatments could harm public trust in healthcare.
