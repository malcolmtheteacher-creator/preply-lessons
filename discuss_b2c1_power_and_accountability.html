Who Do You Know?

Ghislaine Maxwell

British socialite, daughter of media mogul Robert Maxwell. Convicted in 2021 of recruiting and grooming victims. Sentenced to 20 years. The only person convicted in connection with Epstein's crimes.

Prince Andrew

Duke of York, son of the late Queen Elizabeth II. Accused by Virginia Giuffre of sexual abuse when she was 17. Settled a civil lawsuit in 2022 for an undisclosed sum. Stripped of military titles and royal patronages. Has denied all allegations.

Alexander Acosta

US Attorney who approved the 2007 plea deal. Later appointed US Secretary of Labor. Resigned in 2019 when the plea deal came under intense scrutiny. Said he was told Epstein "belonged to intelligence."

Julie K. Brown

Investigative journalist at the Miami Herald. Her 2018 series "Perversion of Justice" re-opened the case after years of silence. Credited with forcing federal prosecutors to act. Wrote a book about it.

Virginia Giuffre

The most prominent of the accusers. Recruited at age 16 while working at Mar-a-Lago. Filed lawsuits against Epstein, Maxwell, and Prince Andrew. Became a central figure in the public case.

The "Little Black Book"

Epstein's contact book contained names of politicians, celebrities, billionaires, and royals. Being in the book doesn't imply involvement in crimes — but it raised questions about who knew what.

Three Lenses

There are many ways to look at this case. Let's explore three angles — and figure out where you stand.

Lens 1: Power & Accountability

Epstein moved in circles of extraordinary wealth and influence. His connections included heads of state, business leaders, and members of royal families. The 2007 plea deal — negotiated behind closed doors, with victims not consulted — is widely seen as one of the most lenient deals in American legal history for crimes of this nature.

Do you think someone with less money and fewer connections would have received the same plea deal? Talk me through your reasoning.

Lens 2: Media & the Role of Journalism

For over a decade, this story was largely ignored by mainstream media. It was a single journalist, Julie K. Brown, whose persistent investigation forced federal action. At the same time, the case has generated enormous amounts of conspiracy theory and speculation online — some based in fact, some entirely fabricated.

What's the difference between investigative journalism and conspiracy theory? Where do you draw the line?

Lens 3: Justice & the System

Only two people have faced criminal consequences: Epstein (who died before trial) and Maxwell (convicted 2021). Despite a contact book of hundreds and accusations against multiple individuals, no other high-profile figure has been criminally charged. Some victims received financial settlements from a compensation fund established after Epstein's death.

Can a financial settlement ever represent justice for victims of this kind of crime? Is it enough?
