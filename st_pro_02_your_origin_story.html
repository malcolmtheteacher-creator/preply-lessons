Your Origin Story

Strategic Storytelling

The Story You Already Have

Everyone at your level has an origin story. Most people just haven't shaped it yet.

Today: We're crafting your professional origin story using the Defining Moment method — so you can tell a compelling 90-second version that makes people remember who you are and why you do what you do.

Here's the problem with most origin stories professionals tell: they're CVs in disguise. A chronological list of jobs, promotions, and credentials. Accurate. Forgettable.

The ones that stick — the ones that make someone lean in and say "tell me more" — have a different shape entirely. They're built around a defining moment: a single point where something shifted, cracked, or came into focus.

Today we're going to find yours and build a story around it.

The Defining Moment Method

Five steps from "how I got here" to a story people remember.

An origin story isn't your whole career. It's the one moment that explains everything that came after. The method works like this:

1

The World Before

One or two sentences establishing who you were or what the situation was before the moment. Just enough context for the shift to land.

2

The Crack

The defining moment itself — something you saw, heard, felt, or realised that changed everything. Be specific. A date, a room, a sentence someone said.

3

The Struggle

What you did next wasn't easy. Include the cost, the doubt, the difficulty. This is what makes it a story and not a LinkedIn post.

4

The Becoming

How that moment shaped who you are now. Connect the crack to your current work, values, or mission. This is the "so what."

5

The Gift

Why telling this story matters to the listener. What does your story unlock for them? This is what turns a personal anecdote into a professional asset.

Two versions of the same person

Here's a CFO explaining how she got into finance:

CV version

"I studied economics at university, did my CPA, spent ten years at Deloitte, then moved into industry. I've been a CFO for seven years now across two companies."

Defining Moment version

"When I was twelve, my father's business went bankrupt. Not because it wasn't profitable — because he didn't understand his own numbers. I watched a good man lose everything over a spreadsheet he never learned to read. That's the day I decided I would be the person in the room who understands the numbers — and translates them so nobody else has to go through what my family did."

What makes the second version work? Discuss this before revealing the breakdown.

World Before: A twelve-year-old watching her father's business.

The Crack: The bankruptcy — not from failure, but from not understanding the numbers. Specific, surprising.

The Struggle: Implied — "I decided" carries the weight of years of work to get there.

The Becoming: "The person in the room who understands the numbers."

The Gift: "...translates them so nobody else has to go through what my family did." This connects her personal pain to her professional mission — and tells every listener why she cares about their numbers too.

Notice the structure underneath: this is BAB from Lesson 1. Before (father's world), After (who she is now), Bridge (the defining moment that connects them).

Build Yours

Let's find your defining moment and shape it into a story.

Finding the moment

Don't reach for the most dramatic thing that ever happened to you. Reach for the moment that explains you. It might be quiet — a conversation, a realisation, something you witnessed. The question is: what moment, if you removed it, would make your current career make no sense?

Talk through a few possibilities. There's no wrong answer at this stage.

Now shape it

Pick the moment with the most energy — the one that felt most alive when you talked about it. Work through each step:

1. The World Before

One or two sentences. Who were you? What was the situation?

2. The Crack

Be specific. Where were you? What happened? What did you see, hear, or realise?

3. The Struggle

What was hard about what came next? What did it cost you?

4. The Becoming

How did that moment shape who you are professionally today?

5. The Gift

Why should the listener care? What does your story unlock for them?

Tell It

Close your notes. Tell your origin story out loud. You have 90 seconds.

First attempt — raw

Don't try to be perfect. Don't try to be polished. Just tell the story from beginning to end, hitting all five steps, in about 90 seconds. Speak naturally — this is a conversation, not a speech.

1:30

Quick self-check after telling it

The cut

Most first drafts are too long. The single most powerful edit in storytelling is cutting. Look at what you just told and answer these:

Which sentence, if you removed it, would nobody miss?

Where did you use two sentences when one would do?

Is The Crack specific enough? Can you see it, hear it, feel it?

Second attempt — refined

Tell it again. Same story, tighter, sharper, more alive. Stay under 90 seconds.

1:30

The Pressure Test

Can you adapt it? Can you explain why it works?

Audience shift

Your origin story isn't one story — it's a core that you adapt. Try telling the same defining moment for these two very different contexts:

Context 1: A job interview

You're interviewing for a senior role. The panel asks: "What drives you in your work?" Tell your origin story in a way that answers this question. Keep it under 60 seconds.

1:00
Context 2: A keynote opening

You're opening a keynote to 500 people. You have 2 minutes before you need to transition to your main topic. Your origin story IS the hook. How do you tell it to pull an audience in?

2:00

Explain the craft

Now step outside the story and explain why the Defining Moment method works. If you were coaching someone else through this process, what would you tell them?

Speak for about a minute — explain the method and why it's more effective than a chronological career summary.

Final Retrieval

1. Name the five steps of the Defining Moment method

2. What's the difference between a CV origin story and a Defining Moment origin story?

3. Why is "The Gift" the most important step for a professional context?

1. The World Before → The Crack → The Struggle → The Becoming → The Gift.

2. A CV story is chronological and comprehensive — it tells what happened. A Defining Moment story is built around a single turning point — it tells why it matters. One is a timeline. The other is a story.

3. Because in professional contexts, your story isn't about you — it's about what you bring to them. The Gift turns a personal anecdote into a reason to trust you, hire you, follow you, or work with you.
