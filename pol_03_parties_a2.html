Two Parties or Many?

In some countries two big parties fight. In other countries many parties must work together.

Today: you will learn why some countries have two parties and some have many, and use many, a few and no.
Talk first:
  • How many parties are there in your country?
  • Do parties work together?
  • Is it good to have only two parties?

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. The party has a new leader.
party (n) — a group of politicians with the same ideas. Example: “The party has a new leader.”
2. The party won ten seats.
seat (n) — a place in parliament that a party wins. Example: “The party won ten seats.”
3. She wants to win the election.
win (v) — to get the most votes or the first place. Example: “She wants to win the election.”
4. They need a majority to pass the law.
majority (n) — more than half. Example: “They need a majority to pass the law.”
5. The two parties made a coalition.
coalition (n) — a government of two or more parties together. Example: “The two parties made a coalition.”
6. They found a compromise.
compromise (n) — an agreement where each side gives up something. Example: “They found a compromise.”
Talk it through:
  • Use three words. Say one sentence about your country.
The stereotype: “Elections are always between two big parties.”

The story

Part 1 — Winner takes all

In the USA and the UK, the person with the most votes in an area wins the seat. So big parties win most seats. Small parties often win only a few. The USA has mainly two big parties.

Quick check:

1. Who wins a seat in the USA and the UK?

2. How many big parties does the USA have?

1. The person with the most votes in the area.

2. Two.

Discuss:
  • Would you vote for a small party? Why or why not?

Part 2 — Many parties

In Germany and the Netherlands, a party gets seats about equal to its votes. So many parties win seats. Usually, no party wins more than half. So the parties make a coalition. A coalition is a government of two or more parties.

Quick check:

1. In Germany, how many parties win seats?

2. What is a coalition?

1. Many parties.

2. A government of two or more parties.

Discuss:
  • Is a coalition a good idea?

Part 3 — Waiting

Making a coalition is slow. After the election in 2021, the Netherlands waited nearly 300 days for a new government. Some people like coalitions because parties compromise. Other people say they are too slow.

Quick check:

1. How long did the Dutch wait in 2021?

2. Why do some people like coalitions?

1. Nearly 300 days.

2. Because parties compromise.

Discuss:
  • Is a slow government a problem?

Grammar — many, a few and no

many + plural noun = a big number: “many parties”.
a few + plural noun = a small number: “a few seats”.
no + noun = zero: “no party”.
Also: two, three …: “two big parties”.

1. Choose the word

1. In the USA, only big parties win most seats. (two / many)

2. In Germany, party usually wins more than half alone. (no / every)

3. In the Netherlands, parties win seats. (many / one)

1. two

2. no

3. many

2. Make your own

1. Finish: In my country, ______ parties win seats.

2. Finish: A small party often wins ______ seats.

3. Finish: After an election, ______ party sometimes wins more than half.

1. ...many / ...a few / ...two

2. ...a few / ...no

3. ...one / ...no
