What Do People Do in Their Free Time?

📺 The average person spends 4 hours and 16 minutes watching TV every day!

Meet Emma, a 32-year-old accountant from Canada. After work, she's exhausted. She comes home, makes a quick dinner, and watches Netflix for 3 hours every evening. On weekends, she sleeps until noon, scrolls on her phone, and watches more TV. Emma says, "I know I should do something more interesting, but I'm always too tired."

Now meet Chen, a 35-year-old engineer from Singapore. He works the same hours as Emma, but his free time is completely different. He goes rock climbing twice a week, plays guitar for an hour every evening, and meets friends for photography walks on weekends. Chen says, "I'm tired after work too, but my hobbies give me energy!"

🎮 People who have hobbies are 34% happier than people who don't!

Researchers at Oxford University studied 5,000 people for 10 years. They found some surprising facts about hobbies and happiness:

  • Active hobbies (sports, music, crafts) make people happier than passive hobbies (watching TV, scrolling social media)
  • People who spend time on hobbies sleep better and feel less stressed
  • The happiest people have 2-3 regular hobbies, not just one
  • It takes only 30 minutes a day on a hobby to feel significantly happier
  • People who gave up their hobbies reported being 28% less satisfied with life
⏰ But here's the problem: 67% of people say they're "too busy" for hobbies!

Dr. Sarah Martinez, a psychologist in Spain, explains: "We think we don't have time for hobbies, but most people spend 3-4 hours on their phones every day. The real problem isn't time - it's energy and motivation. When we're tired, we choose easy activities like watching TV. But research shows that active hobbies actually give us MORE energy, not less."

Think About It:

  • What do you do in your free time?
  • Are your activities active or passive?
  • Do you have hobbies that you stopped doing? Why?
  • Would you like to start a new hobby?

5 Phrases for Talking About Hobbies

Click each phrase to see examples and practice using it!

1. "I'm into..."
Formality: Informal (use with friends)
Meaning: You really like something and spend time on it. This is a very natural, common way to talk about your interests and hobbies.
Example from Chen's story:
"I'm into rock climbing and photography."
"I'm really into cooking these days."
NOW YOU TRY:
What are you into right now? Tell your teacher: "I'm into ________"
(Example: reading, cooking, watching Korean dramas, playing video games)
2. "I used to... but I don't anymore"
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: You did something regularly in the past, but you stopped. Use this to talk about old hobbies or activities you gave up.
Example:
"I used to play tennis every week, but I don't anymore."
"I used to paint, but I stopped when I started working."
NOW YOU TRY:
What hobby or activity did you stop doing? Say: "I used to _______ but I don't anymore."
(Example: play piano, go running, read books, visit museums)
3. "It helps me unwind"
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: The activity helps you relax and reduce stress after work or a busy day. "Unwind" means to relax and let go of tension.
Example:
"Playing guitar helps me unwind after a stressful day."
"Walking in nature helps me unwind on weekends."
NOW YOU TRY:
What activity helps you relax? Tell your teacher: "________ helps me unwind."
(Example: listening to music, cooking, gardening, yoga)

More Phrases for Hobbies & Free Time

4. "I've been meaning to..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: You want to do something and have planned to do it, but you haven't done it yet. It shows intention but also that you've been delaying it.
Example:
"I've been meaning to start yoga for months."
"I've been meaning to learn how to cook properly."
"I've been meaning to join a book club, but I'm always too busy."
NOW YOU TRY:
What have you wanted to do but haven't started yet? Say: "I've been meaning to ________"
(Example: learn guitar, start running, take up photography, join a gym)
5. "I try to make time for..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: You make an effort to do something even when you're busy because it's important to you. Similar to "I make time for..." but shows more effort or difficulty.
Example:
"I try to make time for reading every evening."
"I try to make time for exercise at least three times a week."
"Even though I'm busy, I try to make time for my friends."
NOW YOU TRY:
What do you try to make time for, even when you're busy? Say: "I try to make time for ________"
(Example: family, exercise, hobbies, learning English)

Active vs. Passive

The "Too Busy" Problem

Hobbies You Want to Try
What new hobby would you like to start? What's stopping you? What would you need to begin?
Social vs. Solo
Do you prefer doing hobbies alone or with other people? What are the advantages of each?
Expensive vs. Free
Do you need money to have good hobbies? What are some interesting free or cheap activities people can do?
