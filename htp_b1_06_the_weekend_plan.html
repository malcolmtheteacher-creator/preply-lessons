2

Phrases for making plans

Four kinds of thing you'll need to say. Read them aloud once.

Suggesting

  • How about …?
  • Why don't we …?
  • Let's …
  • We could …

Saying yes

  • That sounds great.
  • Perfect, that works for me.
  • Good idea.
  • Yes, I'm free then.

Saying no

  • Sorry, I can't. I'm … then.
  • That doesn't work for me.
  • I'd love to, but …
  • I'm busy on Saturday morning.

Asking for information

  • Are you free on …?
  • What time does … open?
  • When does the last train leave?
  • Is it open on Sunday?

Listen to how it sounds

AAre you free on Saturday afternoon? BSorry, I can't. I'm visiting my grandad then. How about Sunday? ASunday works for me. What time does the museum open? BAt ten. But your first train arrives at 13:40. AOh, that doesn't work, then. Why don't we go in the evening? BGood idea. It's open until nine. Let's do that.

Which kind of phrase is it?

These are new phrases. Choose the group for each one.

From your diary to a sentence

Your diary has short notes. Say each one as a full sentence about your plans. Then compare.

Say no politely

These sound rude. Say each one again, kindly, with a reason. Then compare.

Key words

arrangement (noun) — a plan you have agreed with someone, with a time and a place. Example: "We made an arrangement to meet at the station at ten."
available (adjective) — free to do something; not busy. Example: "Are you available on Sunday afternoon?"
timetable (noun) — a list that shows when trains or buses leave and arrive. Example: "Check the timetable before you leave home."
platform (noun) — the place in a station where you get on and off a train. Example: "Our train leaves from number 4."
fully booked (adjective) — with no free tables, seats or rooms left. Example: "The restaurant is fully booked on Saturday night."
suit (verb) — be good or easy for someone's plans. Example: "Does two o'clock suit you?"
forecast (noun) — what experts say the weather will be like. Example: "The forecast says it will rain on Sunday."
last train (noun) — the final train of the day. Example: "We need to leave at ten to catch the last train home."
fancy (verb) — want to do or have something (informal). Example: "Do you fancy going to the cinema?"
