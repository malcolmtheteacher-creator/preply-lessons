raise money
To collect or gather funds, often for a specific project or cause
grant
Money given by an organisation (esp. government) for a specific purpose; you don't repay it

A plan showing how much money you will spend and on what; also the total amount available
allocate
To distribute or assign money or resources to different purposes or organisations
sustainable
Able to continue and succeed without ongoing financial support; self-supporting long-term
charity
An organisation that helps people in need; also means giving money to those in need
application
A formal written request for money, funding, or admission to something
outcome
The final result or effect of something; what happens as a consequence

What Makes a Strong Grant Application?

Funding bodies look for specific qualities in the organisations and projects they support. Here are the key criteria:

1. Clear Project Outcomes

What does this mean?
The project must clearly state what will be achieved and who will benefit. For example: "We will teach digital skills to 200 elderly residents" is clear. "We want to help people with technology" is vague.

2. Identified Need

Why is this important?
Organisations must explain why this project is needed in their community. They should provide evidence: statistics, surveys, community feedback. "Local youth have limited access to sports facilities" is stronger than "We want more activities."

3. Delivery Capability

What does "capability" mean here?
Can the organisation actually deliver? Do they have experience, trained staff, facilities, and a proven track record? Funders want to know the organisation CAN do what it promises.

4. Sustainability

How should projects be sustainable?
The project must create lasting benefits beyond the grant period. For example: training volunteers so they can continue running the programme, or developing revenue streams. Funders don't want one-off projects that disappear when money runs out.

5. Sound Budget and Financial Planning

What makes a good budget?
Every pound must be justified. £5,000 for staff is acceptable; £5,000 for "miscellaneous" is not. Budgets should be realistic and detailed. Organisations should show they understand costs and have costed projects carefully.

6. Appropriate Funding Request

What does "appropriate" mean?
The grant should cover a reasonable proportion of running costs (often 70-80% maximum). If an organisation needs £100,000, asking for 100% from one funder is risky. They should show other funding sources too.

7. Risk Awareness

Why acknowledge risks?
Good applications show that leaders have thought about what could go wrong (staff leave, demand is lower than expected, etc.) and have contingency plans. This shows professional thinking.

8. Clear Leadership and Accountability

Who is responsible?
Funders need to know who is responsible for delivering the project and managing the budget. Strong governance structures and clear lines of accountability strengthen applications.

Broader Discussion: Gambling, Lotteries, and Society

Reflect on these bigger questions about lottery funding and gambling:

Is gambling addictive?

Consider: What psychological and financial factors make it risky? Are some people more vulnerable than others? What safeguards exist?

Should governments ban certain forms of gambling?

Think about: What is the difference between regulation and prohibition? Which forms are most harmful? Who should decide?

Do lotteries encourage more gambling than they should?

Consider: Are lotteries marketed responsibly? Is "gambling for good causes" different from other gambling? Is it genuinely better?

How should "good causes" funding be distributed?

Should lottery money prioritise arts, sports, heritage, or social causes? Should all regions get equal funding? Who should decide priorities?

Is it fair that only organisations with strong applications receive grants?

Consider: Does this disadvantage smaller, newer organisations? How can funding be made more accessible? Should there be quotas for different types of causes?

If you could change how lottery funding works, what would you do?

Would you change the percentage split? The criteria? The application process? Support a different type of organisation?

Key Takeaways

  • Funding decisions require balancing multiple factors: need, capability, sustainability, budget planning
  • Successful applications explain clearly WHY the project matters and HOW it will work
  • Sustainability matters: one-off projects funded by grants alone are risky long-term
  • Lottery funding is a social choice: it affects which communities and causes get support
  • Financial vocabulary is essential for discussing and managing money in professional contexts
