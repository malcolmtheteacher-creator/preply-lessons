Which update is better?

Update A

"Costs are bad this quarter. There is a big problem. I think maybe we will be late."

Update B

"Costs rose by 14% this quarter, mainly because of travel. The main risk is that we go over budget by March. I suggest we review travel spending next week."

Which would you rather hear from a colleague? Find three differences before you check.
Check the differences
  • B gives a real number (14%) and a time (this quarter).
  • B gives a reason (travel).
  • B names the risk clearly, with a date.
  • B ends with an action (review next week).
  • A uses vague words: bad, big, maybe.

Deadlines & Risks

A simple plan for any update

  • 1 Number: what happened, with a figure. "Costs rose by 14%."
  • 2 Reason: why. "Mainly because of travel."
  • 3 Action: what next. "I suggest we review it next week."

Say it your way

Here is a situation: a report is due Friday, but the data will arrive Thursday evening. Tell me the risk, then a way to reduce it. Start with "The main risk is..." and finish with "To reduce the risk, we can..."
Example answer
The main risk is that we only have one evening to check the data. To reduce the risk, we can prepare the template now and ask for a first part of the data on Wednesday.

Now answer the questions

Someone in the meeting asks you these. Answer each one out loud before you check the idea.

Question 1: Why did costs go up so much?
Idea: Give the reason first, then the number: 'Mainly travel. It was about 40,000 more than planned.'
Question 2: Is this a one-off, or will it continue?
Idea: Be honest about what you know: 'It looks one-off, but I'd like to check the next two months before I promise.'
Question 3: What is the biggest risk to the year-end target?
Idea: Name one risk and one action: 'The main risk is further cost increases. We can reduce it by reviewing travel monthly.'
Question 4: Could you send me the breakdown by Friday?
Idea: Confirm clearly: 'Yes, I'll send it by Friday morning.' Or negotiate: 'I can send a first version Thursday and the full version Monday.'
Question 5: Sorry, I didn't follow. How much was the increase exactly?
Idea: Say it simply and slowly: 'Costs went from 360,000 to 410,000, so an increase of 50,000, or about 14%.'
