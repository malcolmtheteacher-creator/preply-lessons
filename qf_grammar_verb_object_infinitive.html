Verb + Object + to-infinitive

Master this essential grammar pattern

🔍 Diagnose

Study these two sentences. One is wrong, one is correct. Can you spot the difference?

✗ Incorrect
She told to me to call.
Why is this wrong? The verb "tell" takes the pattern: tell + OBJECT + to-infinitive. The object (the person being told) comes directly after the verb, not before it. Here, "to me" is in the wrong position.
✓ Correct
She told me to call.
Now the object (me) is in the correct position — right after the verb. The structure is: verb (told) + object (me) + to-infinitive (to call).

Here's another example:

✗ Incorrect
I want that he comes.
Why is this wrong? Using "that" + clause is not the correct pattern. The verb "want" needs: object + to-infinitive. Since we're talking about "he," the object is "him."
✓ Correct
I want him to come.
The pattern is clear: want (verb) + him (object) + to come (to-infinitive). This is the standard way to express what you want someone else to do.

Common Verbs

⚠️ Special Note: make, let, help
These three verbs are different! They take the bare infinitive (NO "to"):
• "My teacher made me study." (not "made me to study")
• "They let us go." (not "let us to go")
• "Can you help me carry this?" (or "help me to carry" — both work)
