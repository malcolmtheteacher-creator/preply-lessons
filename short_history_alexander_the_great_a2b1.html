The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The young king

Alexander was born in 356 BC in Macedonia, a country in the north of ancient Greece. His father was King Philip. A famous teacher, the philosopher Aristotle, taught the young Alexander about science, books and ideas.

There is a famous story about him as a boy. A wild horse came to the king's court. The horse was strong and angry, and no man could ride it. But young Alexander watched the horse carefully. He saw that the horse was afraid of its own shadow. So he turned the horse to face the sun — and then he rode it! The horse was called Bucephalus, and Alexander rode it for many years.

In 336 BC, Alexander's father died. Alexander became king when he was only twenty years old. He had a big, strong army, and he had a big dream: he wanted to conquer the Persian Empire, the most powerful empire in the world.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who taught Alexander when he was a boy?

2. How old was Alexander when he became king? What did he want to do?

1. The philosopher Aristotle, a famous teacher.

2. He was twenty years old. He wanted to conquer the Persian Empire.

Talk about it:
  • Alexander became king when he was very young. Do you think it is good or difficult to have big power when you are young?

Part 2 — The great conqueror

Alexander took his army into Asia. He fought the Persian king, Darius, in two big battles: at Issus in 333 BC, and at Gaugamela in 331 BC. Alexander's army was smaller, but he won both times! Both times, King Darius was afraid and ran away. The great Persian Empire was finished.

Alexander did not stop. He marched east, year after year. He built many new cities, and he gave many of them his own name — Alexandria. In 326 BC, he reached India and fought another big battle.

But now his soldiers were very tired. They were far from home, and they had fought for eight years. They did not want to fight any more, so they refused to go on. Alexander was angry, but he had to listen to them. He turned back. In all his life, Alexander never lost a battle — but in the end, his own tired soldiers stopped him.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who did Alexander fight at Issus and Gaugamela? What happened?

2. Alexander never lost a battle. So why did he stop and turn back?

1. He fought the Persian king, Darius. Alexander won both battles, and Darius ran away. The Persian Empire ended.

2. His own soldiers were very tired after eight years. They refused to fight any more, so he had to turn back.

Talk about it:
  • Alexander's soldiers were very tired and said "no more". Was it right for them to stop following their king?

Part 3 — The end and the legacy

Alexander started the long journey home with his army. But he never arrived. In 323 BC, in the old city of Babylon, he became very ill with a fever. After some days, he died. He was only thirty-two years old. People still do not know the real cause: maybe it was an illness, or maybe — some people say — poison.

Alexander had no grown son to be the next king. So after his death, his generals — his army commanders — fought each other for power. They broke the great empire into smaller kingdoms. The empire did not last very long.

But Alexander left something important behind. Everywhere his army went, it brought the Greek language, Greek art and Greek ideas. These mixed with the cultures of Egypt and Asia. This is called Hellenistic culture, and it lasted for hundreds of years. Today, more than two thousand years later, people still tell the story of Alexander the Great.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How and where did Alexander die? How old was he?

2. What happened to his empire after he died? What did he leave behind?

1. He died in the city of Babylon in 323 BC, from a fever. He was only thirty-two years old.

2. His generals fought each other and broke the empire into smaller kingdoms. But he left behind Greek culture (Hellenistic culture), which lasted for hundreds of years.

Talk about it:
  • Alexander died very young, at thirty-two, but we still remember him. Why do you think some people are remembered for so long?

Grammar: past simple & past continuous

To tell a story, we use two past tenses together.

Past simple = a finished action: "he became king," "Darius ran away."
Past continuous (was/were + -ing) = a longer action, happening at that time: "while his army was marching east…"
We often use them together with while and when: a long action (continuous) and a short action that happens inside it (simple).

1. Choose the right tense

1. While Alexander (march) east, his soldiers (get) more and more tired.

2. When the two armies (meet), Alexander (win) the battle.

3. The soldiers (fight) in India when they (decide) to stop.

1. was marching / were getting (both long actions happening at the same time)

2. met / won (two short, finished actions)

3. were fighting / decided (long action + short action inside it)

2. Tell a short part of the story

Put the verbs in the past simple or past continuous. Then read it out loud.

The two armies (wait) in the dust. Suddenly, Alexander (give) the order to attack. King Darius (see) the danger, and he (run) away from the battle.

were waiting · gave · saw · ran

The long "background" action is in the past continuous; the sudden, finished actions are in the past simple.
