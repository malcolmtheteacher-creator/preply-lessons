Question Forms

Click on each question analysis to reveal the explanation. Understanding how these questions work will help you have better conversations about films.

Asking for opinions and descriptions

"How was your weekend?"

"What was the ending like?"

Why are these questions similar?
They ask for a general opinion or description. You answer by giving your impression or what something was like, usually with a longer answer.
Specific vs. general questions

"What did you see?" (specific)

"What kind of films do you like?" (general)

What's the difference?
The first asks for a specific name or title. The second asks for a type or genre. One has a short answer, the other tells us about your preferences.
Negative questions

"Didn't you like it?"

"Don't you like Stallone?"

Why use negative questions?
Negative questions respond to something negative that was said. They show surprise or ask for clarification. For example, if someone says "I hated that film," you might ask "Didn't you like the acting?"

Model Dialogue

Listen to how these questions and answers work together in a real conversation.

Q1: General preference
"What kind of films do you usually watch?"
A1:
"Mostly serious movies, but I sometimes like watching comedies."
Q2: Recent film
"What movie did you watch recently?"
A2:
"I watched Mr Bean's Holiday."
Q3: Opinion (positive response)
"How was it?"
A3:
"I enjoyed it — it was really entertaining."
Q3a: Follow-up question
"What was the acting like?"
A3a:
"Really good. Bean is a fantastic comedian."
Q4: Opinion (negative response)
"How was it?"
A4:
"I hated the film — it was quite disappointing."
Q4a: Negative follow-up
"Didn't you like the acting?"
A4a:
"It was the acting that disappointed me most."
