The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. Sweyn's nickname, Forkbeard, may describe a beard split into two points.
a forked beard (n) — a beard divided into two parts.
2. In 1013 Sweyn set out to conquer the whole of England.
to conquer (v) — to take control of a country by force.
3. He chose to sail up the River Humber and the Trent into the north.
to sail up (v) — to travel by ship along a river into a country.
4. The northern lords were quick to acknowledge him as king.
to acknowledge (v) — to accept or recognise officially.
5. He demanded hostages from each region to guarantee loyalty.
hostages (n) — people held as a guarantee that a promise will be kept.
6. London was the last city to surrender.
to surrender (v) — to stop fighting and accept defeat.
7. His death in February 1014 was sudden.
sudden (adj) — happening quickly and unexpectedly.
8. His fleet chose his son Cnut as king.
a fleet (n) — a group of ships.
9. Sweyn founded a dynasty that would rule England, Denmark and Norway.
a dynasty (n) — a series of rulers from the same family.
10. A later legend said he was struck down by the ghost of St Edmund.
a legend (n) — a traditional story, possibly untrue.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The Danish king

Sweyn Forkbeard was king of Denmark and, for a time, ruler of much of Norway. His fleets had raided England for years and collected huge tribute.

In the summer of 1013 he came not to raid but to conquer. He sailed up the Humber to Gainsborough, where the north and the Danelaw accepted him at once. Hostages were handed over, and his army moved south.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who was Sweyn before 1013?

2. What happened at Gainsborough?

1. King of Denmark (and ruler of much of Norway) who had long raided England.

2. The north and the Danelaw accepted him as king and gave hostages.

Discuss:
  • Why would northern England accept Sweyn so quickly?

Part 2 — King for five weeks

One by one, Oxford, Winchester and the West Country submitted. London held out for a while but was finally forced to surrender. Æthelred fled to Normandy, and around Christmas 1013 Sweyn was accepted as king of England.

Then, on 3 February 1014, he died suddenly at Gainsborough. Later writers said he was killed by the ghost of St Edmund, the East Anglian king the Vikings had murdered long before — a legend, not history.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did Sweyn's conquest end?

2. When and where did he die?

1. London surrendered, Æthelred fled, and Sweyn was accepted as king around Christmas 1013.

2. 3 February 1014, at Gainsborough.

Discuss:
  • Why might a monk explain Sweyn's death with a ghost?

Part 3 — The dynasty

The Danish fleet chose Sweyn's son Cnut as king, but the English invited Æthelred back. Cnut withdrew — for now. Within three years he would return and rule England for nearly twenty years.

Sweyn's reign was only a few weeks long, but his family would rule England, Denmark and Norway together.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened after Sweyn's death?

2. What was Sweyn's legacy?

1. The fleet chose Cnut, but the English brought Æthelred back; Cnut withdrew.

2. A dynasty that would rule England, Denmark and Norway.

Discuss:
  • Is a five-week king really a king?

Grammar — The Passive Voice

Historians often care more about what was done than who did it — or they simply do not know who did it. That is the job of the passive.

How it works:
• Form: be + past participle: "The king was crowned at Westminster."
• Add by only when the doer matters: "The castle was built by Norman masons."
• Reporting passives hedge a legend: "He is said to have…", "It was believed that…"

1. Make it passive

Complete each sentence about Sweyn Forkbeard. Use the word in brackets.

1. Sweyn (accept) as king at Gainsborough.

2. Hostages (hand over) by each region.

3. London (force) to surrender.

1. was accepted

2. were handed over

3. was forced

2. Choose the right passive form

1. Sweyn (say / kill) by a ghost.

2. Cnut (choose) by the fleet.

3. England (rule) by Danes for over twenty years.

1. is said to have been killed

2. was chosen

3. was ruled

Going deeper

Sweyn's speed shows how worn down England was after decades of raids and tribute. Many people simply wanted the fighting to end. Yet power seized by force needs time to settle: his sudden death showed how fragile it was.

Worth noticing: Sweyn is not usually counted among the kings of England on some lists — which tells you how short and disputed his reign was.
Learning from the past — for today: Momentum is not permanence. A fast win — a new job, a takeover — still needs patient building afterwards. Plan for the "day after" as carefully as for the victory.
