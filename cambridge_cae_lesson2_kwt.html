What Makes Part 4 Tricky?

Key Word Transformations test whether you can express the same meaning using different grammar. You get 6 questions worth 2 marks each = 12 marks total.

6
Questions
2-5
Words to Write
12
Marks Available

The Format

Original sentence: I started working here three years ago.

BEEN

I here for three years.

Why Students Lose Marks

Common Mistakes

  • Writing too many words (6+)
  • Changing the key word form
  • Changing the meaning slightly
  • Spelling errors
  • Missing contractions count

What Gets Full Marks

  • Exactly 2-5 words
  • Key word unchanged
  • Meaning identical
  • Perfect spelling
  • Contractions = 2 words

The 3 Decisions

Every Key Word Transformation requires three decisions:

  1. Decision 1: Count your words (2-5 only)
  2. Decision 2: Keep the key word exactly as given
  3. Decision 3: Identify what grammar structure is being tested

Decision 1: Count Your Words

The Rule

Your answer MUST be between 2 and 5 words. No more, no less. If you write 6 words, you get ZERO marks - even if the meaning is perfect.

Counting Rules

Contractions

"don't" = 2 words

"I've" = 2 words

Hyphenated

"well-known" = 1 word

"up-to-date" = 1 word

Numbers

"25" = 1 word

"twenty-five" = 1 word

Phrasal Verbs

"give up" = 2 words

"look after" = 2 words

Practice: Count the Words

How many words in each answer?

1 "have been working"

3 words - have / been / working

2 "shouldn't have told"

4 words - should / not / have / told (contractions count as 2!)

3 "a well-known author"

3 words - a / well-known / author (hyphenated = 1 word)

4 "I'd rather not go"

5 words - I / would / rather / not / go

Common Trap

Students often write 6 words because they forget contractions count as 2. "I wouldn't have gone" = 5 words, not 3!

Decision 2: Keep the Key Word

The Rule

You CANNOT change the key word in any way. No different tense, no plural, no negative prefix - it must appear exactly as given.

What You CANNOT Do

WRONG - Changed the Key

Key word: GIVE

"...he gave up smoking..." X

"...he gives up smoking..." X

"...giving up smoking..." X

CORRECT - Key Unchanged

Key word: GIVE

"...decided to give up smoking..."

The word GIVE appears exactly as written.

Practice: Spot the Error

Which answers are WRONG because the key word was changed?

1 Key: MATTER

Answer: "It doesn't matter what she says"

CORRECT - "matter" is unchanged

2 Key: SUCCEED

Answer: "He succeeded in passing the exam"

WRONG - "succeeded" changes the key word (added -ed)

Would need restructuring: "did not succeed" or similar

3 Key: TAKE

Answer: "It took him two hours"

WRONG - "took" changes the key word (past tense)

4 Key: POSSIBILITY

Answer: "There is a possibility that"

CORRECT - "possibility" is unchanged

Strategy

Before writing your answer, look at the key word and ask: "In my answer, does this word appear EXACTLY like this?" If not, restructure.

Decision 3: Spot the Structure

The Key Insight

Part 4 tests specific grammar structures. The key word is a CLUE telling you which structure to use. Learn to recognize what each key word is asking for.

Practice: What Structure Is Being Tested?

1 "I regret not studying harder at school."

WISH

Wish + past perfect (for past regrets)

Answer: "I wish I had studied harder at school."

2 "Someone stole my bike yesterday."

HAD

Passive or Causative

Answer: "I had my bike stolen yesterday."

3 "It was raining but we still went for a walk."

SPITE

In spite of + -ing / noun (contrast)

Answer: "In spite of the rain, we went for a walk." OR "We went for a walk in spite of the rain."

4 "I would prefer you to come early."

RATHER

Would rather + subject + past simple

Answer: "I would rather you came early."

Note: "came" not "come" - this is a common trap!

Full Practice

Now apply all three decisions to complete transformations. Write your answer, then check.

Question 1

Original: They think the burglar got in through the window.

BELIEVED

The burglar _________________________ in through the window.

is believed to have got (5 words)

Structure: Passive reporting verb (It is believed that... / Subject + is believed to...)

Check: BELIEVED unchanged? Yes. 2-5 words? Yes (5). Same meaning? Yes.

Question 2

Original: I last saw him when I was a child.

SINCE

I _________________________ I was a child.

have not seen him since (5 words)

Structure: Present perfect + since (for duration from past point)

Also correct: "haven't seen him since" (5 words - haven't = 2)

Question 3

Original: Although he was tired, he finished the race.

BEING

Despite _________________________ finished the race.

being tired, he (3 words)

Structure: Despite + -ing (contrast/concession)

Question 4

Original: My parents did not allow me to stay out late.

LET

My parents _________________________ out late.

did not let me stay (5 words)

Structure: let + object + bare infinitive

Also correct: "didn't let me stay" (5 words)

Question 5

Original: "You should see a doctor," she said to me.

ADVISED

She _________________________ a doctor.

advised me to see (4 words)

Structure: Reported speech with advise + object + to infinitive

Question 6

Original: I am sure he did not do it on purpose.

MEANT

I am sure he _________________________ it.

did not mean to do (5 words)

Structure: mean + to infinitive (for intention)

Also correct: "didn't mean to do" (5 words)

Key Takeaways

The 3 Decisions Checklist

  • Decision 1: Is my answer 2-5 words? (Contractions = 2 words!)
  • Decision 2: Is the key word EXACTLY as given? (No tense change, no plural)
  • Decision 3: Have I used the correct grammar structure?

Structures to Review

High-Frequency Structures in CAE Part 4

  • Passive reporting verbs (is believed to, is said to)
  • Wish/If only + past perfect (regrets)
  • Would rather + past simple (preferences about others)
  • Causative have/get (have something done)
  • In spite of / Despite + noun/-ing
  • Reported speech transformations
  • Conditionals (mixed, inverted)

Common Traps to Avoid

Watch Out For

  • Writing 6 words (automatic zero)
  • Changing the key word tense
  • Forgetting "would rather" takes PAST tense for other people
  • Spelling mistakes (especially common words)
  • Missing articles (a/the) which affect meaning
