🦷 /v/ vs /w/ Confusion

A2 • German/Polish/Russian • Distinguish teeth + lip from rounded lips

🔍 Let's notice the V/W problem

Listen to these common mistakes:

❌ "I vant to vin"

Should be: "I want to win"

❌ "very" sounds like "wery"

Should be: "very" (clear /v/ with teeth)

Why do /v/ and /w/ sound so similar? How can you tell them apart?

🎯 The Problem:

German, Polish, and Russian speakers use /v/ more and /w/ less (or not at all). So they often say /v/ where English needs /w/.

🔹 /v/ = top teeth touch bottom lip (voiced continuous sound)

🔹 /w/ = lips rounded (voiced glide, no teeth contact)

The KEY is your MOUTH SHAPE: teeth-and-lip or rounded lips?

🛠️ The /v/ and /w/ sounds explained

🔴 /v/ (VOICELESS TEETH + LIP)

v
Mouth shape:
Top teeth touch bottom lip

Examples:
very, van, visit, give, have, love

🔵 /w/ (ROUNDED LIPS)

w
Mouth shape:
Lips rounded, no teeth contact

Examples:
want, win, water, away, away

💡 How to distinguish them:

The mouth-shape test:

🔹 /v/: Touch your top teeth to your bottom lip. Say "vvvv" — feel the contact?

🔹 /w/: Round your lips like you're whistling (O shape). Say "wwww" — no teeth touching!

✏️ Guided practice — Say these words

Say /v/ words (teeth on lip):

🔴 van → VAN (teeth touch lower lip)

🔴 very → VER-ee (clear /v/ at start)

🔴 visit → VIZ-it (teeth and lip contact)

🔴 love → LUV (teeth-lip contact at end)

Say /w/ words (rounded lips):

🔵 want → WANT (lips rounded, not teeth)

🔵 win → WIN (round your lips like whistling)

🔵 water → WAH-ter (rounded lips at start)

🔵 away → ə-WAY (no teeth contact)

🚫 Common mistakes to AVOID

❌ vant instead of want (using /v/ when you need /w/)

❌ vin instead of win (confusing the sounds)

❌ vine and wine sound the same (not distinguishing mouth shape)

❌ Rounding your lips for /v/ (should have teeth on lip instead)
