The cross-examination game

You both read the same scenario and evidence. The facts are ambiguous — it could be innocent or suspicious. The lawyer uses yes/no questions to try to prove guilt. The witness answers honestly — but defends the innocent interpretation.

The lawyer can ONLY ask yes or no questions. The witness can answer "yes", "no", "I don't remember", or "I can't answer that." The witness must stay consistent — no contradicting themselves.

The roles

⚖️
THE LAWYER
You believe it's suspicious. Ask yes/no questions that expose holes in the witness's story. You have 2 minutes.
🧑‍💼
THE WITNESS
You're innocent. Answer truthfully but defend the innocent explanation. Be consistent. Don't volunteer extra information.

Roles swap every case. After the cross-examination, both give your verdict.

Useful question starters for the lawyer

"Isn't it true that...?" "Can you confirm...?" "Wouldn't you agree...?" "Is it possible that...?" "So you're saying...?" "Did you or did you not...?" "Were you aware that...?" "And yet you claim...?"

Reflection

Which role was harder — lawyer or witness? Is it harder to ask the right question, or to answer without contradicting yourself?
What makes a question powerful? Which yes/no questions were hardest to answer? What made them effective?
Think about real life: When do you need to get information from someone using careful questions? Job interviews? Negotiations? Arguments with your partner?
The language of evasion: "I don't recall", "I can't answer that", "That depends on your definition of..." — where have you heard these phrases before?
