The interrogation game

Something happened. The lawyer knows the situation but not the details. The witness knows everything. The lawyer can ONLY ask yes/no questions. The witness must answer honestly — but can be as vague and unhelpful as legally possible.

The roles

⚖️
THE LAWYER
You see the situation. You DON'T see the secret details. You have 2 minutes to figure out what happened using ONLY yes/no questions. Be strategic.
🧑‍💼
THE WITNESS
You see the secret details. Answer honestly — but you can say "yes", "no", "I don't remember", or "I can't answer that." Be evasive. Make them work for it.

Question starters for the lawyer

"Were you alone?" "Did this happen at night?" "Was money involved?" "Did you plan this in advance?" "Were there other people present?" "Is this something you'd do again?" "Did anyone get hurt?" "Was it your idea?"

Reflection

Which role was harder — lawyer or witness? Is it harder to ask the right question, or to answer without giving too much away?
What questioning strategy worked best? Did you narrow down with big questions first, or go straight for specific details?
Think about real life: When do you need to extract information from someone who doesn't want to give it? Job interviews? Negotiations? Arguments?
