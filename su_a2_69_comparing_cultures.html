Comparing Cultures

Learn to compare and discuss cultural differences

3:00

Getting Started

Think about a country you know well. What makes it different from other places? In this lesson, you'll learn how to describe and compare cultures using English.

Quick Question

What is one thing about your culture that you think is very different from other countries?

Think for a moment: Is it about food, weather, traditions, sport, art, or something else?

What You'll Learn Today

  • Adjectives to describe culture and compare things
  • Comparative structures: "more", "than", "as...as"
  • Superlatives: "the most", "the greatest"
  • How to prepare a cultural briefing

Comparatives and Superlatives

Making Comparisons

Use these structures to compare two things:

Comparative form:
Subject + verb + comparative adjective + than + object

The weather is more extreme than in Poland.
Italian culture is warmer and friendlier than Swedish culture.
France is not as cold as Norway.

Using Superlatives

Use these to compare three or more things:

Superlative form:
Subject + verb + the + superlative adjective

Spanish culture is the most famous in Europe.
This is the greatest festival.
It"s the most important tradition.

Intensity Modifiers

Add these words before comparatives to show how much:

a little + comparative = small difference
Poland is a little colder than Slovakia.

a lot / much + comparative = big difference
The festival is a lot more famous than I thought.

less + adjective = opposite comparison
Iceland is less extreme than Australia.

Practice Gap-Fill Exercise

Complete the sentences with the correct comparative or superlative form:

1. The weather is _____ extreme _____ in Slovakia than in Mexico.

Answer: less / not as ... as (or "not as extreme as")

2. French culture is _____ famous _____ Italian culture.

Answer: as ... as or more famous than

3. The UK is _____ traditional _____ Japan.

Answer: less ... than or not as traditional as

4. This festival is _____ popular _____ any other in the country.

Answer: the most

5. German food is a lot _____ different _____ Spanish food.

Answer: more ... than

6. The music here is _____ great _____ I"ve ever heard.

Answer: the greatest

7. Our traditions are a little _____ different _____ yours.

Answer: more ... than or different from

8. The people here are _____ friendly _____ anywhere else I"ve been.

Answer: the most

9. This event is not _____ important _____ the national holiday.

Answer: as ... as

Speaking Practice

Use these phrases to make comparisons. Practice saying them aloud:

Sport

Which sport is most popular in your country? How does it compare to sports in the UK?

  • less extreme than
  • a little faster than
  • more famous

Music

What music style is traditional in your culture? How is it different?

  • different from
  • not as modern as
  • more traditional than

Films & Entertainment

What films or shows are popular? How do they compare?

  • as popular as
  • the most famous
  • different in style

Weather

Describe the weather where you live. Compare it to the UK.

  • a lot warmer than
  • not as cold as
  • more extreme

Literature & Art

What books or art are important in your culture?

  • more important than
  • the greatest writers
  • less famous worldwide

Food & Traditions

Tell about a special food or tradition. How is it unique?

  • more traditional than
  • a little different
  • the most important

Main Task: Cultural Briefing

Imagine a business colleague from the UK is visiting your country for the first time. Prepare a 2-minute cultural briefing. Include:

  • One comparison about the weather
  • One comparison about a cultural tradition (music, food, sport, or art)
  • One comparison about how young people behave or think
  • One unique thing about your country they should know

Practice this out loud, using the comparison structures you learned!

Conversation & Reflection

These questions invite you to think deeply about culture and different perspectives:

Why is it important to understand other cultures?

Think about: business, friendship, travel, news, or personal growth.

What do you like about your own culture?

Think about: traditions, food, values, art, or the way people behave.

Is there anything about your culture you would like to change?

What would be different, and why?

How do young people in your culture behave differently from older generations?

Think about: technology, values, traditions, fashion, or interests.

What cultures from around the world do you admire? Why?

What aspects appeal to you most?

How is globalization changing your culture?

Are traditions becoming less or more important? Is this good or bad?

Summary

You can now compare cultures using English! You"ve learned how to:

  • Use comparative adjectives with "than"
  • Use "as...as" and "less...than" structures
  • Form and use superlatives
  • Talk confidently about cultural differences

Keep practicing these structures when you talk about your culture or compare different countries!
