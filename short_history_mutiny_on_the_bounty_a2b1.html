The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The ship and the island

In 1787, a small British ship left England. Its name was the Bounty. The ship had a special job. It went to the island of Tahiti, far away in the Pacific Ocean, to collect young fruit plants. Then it had to take the plants to islands in the Caribbean.

The captain was William Bligh. He was a very good sailor, but he was hard on his crew. He often shouted at his men and gave them tough punishments. His second-in-command was a young officer called Fletcher Christian. At first, Bligh and Christian were friends.

The ship stayed at Tahiti for almost five months. The weather was warm and life was easy. The men liked the island very much, and some of them fell in love there. When Bligh said it was time to leave, the men were sad. They did not want to go back to hard work on the ship.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was the ship's job?

2. Why did the men not want to leave Tahiti?

1. It went to Tahiti to collect young fruit plants and take them to the Caribbean.

2. They stayed there for almost five months, life was easy and warm, and some of them fell in love. They did not want hard work again.

Talk about it:
  • Bligh and Christian started as friends. Why do you think friends sometimes become enemies?

Part 2 — The morning of the mutiny

Three weeks after Tahiti, there was big trouble on the ship. Captain Bligh was angry a lot. He said bad things about his officers in front of the other men. One day he told Christian that Christian was a thief, because some coconuts were missing. This was not fair, and Christian was very unhappy.

Early one morning in April 1789, Christian and some men took the ship's guns. They went into the captain's room and woke him up. They tied his hands and took him up to the deck. "The ship is ours now," they told him. This was a mutiny — and mutiny was a very serious crime.

The men did not kill Bligh. Instead, they put him in a small open boat. Eighteen other men who liked Bligh went with him. The crew gave them a little food, some water and a compass. Then they cut the rope. The Bounty sailed away, and Bligh's small boat was alone on the huge ocean, very far from any help.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why was Christian unhappy with the captain?

2. What did the men do with Bligh?

1. Bligh said bad things about him and called him a thief over some missing coconuts. It was not fair.

2. They did not kill him. They put him in a small open boat with eighteen loyal men, gave them a little food and water, and set them adrift on the ocean.

Talk about it:
  • The men did not kill Bligh — they put him in a boat. Do you think this was a kind thing to do, or not?

Part 3 — Two long journeys

Now there were two stories. Captain Bligh was a brilliant sailor. In the tiny boat, with almost no maps, he kept his men alive on the sea for forty-seven days. He sailed nearly 6,700 kilometres and reached land safely. Only one man died. It was one of the most amazing small-boat journeys in history. Later, Bligh went home to England.

The mutineers knew the navy would look for them. They went back to Tahiti. Some men stayed there. Later, a navy ship found them and took them to England. Three of them were hanged for the mutiny.

But Fletcher Christian and a small group did not stay. They wanted a place where no one could find them. They found a tiny, remote island called Pitcairn. They landed there and burned the Bounty so no ship would see it. Life on the island was hard and often violent, and Christian died a few years later. But some people lived. Eighteen years later, a ship found their children still on the island. Their families live on Pitcairn even today.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What amazing thing did Bligh do in the small boat?

2. Where did Christian and his group go, and what did they do to the ship?

1. He sailed the tiny boat nearly 6,700 km in forty-seven days and kept almost all his men alive — one of the greatest small-boat journeys ever.

2. They went to the remote island of Pitcairn and burned the Bounty so no ship would find them. Their families still live there today.

Talk about it:
  • Bligh was hard on his men, but he also saved them in the small boat. Can a person be a bad boss and a hero at the same time?

Grammar: past simple + say / tell

This is a true story from the past, so we use the past simple. We also often need say and tell when we talk about what people said.

Past simple — for finished actions in the past.
• Regular verbs add -ed: sail → sailed, want → wanted, land → landed.
• Many common verbs are irregular: go → went, take → took, give → gave, find → found, be → was / were.
say and tell — both are about speaking, but they work differently.
• tell needs a person after it: he told the men, he told Christian.
• say does not take a person like that: he said the ship was theirs. (NOT "he said the men").
Their past forms: say → said, tell → told.

1. Past simple — write the verb

Put the verb in brackets into the past simple.

1. In 1787, the ship (leave) England.

2. The crew (want) to stay on the island.

3. The men (take) the ship from the captain.

1. left (leave is irregular)

2. wanted (regular: want + -ed)

3. took (take is irregular)

2. say or tell?

Choose the correct verb. Remember: tell has a person after it, say does not.

1. Bligh the men to prepare the ship. (there is a person: the men)

2. Christian that the ship was his now. (no person after it)

3. The captain Christian that he was a thief. (there is a person: Christian)

1. told (tell + person)

2. said (say + no person)

3. told (tell + person)

3. Tell a short part of the story

Fill each gap with the past simple, or with said or told. Read it aloud when you finish.

One morning, Christian and his men (take) the ship. They Bligh that the ship was theirs now. They (put) him in a small boat. Bligh nothing, and the ship sailed away.

took · told · put · said

Use told when there is a person (told Bligh), and said when there is not (said nothing).
