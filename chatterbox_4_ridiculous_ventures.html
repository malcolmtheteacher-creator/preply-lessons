Ridiculous Ventures Inc.

Pitch the world's most unnecessary product — and take my money

← CHATTERBOX

Pick a thing. Sell me a dream.

Every great fortune starts with an ordinary object and a wildly overconfident person. That person is you.

Grab one of these — or anything else within reach:
a single sock · an ice cube · a rubber duck · a paperclip · an umbrella for one finger
Sixty seconds. Go.

Pick your object. Invent a completely pointless product built around it — something the world has survived perfectly well without until this exact moment — and start selling it to me right now. I’m your first customer, and I’m listening.

01:00

Give it a name investors will remember

A sixty-second ramble is not a business. Now you build the whole pitch, properly, out loud.

Cover all five before the timer ends:
  • A name for the product
  • The problem it “solves” — make it up, and the bigger the lie, the better
  • Exactly who desperately needs this
  • A tagline
  • The price — and it should be outrageous
Three minutes. Build the whole pitch, out loud, from nothing.

Don’t plan it silently first — think out loud as you go, the way a real founder would in front of a camera. Name it, justify it, price it.

03:00
Drop these in wherever you can: “Imagine a world where…” · “For a limited time…” · “9 out of 10 people…”

Now defend it

The investors are unimpressed and they want answers. Face these questions one after another, without stopping to think in silence.

Here’s what you’re about to be asked:
  • Who exactly is desperate enough to pay that price?
  • What happens the very first time this breaks?
  • Why hasn’t someone already built this?
  • What do you say to the reviewer who calls this ridiculous?
  • Convince me, right now, that this isn’t a joke.
Two minutes. Face the questions and defend your valuation.

Answer as they come, live. Lie gloriously if you have to — confidence matters more than the truth in this room.

02:00

The market hates it. Reinvent it, live.

Bad news: nobody’s buying it. Reviews are brutal. The market has spoken, and what it said was no.

You have two minutes to save the company. Reinvent the product live, right now, into something even sillier than what you started with.

Two minutes. Pivot, out loud, as you go.

Keep the object if it still serves you, or abandon it entirely. New angle, new audience, new pitch — think on your feet, don’t plan it silently first.

02:00

Ninety seconds. Close the deal.

This is it — your last, best shot. Straight to camera. No hesitation, no filler. Make me want to write the cheque.
Ninety seconds. Your final pitch.

Sell it one more time — the best version of everything you’ve built today, whichever version of the product you ended up with. End on a line I’ll remember.

01:30
Take my money — or don’t. Either way, that’s the deal closed.
