Language Focus A

Click each expression to explore its meaning, form, and use. Then practise immediately!

to be on the cusp of (something)

+
MEANING

To be at the point where something significant is about to happen or change; on the verge of a major transition.

FORM

Subject + be + on the cusp of + noun/gerund

Often used with: revolution, change, breakthrough, transformation, disaster

REGISTER

Formal/professional. Perfect for legal writing, business presentations, and serious discussions. Adds gravitas to your statement.

"The legal profession is on the cusp of its biggest transformation since the invention of email."
"We're on the cusp of closing this deal - don't let anything derail it now."
NOW YOU TRY

Think about your legal career or your country's legal system. What changes do you think the profession is on the cusp of? Tell your teacher.

to automate (someone/something) out of existence

+
MEANING

To use technology to replace a job, role, or process so completely that it no longer exists. Often carries a negative or fearful connotation.

FORM

Subject + automate + object + out of existence

Passive common: "X could be automated out of existence"

REGISTER

Informal-to-neutral. Used in discussions about technology and employment. Slightly dramatic but increasingly common in professional contexts.

"Some fear that AI will automate junior lawyers out of existence within a decade."
"Paralegals worry their roles could be automated out of existence by document review software."
NOW YOU TRY

Which specific legal tasks do you think could realistically be automated out of existence? Which ones definitely cannot? Why?

to exercise professional judgement

+
MEANING

To apply expertise, experience, and ethical reasoning to make decisions in complex situations. The core skill that distinguishes professionals from machines.

FORM

Subject + exercise + professional judgement + (in/when/about)

Collocates with: sound, independent, careful, reasonable

REGISTER

Highly formal. Essential vocabulary for legal ethics, professional standards, and regulatory contexts. Shows sophistication.

"Lawyers must continue to exercise professional judgement, even when using AI tools."
"The court found that she had failed to exercise proper professional judgement when reviewing the AI-generated brief."
NOW YOU TRY

Describe a situation (real or hypothetical) where a lawyer would need to exercise professional judgement that AI simply couldn't replicate. What makes it uniquely human?

Language Focus B

Two more powerful expressions for discussing AI and professional life.

to be a double-edged sword

+
MEANING

Something that has both advantages and disadvantages; a situation or thing that can help you but also harm you.

FORM

Subject + be + a double-edged sword

Often followed by explanation of both edges: "On one hand... on the other..."

REGISTER

Universal - works in both formal and informal contexts. A mature, nuanced expression that shows balanced thinking. Excellent for interviews and debates.

"AI in legal practice is a double-edged sword: it increases efficiency but creates new liability risks."
"Working from home has proven to be a double-edged sword for most law firms."
NOW YOU TRY

Think of something in your professional or personal life that is a double-edged sword. Explain both edges to your teacher.

to remain accountable for (something)

+
MEANING

To continue to be responsible for something; to maintain legal or professional responsibility even when circumstances change. A key concept in legal ethics.

FORM

Subject + remain accountable for + noun/gerund

Alternatives: "be held accountable for", "take accountability for"

REGISTER

Formal/legal. Critical vocabulary for professional ethics, corporate governance, and legal liability discussions.

"Even when using AI, lawyers remain accountable for the accuracy of all filings submitted to the court."
"The partners agreed that senior management must remain accountable for the firm's AI governance policies."
NOW YOU TRY

If an AI tool makes a mistake that harms a client, who should remain accountable? The lawyer? The firm? The AI company? Discuss with your teacher.

Key Takeaways

The Reality: AI isn't replacing lawyers - it's changing what lawyers do. The legal jobs growing fastest combine human judgement with technological proficiency.

The Warning: 660 lawyers have already been caught submitting fake AI citations. The tool doesn't replace your responsibility - it amplifies your accountability.

The Opportunity: For lawyers today, AI literacy isn't optional - it's a competitive advantage. The question isn't whether to use AI, but how wisely.
