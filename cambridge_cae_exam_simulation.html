Welcome to Your CAE Exam Simulation

This simulation will prepare you for the real exam experience. The Cambridge C1 Advanced (CAE) exam tests your English at an advanced level and is recognized worldwide by employers and universities.

Golden Rule: Read Instructions Carefully

Every year, candidates lose marks by not following instructions properly. Pay attention to word limits, number of answers required, and transfer times. The instructions are there to help you!

Paper 1: Reading & Use of English

This paper tests your vocabulary, grammar, and reading comprehension through 8 different parts. Time management is crucial!

Paper 1 Timer: 1 Hour 30 Minutes

Part 4 Key word transformation 6 12 min

Part-by-Part Strategy Reminders

Parts 1-4: Use of English (Grammar & Vocabulary Focus) ▼

Part 1: Multiple-choice cloze 10 min

Strategy: Read the whole text first. Look for collocations, fixed phrases, and phrasal verbs. The four options often look similar - context is key!

Part 2: Open cloze 10 min

Strategy: Usually tests grammar words (articles, prepositions, pronouns, auxiliary verbs). Read around the gap - what comes before AND after matters.

Part 3: Word formation 10 min

Strategy: Identify what part of speech is needed (noun, verb, adjective, adverb). Check for negatives (un-, im-, -less) and plurals.

Part 4: Key word transformation 12 min

Strategy: Use 3-6 words including the key word. Don't change the key word. Cover the original sentence and check your answer conveys the same meaning.

Parts 5-8: Reading (Comprehension Focus) ▼

Part 5: Multiple choice 15 min

Strategy: Read questions first, then scan for relevant sections. The answer is always IN the text - don't use your own knowledge. Watch out for distractors!

Part 6: Cross-text multiple matching 12 min

Strategy: Read all four texts quickly first to understand each writer's opinion. Look for synonyms and paraphrasing when matching.

Part 7: Gapped text 13 min

Strategy: Read the whole text first. Look for reference words (this, these, such, it) and linking devices. Check the logic flows before AND after each gap.

Part 8: Multiple matching 8 min

Strategy: Scan quickly - don't read in detail. Underline key words in questions. One section may match more than one question.

Self-Check After Paper 1

Did I finish all 8 parts within 90 minutes?
Did I answer EVERY question (no blank answers)?
Which parts took me longer than expected?
Did I check my spelling in Parts 2, 3, and 4?

Reflection

Remember: No Blank Answers!

There's no negative marking in CAE. If you're running out of time, make educated guesses rather than leaving blanks. Even a guess has a chance of being correct!

Paper 2: Writing

You'll write two texts of 220-260 words each. Part 1 is compulsory (essay), and Part 2 offers a choice of task types.

Paper 2 Timer: 1 Hour 30 Minutes

01:30:00

Part 1: Compulsory Essay

45 minutes

Task Format

  • You'll be given a topic and two points to discuss
  • You must add your own third point
  • Give your opinion and support it
  • Target: 220-260 words

Essay Structure (5 paragraphs)

  1. Introduction: Introduce topic + thesis
  2. Point 1: First given point + development
  3. Point 2: Second given point + development
  4. Point 3: Your own point + development
  5. Conclusion: Summary + final opinion

Part 2: Choice of Task

45 minutes

Task Options Include:

  • Email/Letter - formal or informal
  • Report - formal, with headings
  • Review - of book, film, restaurant, etc.
  • Proposal - with recommendations

Choosing Your Task

Read ALL options before deciding. Choose based on:

  • Which format you know best
  • Which topic inspires more ideas
  • Which register (formal/informal) you're confident with

Time Management for Writing

Stage Part 1 Part 2
Planning 5 minutes 5 minutes
Writing 35 minutes 35 minutes
Checking 5 minutes 5 minutes

Pre-Submission Checklist

Word count: Is each answer 220-260 words?
Task completion: Have I addressed ALL parts of the question?
Register: Is my tone appropriate (formal/informal)?
Organization: Are my paragraphs clear and logical?
Linking: Have I used connectors and transitions?
Vocabulary: Have I used a range of C1-level vocabulary?
Grammar: Have I checked for errors (tenses, articles, prepositions)?
Spelling: Have I double-checked difficult words?

Word Count Warning

Under 220 words: You'll lose marks for incomplete content.
Over 260 words: Examiners may stop reading, and you risk more errors.
Practice counting: Know how many words you typically write per line in your handwriting.

Paper 3: Listening

You'll hear a variety of recordings and answer questions. You hear each recording TWICE, giving you time to check your answers.

Paper 3 Timer: 40 Minutes

00:40:00

Part-by-Part Breakdown

Part 1: Multiple Choice (Short Extracts) 8 questions

What you hear: 3 short conversations or monologues (about 1 minute each)

Strategy: Read questions during the pause BEFORE listening. Focus on the exact wording - distractors often use words from the recording but with different meanings.

Part 2: Sentence Completion 8 questions

What you hear: A monologue (about 3-4 minutes)

Strategy: Write the exact words you hear (1-3 words). Check grammar - your answer must fit grammatically in the sentence. Spelling must be correct!

Part 3: Multiple Choice (Long Text) 6 questions

What you hear: An interview or discussion (about 4 minutes)

Strategy: Questions follow the order of the recording. Listen for opinion words and attitude (surprised, skeptical, enthusiastic). Don't choose an answer just because you hear those words.

Part 4: Multiple Matching 8 questions

What you hear: 5 short monologues (about 30 seconds each)

Strategy: You match speakers to statements. Listen for the overall message, not individual words. There are extra options you won't use.

Key Strategies

Use Reading Time Wisely

You get time before each part to read the questions. Use this time to:

  • Underline key words in questions
  • Predict what you might hear
  • Note the question type (opinion, fact, attitude)

You Hear Everything TWICE

First listening: Get the general idea and mark possible answers.
Second listening: Confirm your answers and fill in any gaps.
Don't panic if you miss something the first time!

Transfer Answers Carefully

At the end, you get 5 minutes to transfer answers to the answer sheet.

  • Double-check question numbers
  • Make sure spelling is correct for Part 2
  • Don't leave any blanks - guess if necessary

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Don't choose an answer just because you hear those exact words (it's often a distractor)
  • Don't write more than the maximum words allowed in Part 2
  • Don't forget to check your spelling
  • Don't change too many answers on the second listening (your first instinct is often right)
  • Do listen for synonyms and paraphrasing
  • Do pay attention to tone and attitude

Paper 4: Speaking

You'll speak with an examiner and another candidate. The test has 4 parts and lasts about 15 minutes.

Speaking Test Structure

Part 1: Interview (2 minutes)

The examiner asks you questions about yourself, your interests, and opinions on everyday topics.

02:00

Practice Questions:

  • Where are you from? What do you like about living there?
  • What do you enjoy doing in your free time?
  • Tell me about your studies or work.
  • How important is technology in your daily life?
  • What kind of music do you enjoy? Why?

Strategy for Part 1

Give extended answers (not just yes/no). Add reasons, examples, and personal opinions. Speak naturally - this is a warm-up!

Part 2: Long Turn (4 minutes total - 1 minute each + 30 sec response)

You speak about two photographs for 1 minute, then briefly respond to your partner's photos.

01:00

Sample Task:

"Here are your photographs. They show people learning new skills. I'd like you to compare the photographs and say why these people might have chosen to learn these skills."

Photo A: Person taking a cooking class
Photo B: Person learning to code

Strategy for Part 2

  • Compare (don't just describe) - use: "While...", "On the other hand...", "Both photos show..."
  • Speculate - use: "They might...", "It looks as if...", "Perhaps..."
  • Answer the question - don't just describe what you see

Part 3: Collaborative Task (4 minutes)

Discuss a topic with your partner, then make a decision together.

04:00

Sample Task:

"Here are some things that can help people stay healthy. First, talk to each other about how effective these things might be. Then decide which two would be most important for someone starting a healthier lifestyle."

Regular exercise Healthy diet Enough sleep Stress management Regular check-ups

Strategy for Part 3

  • Interact - ask your partner questions, respond to their ideas
  • Use phrases like: "What do you think about...?", "I see your point, but...", "Shall we move on to...?"
  • Don't dominate - share speaking time equally
  • Reach a decision together at the end

Part 4: Discussion (5 minutes)

A deeper discussion on the topic from Part 3. The examiner asks questions to both candidates.

05:00

Sample Questions:

  • Do you think people today are more or less health-conscious than previous generations?
  • What role should governments play in promoting healthy lifestyles?
  • Is it ever possible to be "too healthy"?
  • How has technology changed the way people think about health?

Strategy for Part 4

  • Give developed opinions with reasons and examples
  • Agree or disagree with your partner naturally
  • Show range - use advanced vocabulary and structures
  • Don't memorize answers - examiners can tell!

Self-Record Reminder

Practice Makes Perfect

Record yourself practicing the speaking tasks. Listen back and check:

  • Are you speaking for the full time?
  • Are you using a range of vocabulary?
  • Is your pronunciation clear?
  • Are you hesitating too much?

You can also practice with a partner via video call to simulate the real test!
