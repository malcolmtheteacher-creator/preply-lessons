Polite Canadians: Sorry, Not Sorry?

Canadians say “sorry” all the time. Is it a habit, a law or a joke?

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. I am sorry that I am late.
sorry (adj) — a word you say when you feel bad about something. Example: “I am sorry that I am late.”
2. He made an apology to the customer.
apology (n) — words that say you are sorry. Example: “He made an apology to the customer.”
3. She is always polite.
polite (adj) — kind, with good manners. Example: “She is always polite.”
4. There is a law about apologies.
law (n) — a rule from the government. Example: “There is a law about apologies.”
5. The shop was crowded.
crowded (adj) — with a lot of people. Example: “The shop was crowded.”
6. Canadians love hockey.
hockey (n) — a fast sport on ice. Example: “Canadians love hockey.”

  • Which word is new for you? Say a sentence with it.
The stereotype: “Canadians are very polite and say sorry for everything.”

The story

Part 1 — The joke

Jokes about Canadians saying “sorry” are everywhere. Canadians tell them too. In Canadian English, “sorry” often means “excuse me” or “that's too bad”. It does not always mean “it was my fault”. It helps keep things smooth in a crowded shop or on a bus.

Quick check:

1. What can “sorry” mean in Canada?

2. Where does it help?

1. “Excuse me” or “that's too bad”.

2. In a crowded shop or on a bus.

Discuss:
  • When do you say “sorry”?

Part 2 — The law

In 2009, the province of Ontario made an Apology Act. Other provinces have similar laws. After an accident, people can say “sorry” and the court does not always use it as a sign of fault. People can be human and not be afraid of being sued.

Quick check:

1. Which province made an Apology Act in 2009?

2. Why is the law useful?

1. Ontario.

2. People can say sorry without being afraid.

Discuss:
  • Is it a good law?

Part 3 — Not always polite

Canadians are not polite in every situation. They are very passionate fans of ice hockey, a fast and physical sport. Canadian politics can be fierce. Canadian politeness is strongest in public. Canadians often say they are more modest than Americans.

Quick check:

1. Which sport do Canadians love?

2. Where is politeness strongest?

1. Ice hockey.

2. In public.

Discuss:
  • Are you more polite in public or at home?

Grammar — more … than and the most

more + adjective + than: “Canadians are more modest than Americans.”
the most + adjective: “Hockey is the most popular sport.”
Short adjectives: politer / the politest is also OK.
