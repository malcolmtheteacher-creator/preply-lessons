Giving Examples: Language & Practice

These phrases help you introduce and support your arguments with specific examples. Useful in debates, discussions, and presenting ideas.

Useful Phrases for Giving Examples

  • "Let"s take X as an example." Use this to introduce a specific example formally.
  • "Then of course, there"s..." Add another related example conversationally.
  • "X is a classic example of..." Show that X demonstrates a point perfectly.
  • "Take X" A shorter, more direct way to introduce an example.
  • "One example that immediately springs to mind is..." Show that something comes naturally to your argument.
  • "The film illustrates all the problems..." Use when a single example covers multiple points.
  • "Let"s take the case of..." Formal way to introduce a specific situation or example.
  • "Let me give you another example" Signal that you"re adding more support to your point.

Practice: Topics to Discuss Using Example Phrases

Choose one topic. Use at least two "giving examples" phrases to support your point.

Topic: Good restaurants in your city

"Let"s take [Restaurant Name] as an example — they have amazing seafood. Then of course, there"s [Another Restaurant], which is famous for its fusion cuisine. One example that immediately springs to mind is the street food market near the town centre."

Topic: Popular tourist places you"d recommend

"Take [Place Name] as an example — it"s historically significant and breathtaking. Then of course, there"s [Another Place], which offers stunning views. A classic example of a must-see destination is [Third Place]."

Topic: Tricky aspects of English spelling

"Let me give you another example: the word "rhythm" has no traditional vowels. One example that immediately springs to mind is "necessary" — people often misspell it. Then of course, there"s "accommodate" with its double letters."

Topic: Confusing English grammar rules

"Let"s take the difference between "used to" and "would" as an example. One example that springs to mind is the present perfect vs. past simple. Then of course, there"s the use of articles — "a" vs. "the" — which causes confusion."

Topic: Foods your country is famous for

"Let"s take [Dish] as an example — it"s beloved worldwide and dates back centuries. Then of course, there"s [Another Dish], which represents our culinary heritage. A classic example of comfort food from my country is [Third Dish]."

Topic: Children"s classic films your generation grew up with

"Take [Film Title] as an example — it shaped my childhood imagination. One example that immediately springs to mind is [Another Film]. Then of course, there"s [Third Film], which every child seemed to watch."

Topic: Books kids hate but have to read at school

"Let"s take [Book Title] as an example — the prose feels dated to modern readers. Then of course, there"s [Another Book], which many students find slow-paced. One example that springs to mind is [Third Book], often cited as boring by teenagers."

Let"s Discuss

Question 1
