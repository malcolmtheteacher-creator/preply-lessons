Gradable vs Non-Gradable Adjectives

Learn the rule for using "very" and "absolutely".

The Rule

Show Explanation
Gradable adjectives (funny, scared, embarrassed) can be intensified with "very".

Non-gradable adjectives (hilarious, mortified, stunned) are already extreme and use "absolutely" instead.

✗ Don\'t say: "absolutely funny" or "very hilarious"
✓ Do say: "very funny" and "absolutely hilarious"

Practice: Choose the Correct Intensifier

Show Answers
1. The film I watched last night was absolutely hilarious. (not very)
2. I am always very scared when I stay home alone at night. (not absolutely)
3. She must have been absolutely mortified when she realized she forgot his name. (not very)
4. They were absolutely stunned when they discovered their house was burgled. (not very)

Common Embarrassing Situations

These are situations people often talk about in anecdotes.

  • Dribbling on your shoulder while you sleep on a busy train
  • Standing in front of a group of people with your fly undone
  • Forgetting what to say during a presentation
  • Walking into a glass door
  • Bending down and splitting your trousers
  • Mistaking a stranger for someone you know
  • Talking to someone with a piece of lettuce stuck to your front teeth

Reflection:

Which of these situations sounds most embarrassing to you? Have you experienced anything similar?

Tell Your Personal Anecdote

Prepare and tell a funny, embarrassing, scary, or surprising story from your own life.

Preparation: Make Notes

Think of a story and make notes about:

  • Where you were
  • Why you were there
  • What happened
  • How you felt about it at the time
  • How it ended
Speaking time: 5:00

Telling Your Story

When you\'ve finished telling your story:

  • Use adjectives with "very" or "absolutely" to describe your feelings
  • Use time expressions to organize your story (First, Then, Finally)
  • Add details to make it more interesting
  • After you speak, ask your partner to recap the story and check details

Reflection after telling:

Do you think you are a good storyteller? What makes an anecdote effective?
