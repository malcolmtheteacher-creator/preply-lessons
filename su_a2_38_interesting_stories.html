Interesting Stories

A2 Level English • Storytelling

Objective

Tell interesting stories using narrative tenses (past simple + past continuous). Learn phrases for active listening to show interest during a conversation.

Lead-in

Read these statements. Which ones are true? Which are false? Why?

1. "Stories are for children, not adults."

What do you think? Do adults tell and listen to stories?

2. "We should use a variety of past tenses to make stories sound more interesting."

True — using past continuous and past simple together creates a more vivid narrative.

3. "It's OK to interrupt someone when they are telling a story."

Is it polite to interrupt? How can we show interest without interrupting?

4. "Reading a story is always better than listening to one."

Your Thoughts

Narrative Tenses

I was walking down Bell Street alone when I heard growling behind me.

How It Works

Past Continuous ("was walking")

This shows the longer action or the background — what was happening.

Example: I was walking = I was in the middle of this action.

Past Simple ("heard")

This shows the shorter action or the event that interrupted — what happened suddenly.

Example: I heard = This happened. It was sudden and unexpected.

The Pattern:

• "I was [verb + ing] when I [past simple]"

• "While I was [verb + ing], I [past simple]"

Practice: Fill in the Gaps

1. I was _____ (read) a book when I _____ (hear) a strange noise.

reading / heard

2. While we were _____ (walk) home, it _____ (start) to rain.

walking / started

3. She was _____ (cook) dinner when the phone _____ (ring).

cooking / rang

4. They were _____ (play) football when the ball _____ (break) a window.

playing / broke

5. I _____ (see) an old friend while I was _____ (shop) in town.

saw / shopping

Active Listening

When someone tells you a story, use these phrases to show you're listening and interested.

Click on each phrase to see what it means and when to use it.

That's good!
Shows approval and positive reaction to what they said.
How long ago?
Asking for more detail about the time. Shows you're interested in the details.
Wow! That's amazing!
Expressing surprise and admiration. Shows you find the story interesting.
No way!
Showing disbelief or shock. Expresses strong emotion about what you heard.
Go on...
Encouraging the speaker to continue their story. Shows you want to hear more.
Oh no! What happened next?
Showing concern or sympathy while asking for the next part of the story.
Really? That sounds interesting.
Expressing interest and encouraging the speaker to tell you more.
And then what?
Asking for the next part of the story in a natural, conversational way.

Remember:

The best listeners don't stay silent. They show interest by responding naturally. This makes the speaker feel heard and valued.

Tell Your Story

Your Turn to Speak

Take your time. Don't worry about being perfect.

Key Phrases to Use

• "I was walking/sitting/driving... when..."

• "While I was..., suddenly..."

Your listener may respond with: "Really?", "No way!", "What happened next?"
