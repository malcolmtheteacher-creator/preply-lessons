Section A: Christianity Beliefs (Mock)

Question 1: Give the name of the belief that God is all-powerful. (1 mark)

Model answer: Omnipotence. (Other acceptable: all-powerful, God's power.)

Question 2: Give a theological term for the belief that God became human in Jesus. (1 mark)

Model answer: Incarnation.

Question 3: Explain what Christians mean by salvation. (4 marks)

Model answer: Salvation means being saved from sin and its consequences. Christians believe Jesus' death and resurrection made this possible through His sacrifice. Believers accept this gift by faith, becoming reconciled to God. Different traditions emphasize grace (God's free gift), works (living faithfully), or Spirit (being born again). The result is eternal life and restoration of relationship with God.

Question 4: Evaluate the claim that the Crucifixion is more important than the Resurrection for Christians. (6 marks)

Model answer: Argument 1 (Support): The Crucifixion is the moment of sacrifice. Jesus paid the price for sin through His death. Without it, atonement is impossible. Many Christian hymns emphasize the cross. Argument 2 (Support): But the Resurrection validates the Crucifixion. It proves Jesus' divinity and victory over death. Without Resurrection, the Cross is just execution, not redemption. Argument 3 (Balance): Both are inseparable. The Cross without Resurrection is incomplete; Resurrection without Cross is meaningless. Conclusion: The Resurrection is arguably more important because it gives the Crucifixion its power and meaning. But Christians would say they're equally essential to salvation.

Section B: Christianity Practices (Mock)

Question 5: Give the name of the Christian prayer taught by Jesus, containing petitions for bread, forgiveness, and temptation. (1 mark)

Model answer: Lord's Prayer.

Question 6: Name a major Christian pilgrimage site and explain one reason pilgrims visit. (4 marks)

Model answer: Lourdes, France. Pilgrims visit seeking healing, especially physical miracles. The shrine honors Mary (mother of Jesus) and healing waters. Thousands report spiritual encounters. Alternatively: Iona, Scotland—Celtic spirituality, seeking spiritual renewal and connection to Christian heritage.

Section C: Islam Beliefs (Mock)

Question 7: Give the name of the Islamic belief that Allah is one and unique, with no partners. (1 mark)

Model answer: Tawhid.

Question 8: Explain why angels are important in Islamic theology. (4 marks)

Model answer: Angels are created beings who serve Allah without question, executing divine commands. They deliver messages from Allah to prophets (e.g., Jibril brought Qur'an to Muhammad). They record human deeds for Day of Judgment. They have specific roles (Mika'il provides nourishment, Izra'il takes souls at death). Angels represent perfection and God's presence in creation.

Section D: Islam Practices (Mock)

Question 9: Give the name of the Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca required once in a lifetime if able. (1 mark)

Model answer: Hajj.

Question 10: Explain how Zakah (almsgiving) reflects Islamic values. (4 marks)

Model answer: Zakah requires Muslims to give 2.5% of accumulated wealth to poor, demonstrating social responsibility and community care. It purifies remaining wealth, teaching that possessions are ultimately Allah's. Zakah equalizes wealth distribution, reflecting Islamic justice. It develops compassion and humility in the giver. It ensures vulnerable community members are supported, fulfilling the principle of caring for others' welfare.

Extended Essay Practice: 12-Mark Questions

Extended Question 1 (12 marks)

To what extent is faith more important than practice in Christianity?

Guidance: Consider both faith (belief, trust, conversion) and practice (worship, prayer, sacraments). Weigh theological positions: some traditions emphasize faith alone (sola fide), others emphasize works. Consider counterarguments. Reach nuanced conclusion acknowledging both matter.

Extended Question 2 (12 marks)

How far do the Five Pillars successfully unify Islam across different cultures and regions?

Guidance: Arguments for: universal obligation, all Muslims practice, provides common identity, transcends cultural differences. Arguments against: interpretation varies (Sunni/Shi'a differences), socioeconomic factors affect practice, some pillars culturally adapted. Consider how pillars DO unify despite variations. Balance strengths and limitations.

Self-Assessment & Model Answers

How to Mark Your Own Work

1-mark questions: Mark as correct/incorrect. No partial credit. Check against model answer.

4-mark questions: Award 1 mark per developed point. Max 2 points per answer. Look for evidence, reasoning, examples.

6-mark questions: Award marks for: understanding (2), evaluation (2), quality (2). Do they weigh both sides? Reach conclusion?

12-mark questions: Award marks for: argument structure (4), evidence/reasoning (4), conclusion (2), SPaG (2).

Common scoring error: Being too generous. Use mark scheme strictly. Vague answers should score lower.

Feedback on Mock Essays

What to look for in your own essay:

  • Clear thesis statement in introduction (1 sentence stating your position)
  • Three developed paragraphs, each with topic sentence and supporting evidence
  • Counterargument acknowledged (shows nuanced thinking)
  • Conclusion that reaffirms thesis and directly answers the question
  • Accurate religious terminology spelled correctly
  • No vague phrases ("many people," "some believe")—be specific
  • Evidence or examples supporting each claim

Guidance

Revision strategy after this lesson: Rework any question you scored below 75% on. Check model answer. Identify what was missing (evidence? explanation? evaluation?). Rewrite to full marks. Repeat for all four sections until you can answer all questions fluently.
