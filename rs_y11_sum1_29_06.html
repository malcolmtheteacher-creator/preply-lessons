Self-Marking Guidance

Use this detailed guidance to mark your mock exam.

1-Mark Questions: Marking Scheme

Full mark (1): Correct identification or statement.

Partial mark (0): Wrong or vague answer.

Example (Q1): Acceptable: 'Omnipotent', 'All-powerful', 'Creator', 'Loving'.

4-Mark Questions: Marking Scheme

Knowledge (2 marks): Accurate understanding of the belief/practice.

Application (2 marks): Explanation of why it matters or how it applies.

Example (Q3 model):

Reason 1: The Resurrection proves Jesus is God's son (Knowledge 1). This validates his teachings and authority, giving believers confidence in Christian faith (Application 1).

Reason 2: It offers hope of eternal life (Knowledge 1). This comforts believers in grief and motivates moral living (Application 1).

Total: 2 + 2 = 4 marks.

6-Mark Questions: Marking Scheme

Knowledge (2 marks): Two clear, accurate explanations.

Application (2 marks): Connection to belief or context.

Analysis (2 marks): Evaluation or deeper understanding.

Example (Q4 model opening):

Christians believe God is omnipotent, meaning all-powerful. This is shown in the creation narrative where God creates with authority. [Knowledge 1] This belief affects worship through prayer, as believers trust God can answer petitions. [Application 1] Second belief: Omnibenevolence (all-good). This is demonstrated through Jesus' sacrificial love. [Knowledge 1] In worship, this inspires hymns of gratitude and thanksgiving. [Application 1] Overall, belief in God's nature shapes both the content and emotion of Christian worship. [Analysis 2]

12-Mark Questions: Marking Scheme (plus 3 SPaG)

Knowledge (4 marks): Accurate, detailed knowledge of the topic.

Application (4 marks): Evidence and examples; addresses the statement.

Analysis (4 marks): Weighs both sides; reasoned conclusion.

SPaG (3 marks): Spelling, punctuation, grammar, and sentence demarcation.

12-mark structure:

  • Para 1 (Intro): Restate the claim; show you understand the question.
  • Para 2 (For): Arguments supporting the claim with examples.
  • Para 3 (Against): Counterarguments and nuance.
  • Para 4 (Conclusion): Weighed judgment: 'To a large extent...' or 'However, the evidence suggests...'
