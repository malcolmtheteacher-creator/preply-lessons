Breaking Tasks Into Smaller Steps

Large projects feel overwhelming. Breaking them into smaller, manageable pieces makes them achievable.

How to Break Down Tasks

  • Identify the goal: What is the final product? (essay, presentation, exam)
  • List all steps: What must happen to reach that goal?
  • Estimate time: How long will each step take?
  • Set deadlines: When must each step be complete?
  • Work backwards: If the essay is due Friday, research must finish Wednesday

Example: 5,000-Word Essay Due in 4 Weeks

  • Week 1: Read sources and take notes (15 hours)
  • Week 2: Plan outline and organise ideas (5 hours)
  • Week 3: Write first draft (15 hours)
  • Week 4: Revise, edit, proofread (8 hours)

The Pomodoro Technique

A simple time management method that uses focused work intervals with short breaks.

How It Works:

1. Choose a task — One specific piece of work
2. Set a 25-minute timer — Work with full focus
3. Take a 5-minute break — Step away, rest, stretch
4. Repeat — After 4 cycles, take a longer 15-30 minute break

Why This Works

  • 25 minutes is long enough to make real progress
  • Knowing a break is coming helps you focus
  • Regular breaks prevent fatigue and improve concentration
  • You can see your progress (number of completed pomodoros)

Avoiding Procrastination

Why We Procrastinate

  • Task feels too large or overwhelming
  • Fear of failure or not being "perfect"
  • Lack of clear deadline or structure
  • Other tasks seem more interesting or urgent

Strategies to Overcome Procrastination

  • Start small: Commit to just 10 minutes—momentum builds
  • Remove distractions: Phone away, notifications off
  • Set a specific deadline: "Tuesday 6pm" not "soon"
  • Use the Pomodoro Technique: Makes large tasks manageable
  • Track progress: Crossing things off feels rewarding
  • Reward yourself: After finishing, do something enjoyable

What does procrastination look like for you? What triggers it? How can understanding this help you manage your time better?
