Warm, Loud and Touchy?

Latin Americans are imagined as warm and loud, with kisses and hugs. The reality varies.

Key words

1. In Argentina, one kiss is common.
kiss (n) — a touch with the lips, often on the cheek. Example: “In Argentina, one kiss is common.”
2. He gave me a big hug.
hug (n) — putting your arms around someone. Example: “He gave me a big hug.”
3. She kissed me on the cheek.
cheek (n) — the soft side of your face. Example: “She kissed me on the cheek.”
4. The party was very loud.
loud (adj) — making a lot of noise. Example: “The party was very loud.”
5. Business greetings are more formal.
formal (adj) — serious and following the rules. Example: “Business greetings are more formal.”
6. Use a title at a business meeting.
title (n) — a word before a name, like Doctor. Example: “Use a title at a business meeting.”

The stereotype: “Latin Americans are all warm, loud and touchy.”

The story

Part 1 — Kisses and hugs

In much of Latin America, friends and often new people greet with a kiss on the cheek. In Argentina and Uruguay, one kiss is common, even for male friends. In Brazil, the number varies from one to three. A hug (“abrazo”) is common between friends. In Mexico, men often give an abrazo with a pat on the back.

Quick check:

1. How many kisses are common in Argentina?

2. What is an abrazo?

1. One.

2. A hug.

Discuss:
  • How do you greet your friends?

Part 2 — Space and talking

In a 2017 study of 42 countries, Argentinians wanted the smallest distance from strangers. That is much smaller than most Europeans. In many Latin American conversations, people talk at the same time. This can show interest, not rudeness. But some places, for example parts of the Andes, are more reserved.

Quick check:

1. Who wanted the smallest distance from strangers?

2. What can talking at the same time show?

1. Argentinians.

2. Interest and enthusiasm.

Discuss:
  • Do you talk at the same time as others?

Part 3 — Business

Business greetings are usually more formal than social ones. A handshake is safer at a first meeting. Titles such as “Licenciado” or “Doctora” are important in some countries. People from the highlands of Bolivia or Peru can be quieter than people from coastal Brazil or the Caribbean. Many Latin Americans say that foreigners seem colder than they are.

Quick check:

1. What is safer at a first business meeting?

2. Who can be quieter?

1. A handshake.

2. People from the highlands of Bolivia or Peru.

Discuss:
  • How do you greet in a business meeting?

Grammar — comparatives

Short adjectives: add -er + than: “Argentinians stand closer than Europeans.”
Long adjectives: more + adjective + than: “Business greetings are more formal than social ones.”
Irregular: good → better.

1. Choose the word

1. Argentinians stand than Europeans. (closer / more close)

2. Business greetings are than social ones. (more formal / formaler)

3. A hug is than a handshake. (friendlier / more friendly)

1. closer

2. more formal

3. friendlier

2. Make your own

1. A kiss is ______ than a handshake.

2. My town is ______ than my capital.

3. A party is ______ than a meeting.

1. ...friendlier. / ...closer.

2. ...quieter. / ...smaller.

3. ...louder. / ...more fun.
