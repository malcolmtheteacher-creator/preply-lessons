Must, Have to, Don't Have to, Mustn't

Learn the difference between these important modal verbs for talking about obligations and rules.

1. Must vs Have to (Obligation)

MUST = It's a rule. Very important.

You must have perfect vision to be a pilot.

You must complete secondary school.

Note: Must is stronger. It's often about laws or very important rules.

HAVE TO = It's necessary. You need to do it.

You have to have a degree for this job.

You have to complete the training course.

Note: Have to is about requirements. It's what the job needs.

2. Don't Have to (No Obligation)

DON'T HAVE TO = It's not necessary. You can choose.

You don't have to speak Chinese for this job.

You don't have to have a university degree.

Note: It's optional. You can do it, but you don't need to.

3. Mustn't (Not Allowed)

MUSTN'T = It's not allowed. It's against the rules.

You mustn't talk while the teacher is talking.

You mustn't arrive late for work.

Note: Mustn't is the opposite of must. It's forbidden.

Grammar Patterns

Questions:

Do you have to have a degree? (most common)

Must you be over 21? (formal, less common)

Negatives:

You don't have to have a degree.

You must not arrive late. (or: You mustn't arrive late.)

Future:

I'm going to have to learn Chinese.

You will have to complete the training.

Note: Must doesn't change for future. Use 'have to' for future tense.

Quick Practice

1. Complete the sentence: "To be a doctor, you _______ have a medical degree."

have to (it's necessary)

2. Complete the sentence: "You _______ have a driving license to be a taxi driver."

must (it's a legal rule)

3. Complete the sentence: "You _______ speak five languages for this job. Two is enough."

don't have to (it's not necessary)

4. Complete the sentence: "You _______ be late to your job interview."

mustn't (it's not allowed / forbidden)

Speaking Task: Job Requirements

Describe a job you know about. Talk about the qualifications and requirements needed. Use the language you've learned.

What to cover in your description:

  • The job title and what the person does
  • What qualifications they must have
  • What they have to have in terms of experience
  • What they don't have to have
  • Any rules they mustn't break in the job

Remember to use:

  • MUST: "A pilot must have perfect vision"
  • HAVE TO: "You have to complete the training"
  • DON'T HAVE TO: "You don't have to speak English, but it helps"
  • MUSTN'T: "You mustn't be late to work"
