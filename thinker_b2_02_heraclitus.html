Heraclitus: You Can't Step into the Same River Twice

If almost everything about you changes over the years, are you still the same person?

Look at a photo of yourself at five years old. Your body, your tastes, your friends and even your opinions have changed. And yet you point at the photo and say, "That's me." Around 500 BC, a man in Ephesus claimed that change is the one thing that never changes.

Today: You'll meet Heraclitus and his idea that everything flows, so you can describe how things change and discuss whether anything really stays the same.

Meet Heraclitus

Read one part at a time. After each part, close your eyes and answer the question before you check.

I

The noble who walked away

Heraclitus was born around 535 BC into a noble family in Ephesus, a rich Greek city on the coast of what is now Turkey. He is said to have given his royal title to his brother, so that he could think in peace. He had little patience with popular opinions and did not try to be liked. He wrote one book, but today only about 130 short sayings from it survive.

What is Heraclitus said to have given away, and why?

His royal title. He gave it to his brother so that he could think in peace.
II

The river

His most famous idea is about a river. You cannot step into the same river twice, because new water is always flowing past your feet. The river keeps its name and its shape, but it is never exactly the same. Heraclitus believed the whole world is like this. Day becomes night, summer becomes winter, the young become old. For him, everything flows, and nothing stands still.

Why can't you step into the same river twice?

Because new water is always flowing, so the river is never exactly the same, even though its name and shape stay.
III

Fire and a hidden order

Thales had said that everything is water. Heraclitus chose fire instead: always moving, always turning one thing into another. But he did not think the world was chaos. Behind all the change, he said, there is a hidden order, a pattern that he called the logos. His sayings were so short and puzzling that later Greeks called him 'the Obscure', and people still argue about what he meant.

Why was fire a good picture of the world for Heraclitus?

Because fire is never still: it is always moving and turning one thing into another, just like the world.
Talk: Heraclitus says change never stops. Does that idea make you feel worried, or free? Why?

Key words

Each word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means. Then reveal and compare.

1. The conversation flowed so naturally that we talked for four hours without noticing the time.
flow (v) — to move along smoothly and without stopping, like water in a river. Example: "On Sunday morning, traffic flowed easily through the city centre."
2. Learning to cook transformed his life: he ate better, saved money and made new friends.
transform (v) — to change something completely, so that it becomes very different. Example: "The new park has transformed an ugly car park into a lovely green space."
3. Babies need constant care; you can't leave them alone for a minute.
constant (adj) — happening all the time, without stopping. Example: "The constant noise from the road made it hard to sleep."
4. A tattoo is permanent, so think carefully before you get one.
permanent (adj) — lasting for a very long time or for ever. Example: "After six months, she got a permanent job instead of a temporary contract."
5. After the band changed every single member, fans argued about whether it had lost its identity.
identity (n) — who or what a person or thing is; the qualities that make them themselves. Example: "Moving abroad made him think a lot about his identity."
6. My brother loves loud parties and I love quiet evenings: we are complete opposites.
opposite (n) — a person or thing that is completely different from another. Example: "Hot is the opposite of cold."
7. The change was so gradual that nobody noticed it until they looked at old photos.
gradual (adj) — happening slowly, over a long period, in small steps. Example: "There has been a gradual rise in prices this year."

The idea: Everything flows

1
FlowEverything is always moving and changing, like a river.
2
OppositesDay and night, hot and cold belong together, and each turns into the other.
3
FireFire is the picture of this: it is never still.
4
Hidden orderBehind the change there is a pattern: the logos.

See it in action

Here is the idea in action. Take a football club that is a hundred years old. Every player has changed many times, the stadium has been rebuilt, and even the shirts look different. So is it the same club? Heraclitus would say it is like a river. What stays the same is not the material but the pattern: the name, the fans, the way it keeps renewing itself. Change is not the enemy of the club; it is how the club stays alive.

Heraclitus believed that everything is always changing and nothing stands still. His most famous example is the river: you can't step into the same river twice, because new water is always flowing. Thought experiment: The ship that was rebuilt

A wooden ship sails for fifty years. Every year, a few old planks are replaced with new ones, until not one original plank is left. Meanwhile, someone collects all the old planks and builds them back into a ship. Now there are two ships.

Now give two reasons for your choice. Then argue for the other ship for thirty seconds. What would Heraclitus say?

Connect

Compare

Thales and Heraclitus: Thales said everything is made of water; Heraclitus pointed to fire. But they were also asking different questions. What was Thales looking for, and what was Heraclitus more interested in?
Thales looked for the one material that everything is made of. Heraclitus was more interested in change itself: the way everything turns into something else. For Thales, the important thing was the stuff; for Heraclitus, it was the process and the hidden order behind it.

From Thales

What new question did Thales ask about the world?

Not who made the world, but what it is made of. He looked for a natural answer instead of a story about the gods, and his answer was water.

That everything is always changing. New water is always flowing, so the river, like the whole world, is never exactly the same twice.

Because fire is never still: it is always moving and turning one thing into another.

The logos.
