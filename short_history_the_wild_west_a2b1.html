The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — Going west

The "Wild West" is a famous time in American history. It happened in the big, open lands in the west of the United States, between about 1865 and 1895 — only about thirty years.

At that time, the government said: "Come west! You can have free land if you live on it and farm it." So lots of people set off for the west. Farmers, miners, ex-soldiers and families from many countries all moved there to find land, gold, or a new life. In 1869, a long new railroad joined the east of the country to the west, so the journey became much easier.

It was a land of grass and cattle. Cowboys rode their horses for weeks and brought huge groups of cattle north to the new towns by the railroad. There, the cattle went onto the trains and travelled east to be sold. Life on the frontier was hard, exciting and often dangerous — and this is where the legend of the Wild West began.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did so many people move west after 1865?

2. What did the cowboys do with the cattle?

1. The government gave away free land, and a new railroad made the journey easier. People wanted land, gold or a new life.

2. They rode north and brought the cattle to the railroad towns, where the cattle went onto trains and travelled east to be sold.

Talk about it:
  • People left their homes to start a new life in an unknown, wild place. Would you do this? Why or why not?

Part 2 — Outlaws and sheriffs

On the frontier, there were often no police and no judges. So some men became outlaws — criminals who lived outside the law. The most famous was Jesse James. With his gang, he would hold up banks and trains and run off with the money. Some people were afraid of him. Other people liked him, because he stole from rich banks and railroads. In 1882, a young man in his own gang shot him dead, to get the bounty on his head.

Another famous outlaw was a young man called Billy the Kid. He broke out of jail more than once, and people say he killed several men before he was twenty-one. A sheriff called Pat Garrett set off to find him, and in 1881 he caught up with him and shot him.

The most famous lawman was Wyatt Earp. In 1881, in a town called Tombstone, Wyatt, his brothers and their friend Doc Holliday had a fight with a group of outlaws near a place called the O.K. Corral. The fight lasted only about thirty seconds, but three men died — and people still talk about it today.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did some people like the outlaw Jesse James?

2. What happened at the O.K. Corral?

1. Because he stole from rich banks and railroads, so some people saw him as a hero who stood up to powerful people.

2. Wyatt Earp, his brothers and Doc Holliday had a gunfight with a group of outlaws. It lasted about thirty seconds, and three men died.

Talk about it:
  • Jesse James was a criminal, but some people called him a hero. Why do you think people sometimes love a criminal?

Part 3 — The legend, and the cost

The Wild West became a story very fast. Cheap books made the outlaws and sheriffs sound like giant heroes. From 1883, a man called Buffalo Bill travelled around America and Europe with a big "Wild West" show, with real cowboys and pretend battles. Later, Hollywood made hundreds of cowboy films. The cowboy became one of the biggest symbols of America.

But this exciting story hides a sad truth. The land was not empty. It was the home of the Native American peoples — the Lakota, the Cheyenne, the Apache and many others. When the settlers and the railroad came west, they took the land. They hunted the buffalo, the animal that many of these peoples needed to live, almost to zero. After many wars and broken promises, the Native Americans lost their land and were moved to small, poor areas. In 1890, soldiers killed about 300 Lakota people — men, women and children — at a place called Wounded Knee.

That same year, the government said the frontier was finished: the west was now full of towns. The Wild West had lasted only about thirty years. But the legend has never died. More than a hundred years later, its cowboys and outlaws still ride through our films — a story of adventure, but also of a great cost to the people who were already there.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did the Wild West become a famous story so fast?

2. What did westward expansion cost the Native American peoples?

1. Through cheap books, Buffalo Bill's big "Wild West" show from 1883, and later hundreds of Hollywood films.

2. They lost their land, the buffalo they needed were almost all killed, and after many wars and broken promises they were moved to small, poor areas. About 300 Lakota were killed at Wounded Knee in 1890.

Talk about it:
  • The same time was exciting for some people and very sad for others. Can a story be happy and sad at the same time?

Grammar: phrasal verbs

A phrasal verb is a verb plus a small word like up, out, off, away, for. Together they make one new meaning. We use them all the time when we speak. This story is full of them.

Six everyday phrasal verbs:
• run away = to leave quickly to escape — "The thief ran away."
• break out = to escape (from a place like a jail) — "The outlaws broke out of jail."
• look for = to try to find — "The sheriff looked for the gang."
• find out = to learn something new — "He found out where they were hiding."
• give up = to stop trying — "They did not give up."
• set off = to start a journey — "The sheriff set off to look for them."
From the story:
• The outlaws broke out of jail.
• The sheriff set off to look for them.
• The gang held up a train and ran away with the money.
• The people wanted to find out the truth, and they did not give up.
