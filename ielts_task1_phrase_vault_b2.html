Why this series exists

In IELTS Writing Task 1, examiners look for specific language to describe change over time. Most candidates lose marks not because they don't understand the chart — but because they describe it in vague, repetitive English: "went up a lot", "became the biggest", "decreased to one third".

This three-lesson series fixes that. You will master 35 set phrases across three sittings:

Vault 1 — Verbs of Change

Click each card to see a model sentence and what kind of change it describes.

↑ Rise / Increase
rose / increasedGeneral upward movement.
Car use rose from 4m to 12m trips.
climbedSteady, neutral rise.
The figure climbed to 50% by 2010.
surgedFast, strong rise.
Online shopping surged during 2020.
soaredDramatic rise, reaching a high point.
Housing prices soared between 2015 and 2020.
skyrocketedExtreme, sudden rise (one word!).
Crypto prices skyrocketed in 2017.
↓ Fall / Decrease
fell / decreasedGeneral downward movement.
Cycling fell by two-thirds in 20 years.
declinedGradual, neutral fall.
Bus use declined steadily from 2000.
plummetedDramatic, sudden fall.
Share prices plummeted after the crash.
plungedVery sharp fall, like a dive.
Temperatures plunged in January.
slumpedFall into a low, weak state.
Sales slumped in the final quarter.
↔ Stay the same
levelled offStopped changing after a rise or fall.
After rising, the figure levelled off at 60%.
plateauedReached a steady flat line.
Growth plateaued in 2015.
remained stableStayed at the same level.
Numbers remained stable at around 5m.
stabilisedBecame steady after movement.
Prices stabilised in late 2022.
↕ Up and down
fluctuatedMoved up and down irregularly.
The exchange rate fluctuated all year.
waveredSmall, unsteady changes.
Attendance wavered between 80 and 90.

Quick check

Which of these is the most dramatic word for a fall?

plummeted (or plunged). Both describe a sudden, sharp fall. Declined and fell are more neutral.

Vault 2 — Multiples & Adverbs

This is where most students lose marks. Saying "by almost a triple" is wrong — you need the right verb and structure.

× Multiples (proportions)
nearly doubledBecame almost 2× bigger.
Car use nearly doubled between 1990 and 2010.
nearly tripledBecame almost 3× bigger.
Private car trips nearly tripled in 20 years.
more than doubledIncreased by over 2×.
Internet use more than doubled by 2015.
fell by halfDropped to 50% of the start.
Walking fell by half over the period.
fell by a thirdDropped by ~33%.
Bus trips fell by a third between 2000 and 2020.
fell by two-thirdsDropped by ~67% — only a third left.
Cycling fell by two-thirds in the period.
⚡ Adverbs of degree

These go after the verb: rose sharply, fell gradually.

dramaticallyVery big, strong change.
Use of cars rose dramatically.
sharplySudden, strong change.
Prices fell sharply in March.
significantlyNoticeable, important change.
Numbers decreased significantly.
steadilySlow and consistent.
The figure rose steadily each year.
graduallySlowly, little by little.
Cycling gradually declined.
slightlyA small, minor change.
Bus use fell slightly.
marginallyVery small, almost unnoticeable.
The figure rose marginally.

The fix

A student wrote: "Car use increased by almost a triple." What's the correct version?

Car use nearly tripled. We use the verb triple, not the noun phrase a triple. Same with doubled and quadrupled.

Vault 3 — Six Sentence Frames

Memorise these six patterns. They are the skeleton of almost every Task 1 paragraph.

Frame 1 — Simple rise/fall with dates

X rose from [A] to [B] between [year] and [year].

Example: Car use rose from 4 million to 12 million between 1990 and 2010.

Frame 2 — Multiple with data

X nearly tripled, going from [A] to [B].

Example: Car use nearly tripled, going from 4 million to 12 million trips.

Frame 3 — Proportion fall

X fell by around half, from [A] to [B].

Example: Walking fell by around half, from 8 million to 4 million trips.

Frame 4 — Change in ranking

X changed from being the least-used to the most-used [mode / method / option].

Example: The car changed from being the least-used to the most-used mode of transport.

Frame 5 — Contrast two trends

While X increased sharply, Y declined steadily.

Example: While car use increased sharply, cycling declined steadily.

Frame 6 — Third item, stable

Meanwhile, Z remained relatively stable at around [figure].

Example: Meanwhile, bus use remained relatively stable at around 6 million trips.

Matching — which frame fits the trend?

The trend

a. Car use goes from 4m → 12m (big jump)
b. Walking goes from 8m → 4m
c. Bus use stays at 6m across the period
d. Cars go up, cycling goes down
e. Cars used to be last, now first

The frame

Frame 1 or 2 (rise with data / multiple)
Frame 3 (fell by half)
Frame 6 (remained stable at around…)
Frame 5 (While X…, Y…)
Frame 4 (changed from being least to most)

Check your matches

a → Frame 1 or 2 · b → Frame 3 · c → Frame 6 · d → Frame 5 · e → Frame 4
