The Keyword Method

How to speak without reading

🎯 Fluency

Two Ways to Prepare

When language learners prepare for a speaking topic, most people do the same thing: they write out what they want to say. It makes sense — it feels safe and organised.

But here's the problem. Look at two different ways to prepare for: "Tell me about your last holiday."

📝 Way 1: Full Notes

"Last summer I went to Italy with my family. We stayed in a hotel near the beach. The weather was very hot. We visited Rome and saw the Colosseum. The food was delicious."

Looks at the notes → reads them aloud.

🔑 Way 2: Just Keywords

Italy beach Rome

Sees a keyword → builds the sentence live.

Both ways involve preparation. But which way actually makes your speaking stronger?

🤔 Why does this matter? (click to discuss)

With full notes, your brain does: see words → say words. That's reading practice.

With keywords, your brain does: see a word → find the grammar → build the sentence → say it. That's speaking practice.

Most learners naturally write full notes — it feels better. But the more your brain has to build, the faster it gets at building.

Today's Goal: Learn the keyword method — a simple technique that turns your preparation into real speaking practice.

The Keyword Method

The rule is simple: maximum 3 keywords per question. No sentences. No grammar. Just the important words.

How it works

Question: "What do you do at the weekend?"

❌ Don't write this:

"At the weekend I usually go to the gym in the morning. In the afternoon I meet my friends and we go to a café. Sometimes I cook dinner for my family."

✅ Write this:

gym friends / café cook

Then when you speak, your brain does the work: "gym" → "At the weekend I... usually go to the gym" → you build the sentence LIVE.

Every time your brain builds a sentence, it gets stronger at building sentences.

More examples

"Tell me about your home town."

Keywords:

small mountains quiet

From these 3 words, you could say: "My home town is quite small. It's near the mountains, so it's very beautiful. It's a quiet place — not many tourists go there."

See? Your brain built 3 sentences from 3 words. That's real practice.

"What did you do yesterday?"

Keywords:

work supermarket tired

From these words: "Yesterday I went to work — it was a long day. After work I stopped at the supermarket to buy some food. When I got home I was really tired, so I went to bed early."

Your brain chose the past tense, added connectors, built the story. That's the skill we're building.

💡 Important:

It's OK to make mistakes when you speak from keywords. Mistakes mean your brain is working. If you read perfect sentences, your brain does nothing — and you learn nothing.

The New Rule for Homework

From now on, when you prepare for a lesson:

1. Read the prep pack — learn the vocabulary and ideas.

2. For each discussion question, write maximum 3 keywords.

3. Before the lesson, practise speaking from the keywords out loud — not in your head, not in writing. Out loud.

You will make more mistakes in lessons. That is exactly what we want.

Today's Key Idea

🎙 Explain the keyword method to someone who doesn't know it. What is it? Why does it work?

What: You prepare for speaking by writing maximum 3 keywords — not full sentences.

Why: When you read sentences, your brain doesn't practise building them. When you speak from keywords, your brain has to choose grammar, vocabulary, and structure — and that's what makes you better.

The rule: Mistakes from keywords > perfect sentences from a script.
