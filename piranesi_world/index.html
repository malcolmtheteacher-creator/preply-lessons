Key words

awe (noun) — a feeling of great wonder and respect, sometimes mixed with a little fear. Example: "We stood in the huge hall, silent with amazement."
infinite (adjective) — without any end or limit. Example: "The halls seemed to go on for ever, with no last room."
tide (noun) — the regular rise and fall of the sea, usually twice a day. Example: "He wrote down when the water would come in and go out."
solitude (noun) — the state of being alone, often in a peaceful way. Example: "Some people need a few hours alone every day to think."
gratitude (noun) — the feeling of being thankful for something. Example: "She wrote a letter to thank the nurse who had looked after her."
manipulate (verb) — to control someone cleverly or unfairly for your own benefit. Example: "He used kind words to get exactly what he wanted from her."
read between the lines (phrase) — to understand what someone really means, not only what they say. Example: "He said he was fine, but I could tell he was upset."
identity (noun) — who or what a person is; the qualities that make them themselves. Example: "Losing his memory made him question who he really was."
speculate (verb) — to make guesses about something when you do not have all the facts. Example: "We can only guess who wrote the message on the floor."
wonder (noun) — a feeling of surprise and admiration for something beautiful or strange. Example: "Children look at the sea with complete amazement."
