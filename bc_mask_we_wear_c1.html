Idioms of Identity and Emotion

Click each card to reveal the meaning. Then choose one expression that connects to your own experience and tell a short story.

put on a brave face Try to appear calm or confident even when you're not — hiding fear, sadness, or stress behind a composed exterior.
wear your heart on your sleeve Show your emotions openly and easily — you don't hide how you're feeling; others can always tell.
keep up appearances Behave as though everything is fine in public, even when it isn't — maintaining a facade for the sake of others' perception.
let your guard down Allow others to see your real emotions or vulnerability — stop protecting yourself from being truly known.
play a role Act according to what people expect rather than how you really feel — performing a persona that isn't fully you.
two-faced Behave differently in front of different people — pretending to be friendly while having very different private opinions. (Usually negative.)

Emphatic Emotional Clauses: "What I..."

We use these structures to focus on what stands out — a feeling, a reaction, a realisation. They're powerful for storytelling and reflective conversation.

Pattern
What + subject + verb + was/is + [the thing you want to emphasise]
Examples
"What struck me was how silent the room became."
"What I noticed was how different I looked from how I felt."
"What stayed with me was the way he avoided eye contact."

Transform these simple sentences:

Try rewriting each one using the "What I..." pattern:

1. "I felt nervous in the meeting."

"What I felt most in that meeting was a kind of quiet, creeping nervousness."

2. "She didn't react at all."

"What surprised me most was that she didn't react at all."

3. "I was pretending to be confident."

"What no one could see was that I was performing confidence I didn't actually feel."

Contrastive Discourse Markers

Combine an emphatic clause with a contrastive marker to create depth and nuance:

"On the surface, I looked calm. But what I remember most is the panic underneath."

Useful phrases: On the surface... / In reality... / Despite the impression that... / While it may have seemed... / What people didn't realise was...
