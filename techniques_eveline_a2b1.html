Eveline

James Joyce (1914) — a young woman must choose: stay at home, or leave on a ship for a new life

Original version available: read this lesson at B2/C1 level →

Read the story in three parts

This is a famous short story, told here in simple English. Read each part, then stop and talk about the questions. The end is quiet, but important — read slowly and do not jump ahead.

Part 1 — At the window

Eveline is a young woman. It is evening. She sits at her window and looks at the street. She is tired, and she thinks about her life.

Her life at home is not easy. She works hard in the house. She also works hard at her job. Her father is often difficult with her. Sometimes he shouts. Sometimes he does not give her enough money for the house.

When she was a child, things were better. She used to play in a field with the other children. Her mother was alive then. But now her mother is dead, and Eveline is tired and sad.

Stop and talk:
  • Where is Eveline, and what time of day is it?
  • Is her life at home easy or difficult? Why?
  • How was her life different when she was a child?

Part 2 — Frank and the new life

A young sailor likes Eveline very much. His name is Frank. He is kind and friendly. He has a home in another country, far away.

Frank wants Eveline to leave with him. Tonight, they can go on a big ship together. They can start a new, happy life in the other country. "Come with me," he says. "We can be happy together."

Eveline thinks about the good things and the bad things. The new life is exciting. But she is also afraid. She remembers happy times when she was a child. She also remembers a promise. Long ago, she made a promise to her mother to keep the family together. Now she does not know what to do.

Stop and talk:
  • Who is Frank? What does he want?
  • Why is Eveline excited? Why is she also afraid?
  • What promise did she make to her mother?

Part 3 — At the port

Later, Eveline stands at the port. It is night. The big ship is ready. People are getting on the ship. Frank is next to her. He holds her hand.

"Come!" he says. "Come with me now!"

But at the last moment, Eveline is too afraid. She cannot move. She holds the rail with both hands. Frank pulls her hand, but she does not go.

Frank walks to the ship. He calls her name again and again. "Eveline! Eveline!" But she does not move. She watches him go. Her face shows nothing. She stays.

The end — stop and talk:
  • Frank says "Come!" What does Eveline do?
  • Why do you think she cannot move?
  • At the end, her face "shows nothing." How do you think she really feels?

Grammar — "used to"

We use used to to talk about the past. It means something was true many times, or for a long time in the past — but it is not true now.

How to make it: used to + verb (base form).
We used to play in the field. (Before, yes — now, no.)
My father used to work here. (Before, yes — now, no.)

It is a habit or a situation in the past, but it is finished now.

How the writer surprises you

In many stories, the big moment is loud and exciting. But this story is different. The writer surprises you with a very quiet ending. Here are two simple ideas.

1. The quiet ending

We wait for Eveline to get on the ship. We want her to be happy with Frank. But she does not go. There is no shouting, no big fight. She just holds the rail and stays. Her face "shows nothing." This quiet ending is a surprise — and it makes us feel sad.

2. She cannot choose

Eveline is afraid. The old life at home is hard, but it is safe — she knows it well. The new life with Frank is exciting, but it is unknown, and that is frightening. At the last moment, fear is stronger. She cannot move. So she stays in a safe life — but it is also a sad life.

Task C · What do you think? (3 minutes)

Sentence starters
  • "I think it was a good / bad choice because ..."
  • "If I were Eveline, I would ..."
  • "I understand her, but ..."

To finish — easy questions
