Key Vocabulary

Click the cards to flip and see the definitions:

Superstition A belief that is not based on logic or science; a belief in magical thinking
Omen A sign that something good or bad will happen in the future
Charm An object believed to bring good luck or protect from bad luck
Jinx Bad luck, or to bring bad luck to someone or something
Ritual A repeated action done in a particular way, often for a special reason
Taboo Something forbidden or avoided because it is believed to be dangerous or unlucky
Fortune Luck, or what will happen to someone in the future; destiny
Unlucky Bringing or having bad luck; unfortunate

Common Superstitions

Walking under a ladder — considered to bring bad luck
Breaking a mirror — believed to bring seven years of bad luck
A black cat crossing your path — often considered an omen of bad luck
Spilling salt — traditionally considered bad luck; some believe throwing it over your shoulder reverses the luck
Opening an umbrella indoors — believed to bring bad luck
The number 13 — many cultures consider this number unlucky
Knocking on wood — done to prevent bad luck or bring good luck
Friday the 13th — a day believed to be especially unlucky

True or False: Breaking a mirror brings seven years of bad luck.

This is a superstition, not a fact. There is no scientific basis for this belief. However, it is a widely known superstition in many Western cultures. Some people genuinely believe in it, while others see it as just a story.

Discussion and Role Play

Superstitions in Your Culture

What superstitions are common in your country? Are there any objects, numbers, or actions that people believe bring good or bad luck?

Do You Believe?

Do you personally believe in any superstitions? Why or why not? What superstitions do you think are silly, and what superstitions do you take seriously?

Have You Had a Lucky or Unlucky Day?

Describe a day when you felt especially lucky or unlucky. What happened? Do you think it was coincidence, or was superstition involved?

Role Play: A Superstition Scenario

Imagine this situation:

You forgot your lucky charm before an important exam. You're worried this will cause you to do badly. A friend asks what's wrong. Explain the situation and discuss what you could do about it.

Questions to consider:

  • Why do you think you need a lucky charm?
  • What would you do if you realized it was forgotten?
  • Would it really affect your exam performance? Why or why not?
  • What advice would you give a friend in this situation?
