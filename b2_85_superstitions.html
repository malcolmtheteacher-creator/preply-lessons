Common Superstitions Around the World

Learn about these popular superstitions and cultural beliefs.

Walking Under a Ladder

Many cultures believe that walking under a ladder brings bad luck. The ladder is seen as an unlucky symbol or as creating a triangular shape associated with bad omens.

Spilling Salt

Historically, salt was valuable and precious. Spilling salt was seen as wasteful and unlucky. Some believe you can reverse the bad luck by throwing salt over your shoulder.

Black Cat Crossing Your Path

In many Western countries, a black cat crossing your path is considered bad luck. This superstition has roots in medieval times and folklore.

Breaking a Mirror

Breaking a mirror is believed to bring seven years of bad luck. Some traditions suggest ways to reverse this bad fortune.

Finding a Penny

Depending on which way it lands, finding a penny can be either good luck (heads up) or bad luck (heads down). This is a common superstition in North America.

Friday the 13th

In Western culture, Friday the 13th is considered an unlucky day. People may avoid making important decisions or starting new projects on this day.

True or False?

Read these statements about superstitions and decide if they are true or false. Then explain your thinking.

1. Breaking a mirror brings seven years of bad luck.

Is this true or false? Why do you think so? Do you believe in this superstition?

This is a superstition, not a fact. There is no scientific evidence that breaking a mirror affects luck. This belief comes from ancient times when mirrors were rare and expensive. The seven-year period may relate to different ancient beliefs about cycles of time.

2. Finding a penny means good luck.

Is this true or false? What about in your culture? Is there a similar superstition?

This is a common superstition in some cultures. In North America, finding a penny heads-up is considered good luck. However, this belief varies by culture and there is no scientific basis for it. It's interesting how different cultures have different interpretations of the same event.

3. Black cats bring bad luck.

Is this true or false in your culture? How is this superstition different around the world?

This superstition varies by culture. In Western countries, black cats are often seen as unlucky. However, in Japanese and some European cultures, black cats are considered lucky. This shows how superstitions are cultural beliefs, not universal truths.
