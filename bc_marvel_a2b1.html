The Heroes in the Basement

A comic book company went bankrupt and sold its most famous heroes. Then it used the heroes nobody wanted to make the biggest film series in history.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then answer the questions before you read the next part.

Part 1 — The company that lost its heroes

Marvel is a company that makes comics. It owns hundreds of characters, like Spider-Man and the X-Men.

In the early 1990s, many people bought comics as an investment. They thought old comics would be worth a lot of money one day.

People bought five or ten copies of the same comic. But there were millions of copies, so they were never going to be rare.

Suddenly people understood this. They stopped buying, and the prices fell. Thousands of comic shops closed.

Marvel had borrowed a lot of money. In December 1996, the company went bankrupt.

To get money, Marvel sold the film rights to its most famous heroes. Sony got Spider-Man. Another studio, Fox, got the X-Men.

In 2002, the film Spider-Man earned about 800 million dollars. But Marvel got only a small part of that money. It couldn't control the film either.

True or false? Click your answer.

1. In the early 1990s, people bought comics because they hoped to make money.

2. Marvel went bankrupt in 2002.

3. Marvel got most of the money from the 2002 Spider-Man film.

Talk about it
  • Marvel sold its most famous heroes to get money. Was this a good or bad decision?
  • Have you ever bought something because you thought it would be worth more later? What happened?

Part 2 — A big loan and an unknown hero

In the 2000s, Marvel's bosses had a new idea. They wanted to make their own films and keep the money.

But a superhero film costs more than 100 million dollars. Marvel didn't have that money.

In 2005, a bank gave Marvel a loan of about 525 million dollars.

But there was a big risk. If the films failed, the bank could take the rights to about ten Marvel characters, like Captain America.

Marvel's first film was Iron Man, in 2008. At that time, most people did not know Iron Man.

The actor, Robert Downey Jr., was very talented, but he had had personal problems in the past. Many people thought it was a risky choice.

The film was a big success. It earned about 585 million dollars.

Marvel planned all its next films as one connected series. In 2009, Disney bought Marvel for about four billion dollars.

True or false? Click your answer.

1. Marvel had enough money to make its first film without help.

2. If the films failed, the bank could take some of Marvel's characters.

3. Before 2008, Iron Man was not very famous.

Talk about it
  • Would you take a loan like Marvel's? What could go wrong?
  • Why do you think an unknown hero became so popular?

Part 3 — Too much of a good thing?

The Marvel films became the most successful film series in history.

The Avengers earned about one and a half billion dollars in 2012. Avengers: Endgame earned about 2.8 billion dollars in 2019.

Marvel was also able to get back some of its old heroes. In 2015, Sony agreed to share Spider-Man. In 2019, Disney bought most of Fox's film business and got the X-Men back.

Then Disney started a streaming service. Marvel made more and more films and TV series.

Some were very good, but some were not. Many people felt they couldn't understand a new film without watching all the others.

In 2023, The Marvels earned the least money of any film in the series. Disney's boss said Marvel had made too much.

So here is the question. The heroes nobody wanted built a huge success. But can a series stay special when there is so much of it?

True or false? Click your answer.

1. Avengers: Endgame earned about 2.8 billion dollars.

2. Marvel was never able to get Spider-Man back in any way.

3. Disney's boss said Marvel had made too many films and series.

Talk about it
  • Can there be too much of something you love — a TV series, a singer, a food? Give an example.
  • Marvel lost its famous heroes, and this forced it to try something new. Can a problem sometimes help?
  • Tell the story again in six sentences. Use: couldn't · could · was able to · will be able to.

can, could, be able to — ability and possibility

This story is about what Marvel couldn't do, and then what it was able to do.

can / can't — now: My sister can play the piano.

could / couldn't — in general in the past: I couldn't ride a bike until I was nine.

was / were able to — one special success in the past: The road was closed, but we were able to find another way.

will be able to — the future. can has no future form: Next year I will be able to drive. (NOT will can)

Careful: after can and could, use the verb with no to and no -ing: I can swim.

TimePositiveNegative
NowI can speak Spanish.I can't speak Chinese.
Past (general)She could read at four.He couldn't swim as a child.
Past (one success)We were able to fix it.We weren't able to fix it.
FutureYou will be able to see it.You won't be able to see it.
