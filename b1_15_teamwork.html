Language Focus 2: "Work" Collocations

These phrases use the word "work" in different ways. Study them, then we'll use them in the interview practice.

work to do ▼

Meaning: tasks or jobs that need to be completed

Example: "We have a lot of work to do before Friday."

working towards ▼

Meaning: trying to reach or achieve a goal

Example: "We're all working towards the same goal."

working in a team ▼

Meaning: being part of a group that works together

Example: "I enjoy working in a team because everyone has different skills."

working by yourself ▼

Meaning: doing tasks alone without help from others

Example: "I prefer working by myself, but I can adapt to team projects."

work well with others ▼

Meaning: be able to cooperate and communicate effectively in a group

Example: "I work well with others because I"m a good listener."

work out how to do ▼

Meaning: figure out or solve how to do something

Example: "We had to work out how to do the project with limited resources."

work together ▼

Meaning: cooperate as a group toward a shared goal

Example: "If we work together, we can finish on time."

The STAR Method

Use the STAR method to structure your answers in job interviews. It helps you tell a clear, complete story.

S — Situation

Describe the context. What was happening? When was it? Who was involved?

Example: "I was working in a marketing team with four other people. We were tasked with launching a new product in two weeks."

T — Task

What was your responsibility? What was the challenge?

Example: "My role was to coordinate the team meetings and ensure everyone understood the deadline. The challenge was that we all worked different schedules."

A — Action

What specific steps did you take? What did you do to handle the situation?

Example: "I created a shared calendar and scheduled meetings that worked for everyone. I also sent daily updates so the team stayed on track."

R — Result

What happened? How did it end? What did you learn? Use numbers if possible.

Example: "We launched the product on time. The team was happy with our organization, and the company decided to use the same process for future projects."

More Interview Questions

Practice answering these questions using complete sentences and the STAR method where possible.

"Describe a situation where you had to deal with a difficult team member. How did you handle it?"

"Give an example of when you took initiative in a team project."

"Tell me about a team goal you achieved. What role did you play?"

"How do you work best with others? What do you bring to a team?"

Deeper Thinking: Team Dynamics

These questions help you think more deeply about teamwork. Be ready to explain your ideas.

Leadership: In a team, does there always need to be one leader? Or can different people lead in different situations? Tell me about a time you led part of a project.
Disagreement: What's the difference between a healthy disagreement and a serious conflict in a team? How do you handle it?
Different styles: Some people like to plan everything carefully. Others like to work fast and figure things out as they go. How do you handle working with both types of people?
Responsibility: If a team project fails, whose fault is it? The individual members? The leader? Everyone together?
Remote teams: How is working in a remote team different from working in an office? What are the challenges? What are the advantages?
Feedback: How do you react when a team member gives you critical feedback? Can you give an example?
Your ideal team: What qualities would your perfect team have? What size team works best for you (2 people, 5, 10)?

💡 Tip for interviews: Employers want to hear that you can reflect on your experiences and learn from them. Use phrases like "I learned that..." or "In the future, I would..."
