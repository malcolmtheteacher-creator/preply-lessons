Giving Opinions

In negotiations, how you express your opinion matters. Here are the main ways to do it:

When You Agree

  • "You've got a good point there"
  • "I see what you mean"
  • "That makes sense"

When You Disagree (Gentle)

  • "I've got a few reservations about that"
  • "You know, I reckon..."
  • "That's a good idea, but..."

When You Disagree (Strong)

  • "Sorry, that has some serious flaws"
  • "I'm not convinced by that"
  • "That won't work because..."

When You Need to Interrupt

  • "Hang on a second"
  • "Can I just say something?"
  • "Hold on, I think..."
  • "Before you continue..."

Stress for Clarity

In English, the words you emphasize (stress) can completely change your meaning. This is especially important in negotiations.

Example:

"If you are willing to finance the survey, we are happy to carry it out."
(Meaning: it depends on YOU doing this)
"If you are willing to finance the survey, we are happy to carry it out."
(Meaning: we are the willing ones, not necessarily you)

Try This

Which word should be stressed in each sentence? Read it aloud with emphasis and notice the change in meaning.

Sentence 1: "We're happy to carry out TRAINING, as long as you finance the MARKET SURVEY."
Click to Reveal
The words TRAINING and MARKET SURVEY are stressed. This clarifies exactly what each side will do—we do training, you pay for the market research. No confusion.
Sentence 2: "At the moment, I'm receiving 5% commission. If we do this, I'll need 10% commission."
Click to Reveal
Stress "need" to emphasize this is non-negotiable. Stress "10%" to highlight the exact new amount. Stress "at the moment" to show things will change. The stress makes your position clear.
Sentence 3: "I can agree to your proposal IF you provide the facilities."
Click to Reveal
Stress "IF" to show this is a condition. Stress "you" to show it's their responsibility. Stress "facilities" to clarify exactly what you need. This removes ambiguity.
Sentence 4: "We can REDUCE costs, but we CANNOT reduce quality."
Click to Reveal
Stress "REDUCE" (showing it's possible) and "CANNOT" (showing it's impossible). This makes your limits crystal clear without being rude.
Sentence 5: "I didn't say the price was too HIGH—I said the timeline was too SHORT."
Click to Reveal
Stress "HIGH" and "SHORT" to contrast what you didn't say with what you did say. This clarifies your actual concern and avoids misunderstanding.

The Joint Venture

Eagle Spirit & Xing Motorcycles

Eagle Spirit is a luxury motorcycle company from the United States. They make high-end, custom bikes.

Xing Motorcycles is a Chinese manufacturer with excellent production facilities and lower costs.

Both companies want to work together on a new product line: affordable luxury motorcycles for the Asian market.

Key Negotiation Points

1. Financing: Who pays for the market research, facilities, and initial production?
2. Training: Who trains the workforce? Will it be in China, the US, or both?
3. Commission & Profit: How will profits be split? What commission will sales teams receive?
4. Brand Control: Whose name goes on the product? Can each company use their own branding?
5. Quality Standards: What happens if quality drops? Who's responsible for inspections?
6. Timeline: How long until the first bikes are ready? What are the production targets?

Your Task

Discussion Time
3:00

You are the Eagle Spirit representative. Your priority is quality and brand protection. You're willing to compromise on financing, but not on training standards or the "Eagle Spirit" name visibility.

Your partner is Xing Motorcycles. They want to minimize costs and get the partnership moving fast. They own the facilities but expect Eagle Spirit to invest heavily in training.

Your job: Negotiate these six points. Use the opinion language and stress techniques you've learned to make your position clear. Try to reach a consensus on all points.

Remember: Use "You've got a good point" when they're right. Use "I've got reservations" when you have doubts. Use contrastive stress to clarify what you can and cannot do. For example: "We can compromise on FINANCING, but we CANNOT compromise on QUALITY."

Reflection

After the negotiation, think about these questions:

1. What worked well in your negotiation? Did opinion phrases help you be clearer?

2. Did you use contrastive stress? Did it help the other person understand your position?

3. What was hardest to agree on? How did you reach compromise?

4. If you did this negotiation again, what would you change?
