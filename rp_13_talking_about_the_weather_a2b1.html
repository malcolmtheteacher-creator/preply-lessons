Talking About the Weather

The sky is grey and you forgot your coat. Next to you at the bus stop is a neighbour. Say something — a little talk about the weather can grow into a real chat.

The Scene 🎬

You moved to this street six weeks ago. You do not know many people yet. You say hello in the hall, but you do not know their names. It is a friendly street, but nobody has really talked to you — and you have not really talked to them.

This morning the sky is grey. One hour ago it was sunny! Now it looks like rain, and you came out with no coat. You are at the bus stop. Next to you is a neighbour — the one who always has a garden tool and dirty hands. The bus comes in three minutes.

You can say nothing. Or you can say something small about the weather. The weather is an easy way to start. After that, who knows? Maybe you make a new friend.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you talk to strangers at the bus stop or in a shop? Or do you stay quiet? Why?
  2. Where you live, do people talk about the weather? What else do people say to start a chat?

Who's who

You
The New Neighbour 📦

You are new here. You are a little shy, but friendly. You want to meet people and feel at home on your new street.

The other person
The Neighbour 🌿

They have lived here for twenty years. They look after the garden. They are kind and like to talk — and they can help you feel welcome.

Don't worry: you can speak slowly. You can say "Sorry, can you say that again?" It is OK. A small chat with a smile is all you need.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each, then change. Find the phrases from your toolkit. See how a small chat about the weather grows into a friendly talk.

NeighbourIt's cold today, isn't it? One hour ago the sky was so sunny!
YouYes, very cold. And typical — I forgot my coat today!
NeighbourHa! That's bad luck. Are you waiting for the number 30 bus?
YouNo, the 46. Do you live here too? I think I see you sometimes.
NeighbourYes! I live at number 11 — the blue door. I have been here for twenty years.
YouI'm new here. I live at number 3. I moved in six weeks ago.
NeighbourWelcome to the street! Do you like it here?
YouI love it. People are friendly. And the garden is beautiful. Is it your garden?
NeighbourYes, the garden is mine. I work there every week, all year.
YouIt must be a lot of work! But I love flowers, and I would love to learn about plants.
NeighbourThen come and see the garden sometime! You can help me. Oh — here is my bus.
YouThank you! It was nice to meet you. See you soon!
NeighbourSee you soon! And go and buy a coat — it's going to rain!

The Echo 🕰️

In 1883, a volcano called Krakatoa exploded. It was on a small island near Indonesia. The sound was so loud that people heard it nearly 3,000 miles away. It is maybe the loudest sound ever.

The volcano threw a lot of ash high into the sky. The ash travelled all around the world. For months, the sky at evening was a strange, deep red. People in London and New York looked up and talked to strangers about the beautiful, red sky.

They were talking about the weather — the same easy way we do today. They did not know they were looking at ash from a volcano far away. So when you say "It's cold today, isn't it?" to a neighbour, you are using the oldest, friendliest way to start a chat.

Two questions to talk about

  1. Everyone can see the same sky. Is that why the weather is such an easy thing to talk about? What else can two strangers always share?
  2. A small chat about the weather can grow into a friendship. Has this ever happened to you — a big friendship that started with small words?
