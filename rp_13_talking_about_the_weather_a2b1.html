Talking About the Weather

The sky is grey and you forgot your coat. Next to you at the bus stop is a neighbour. Say something — a little talk about the weather can grow into a real chat.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

You moved to this street six weeks ago. You do not know many people yet. You say hello in the hall, but you do not know their names. It is a friendly street, but nobody has really talked to you — and you have not really talked to them.

This morning the sky is grey. One hour ago it was sunny! Now it looks like rain, and you came out with no coat. You are at the bus stop. Next to you is a neighbour — the one who always has a garden tool and dirty hands. The bus comes in three minutes.

You can say nothing. Or you can say something small about the weather. The weather is an easy way to start. After that, who knows? Maybe you make a new friend.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you talk to strangers at the bus stop or in a shop? Or do you stay quiet? Why?
  2. Where you live, do people talk about the weather? What else do people say to start a chat?

Who's who

You
The New Neighbour 📦

You are new here. You are a little shy, but friendly. You want to meet people and feel at home on your new street.

The other person
The Neighbour 🌿

They have lived here for twenty years. They look after the garden. They are kind and like to talk — and they can help you feel welcome.

Don't worry: you can speak slowly. You can say "Sorry, can you say that again?" It is OK. A small chat with a smile is all you need.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. See how a small chat about the weather grows into a friendly talk.

NeighbourIt's cold today, isn't it? One hour ago the sky was so sunny!
YouYes, very cold. And typical — I forgot my coat today!
NeighbourHa! That's bad luck. Are you waiting for the number 30 bus?
YouNo, the 46. Do you live here too? I think I see you sometimes.
NeighbourYes! I live at number 11 — the blue door. I have been here for twenty years.
YouI'm new here. I live at number 3. I moved in six weeks ago.
NeighbourWelcome to the street! Do you like it here?
YouI love it. People are friendly. And the garden is beautiful. Is it your garden?
NeighbourYes, the garden is mine. I work there every week, all year.
YouIt must be a lot of work! But I love flowers, and I would love to learn about plants.
NeighbourThen come and see the garden sometime! You can help me. Oh — here is my bus.
YouThank you! It was nice to meet you. See you soon!
NeighbourSee you soon! And go and buy a coat — it's going to rain!

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases to start a chat and make a friend. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Small and friendly is perfect. You do not need long sentences. A short phrase, a smile, and a slow, clear voice do all the work. If you don't understand, just say "Sorry?" — it is friendly, not rude.

🌦️ Starting to talk

Phrase 1

You are at the bus stop with a neighbour. You want to start talking about the weather. Which one makes the other person want to answer?

A. "Cold."
B. "It's cold today, isn't it?"
C. "I don't like winter."
B — "It's cold today, isn't it?"
The small words "…isn't it?" turn it into a question, so the other person can easily say "Yes, it is!" A is too short and B is warmer than C. You can use it every day: "It's hot today, isn't it?", "It's windy, isn't it?"
Phrase 2

You came out with no coat, and now it is cold! You laugh at your bad luck. Complete it (one word that means "this always happens to me"): "___! I forgot my coat."

"Typical! I forgot my coat."
"Typical!" means "of course — this always happens to me!" You say it with a smile, and the other person smiles too. You can also say "Just my luck!"

💬 Getting to know them

Phrase 3

You think this person lives on your street. You want to ask, in a friendly way. Complete it: "Do you ___ here too?"

"Do you live here too?"
This is a nice, easy question to find out you are neighbours. "…too?" means "like me". You can also ask: "Do you work near here?", "Do you come here often?"
Phrase 4

You want to tell them you are new on the street. Put the words in order:

hereI'mnew
"I'm new here."
Two small words tell your whole story: you just arrived. People are kind when they hear this. You can add: "I moved in six weeks ago."

🙂 Being friendly

Phrase 5

The neighbour works hard in the garden every week. You want to say something kind. Which one is warm and friendly?

A. "OK."
B. "It must be a lot of work."
C. "Why do you do that?"
B — "It must be a lot of work."
"It must be…" is a kind way to think about the other person's life. It shows you care. You can also say: "That must be hard." / "That must be nice."
Phrase 6

The neighbour says they love the garden. You love plants too! You want to say you are the same, in two words. Complete it: "Me ___!"

"Me too!"
Two little words that say "I am the same!" They make a fast, warm link with the other person. If they say something they don't like, you say "Me neither!"

👋 Leaving the door open

Phrase 7

You would like to see this person again. You ask them to visit — in an easy, friendly way. Complete it: "Come and ___ the garden sometime."

"Come and see the garden sometime."
"Come and see…" and "sometime" make a soft, easy invitation. It is friendly, but there is no pressure. You can also say: "Come for a coffee sometime."
Phrase 8

The bus is here. You want to say a warm goodbye — the kind that says "I liked this chat!" What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"It was nice to meet you! See you soon!"
"Nice to meet you" is the friendly goodbye after a first chat. "See you soon" keeps the door open for next time. A smile and a wave help too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work at any bus stop, shop, or door. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"It's ___ today, isn't it?" — Look outside now. What is the weather like today?

"Do you live here too?" / "I'm new here." — Think of a new place. What would you say to a person there?

"It must be a lot of work." — Think of someone who works hard (a cook, a gardener, a parent). Say something kind.

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole chat out loud — start with the weather → make a small joke → find out you are neighbours → be kind → invite them → say a warm goodbye. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your bus stop, your street, your neighbour. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the new neighbour 📦

The neighbour speaks — you answer. Be friendly. Start with the weather, then find something to talk about.

NeighbourHa! Bad luck. Are you waiting for the number 30 bus?
NeighbourYes, number 11, the blue door. Twenty years now!
NeighbourWelcome! I look after the garden round the back.
NeighbourYou can help me sometime! Oh — here is my bus.

Round 2 — now YOU are the neighbour 🌿

Change places. It is your street, twenty years. You look after the garden. You see a new face at the bus stop. Be kind and friendly.

New neighbourIt looks like rain, doesn't it? And I have no coat today!
New neighbourYes! I'm waiting for the 46 bus.

The Twist

🌩️ The neighbour is sad today

One more time. You say your friendly line about the weather — but the neighbour looks sad. They give short answers: "Yes." "Fine." They are not smiling. The easy thing is to say "OK, bye" and stop. But you feel something is wrong. Start the timer. Ask ONE kind, gentle question — for example: "Are you OK today?" Do not push. Just be kind. Try to make them feel a little better before the bus comes.

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I started with the weather in a friendly way.
  • I asked a question, so the other person could talk.
  • I told them a little about me ("I'm new here").
  • I said something kind ("It must be a lot of work").
  • I said a warm goodbye and left the door open.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

In 1883, a volcano called Krakatoa exploded. It was on a small island near Indonesia. The sound was so loud that people heard it nearly 3,000 miles away. It is maybe the loudest sound ever.

The volcano threw a lot of ash high into the sky. The ash travelled all around the world. For months, the sky at evening was a strange, deep red. People in London and New York looked up and talked to strangers about the beautiful, red sky.

They were talking about the weather — the same easy way we do today. They did not know they were looking at ash from a volcano far away. So when you say "It's cold today, isn't it?" to a neighbour, you are using the oldest, friendliest way to start a chat.
