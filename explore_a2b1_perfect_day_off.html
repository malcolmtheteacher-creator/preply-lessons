Explain it your way

What is the difference between "I want" and "I'd like"? When do you use each one? Explain in your own words.

"I want" is direct and strong — good with friends or family. "I'd like" is softer and more polite — good in a café, a shop or at work.

What can you remember?

Answer out loud first. Then click to check.

1. Tell me three different ways to say you like something.
I like it · I love it · I enjoy it · I'm into it · I'm a big fan of it · It's my favourite.
2. What is the past simple of eat, see, buy and take?
ate · saw · bought · took
3. How do you order politely in a café?
"Could I have a coffee, please?" · "I'd like a tea, please." · "Can I get …?"
4. Explain the difference between since and for with the present perfect.
For + a length of time (for two years). Since + a starting point (since 2020).
5. Give me three ways to talk about the future.
I'm going to … (plan) · I'll … (decision now) · I'm meeting … tomorrow (arrangement).
