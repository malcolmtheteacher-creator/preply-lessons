C1 Level · Travel, Holidays & the Environment Focus

What Will the Future Hold?

In this lesson, you'll make predictions based on current trends and learn advanced grammar for expressing complex ideas about the future.

Think About Your Future:

In 3 months, what will you be doing?

In one year, what will you have done?

In five years, how will your life be different?

Understanding Future Tenses:

Future Continuous: will be + -ing

"In 3 months, I will be studying for my exams."

Use this for actions in progress at a specific future time.

Future Perfect: will have + past participle

"By next year, I will have completed my degree."

Use this for actions that will be finished before a specific future time.

Cleft Sentences for Emphasis

Cleft sentences emphasize one part of a sentence. They are very common in C1 English and sound more sophisticated.

What Are Cleft Sentences?

Instead of: "People don't enjoy the fact that roads haven't been built."

You can say: "What people don't enjoy is the fact that roads haven't been built."

This structure emphasizes what people don't enjoy — the roads.

Structure: What + [verb] + is + [emphasis]

More Examples:

Example 1:

Original: Children who are being born now will probably live until the end of this century.

Cleft: "What is remarkable is that children being born now will probably live until the end of this century."

Example 2:

Original: Every family makes a decision about how many kids they're going to have.

Cleft: "What's important to understand is that every family makes a decision about how many kids they're having."

Example 3:

Original: Population and communities naturally manage where they're at.

Cleft: "What happens is population and communities naturally manage where they're at."

Speculation Phrases: Predicting the Future

Use these phrases when you're making predictions and discussing consequences of trends.

Phrases for Making Predictions:

We could possibly... (less certain)

"We could possibly run out of food in the next century."

There won't be enough... (direct statement)

"There won't be enough fresh water for everyone."

will probably... (fairly certain)

"Children being born now will probably live until the end of this century."

We will end up with... (consequence)

"We will end up with 100 million people in Australia."

There's simply no way... (impossibility)

"There's simply no way we could feed that many people."

There will be... (prediction)

"There will be starvation everywhere."

We'll just keep... (ongoing trend)

"We'll just keep growing exponentially until we've got some ridiculous number."

Your Speaking Task: Make Arguments About the Future

Choose a Trend to Discuss:

Select one of these areas and think about the future:

  • Technology
  • Fashion
  • Social media
  • Climate change
  • Work and employment
  • Education

Planning Your Argument:

Make brief notes only — don't write full sentences!

  • What is the current trend?
  • What will happen in the future? (Use speculation language)
  • What are the consequences?
  • What is your opinion? (Use cleft sentences for emphasis)

Speaking Time:

Time for your argument: 10:00

During Your Presentation:

Try to use:

□ At least 2 cleft sentences to emphasize your points

□ At least 3 speculation phrases to make predictions

□ One future continuous or future perfect sentence

□ Clear reasoning and specific examples

After You Speak:

When your classmate finishes, say whether you agree or disagree, and why:

  • "I agree because..."
  • "I'm not sure about that because..."
  • "That's an interesting point. Have you considered...?"
