The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. Edward organised a coup at Nottingham Castle.
a coup (n) — a sudden seizure of power.
2. He made a claim to the French throne.
a claim (n) — a right you say you have.
3. English longbow archers won at Crécy.
a longbow (n) — a tall bow that shoots arrows far.
4. He founded the Order of the Garter, a symbol of chivalry.
chivalry (n) — the medieval code of knightly behaviour.
5. The Black Death was a terrible plague.
a plague (n) — a deadly disease spreading widely.
6. It wiped out perhaps a third or more of the population.
to wipe out (v) — to destroy completely.
7. After the plague, labourers demanded higher wages.
a labourer (n) — a worker who does physical work.
8. Laws tried to stop wages rising.
a wage (n) — money paid for work.
9. The French king was captured and ransomed.
to ransom (v) — to free in return for money.
10. In old age he became weak and perhaps senile.
senile (adj) — mentally weak from old age.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — Taking power

Edward III became king in 1327 at fourteen, but his mother Isabella and Roger Mortimer ruled. In 1330, aged seventeen, he led a small group of friends into Nottingham Castle — tradition says through a secret passage — and arrested Mortimer, who was executed.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who ruled at first?

2. What did Edward do in 1330?

1. Isabella and Roger Mortimer.

2. Arrested Mortimer at Nottingham Castle.

Discuss:
  • What makes a young leader take control?

Part 2 — War with France

Through his mother, Edward claimed the French throne, starting the Hundred Years' War in 1337. In 1346 his archers won a great victory at Crécy; his son, the Black Prince, captured the French king at Poitiers in 1356. Edward founded the Order of the Garter, the oldest English order of chivalry.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did Edward claim France?

2. What happened at Crécy?

1. Through his mother Isabella, a French princess.

2. English archers won a great victory.

Discuss:
  • Why might a king start a war for a throne?

Part 3 — The Black Death

In 1348 the Black Death reached England, killing perhaps a third to a half of the population. With fewer workers, survivors demanded higher wages; the government tried to stop them with laws. Edward's final years were less glorious: his son the Black Prince died in 1376, and Edward himself in 1377.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was the Black Death?

2. How did it change work?

1. A plague that killed perhaps a third to a half of the population.

2. Fewer workers demanded higher wages.

Discuss:
  • How do disasters change who has power?

Grammar — Used to & Would — Past Habits

To describe how life used to be — the habits and customs of a reign — English uses used to and would.

How it works:
• used to + verb for past habits and states: "People used to believe the king could heal."
• would + verb for repeated actions only (not states): "Every Christmas he would hold court."
• Negative: didn't use to.

1. used to or would?

Complete each sentence about Edward III. Use the word in brackets.

1. Knights (fight) on horseback.

2. Archers (practise) every Sunday.

3. Before the plague, workers (earn) very little.

1. used to fight / would fight

2. would practise / used to practise

3. used to earn

2. Complete with the correct form

1. Edward (hold) great tournaments.

2. Peasants (not / move) freely.

3. People (believe) bad air caused the plague.

1. would hold

2. didn't use to move

3. used to believe

Going deeper

Edward III was admired in his time for victories and chivalry. But the war drained money, and the Black Death transformed English society, giving workers more bargaining power.

Worth noticing: legend says Edward created the Garter motto "Honi soit qui mal y pense" ("Shame on him who thinks evil of it") after picking up a lady's garter — a story that appeared much later.
Learning from the past — for today: Scarcity shifts power. When skills or workers are scarce, they gain bargaining power — as after the Black Death and, some say, after recent pandemics.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Glory or cost?
  • How did the Black Death change England?
  • Where do you have bargaining power?
