How Often Do You Listen?

Talk about your music habits — always, usually, sometimes, never.

  • "I always listen to music on the bus."
  • "She usually watches the new videos at night."
  • "We sometimes learn the dances."
  • "He never misses a comeback."

Where does the frequency word go? Say it, then check.

Check the patternCLICK
The frequency word goes before the main verb: I always listen. But it goes after "be": She is always happy. Word order: subject + always/usually/sometimes/never + verb.
subject + always / usually / sometimes / never + verb

"I to my favourite song."

2. Your friend doesn't ever learn the dances. Put "never" in the right place with the verb "learn":

"She the dances."

Better, Best, Catchiest

Compare your songs, singers and dances.

  • "This song is catchier than that one."
  • "He's a better dancer than me."
  • "It's the best album of the year."
  • "That's the catchiest chorus ever."

Two rules hide here. Say them, then check.

Check the patternCLICK
Short words: add -er ... than (catchier than) and the -est (the catchiest). Special words: good → better → the best. Never say "more better" or "more catchier" — pick one, not both.
short word + -er + than  ·  the + short word + -est
good → better → the best

1. Compare two songs. Use the comparative of "catchy":

"The new single is the old one."

2. Say this dancer is number one of everybody. Use the superlative of "good":

"In my opinion, she is dancer in the group."

Have You Ever...?

Talk about your life as a fan — things you have done, ever and never.

  • "Have you ever been to a concert?"
  • "I've listened to that album a hundred times."
  • "I've never seen them live."
  • "She's learned all their dances."

What are the building blocks? Say it, then check.

Check the patternCLICK
have / has + past participle (listened, been, seen, learned). Use ever in questions ("Have you ever...?") and never for no times ("I've never..."). We use it for life experience — no exact time.
have / has + past participle  (+ ever / never)

1. Ask a friend about their concert experience. Use "ever" + the participle of "be":

"Have you to a K-pop concert?"

2. You have zero times seen them in real life. Use "never" + the participle of "see":

"I them live."
