Meet Hobbes

Read one part at a time. After each part, close your eyes and answer the question before you check.

🌊

Born with fear

Thomas Hobbes was born in England in April 1588. That year, a huge Spanish fleet, the Armada, was sailing to attack England. According to Hobbes, his mother was so frightened by the news that he was born early. Later he joked that his mother gave birth to twins: himself and fear. He studied at Oxford, and in 1608 he became tutor to the Cavendish family, who were rich and powerful and gave him books and travel.

What did Hobbes mean when he said that he and fear were born twins?

His mother was frightened by news of the Spanish Armada and gave birth to him early, so fear came into the world at the same time as him.
⚔️

A country at war

In 1640, as England moved towards a civil war between the King and Parliament, Hobbes escaped to Paris. He stayed there for about eleven years, meeting scientists and thinkers, and for a time he taught mathematics to the young prince who later became King Charles II. Back home, neighbours were fighting neighbours. Hobbes watched his country tear itself apart, and he asked one question: what stops human beings from destroying each other?

Why did Hobbes leave England in 1640?

Because the country was moving towards civil war, and he felt in danger. He escaped to Paris.
🐉

The great sea monster

In 1651 Hobbes published Leviathan, named after a giant sea monster in the Bible. In it he imagined life with no government at all, which he called the 'state of nature'. Everyone would fear everyone, there would be no farming, no trade and no art, and life would be "nasty, brutish and short". To escape, people agree to give up some freedom to a strong ruler in return for safety. Hobbes died in 1679, aged 91.

What did Hobbes call life with no government, and what would it be like?

The 'state of nature'. Everyone would fear everyone, and life would be "nasty, brutish and short".
Talk: Hobbes lived through a civil war. Do you think his own experience made his view of people too dark, or did it help him see the truth?

Key words

Each word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means. Then reveal and compare.

1. After the storm, the town was in chaos: roads were blocked and nobody knew what to do.
chaos (n) — a state of complete confusion and disorder. Example: "When the traffic lights stopped working, the city centre was in chaos."
2. Only the referee has the authority to send a player off the pitch.
authority (n) — the power to give orders and make people follow them. Example: "The new manager doesn't have the authority to change our holidays."
3. Some people would give up a little freedom for more security, such as cameras in every street.
security (n) — the state of being safe and protected from danger. Example: "Many people choose a job for its security, not its salary."
4. The soldiers finally surrendered and handed their weapons to the enemy.
surrender (v) — to give something up to someone else, often because you are forced to. Example: "At the border, travellers had to surrender their passports for checking."
5. The dog was well trained: it obeyed every command its owner gave.
obey (v) — to do what a person, rule or law tells you to do. Example: "Most drivers obey the speed limit when they see a camera."
6. Before you start the job, read the contract carefully so you know what both sides have agreed.
contract (n) — a formal agreement between two or more people or groups. Example: "She signed a two-year contract with a new company."
7. It was a brutal winter: many animals died from the cold and hunger.
brutal (adj) — very cruel, violent or hard. Example: "The film shows the brutal reality of war."

The idea: The social contract

👑

See it in action

Here is the idea in action. Imagine a block of flats with no rules about noise or rubbish. One family plays loud music at 3 a.m., so another family does the same. Soon everyone is tired and angry, and nobody trusts anyone. So the neighbours agree: they will all give up the freedom to make noise at night, and a building manager will make sure everyone keeps the rule. Each person loses a little freedom, but everyone gains peace.

Your turn

Now you: explain Hobbes's social contract to a friend who has never heard of it. Speak for about a minute. Use the starters if they help.

Hobbes imagined a world without...In that world, people would...So we agree to...In return, we get...
Hobbes imagined a world without government, which he called the state of nature. In that world, people would fear each other and fight over everything, and life would be nasty, brutish and short. So we agree to give up some of our freedom and obey a strong ruler. In return, we get peace and security. For Hobbes, even a bad government was better than no government at all.

Thought experiment: The island with no rules

A ship sinks and two hundred people reach an empty island. There is enough food for everyone, but only if they share it. There is no captain and no law. One group wants to choose a strong leader with the power to punish anyone who steals. Another group says, "We're good people. Let's just trust each other."

Now give two reasons for your choice. Then argue for the other side for thirty seconds. Which side was harder to defend?

Connect

Compare

Aristotle and Hobbes: Aristotle said humans are 'political animals': we naturally live together in communities. Hobbes thought that, without a ruler, we would naturally fight. Which view of human nature do you find more convincing?
For Aristotle, living in a community is natural and helps us become our best selves. For Hobbes, society is something we build out of fear, to stop ourselves from fighting. Aristotle trusts human nature more; Hobbes thinks we need strong rules to protect us from each other.

Remember earlier thinkers

No looking back. Answer from memory first, then check.

From René Descartes

What was the one thing Descartes could not doubt?

That he was thinking, and so that he existed: "I think, therefore I am."
From William of Ockham

What does Ockham's razor say?

Don't assume more things than you need: if two explanations fit the facts, choose the simpler one.
From Boethius

What did Lady Philosophy teach Boethius about Fortune?

That Fortune is a turning wheel: it gives and takes away money, power and fame, but it can never take what is inside you, like your mind and your goodness.
From Aristotle

What is Aristotle's golden mean?

The idea that a virtue, a good quality, is the middle point between two extremes, too much and too little. For example, courage lies between cowardice and recklessness.
