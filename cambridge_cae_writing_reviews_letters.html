Writing Part 2: Reviews & Letters/Emails

Master the decisions that lead to higher scores

The Real Challenge

In Writing Part 2, you choose from multiple tasks. Reviews and Letters/Emails are popular choices, but many candidates miss key points that separate Band 6 from Band 8.

What You're Actually Being Tested On

*
Reviews: Recommend OR not recommend something (book, film, restaurant, product, course) to a specific audience with clear reasoning
*
Letters/Emails: Communicate effectively with appropriate register (formal or informal) depending on who you're writing to

What Examiners Actually Want

Engaging Style
Your writing should be interesting to read, not flat and boring. Hook your reader!
Clear Opinion
Take a stance. Wishy-washy "it was good but also bad" responses score lower.
Appropriate Register
Match your language to your audience. Formal for business, informal for friends.
Task Achievement
Answer ALL parts of the question. Missing points = lower score.

Band 6 vs Band 8: The Difference

Band 6 Review Opening

"I went to a restaurant last week. It was called The Golden Spoon. I will tell you about it."

  • Generic, boring start
  • No hook for the reader
  • Tells rather than shows
  • No personality in the writing
Band 8 Review Opening

"Hidden down a narrow side street, The Golden Spoon is the kind of place you'd walk past without a second glance - and that would be a mistake."

  • Creates intrigue immediately
  • Shows personality and style
  • Engages the reader
  • Sets up a recommendation
Band 6 Letter Opening

"Dear Sir/Madam, I am writing to complain about the service I received. It was very bad."

  • Vague complaint
  • No specific details
  • Weak vocabulary
  • Unclear purpose
Band 8 Letter Opening

"Dear Sir/Madam, I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with the service I received at your Oxford Street branch on 15th January."

  • Specific and clear
  • Professional vocabulary
  • Includes relevant details
  • Purpose immediately clear
The Key Insight

The difference between Band 6 and Band 8 isn't just vocabulary - it's the DECISIONS you make about how to engage your reader and achieve your purpose. The next tabs will teach you these decisions.

Decision 1: Engage Your Reader

A review is not just a description - it's a recommendation to a specific audience. Your job is to help readers decide whether something is right for THEM.

The Review Structure

Hook Opening
-->
Description
-->
Evaluation
-->
Recommendation

Purpose: Make the reader want to continue reading

Techniques:

  • Start with an intriguing statement or question
  • Use a surprising fact or statistic
  • Create a vivid scene or image
  • Challenge a common assumption

Example: "If you think all Italian restaurants in London are overpriced tourist traps, prepare to change your mind."

Purpose: Give the reader essential information

Include:

  • What it is (restaurant type, book genre, course format)
  • Key features or highlights
  • Atmosphere/setting/style

Tip: Be selective - don't describe everything, focus on what matters for your recommendation.

Purpose: Show critical thinking with both positives and negatives

Key principle: Even positive reviews should mention a minor drawback. Even negative reviews should acknowledge something good.

Example balance:

  • Positive review: "While the wait for a table can be frustrating during peak hours, the food more than makes up for it."
  • Negative review: "Despite the friendly staff, the overpriced menu and disappointing portions make this restaurant hard to recommend."

Purpose: Tell the reader clearly what to do

Remember: Your recommendation should match your TARGET AUDIENCE

  • "Perfect for anyone looking for a romantic dinner spot"
  • "I wouldn't recommend this for families with young children"
  • "Ideal for intermediate learners who want to improve quickly"
Avoid Plot Spoilers!

For book and film reviews, NEVER reveal key plot twists or endings. Focus on themes, style, and overall experience instead.

Decision 2: Language That Works

Strong reviews use specific language patterns. Master these phrases and you'll write more confidently and score higher.

Opinion Phrases That Impress

"I was particularly impressed by..."
Use to highlight a standout positive feature
"What struck me most was..."
Emphasize something memorable or surprising
"One minor drawback is..."
Introduce a negative point diplomatically
"What sets it apart from..."
Compare to similar options
"The only downside is..."
Acknowledge a weakness while staying positive overall
"It's worth noting that..."
Add an important detail the reader should know

Recommendation Language

"I would highly recommend this to..."
Strong positive recommendation for specific audience
"It's definitely worth..."
...trying / visiting / the investment / a watch
"I wouldn't hesitate to..."
...recommend / return / suggest this to a friend
"Unless you're looking for..., I'd suggest..."
Conditional recommendation with alternative
"This is ideal/perfect for..."
Match recommendation to audience
"I'd give this a miss if..."
Negative recommendation for certain readers

Descriptive Vocabulary by Review Type

Review Type Positive Vocabulary Negative Vocabulary
Restaurant mouthwatering, exquisite, cosy, welcoming, attentive service overpriced, underwhelming, bland, cramped, indifferent staff
Book/Film gripping, thought-provoking, beautifully written, compelling, moving predictable, slow-paced, cliched, disappointing, forgettable
Course comprehensive, well-structured, practical, engaging, excellent value disorganised, outdated, superficial, poor value, uninspiring
Product user-friendly, innovative, reliable, excellent quality, good value overpriced, poorly designed, unreliable, disappointing, cheaply made

Practice: Upgrade These Sentences

"The food was good and the service was nice."
"I was particularly impressed by the mouthwatering seafood dishes, and the attentive yet unobtrusive service made the evening truly memorable."
"The course was useful but some parts were boring."
"While the practical workshops were comprehensive and highly engaging, the theoretical modules felt somewhat slow-paced and could benefit from more interactive elements."
"I think people should read this book."
"I wouldn't hesitate to recommend this gripping novel to anyone who enjoys thought-provoking fiction - it's definitely worth clearing your schedule for."

Decision 3: Get the Register Right

The biggest mistake in CAE letters and emails is using the wrong register. Mixing formal and informal language will cost you marks.

When to Use Each Register

FORMAL Use formal register
  • Job applications
  • Complaints to companies
  • Enquiries to organisations
  • Letters to newspapers/magazines
  • Business proposals
  • Official requests
INFORMAL Use informal register
  • Letters to friends
  • Advice columns (as a reader)
  • Thank you notes to someone you know
  • Invitations to friends
  • Sharing news with family

Opening and Closing Conventions

Situation Opening Closing
Unknown recipient (formal) Dear Sir/Madam, Yours faithfully,
Known name (formal) Dear Mr/Ms/Dr [Surname], Yours sincerely,
Semi-formal (first name basis) Dear [First name], Best regards, / Kind regards,
Friend (informal) Hi [Name], / Dear [Name], Love, / Best wishes, / Take care,

Formal vs Informal Vocabulary

Informal Formal
I want to... I would like to... / I wish to...
Can you...? Would it be possible to...? / Could you kindly...?
Thanks for... I am grateful for... / Thank you for...
Sorry about... I apologise for... / Please accept my apologies for...
I think... In my opinion... / I believe... / It seems to me that...
Also Furthermore / In addition / Moreover
But However / Nevertheless / On the other hand
So Therefore / Consequently / As a result
Get back to me I look forward to hearing from you / Please respond at your earliest convenience
ASAP At your earliest convenience / As soon as possible

Decision 4: Common Letter Types

Each letter type has a specific structure and key phrases. Master these patterns.

Letter of Complaint

Dear Sir/Madam, [or name if known]
Para 1: State purpose + when/where problem occurred
Para 2: Describe the problem in detail
Para 3: Explain impact on you
Para 4: Request specific action + deadline
Yours faithfully/sincerely,
Opening phrases
"I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with..." / "I wish to draw your attention to..." / "I am writing to complain about..."
Impact phrases
"This caused considerable inconvenience..." / "As a result, I was left feeling..." / "The situation left me extremely disappointed..."
Action phrases
"I would appreciate a full refund..." / "I trust you will..." / "I expect to receive... within [timeframe]"
Closing phrases
"I look forward to receiving your response..." / "I trust this matter will be resolved promptly..." / "Please respond within 14 days..."

Letter of Application

Dear [Mr/Ms Surname or Sir/Madam],
Para 1: State what you're applying for + where you saw it
Para 2: Your relevant experience/qualifications
Para 3: Your skills/qualities that match requirements
Para 4: Availability + enthusiasm + interview request
Yours sincerely/faithfully,
Opening phrases
"I am writing to apply for the position of..." / "With reference to your advertisement in..." / "I would like to be considered for..."
Experience phrases
"I have extensive experience in..." / "For the past [X] years, I have..." / "My background includes..."
Skills phrases
"I am particularly skilled in..." / "I believe my strengths in... would be valuable..." / "I am confident that my ability to... makes me an ideal candidate"
Closing phrases
"I would welcome the opportunity to discuss..." / "I am available for interview at your convenience..." / "I look forward to the opportunity..."

Letter Giving Advice/Suggestions

Hi [Name], / Dear [Name],
Para 1: Acknowledge their situation/problem
Para 2: First suggestion with reasons
Para 3: Second suggestion with reasons
Para 4: Encouragement + offer of further help
Best wishes, / Take care,
Empathy phrases
"I was sorry to hear about..." / "I understand how difficult..." / "It sounds like you're going through a tough time..."
Suggestion phrases
"Have you considered...?" / "Why don't you...?" / "If I were you, I'd..." / "You might want to think about..."
Reason phrases
"This would help because..." / "The advantage of this is..." / "That way, you'd be able to..."
Closing phrases
"I'm sure things will work out..." / "Let me know how it goes..." / "Don't hesitate to contact me if you need more advice..."
Key Decision: Match Structure to Purpose

Before writing, ask yourself: What is my main PURPOSE? Then choose the structure that achieves it. A complaint should lead to ACTION. An application should lead to an INTERVIEW. Advice should be PRACTICAL and SUPPORTIVE.

Task 1: Restaurant Review

Your Task

You see this announcement in an international student magazine:

Restaurant Reviews Wanted!

Have you been to a restaurant recently that you'd recommend to other students? We're looking for reviews of restaurants that offer good food at student-friendly prices. Tell us what makes this restaurant special and whether you'd recommend it.

Write your review in 220-260 words.

Planning Template

Tucked away in a quiet corner behind the main library, The Noodle House is the kind of hidden gem that every student should know about.

This cosy Asian fusion restaurant seats only about 30 people, which creates an intimate atmosphere that's perfect for catching up with friends after a long day of lectures. The decor is simple but stylish, with warm lighting and wooden furnishings that make you feel instantly relaxed.

What struck me most was the exceptional value for money. For under 10 pounds, you can get a generous portion of their signature dan dan noodles, which are absolutely mouthwatering - spicy, flavourful, and served piping hot. The vegetarian options are equally impressive, and the staff are always happy to accommodate dietary requirements.

The only downside is that it can get quite busy during peak hours, so you might have to wait for a table on Friday evenings. However, this is easily solved by going slightly earlier or on a weeknight.

I would highly recommend The Noodle House to any student looking for delicious, affordable food in a welcoming environment. Whether you're celebrating the end of exams or just need comfort food after a tough week, this place delivers every time. It's definitely worth the short walk from campus.

Word count: 256 words

Task 2: Formal Letter of Complaint

Your Task

You recently attended an online course advertised as "Professional Photography Masterclass." However, you were very disappointed with the course. Write a letter of complaint to the course provider explaining:

  • what problems you experienced
  • how this affected you
  • what action you expect them to take

Write your letter in 220-260 words.

Planning Template

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to express my deep dissatisfaction with the "Professional Photography Masterclass" I purchased from your website on 10th January. Despite being advertised as a comprehensive course for aspiring professionals, the reality fell far short of my expectations.

Firstly, the course content was significantly outdated, with tutorials featuring software versions from 2019. Furthermore, several video lectures were of extremely poor quality, with muffled audio that made them virtually impossible to follow. Most disappointing of all, the "24-hour expert support" promised on your website was non-existent - my enquiries went unanswered for over two weeks.

As a result of these issues, I have wasted valuable time and money on a course that has done nothing to advance my skills. I enrolled specifically to improve my portfolio before applying for photography positions, and this experience has set back my career plans considerably.

I therefore request a full refund of the 199 pounds I paid, as the service provided bears no resemblance to what was advertised. I expect to receive confirmation of this refund within 14 days.

I trust this matter will be resolved promptly. I look forward to your response.

Yours faithfully,
[Your name]

Word count: 242 words

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Mixing Registers

Starting formally ("Dear Sir") but using informal language ("gonna", "loads of", "stuff") - or vice versa. Stay consistent!

Mistake 2: Boring Openings

"I am going to write about..." / "In this review I will describe..." - These waste words and lose the reader immediately.

Mistake 3: No Clear Recommendation

Reviews that describe but never actually recommend anything. Always end with a clear verdict!

Mistake 4: Wrong Opening/Closing Combination

"Dear Sir/Madam" requires "Yours faithfully" (don't know the name)
"Dear Mr Smith" requires "Yours sincerely" (know the name)

Mistake 5: Not Answering All Parts

Read the task carefully. If it asks for three things, make sure you address all three!

Mistake 6: Plot Spoilers in Reviews

Revealing endings or major plot twists in book/film reviews. Focus on experience and quality, not storyline details.

Remember

The difference between Band 6 and Band 8 is not just vocabulary - it's the decisions you make about engaging your reader, maintaining register, and achieving your purpose. Use these templates and phrases, but always adapt them to your specific task.
