Why Did That Happen?

Every day, we explain causes and effects. Think about today...

🤔 Quick challenge: Why are you learning English?

If you answer with just one word ("work" or "travel"), you're missing an opportunity to practice speaking!

The Difference

❌ Basic answer:

"I learn English for my job."

✓ Extended answer:

"I learn English because I need it for my job. Because of this, I practice every day. As a result, my confidence is improving."

Today's Goal: Learn 5 phrases that connect causes to their effects, making your explanations longer and clearer.

The Cause-Effect Chain

Good explanations link causes to effects in a clear chain. Here's the structure:

CAUSE
Why?
→
connector phrase
→
EFFECT
What happened?

5 Cause-Effect Connectors

Click each card to reveal the full explanation:

1. "So..." (Result)

"I was tired, so I went to bed early."
MEANING

Shows the result of something

FORM

Cause, + so + effect

PRONUNCIATION

/səʊ/ - quick, linking word

More examples: "It was raining, so we stayed home." / "She studied hard, so she passed."

2. "Because of this,..." (Consequence)

"The train was late. Because of this, I missed my meeting."
MEANING

Introduces result in a new sentence

FORM

Sentence. + Because of this, + result.

PRONUNCIATION

/bɪˈkɒz əv ðɪs/ - pause after

Tip: Great for starting a second sentence that explains the consequence.

3. "That's why..." (Explanation)

"I love music. That's why I play guitar every day."
MEANING

Explains the reason for something

FORM

Cause. + That's why + action/result.

PRONUNCIATION

/ðæts waɪ/ - slight stress on "why"

Note: Very natural in conversation! Works perfectly with Lesson 8's reasons.

4. "As a result,..." (Formal consequence)

"I practiced every day. As a result, my English improved."
MEANING

Shows outcome (slightly formal)

FORM

Action. + As a result, + outcome.

PRONUNCIATION

/æz ə rɪˈzʌlt/ - pause after

Best for: More formal situations, presentations, explaining achievements.

5. "This means..." (Implication)

"The shop is closed. This means we need to go somewhere else."
MEANING

Explains what follows from something

FORM

Fact. + This means + implication.

PRONUNCIATION

/ðɪs miːnz/ - stress on "means"

Tip: Perfect for explaining what something implies or what needs to happen next.

Guided Practice

Complete each cause-effect chain. Say your answer aloud before checking!

1. "I forgot my umbrella..."

Complete with "so..."

Think: what happened because you forgot it?

2. "The restaurant was full."

Continue with "Because of this,..."

What did you have to do?

3. "I really enjoy cooking."

Continue with "That's why..."

What do you do because of this?

4. "She worked overtime for six months."

Continue with "As a result,..."

What was the outcome?

5. "The bus drivers are on strike tomorrow."

Continue with "This means..."

What's the implication for you?

Build a Chain

Now connect multiple effects. Use 2-3 connectors in one response:

🔗 "I started learning English two years ago..."

Build a chain with: so... → Because of this... → As a result...

Example: "I started learning English two years ago, so I watch English shows now. Because of this, I understand more words. As a result, I feel more confident."

Quick Summary

Today you learned 5 cause-effect connectors:

  • "so..." - joins cause and effect in ONE sentence
  • "Because of this,..." - starts a NEW sentence with consequence
  • "That's why..." - explains reasons naturally
  • "As a result,..." - formal consequence (presentations)
  • "This means..." - explains implications
