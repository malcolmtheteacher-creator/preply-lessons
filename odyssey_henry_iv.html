The words for today's story

1. Some saw Henry as a usurper.

2. He worried about his legitimacy.

3. Owain Glyndŵr led an uprising in Wales.

4. The Percys had been his allies.

5. The Percys chose to rebel.

8. He was an uneasy king.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The new king

Henry Bolingbroke, son of John of Gaunt, took the crown from Richard II in 1399. He was the first king since 1066 whose mother tongue was English, and he addressed parliament in English.

But many saw him as a usurper.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did Henry become king?

2. What was unusual about his language?

1. He took the crown from Richard II.

2. He was the first king since 1066 whose mother tongue was English.

Discuss:
  • Why did language matter for a king?

Part 2 — Rebels

In Wales, Owain Glyndŵr began a long uprising in 1400. In England, the Percy family, who had helped Henry gain the crown, rebelled. At Shrewsbury in 1403 Henry defeated them; Henry "Hotspur" Percy was killed. Henry's son, Prince Henry, was wounded in the face by an arrow but survived.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who led the Welsh uprising?

2. What happened at Shrewsbury?

1. Owain Glyndŵr.

2. Henry defeated the Percys; Hotspur was killed.

Discuss:
  • Why might former allies turn against you?

Part 3 — Illness and Jerusalem

Henry's later years were troubled by illness, possibly a skin disease and heart problems. A prophecy said he would die in Jerusalem. In 1413 he collapsed while praying at Westminster and was taken to a room called the Jerusalem Chamber, where he died.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did the prophecy say?

2. Where did he die?

1. That he would die in Jerusalem.

2. In the Jerusalem Chamber at Westminster.

Discuss:
  • Why do people love stories of prophecies that come true?

Grammar — Contrast — Although, Despite, However

Few rulers were simply good or bad. To hold two truths at once, English uses contrast linkers.

How it works:
• although / even though + clause: "Although he was brave, he was a poor king."
• despite / in spite of + noun or -ing: "Despite losing the battle, he won the war."
• however starts a new sentence, followed by a comma.

1. Choose the linker

Complete each sentence about Henry IV. Use the word in brackets.

1. he won the crown, he never felt safe.

2. his victory at Shrewsbury, rebellion continued in Wales.

3. He was strong. , he was often ill.

1. Although

2. Despite

3. However

2. Rewrite with the linker given

1. Henry was a usurper. He ruled for 14 years. (although)

2. The Percys helped him. They rebelled. (despite)

3. The prophecy seemed false. It came true in a way. (however)

1. Although Henry was a usurper, he ruled for 14 years.

2. Despite helping him, the Percys rebelled.

3. The prophecy seemed false. However, it came true in a way.

Going deeper

Henry IV's story shows the cost of taking power by force: every rival thinks they can do the same. Shakespeare's Henry IV plays show him tired and anxious.

Worth noticing: Shakespeare gives him the line "Uneasy lies the head that wears a crown."
Learning from the past — for today: How you win shapes how you lead. Power taken unfairly invites challenges. Win support honestly, and keep your allies informed.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Did Henry have a right to rule?
  • Why did allies rebel?
  • Which "prophecy" in your life came true unexpectedly?
