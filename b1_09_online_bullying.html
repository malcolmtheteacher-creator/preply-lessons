What is Online Bullying?

Think about your experience with social media:

Which quote stands out most to you? Why? What does it tell you about online bullying?

Key idea: Online bullying is real bullying. Words written online can spread quickly, hurt deeply, and cause lasting damage.

Why Does Online Bullying Happen?

Think about these reasons:

  • People feel free to say whatever they want online because there are fewer face-to-face consequences
  • Bullies can hide behind fake accounts or anonymity
  • Comments can be shared and seen by thousands instantly
  • There's often less supervision than at school or work

Vocabulary: Online Bullying

These words help us discuss the serious topic of online bullying. Learn them by category:

Adjectives (How People Feel or Behave)

withdrawn — staying away from others; not wanting to communicate or join activities
nasty — unkind, mean, or cruel; unpleasant
cruel — deliberately causing pain or suffering to others

Verbs (Actions Related to Bullying)

bully — to use strength, power, or position to hurt, frighten, or threaten someone weaker
harass — to trouble, annoy, or cause problems for someone repeatedly
humiliate — to make someone feel ashamed, embarrassed, or lose their dignity
intimidate — to frighten or threaten someone into doing something against their will
deny — to say something is not true; to refuse to admit something
keep a close eye on — to watch carefully; to monitor closely

Nouns (People & Things)

fault — responsibility for something wrong; blame
campaign — a planned series of activities designed to achieve a particular goal

When you discuss important topics like online bullying, you need to express agreement and disagreement clearly and respectfully.

Ways to Agree

Click to see agreement phrases ▼

"Yes, that seems like a good starting point."

Use this when you think someone has a point worth discussing further.

"Yes, that's correct."

Use this when you completely agree with what was said.

"You're right."

A simple, direct way to show agreement with someone's opinion.

Example: "Online bullying can cause real psychological harm." "You're right. It affects people's confidence and wellbeing."

Ways to Disagree (Politely)

Click to see disagreement phrases ▼

"Well, yes, that is true but..."

Use this to acknowledge their point while introducing your different view.

"OK, I get what you're saying, but I still feel that..."

This shows you understand them but have a different opinion.

"Yes, it is true that... but the thing is..."

Use this to add more nuance or a different perspective.

"I see your point, but I think..."

A respectful way to introduce a different viewpoint.

Example: "Parents should control everything their children do online." "I understand, but I think there has to be some trust and privacy for teenagers to develop."

Create an Awareness Campaign

Now you'll work together to create a campaign that raises awareness about online bullying and its effects on people.

Your Task

Design an anti-bullying campaign that is strong and effective.

Consider these important points:

  • Target audience: Who do you want to reach? (Young people? Parents? Teachers?)
  • Main aim: What do you want people to learn or do? (Raise awareness? Change behavior? Show support?)
  • Key message: What is the most important thing you want to communicate?
  • How to promote: Where and how will people see your campaign? (Social media? Posters? Video?)

Planning Your Campaign

Planning Time: 3 minutes

3:00

✓ Use vocabulary from this lesson to make your message powerful and clear

Campaign Ideas

Example campaigns you might create:

🛑 Stop the Spread

Target: Social media users (especially young people)

Aim: To prevent people from sharing or spreading hurtful content

Key message: "Before you share, think about who might be hurt."

💬 Speak Up

Target: Bystanders who witness online bullying

Aim: To encourage people to support victims and challenge bullies

Key message: "Silence is not neutral. Speak up for others."

❤️ Words Matter

Target: All internet users

Aim: To show the real impact of online words on people's mental health

Key message: "Words may seem small online, but their impact is real."

🔍 Know Before You Act

Target: Parents and educators

Aim: To help adults understand online bullying and support young people

Key message: "Understand cyberbullying. Support your young people."

Your Campaign Content

What will your campaign include?

  • A slogan or catchy phrase?
  • An image or video concept?
  • Facts about online bullying?
  • Stories or testimonials?
  • A call to action?
  • Make it personal: Use real effects and real feelings
  • Be clear: Your message should be easy to understand
  • Be powerful: Use strong words and images that stick in people's minds
  • Call to action: Tell people what they should do or think differently
  • Use the vocabulary: Words like "harass," "humiliate," "cruel," and "campaign" show you understand the topic

What makes young people vulnerable to online bullying?

Should parents and schools control young people's use of social media?

How can they balance safety with the need for young people to develop independence?

If you knew someone was being bullied online, what would you do?

What support could you offer? Who else might help?
