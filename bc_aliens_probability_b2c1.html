Conditionals Review

Click each type to see the structure and examples:

Second Conditional (Unreal Present/Future)
If + past simple, would + infinitive
"If I were a cosmologist, I would spend my life searching for alien signals."
Third Conditional (Unreal Past)
If + past perfect, would have + past participle
"If we had discovered alien life in the 1960s, our world would have changed dramatically."
Mixed Conditional (Past → Present)
If + past perfect, would + infinitive
"If aliens had visited Earth thousands of years ago, we would have evidence today."
"Supposing" (Alternative to "If")
Supposing + past simple, would + infinitive
"Supposing we are indeed alone in the universe, what would that mean for humanity?"

More Probability Expressions

High probability: "It's almost certain that...", "There's every chance that...", "It's bound to..."

Medium probability: "There's a fair chance...", "It's quite possible...", "It could well..."

Low probability: "It's highly unlikely...", "The odds are against...", "There's little chance..."

Arguments For & Against Alien Life

Study these arguments to prepare for the debate:

FOR

The Drake Equation: Given the billions of stars and planets in the universe, the mathematical probability of intelligent life elsewhere is extremely high.

FOR

Extremophiles: We've discovered life on Earth in extreme conditions (deep sea vents, acidic pools). Life is more adaptable than we thought.

FOR

Ancient Evidence: Cave paintings and ancient texts describe beings from the sky. Could these be early encounters?

AGAINST

The Fermi Paradox: If aliens exist, why haven't we found any evidence? "Where is everybody?"

AGAINST

The Great Filter: Perhaps civilizations destroy themselves before achieving interstellar travel. Intelligence may be self-limiting.

AGAINST

Lack of Evidence: Despite decades of SETI research, we have found no confirmed signals. Absence of evidence suggests absence.
