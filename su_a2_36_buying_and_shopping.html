Language for Shopping

Here are useful phrases you'll hear in shops. Click each card to reveal who says it and when.

Hello, can I help you?
Used by: shop assistant (greeting customer)
I'm just looking, thanks.
Used by: customer (browsing)
Can I have some of that special Italian balsamic vinegar?
Used by: customer (asking for something specific)
How many would you like?
Used by: shop assistant (asking about quantity — countable)
How much is a kilo?
Used by: customer (asking about price/quantity — uncountable)
We haven't got any beef today, but we have some fresh lamb chops.
Used by: shop assistant (offering alternatives)

Countable and Uncountable Nouns

Countable Nouns (we can count them: 1, 2, 3...)

Use: a few, some, many, a lot of, lots of, plenty of

Example: "I bought a few eggs" or "We have many customers today."

Uncountable Nouns (we measure them, not count them)

Use: a little, some, much, a lot of, lots of, plenty of

Example: "I need a little milk" or "We don't have much time."

Complete the Sentences

Choose the correct quantifier to complete each sentence.

1. "Have you got eggs?" (any)
2. "I've got tomatoes, but not many." (a few)
3. "We don't have milk left." (much)
4. "There are apples in the bag — about ten!" (plenty of / a lot of)
5. "Can I have bread, please?" (some)
6. "How sugar do you need?" (much)

Role Play: Shopping Scene

You are shopping at LeShop, a premium food store. Work through this role play with both roles. Try to use the language and quantifiers you've learned!

SHOPPER

Your budget: £100

You need to buy:

  • Some fruit, milk, coffee and croissants for breakfast tomorrow
  • Some chocolate for a cake you're making
  • Some steak for dinner for six people tonight
  • Vegetables for dinner too

Remember to ask about prices and use quantifiers correctly!

SHOP ASSISTANT

You work at LeShop. This week's specialities:

Useful Phrases to Remember

  • "How much is...?" / "How many are...?"
  • "Have you got any...?"
  • "I'd like some..." / "Can I have...?"
  • "What's the price of...?"
  • "That's £... altogether."
