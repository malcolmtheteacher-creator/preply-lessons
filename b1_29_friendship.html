About This Lesson

In this lesson, you will learn how to identify the main ideas in a conversation about friendship. You will focus on finding reasons and examples—the words and phrases that help explain why something happens.

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to understand and use vocabulary to talk about relationships and the benefits of friendship.

Finding Reasons and Examples

When you listen to or read about why something happens, look for the reason. When you listen for examples of something, you are looking for specific cases that show what you mean.

Key Ideas

Cute videos don't always show friendship between animals because they do not always demonstrate a long-lasting relationship.

Why? Look for "because"—this tells you the reason.

Research has shown friendships forming between animals from different species, such as chimpanzees, horses, elephants and dolphins.

Examples: "such as" introduces specific examples.

One example was a study in Kenya that recorded a friendship between a turtle and a hippopotamus.

The phrase "one example" directly tells you this is an example.

These friendships probably form due to benefits for participants such as improved health and reduced stress.

Reason: "due to" shows cause and effect.

Signal Words to Listen For

because
such as
one example
due to

These words and phrases help you find the reasons WHY something happens and the examples that support the main idea. Listen carefully for them!

Understanding Key Words and Synonyms

In listening and reading comprehension, you often need to understand synonyms—different words that mean similar things. This helps you recognize the same idea expressed in different ways.
