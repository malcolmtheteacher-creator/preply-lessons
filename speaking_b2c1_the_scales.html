      Key words

      weigh up (phrasal verb) — think carefully about the good and bad points of something before you decide. Example: "She weighed up the pros and cons before accepting the offer."
      outweigh (verb) — be more important or have more effect than something else. Example: "The benefits far outweigh the risks."
      drawback (noun) — a disadvantage or problem that makes something less attractive. Example: "The main drawback of the flat is the noise."
      trade-off (noun) — a balance between two things you cannot fully have at the same time. Example: "There is always a trade-off between price and quality."
      concede (verb) — admit that something is true, often unwillingly. Example: "I concede that the plan is expensive, but it will save money in the long run."
      compelling (adjective) — so strong or convincing that it persuades you. Example: "She made a compelling case for change."
      merit (noun) — a good quality that deserves attention or praise. Example: "The idea has some merit, but it is too expensive."
      sway (verb) — persuade somebody to change their opinion or decision. Example: "Nothing you say will sway me."
      deciding factor (noun) — the one reason that finally makes you choose. Example: "In the end, the price was the deciding factor."
      on balance (phrase) — after thinking about all the points for and against. Example: "On balance, I think we made the right choice."
