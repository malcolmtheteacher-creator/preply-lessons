Something to think about

We often judge a decision by how we feel right after making it. But feelings change. Relief can turn into doubt, and doubt can turn into gratitude. Looking back later, with a little distance, often tells a different story.

Near neighbours

Two people describe the same event:

A: "I'm happy and sad at the same time. It's a bittersweet day."

B: "Honestly, I keep changing my mind. One minute I'm sure, the next I'm not. I'm ambivalent."

SPEAK What is the difference between bittersweet and ambivalent? Give an example of a situation for each.
Bittersweet describes an experience or moment that holds both joy and sadness together, and you can feel both fully at once. Ambivalent describes being pulled in two directions about a choice or situation, unsure what you really think. A farewell party can be bittersweet; a job offer you can't decide on can leave you ambivalent.

    Say it better

    Rewrite each blunt sentence in your own words, then reveal a possible version.

    1. "I'm leaving because I'm bored and the pay is bad."

    "I've valued my time here, but I feel ready for a different kind of challenge."

    2. "Nobody ever listened to my ideas."

    "I've come to feel that my strengths could be put to better use somewhere else."

    3. "I just couldn't take it any more."

    "It became clear that the time had come to move on, and it wasn't an easy decision."
