The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. Edward was pious, spending much time in prayer.
pious (adj) — deeply religious.
2. He rebuilt the abbey at Westminster.
an abbey (n) — a large church connected to a monastery.
3. Edward had spent his youth as an exile in Normandy.
an exile (n) — a person forced to live abroad.
4. The most powerful earl was Godwin.
an earl (n) — a high-ranking noble.
5. Edward chose to quarrel with Godwin in 1051.
to quarrel (v) — to argue angrily.
6. The king remained childless.
childless (adj) — without children.
7. He had no clear heir.
an heir (n) — a person who will inherit a title.
8. On his deathbed, he is said to have named Harold.
a deathbed (n) — the bed in which a person dies.
9. He was canonised as a saint in 1161.
canonised (adj) — officially declared a saint.
10. Westminster Abbey was consecrated in December 1065.
consecrated (adj) — made holy in a religious ceremony.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The exile who came home

Edward, son of Æthelred and Emma, grew up in exile in Normandy. In 1042, after Harthacnut's death, he became king. He married Edith, daughter of the powerful Earl Godwin — the man many blamed for the death of Edward's brother Alfred.

In 1051 Edward quarrelled with Godwin and drove the family into exile. In 1052 they returned with an army and Edward had to take them back.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did Edward grow up?

2. Why was his relationship with Godwin difficult?

1. In exile in Normandy.

2. Godwin was blamed for his brother's death; they quarrelled and Godwin was exiled, then forced his return.

Discuss:
  • Why would Edward marry the daughter of a man he distrusted?

Part 2 — Westminster

Edward's great project was Westminster Abbey, which he rebuilt in the Norman style. It was consecrated on 28 December 1065, but the king was too ill to attend.

His later reputation as a holy man led to his being called "the Confessor" and canonised in 1161.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did Edward build?

2. Why is he called "the Confessor"?

1. Westminster Abbey.

2. He was remembered as a holy man and later made a saint.

Discuss:
  • Why might people remember a king more for a building than for his politics?

Part 3 — The question of the crown

Edward had no children. Norman writers later claimed that he had promised the crown to his cousin, Duke William of Normandy, and that Harold Godwinson had sworn to support William. English sources say that on his deathbed, on 5 January 1066, Edward named Harold.

Harold was crowned the next day. Within the year, three men would fight for England.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who claimed to have been promised the crown?

2. Whom did Edward name on his deathbed, according to English sources?

1. William, Duke of Normandy.

2. Harold Godwinson.

Discuss:
  • Why are both stories possible, and why couldn't both be true?

Grammar — Relative Clauses

To pack a lot of information into one sentence — who someone was, where something happened — we use relative clauses.

How it works:
• who for people, which for things, where for places, whose for possession.
• Non-defining clauses add extra information with commas: "Edward, who was deeply religious, rebuilt Westminster Abbey."
• Defining clauses identify which one — no commas.

1. Choose who, which, where or whose

Complete each sentence about Edward the Confessor. Use the word in brackets.

1. Edward was the son of Emma, had married two kings.

2. Westminster, the abbey stands, became the heart of royal power.

3. Godwin, daughter married Edward, was the most powerful earl.

1. who

2. where

3. whose

2. Join the sentences

1. Harold was crowned. He had been named on the deathbed. (who)

2. The abbey was consecrated in 1065. It was built in the Norman style. (which)

3. Edward had lived in Normandy. He had Norman friends. (who)

1. Harold, who had been named on the deathbed, was crowned.

2. The abbey, which was built in the Norman style, was consecrated in 1065.

3. Edward, who had lived in Normandy, had Norman friends.

Going deeper

Edward's reign was long and fairly peaceful, but he failed at the one task that mattered most for a medieval king: a clear succession. The Bayeux Tapestry, made for the Norman side, opens with him — and the question of his promise.

Worth noticing: Westminster Abbey has hosted every English and British coronation since 1066.
Learning from the past — for today: Plan your succession. In business, family and clubs, failing to name who will take over causes conflict. Write it down, tell people, and make it clear.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Did Edward cause 1066?
  • Why does a building become a symbol of a nation?
  • What succession plan exists in your workplace?
