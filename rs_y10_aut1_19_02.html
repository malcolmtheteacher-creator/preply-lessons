Who is the Son in the Trinity?

🔒Reveal answer

The Trinity: One God, Three Persons

The Trinity is unique to Christianity. Unlike Judaism and Islam (which emphasise strict monotheism), Christians believe God is one divine being existing as three distinct persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

This is NOT the same as saying there are three gods. Christians are strictly monotheistic — there is only one God. But that one God exists as a Trinity.

Scripture Evidence for the Trinity

The Baptism of Jesus (Matthew 3:16-17)

"As soon as Jesus was baptised, he went up out of the water. At that moment heaven was opened, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and alighting on him. And a voice from heaven said, 'This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased.'"

In this moment, all three persons of the Trinity are present and active: the Son (Jesus) is baptised, the Holy Spirit descends, and the Father speaks from heaven.

The Creation Account (Genesis 1:1-3)

"In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. Now the earth was formless and empty, darkness was over the surface of the deep, and the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters. And God said, 'Let there be light.'"

The Father (God) creates; the Spirit of God (Holy Spirit) hovers; later we learn the Word (Son/Jesus) was also active in creation (John 1:1-3).

The Word in Creation (John 1:1-3)

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was with God in the beginning. Through him all things were made; without him nothing has been made that has been made."

"The Word" is a theological term for Jesus Christ. This passage shows the Son's active role in creation alongside the Father.

The Nicene Creed: Ancient Definition of the Trinity

The Nicene Creed (325 AD) was formulated at the Council of Nicaea to define Christian orthodoxy on the Trinity. It states:

"[I believe in] one God, the Father Almighty... And in one Lord Jesus Christ, the only-begotten Son of God... And [in] the Holy Ghost."

The Creed emphasises that there is ONE God, but within that oneness are three distinct persons, each fully divine. This formulation has shaped Christian belief for nearly 1,700 years.

Why the Trinity Matters for Christian Worship and Belief

Worship: Christians address prayers and worship to all three persons. A typical Christian prayer might invoke "God the Father," ask for "Jesus's help," and invite "the Holy Spirit's guidance." This reflects belief in the Trinity.

Christian liturgy and sacraments: Baptism is done "in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19). The Trinity is central to Christian rituals.

Theological meaning: The Trinity reveals God as both transcendent (the Father, creator and sustainer) and immanent (the Son became human; the Spirit dwells within believers).

Salvation history: Father creates, Son redeems (through incarnation and crucifixion), Holy Spirit sanctifies (makes believers holy and guides the Church). Each person plays a role in humanity's salvation.

Different Christian Perspectives on the Trinity

Catholic & Orthodox: Both emphasise the Trinity as an essential mystery that cannot be fully understood by human reason. The Trinity reflects God's infinite nature.

Protestant: Emphasise biblical evidence for the Trinity. Some focus on how the Trinity allows both God's transcendence (Father) and personal relationship (Son and Spirit).

Historical challenge: Arians (4th century) denied that the Son was fully divine. Their view was condemned as heresy at the Council of Nicaea. This shows how important Trinitarian belief is to Christian orthodoxy.

Analogies for Understanding the Trinity

Christians often use imperfect analogies to help explain the Trinity:

Water: Water can be ice, liquid, or steam — three states of one substance. (Problem: This suggests God has only one person at a time, which isn't what Christians believe.)

An egg: Shell, white, yolk — three parts of one egg. (Problem: Similar issue; also downplays the distinctiveness of Father, Son, Spirit.)

The sun: We see the sun's light, feel its heat, and see it shine — all from one sun. (Problem: These are effects, not separate persons.)

Most theologians acknowledge these analogies are inadequate. The Trinity is ultimately a mystery — Christians accept it as revealed truth, even if they cannot fully explain how it works.
