Key words

adaptive (adjective) — changing to suit the person; in a test, the questions become harder or easier depending on how you answer. Example: "An adaptive test gives a stronger student harder questions."
subscore (noun) — a smaller result for one skill, shown beside your overall result. Example: "Her speaking subscore was higher than her writing subscore."
proficiency (noun) — how well you can use a language. Example: "The test measures your proficiency in English."
summarise (verb) — tell the main points of something in a short way. Example: "Summarise the conversation in two or three sentences."
fluency (noun) — speaking smoothly, without long stops or repeated starts. Example: "Short pauses to think do not damage your fluency."
highlight (verb) — mark part of a text, for example with your mouse. Example: "Highlight the sentence that answers the question."
prompt (noun) — the question or instruction that tells you what to write or say. Example: "Read the prompt carefully before you plan your answer."
unfinished (adjective) — not complete. Example: "In Read and Complete you type the ends of unfinished words."
respond (verb) — answer or react to something that was said. Example: "In the test you respond to a conversation by choosing the best reply."
