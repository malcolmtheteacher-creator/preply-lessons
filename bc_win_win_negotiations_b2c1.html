Win-Win vs Win-Lose

The goal of effective negotiation is to find a solution where both parties feel satisfied. This is called a "win-win" outcome.

✓ Win-Win

Both parties make concessions and both get something valuable. Relationship is preserved.

✗ Win-Lose

One party dominates. Short-term gain but damages the relationship long-term.

Direct vs Tentative Offers

The language you use affects how your offer is received. Compare these two approaches:

Direct Offer (First Conditional)

"If you agree to a later delivery date, we'll give you a bigger discount."

Sounds like a firm commitment - leaves less room to negotiate

Tentative Offer (Second Conditional)

"If you agreed to a later delivery date, we'd give you a bigger discount."

More hypothetical - keeps options open, sounds more polite

Key Insight

In negotiations, tentative language (second conditional) is often more effective because it:

  • Shows you're exploring possibilities, not making demands
  • Leaves room for both parties to manoeuvre
  • Sounds less aggressive and more professional

Conditional Alternatives

Instead of "if", you can use these alternatives:

Alternative Example Tone
Provided (that) "Provided you agree to a later date, we'll give you a discount." Formal, conditional
Supposing "Supposing you agreed to a later date, we'd be able to..." Hypothetical, exploratory
As long as "As long as you agree, we can give you a discount." Emphasizes the condition
What if "What if we were to give you a bigger discount?" Very tentative, inviting

Degrees of Tentativeness

Click to reveal the analysis of each sentence:

1. "If we give you a bigger discount, will you agree?"

Analysis: First conditional - direct, sounds like a firm offer

⚠️ Less flexible, might feel like pressure

2. "If we gave you a bigger discount, would you agree?"

Analysis: Second conditional - more hypothetical, more polite

👍 Good balance of clarity and flexibility

3. "If we were to give you a bigger discount, would you consider agreeing?"

Analysis: "Were to" + "consider" - very tentative and polite

✓ Most diplomatic, best for sensitive negotiations

Practice: Transform the Offers

Make these direct offers more tentative and diplomatic:

1. "If you pay upfront, we will deliver next week."

Tentative version:

"If you were to pay upfront, we'd be able to deliver next week."

Or: "Supposing you paid upfront, we could deliver next week."

2. "If you order 500 units, we will give you 15% off."

Tentative version:

"If you ordered 500 units, we'd give you 15% off."

Or: "What if we were to offer 15% off for 500 units?"

3. "If you sign a 2-year contract, we will freeze prices."

Tentative version:

"Supposing you signed a 2-year contract, we'd be prepared to freeze prices."

Or: "As long as you agreed to a 2-year contract, we could freeze prices."

Useful Phrases for Each Stage

🎯 Opening

  • "We're looking for around..."
  • "Our initial position is..."
  • "We were thinking in terms of..."

🔍 Clarifying

  • "Can you expand on that?"
  • "What exactly did you have in mind?"
  • "Let me check I've understood..."

💡 Making Offers

  • "What if we were to...?"
  • "Supposing we agreed to...?"
  • "We'd be prepared to..."

✅ Closing

  • "So we're agreed on..."
  • "Let me summarize..."
  • "I think we have a deal."
