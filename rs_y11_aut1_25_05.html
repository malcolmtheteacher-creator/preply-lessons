🔒Reveal answer

Zakah: Obligatory Charitable Giving

Zakah is the third pillar of Islam — an obligatory annual payment of 2.5% of savings (above a minimum threshold called nisab) given to those in need. It is not optional charity — it is a religious duty commanded by Allah.

The Qur'an frequently links prayer and zakah together: 'Establish prayer and give zakah' (Surah 2:43). This shows that worship of Allah and care for others are inseparable in Islam.

The word 'zakah' comes from the Arabic root meaning 'to purify.' Paying zakah purifies a Muslim's remaining wealth by removing greed and selfishness. It also purifies the soul.

How Zakah Works

Muslims must pay zakah if their savings exceed the nisab (minimum threshold — roughly the value of 85g of gold or 595g of silver). 2.5% of total qualifying wealth is paid annually. Qualifying wealth includes: savings, gold, silver, investments, and business stock. It does not include: the family home, personal clothing, or essential items.

Who Receives Zakah?

The Qur'an specifies eight categories of recipients (Surah 9:60): the poor, the needy, zakah collectors, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, freeing slaves, those in debt, in the cause of Allah, and travellers in need. This ensures zakah reaches those who need it most, creating a fair society.

Khums in Shi'a Islam

In addition to zakah, Shi'a Muslims pay Khums — a 20% tax on surplus annual income (after expenses). This is based on the Qur'anic verse: 'Know that whatever you gain, a fifth of it is for Allah, the Messenger, the relatives, the orphans, the needy, and the traveller' (Surah 8:41).

Half of Khums goes to Sayyids (descendants of the Prophet Muhammad). The other half goes to the marja (leading Shi'a scholar/authority) to fund religious institutions, education, and community services. Khums is a significant additional financial obligation that supports Shi'a religious infrastructure worldwide.

Why Zakah Matters: Social Justice in Islam

Zakah is Islam's built-in system of social justice. It is based on the principle that wealth belongs to Allah — humans are merely its custodians (khalifah). The Qur'an states: 'And in their wealth is a right for the beggar and the deprived' (Surah 51:19).

This means the poor have a right to a share of the wealthy person's money — it is not a gift or an act of generosity. Refusing to pay zakah is theft from the poor.

Zakah vs Sadaqah

Zakah is obligatory — a fixed 2.5% with specific rules about who pays and who receives. Sadaqah is voluntary charity — any amount, any time, to anyone. Both are valued in Islam, but zakah is a pillar and a duty, while sadaqah is encouraged but not compulsory. The Prophet said: 'Even a smile is sadaqah' — showing that generosity goes beyond money.

The Impact of Zakah

If every Muslim paid zakah, it would generate billions of pounds annually for poverty relief worldwide. Zakah creates a safety net for the poorest in society, reduces inequality, prevents hoarding of wealth, and strengthens the ummah. It teaches that individual prosperity must benefit the community.

What is the nisab?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The minimum threshold of wealth above which zakah must be paid — roughly the value of 85g of gold or 595g of silver. If savings exceed this, 2.5% must be given.

What is the difference between zakah and sadaqah?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Zakah is obligatory (2.5% of savings, specific rules). Sadaqah is voluntary charity (any amount, any time). Both are valued, but zakah is a pillar — a religious duty.

What is Khums?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: A 20% tax on surplus annual income paid by Shi'a Muslims. Half goes to Sayyids (descendants of the Prophet), half to the leading Shi'a scholar (marja) for religious institutions and community services.

12-Mark Evaluation

'Zakah is the pillar that best demonstrates Islam's commitment to social justice.' Evaluate this statement.

Guidance

For: Zakah is a mandatory redistribution of wealth — not optional charity. The Qur'an says the poor have a 'right' to wealth (51:19). It creates a systematic safety net. The eight categories ensure comprehensive poverty relief. No other pillar directly addresses economic inequality.

Against: Sawm also teaches empathy with the poor. Hajj demonstrates equality (all wear same clothes). The Shahadah's declaration of one God implies all humans are equal before Allah. Also, sadaqah (voluntary charity) can have greater impact because it has no limits. Some argue that modern welfare states have replaced zakah's original function.
