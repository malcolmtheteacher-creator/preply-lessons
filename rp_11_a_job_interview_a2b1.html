A Job Interview

You want a new job. Today you meet the boss. Time to say your name, talk about your work, and be friendly.

The Scene 🎬

You want a new job. You saw the job online, and you liked it. You sent your CV, and now they want to meet you. Today is the day.

You are a little nervous — that is normal. But the interview is only a talk. The other person wants to meet you and hear about your work. You do not need long words. You need to say your name, say what you do, talk about one thing you are good at, and ask one question.

The other person is the interviewer — the boss, or someone from the company. They are friendly. They read a lot of CVs, but today they want to meet you.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. What job do you have now, or what job do you want? Why do you like it?
  2. How do you feel before an interview or a test — calm, or nervous? What helps you feel better?

Who's who

You
The Candidate 💼

You want this job. You are a little nervous, but friendly. Today you say your name, talk about your work, and ask a question.

The other person
The Interviewer 🪑

Friendly and kind. They ask easy questions. They want to know about you and your work — and to see a warm smile.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each, then change. Find the phrases from your toolkit. Listen for the friendly, simple words.

InterviewerHello, come in. Please, sit down. Thank you for coming today.
YouHello. Nice to meet you. Thank you for your time.
InterviewerLet's start. Tell me a little about yourself.
YouMy name is Sara, and I am a nurse. I work in a big hospital.
InterviewerGood. And what are you good at? What is your strength?
YouI am good at helping people. For example, I stay calm when a patient is afraid.
InterviewerThat is very good. And why do you want this job?
YouI would like to work here because your hospital helps a lot of people. I want to be part of it.
InterviewerNice. Do you have a question for me?
YouYes. Can I ask a question? What time does the day start?
InterviewerOf course. The day starts at eight o'clock.
YouThank you. That is helpful.
InterviewerWell, thank you, Sara. We will call you next week.
YouThank you very much for your time. I hope to hear from you.

The Echo 🕰️

In 1942, a scientist called Oppenheimer had a very strange job to fill. He needed the best scientists in the world for a secret job. But he could not tell them what the job was!

So the interview was hard: "Come and live in a secret town in the desert, and work on a secret thing. I can't tell you more — but say yes." And many people said yes.

Why? Because they trusted the person in front of them. In your interview, it is the same small thing: the other person wants to feel "I can trust you, and I want you on my team."

Two questions to talk about

  1. The scientists said yes to a job they did not really understand. Would you say yes? What do you need to know before you take a job?
  2. In an interview, what is more important — what you can do, or how you talk and feel? Why?
