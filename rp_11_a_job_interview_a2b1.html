A Job Interview

You want a new job. Today you meet the boss. Time to say your name, talk about your work, and be friendly.

The Scene 🎬

You want a new job. You saw the job online, and you liked it. You sent your CV, and now they want to meet you. Today is the day.

You are a little nervous — that is normal. But the interview is only a talk. The other person wants to meet you and hear about your work. You do not need long words. You need to say your name, say what you do, talk about one thing you are good at, and ask one question.

The other person is the interviewer — the boss, or someone from the company. They are friendly. They read a lot of CVs, but today they want to meet you.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. What job do you have now, or what job do you want? Why do you like it?
  2. How do you feel before an interview or a test — calm, or nervous? What helps you feel better?

Who's who

You
The Candidate 💼

You want this job. You are a little nervous, but friendly. Today you say your name, talk about your work, and ask a question.

The other person
The Interviewer 🪑

Friendly and kind. They ask easy questions. They want to know about you and your work — and to see a warm smile.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. It is OK to speak slowly. It is OK to say "Can you say that again, please?" A calm, friendly voice is the most important thing.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the friendly, simple words.

InterviewerHello, come in. Please, sit down. Thank you for coming today.
YouHello. Nice to meet you. Thank you for your time.
InterviewerLet's start. Tell me a little about yourself.
YouMy name is Sara, and I am a nurse. I work in a big hospital.
InterviewerGood. And what are you good at? What is your strength?
YouI am good at helping people. For example, I stay calm when a patient is afraid.
InterviewerThat is very good. And why do you want this job?
YouI would like to work here because your hospital helps a lot of people. I want to be part of it.
InterviewerNice. Do you have a question for me?
YouYes. Can I ask a question? What time does the day start?
InterviewerOf course. The day starts at eight o'clock.
YouThank you. That is helpful.
InterviewerWell, thank you, Sara. We will call you next week.
YouThank you very much for your time. I hope to hear from you.

From memory — don't look up!

1. What is Sara's job, and where does she work?

2. Sara says one thing she is good at. What is it — and what example does she give?

3. At the end, Sara asks the interviewer one question. What does she want to know?

1. She is a nurse. She works in a big hospital.
2. She is good at helping people. Her example: she stays calm when a patient is afraid.
3. She wants to know what time the day starts. (The interviewer says eight o'clock.)

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use in any job interview. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Speak slowly and smile. You do not need long sentences. Short and clear is perfect. A calm, friendly voice tells the interviewer: "I am nice to work with." If you don't understand, it is OK to say "Sorry, can you say that again, please?"

🚪 Saying hello

Phrase 1

You go into the room. You want to say hello in a warm, polite way. Which one is best?

A. "Hi. I want the job."
B. "Hello. Nice to meet you. Thank you for your time."
C. "Hello. Where do I sit?"
B — "Hello. Nice to meet you. Thank you for your time."
"Nice to meet you" is warm and polite. "Thank you for your time" tells them you know they are busy. A and C are too fast and cold — start friendly.
Phrase 2

The interviewer says: "Tell me about yourself." You start with your name and your job. Complete it: "My name is Sara, and I ___ a teacher."

"My name is Sara, and I am a teacher."
Start with your name, then your job: "I am a nurse", "I am a driver", "I am a cook". Say it slowly and clearly.

🙋 Talking about your work

Phrase 3

Now you want to say where you work. Complete it: "I ___ in a hospital."

"I work in a hospital."
"I work in / at…" tells them where: "I work in a shop", "I work at a school", "I work in an office". If you have no job now, say "I am looking for a job."
Phrase 4

You want to say what you enjoy at work. Put the words in order:

with peopleI likeworking
"I like working with people."
"I like working with…" is an easy way to say what you enjoy: "I like working with children", "I like working with computers", "I like working in a team".

💪 One thing you are good at

Phrase 5

The interviewer asks about your strength. You want to name one thing you do well. Complete it: "I am ___ ___ helping people."

"I am good at helping people."
"I am good at…" + an -ing word: "I am good at cooking", "I am good at fixing things", "I am good at listening". Pick one real thing — you don't need many.
Phrase 6

A strength sounds better with a small example. Which answer is best?

A. "I am the best. Everyone likes me."
B. "I am friendly. For example, I help new people at work."
C. "I don't know. Maybe nothing."
B — "I am friendly. For example, I help new people at work."
Say one strength, then give a small example with "For example…". A sounds too big; C says nothing. A small, true example is the best.

❓ Asking and saying goodbye

Phrase 7

At the end, the interviewer says: "Do you have a question?" Never say no! Put the words in order to ask politely:

ask aCan Iquestion?
"Can I ask a question?"
"Can I ask a question?" is a soft, polite start. Then ask something easy: "What time does the day start?", "Is there a team?", "When can I start?"
Phrase 8

The interview is finished. You want to say a warm goodbye. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you very much for your time. I hope to hear from you."
Thank them again for their time, and say you hope they call. It is warm and polite — a good last picture of you. A smile helps too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in any interview. Write your own sentences — about your real life. Say each one out loud first.

"My name is ___, and I am a ___." — Your name and your job (or the job you want).

"I am good at ___." — Write one real thing you are good at.

"Can I ask about ___?" — Think of one question to ask in an interview (the hours, the team, the days…).

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole interview out loud — hello → your name and job → one strength → ask a question → goodbye. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your interview. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the candidate 💼

The interviewer speaks — you answer. Be friendly and clear. Use your real job, or a job you want.

InterviewerHello, come in. Please, sit down. Thank you for coming today.
InterviewerLet's start. Tell me a little about yourself.
InterviewerGood. And what are you good at? What is your strength?
InterviewerAnd why do you want this job?
InterviewerNice. Do you have a question for me?
InterviewerWell, thank you. We will call you next week.

Round 2 — now YOU are the interviewer 🪑

Change places. You are the boss now. Be friendly. Ask easy questions and listen. Say "Come in", "Good", "That is nice", "Thank you".

CandidateHello. Nice to meet you. Thank you for your time.
CandidateMy name is Pavel, and I am a cook. I work in a small café.
CandidateI am good at working fast. For example, I make twenty meals in an hour.
CandidateI would like to work here because your café is famous and busy.
The Twist

🌩️ A surprise question

One more time — no script. In the middle of the interview, the interviewer asks a question you did not expect: "Why do you want to leave your job now?" Stay calm and friendly. Give a short, kind answer — for example, "I want to learn new things" or "I want to work near my home." Then trade a few more easy questions: "What do you like about your work?", "Can you work on Saturdays?" Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said hello warmly and thanked them for their time.
  • I said my name and my job clearly.
  • I said one thing I am good at, with a small example.
  • I asked one question — I did not say "no".
  • I stayed calm and friendly the whole time.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

In 1942, a scientist called Oppenheimer had a very strange job to fill. He needed the best scientists in the world for a secret job. But he could not tell them what the job was!

So the interview was hard: "Come and live in a secret town in the desert, and work on a secret thing. I can't tell you more — but say yes." And many people said yes.

Why? Because they trusted the person in front of them. In your interview, it is the same small thing: the other person wants to feel "I can trust you, and I want you on my team."

Two questions to talk about

  1. The scientists said yes to a job they did not really understand. Would you say yes? What do you need to know before you take a job?
  2. In an interview, what is more important — what you can do, or how you talk and feel? Why?
