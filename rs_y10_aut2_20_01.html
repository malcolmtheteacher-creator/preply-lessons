The Last Days of Jesus (Mark 15:33–39)

Jesus was arrested, tried, and executed by Roman crucifixion. Here's the biblical account:

The Crucifixion: Jesus was nailed to a wooden cross on a hill called Golgotha ("the place of the skull"). The gospel says there was darkness from noon until 3 p.m. At 3 p.m., Jesus cried out, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (Mark 15:34). Then he died. The Roman centurion (officer) who watched said, "Surely this man was the Son of God!" (Mark 15:39).

Why crucifixion? This was the Roman method for executing criminals—slow, painful, and humiliating. Being "cursed" on a cross meant being rejected by God in Jewish understanding.

Key scripture: "When the sixth hour came, darkness fell over the whole land until the ninth hour. And at the ninth hour Jesus cried out with a loud voice, 'Eloi, Eloi, lema sabachthani?' which means, 'My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?'" (Mark 15:33–34).

Jesus as the Suffering Servant (Isaiah 53)

Christians see Jesus's death as fulfilment of prophecy. The Old Testament book of Isaiah describes a "Suffering Servant":

  • He would be despised and rejected — "He was despised and rejected by men"
  • He would suffer for others' sins — "He took up our infirmities and carried our sorrows"
  • He would be pierced — "He was pierced for our transgressions"
  • His suffering would bring healing — "By his wounds we are healed"

Christian interpretation: These words, written 700 years before Jesus, describe Jesus perfectly. This is why Christians believe Jesus's death was not accidental—it was God's plan.

Three Views on Why Jesus Died

Christians have interpreted Jesus's death in different ways:

RANSOM THEORY Jesus's death paid a ransom to free humans from sin and Satan's power. His death was the price of redemption.
MORAL EXAMPLE Jesus showed us how to live and die—with love and sacrifice. His death inspires us to follow his values. (Abelard)
PENAL SUBSTITUTION Jesus died as a substitute for human sin. God punished Jesus instead of us. His death satisfies God's justice. (Calvin, Martin Luther)

Different Christian Perspectives on the Crucifixion

Catholic Perspective

Catholics emphasise Jesus as the Lamb of God who takes away sin. The crucifixion is central to the Eucharist (Mass), where the sacrifice is symbolically re-enacted. They balance Jesus's suffering with his triumph over death through resurrection.

Protestant Perspective

Protestants stress penal substitution—Jesus paid the penalty for sin so believers don't have to. This is why "faith alone" is central: believers receive Jesus's sacrifice by trusting him.

Orthodox Perspective

Orthodox Christians see the crucifixion as deification—God becoming human so humans can become united with God. They emphasise theosis (union with God), not just forgiveness of sin.

Liberal Christian Perspective

Some modern Christians see the crucifixion as Jesus standing against injustice. He challenged Roman power and Jewish religious authority, and was executed for it. His death shows commitment to justice, not just sacrifice.

Review: Key Concepts

What is the Crucifixion, and when did it happen?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Jesus's execution by Roman crucifixion outside Jerusalem around 30 CE. He was nailed to a wooden cross and died in public.

What is the Suffering Servant prophecy (Isaiah 53)?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Old Testament prediction that a servant would suffer for others' sins. Christians believe this describes Jesus.

What is the Ransom Theory of atonement?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The theory that Jesus's death paid a ransom to free humanity from sin and Satan's power.

What is Penal Substitution?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Jesus died as a substitute for human sin. God punished Jesus instead of us to satisfy divine justice.

Why do Christians see the crucifixion as victory, not defeat?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Because it defeated sin and death, showed God's love, and fulfilled prophecy. Resurrection proved it was victory.
