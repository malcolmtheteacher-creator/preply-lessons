The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. The name John seemed unlucky.
unlucky (adj) — bringing bad luck.
2. A horse's kick left him with an injury.
an injury (n) — physical harm.
3. His brother acted almost as regent.
a regent (n) — a person who rules for a king.
4. His son died in custody.
custody (n) — being kept by authorities.
5. Rumour said he was starved.
to starve (v) — to suffer or die from hunger.
6. Pirates captured the prince's ship.
to capture (v) — to take as a prisoner.
7. He called himself wretched.
wretched (adj) — very unhappy.
8. He asked to be buried in a midden.
a midden (n) — a rubbish heap.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — A new name

Robert III was born John, the eldest son of Robert II. When he became king in 1390, he took the name Robert. A chronicler suggests John seemed unlucky — King John Balliol had been humiliated by England. He had also been badly injured by a horse's kick years before.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was his real name?

2. Why change it?

1. John.

2. John seemed an unlucky royal name, after John Balliol.

Discuss:
  • Does a name matter for a leader?

Part 2 — Brothers and sons

Real power lay with his brother Robert, Duke of Albany. In 1402 Robert's elder son, David, Duke of Rothesay, died while held in Albany's custody at Falkland. Rumour said he had been starved. Albany was officially cleared.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who held real power?

2. What happened to Rothesay?

1. The king's brother, the Duke of Albany.

2. He died in Albany's custody at Falkland in 1402.

Discuss:
  • Why could a king not protect his own son?

Part 3 — The sea and the end

In 1406 Robert sent his younger son James, aged about 11, to safety in France. English sailors captured his ship, and James was taken to England. Robert died soon after, in April 1406. The chronicler Walter Bower reports that Robert said he was "the worst of kings and the most wretched of men" and asked to be buried in a midden. His words may be a chronicler's dramatic invention.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened to James?

2. What did Robert reportedly say?

1. His ship was captured and he was taken to England.

2. That he was the worst of kings and most wretched of men.

Discuss:
  • Why might a chronicler invent last words?

Grammar — Reported Speech

Much of what we know about the past comes from chroniclers reporting what people said. Reported speech moves the tense back and changes the pronouns.

How it works:
• "I will return," he said → He said that he would return.
• "We have lost," she said → She said that they had lost.
• Reporting verbs: claimed, promised, warned, insisted, denied.

1. Report the words

Complete each sentence about Robert III. Use the word in brackets.

1. "John is an unlucky name," he said. → He said that John an unlucky name.

2. "I am the worst of kings," he said. → He said that he the worst of kings.

3. "Bury me in a midden," he said. → He asked them him in a midden.

1. was

2. was

3. to bury

2. Choose the reporting verb

1. "My son has been captured," he said. → He said his son captured.

2. "Albany is innocent," the council said. → The council declared that Albany innocent.

3. "We will send James to France," he said. → He said they send James to France.

2. was

3. would

Going deeper

Robert III's reign shows the danger of a weak centre. When the king could not control his family, his family fought itself.

Worth noticing: Robert III was buried at Paisley Abbey — not, in the end, in a midden.
Learning from the past — for today: Be honest about your limits — then act on them. Robert seems to have known he was unsuited to rule but never solved it. If you're in the wrong role, say so early and help find the right person.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Was Robert to blame?
  • Can you change your luck by changing your name?
  • What would you tell a struggling leader?
