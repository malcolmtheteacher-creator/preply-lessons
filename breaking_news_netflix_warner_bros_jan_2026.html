The proposed deal is awaiting shareholder approval in Spring 2026.

💬 Expressions 1-2

"The writing is on the wall" Neutral / Informal
👆 Click to reveal
Meaning: It's obvious what's going to happen (usually something negative or unavoidable). The signs are clear for anyone paying attention.
📰 From the story:

"For traditional movie theaters, the writing has been on the wall for years. Streaming was always going to dominate—this merger just accelerates the inevitable."

💡 General example:

"The writing was on the wall for months before the company announced layoffs. Sales were down, offices were closing, everyone knew it was coming."

🎯 NOW YOU TRY:
Think of a situation where you (or someone you know) ignored obvious warning signs. What happened? When did you realize the writing was on the wall?
"A double-edged sword" Neutral
👆 Click to reveal
Meaning: Something that has both advantages and disadvantages; a situation where the positive aspects come with negative consequences too.
📰 From the story:

"For consumers, this merger is a double-edged sword. Yes, you'll have HBO, DC, and Harry Potter all on Netflix—but if competition disappears, subscription prices will inevitably rise."

💡 General example:

"Working from home is a double-edged sword. The flexibility is amazing, but it's harder to separate work and personal life."

🎯 NOW YOU TRY:
What's something in your life or work that's a double-edged sword? Technology? Social media? A skill you have?
💡 Quick Review:

1. The writing is on the wall = It's obvious what will happen
2. A double-edged sword = Has good and bad sides

💬 Expressions 3-5

"Call the shots" Informal
👆 Click to reveal
Meaning: To be in charge; to make the important decisions; to have control over what happens.
📰 From the story:

"With HBO, DC, Warner Bros Studios, AND Netflix's existing library, one company will now call the shots for roughly 40% of the streaming market. That's an enormous amount of power."

💡 General example:

"Since the promotion, she's really been calling the shots on all major projects. Nothing happens without her approval."

🎯 NOW YOU TRY:
In your workplace or family, who calls the shots? Is it always the same person, or does it depend on the situation?
"Go head-to-head (with)" Neutral
👆 Click to reveal
Meaning: To compete directly against someone or something; to face opposition in a direct confrontation.
📰 From the story:

"Netflix and Paramount went head-to-head in an intense bidding war, with Larry Ellison personally backing Paramount's $40 billion hostile takeover attempt."

💡 General example:

"Our startup is now going head-to-head with Amazon in the delivery space. It's terrifying but exciting."

🎯 NOW YOU TRY:
Have you ever gone head-to-head with someone—in business, sports, or even an argument? What happened?
"A tough pill to swallow" Informal
👆 Click to reveal
Meaning: Something difficult or unpleasant to accept, especially a fact or situation that you don't want to be true.
📰 From the story:

"For cinema purists, this is a tough pill to swallow. Warner Bros—the studio behind Casablanca, The Dark Knight, and Harry Potter—will now be owned by a streaming company that releases movies straight to TV."

💡 General example:

"Finding out I didn't get the job after three interviews was a tough pill to swallow, but I eventually got something better."

🎯 NOW YOU TRY:
What's been a tough pill to swallow in your life recently? How did you eventually accept it?
💡 All 5 Expressions:

1. The writing is on the wall = It's obvious what will happen
2. A double-edged sword = Has good and bad sides
3. Call the shots = Be in control, make decisions
4. Go head-to-head (with) = Compete directly against
5. A tough pill to swallow = Difficult to accept
