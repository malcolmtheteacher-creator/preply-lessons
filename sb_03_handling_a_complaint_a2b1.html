Handling a Complaint

The first week is finished — and the customer is not happy. Time to listen, say sorry once, find out what went wrong, and put it right. A good answer can keep the customer for years.

Part 3 of a set. ← Part 2: Giving a Quote (the price)

The Scene 🎬

You run a small cleaning business. The customer agreed your price, and you cleaned their house for the first time on Thursday. On Friday morning your phone rings. It is the customer — and they do not sound happy.

Nobody likes a complaint. Your heart goes fast, you want to explain, you want to say "but I did my best!" Many people argue straight away, or say sorry ten times, or promise too much just to end the call.

You do not need to do any of that. A complaint is a chance. Listen first, say sorry once, ask two or three questions, and offer one clear way to fix the problem. Customers who are treated well after a mistake often stay longer than customers who never had a problem.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Think of a time you were not happy with a shop, a restaurant or a repair. What did you say? What did the person do — and did it help?
  2. When someone is angry with you, what is difficult? Staying calm, listening, or saying sorry?

Who's who

You
The Cleaner 🧽

You own the business and you care about your customers. You stay calm, you listen, and you offer to put things right quickly.

The other person
The Customer 🏠

They liked you at first, but this week something went wrong. They are not shouting — they are disappointed, and they want to hear a clear plan.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Watch what the cleaner does before they offer a solution. They do not explain, they do not argue — they listen, check, and then act. Watch how, not just what.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the one "sorry", and for the questions.

CustomerHello, it's Sam. Do you have a minute? I'm a bit unhappy about yesterday.
YouOf course, Sam. I'm sorry to hear that. Please tell me what happened.
CustomerWell, the downstairs bathroom was perfect. But nobody cleaned the upstairs bathroom at all.
YouI see. So the upstairs bathroom wasn't cleaned. Is that the only problem?
CustomerNo. And there's a glass in the kitchen — it's broken. It was on the table yesterday morning.
YouThank you for telling me. Could you tell me a bit more about the glass? Which glass was it?
CustomerA big wine glass, one of a set of six. I don't know what happened. I just found it in the bin.
YouI understand. I'm really sorry about that. I'll check with my assistant today, and I'll call you back before lunch.
CustomerOK. But what about the bathroom? I'm paying for three hours.
YouYou're right. That's my mistake. I can come back this afternoon and clean it, and there's no charge for that. Would that be OK?
CustomerThis afternoon is fine. I'll be home after two.
YouPerfect. And for the glass — I'll pay for a new one. Could you send me a photo of the set?
CustomerOK, thank you. I'm glad you didn't argue. I was ready for an argument!
YouI want you to be happy. So, just to confirm — I'll clean the bathroom this afternoon at two, I'll pay for the glass, and I'll call you before lunch. Is that right?
CustomerYes, that's right. Thanks.
YouThank you for calling me, Sam. It helps me to be better. Speak to you soon.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases for a complaint. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say sorry once — and mean it. "I'm sorry about that." Then ask a question. One clear sorry sounds professional. Five sorries sound frightened, and the customer starts to worry. After "sorry", move to "what happened?" and then to "here is what I'll do".

👂 Listening first

Phrase 1

The customer says "I'm unhappy about yesterday." You want to show you are listening, and invite them to explain. Complete it: "I'm sorry to ___ that. Please tell me ___ happened."

"I'm sorry to hear that. Please tell me what happened."
You do not explain yet. You do not say "but". You open the door and let the customer talk. Many complaints get smaller when the customer feels heard.
Phrase 2

The customer says the upstairs bathroom was not cleaned. You want to check you understood — without arguing. Which one is best?

A. "That's not possible. I'm sure I cleaned it."
B. "I see. So the upstairs bathroom wasn't cleaned. Is that the only problem?"
C. "Mmm. OK."
B — "I see. So the upstairs bathroom wasn't cleaned. Is that the only problem?"
You say the problem back, and then ask if there is more. That way there are no surprises later. A starts a fight, and C does not show you are listening.

🙏 Saying sorry

Phrase 3

You made a mistake and you know it. You want to say it simply, without excuses. Put the words in order:

my mistake.right.That'sYou're
"You're right. That's my mistake."
Short and honest. Do not add a long reason ("I was very busy, my car broke down…"). The customer does not need a reason — they need a plan.
Phrase 4

The customer says a glass is broken, but you are not sure what happened. You do not want to say "it wasn't me" and you do not want to promise something you don't know. Which one is best?

A. "I'll check with my assistant today, and I'll call you back before lunch."
B. "We never break things."
C. "It must have been the dog."
A — "I'll check with my assistant today, and I'll call you back before lunch."
You do not know yet, so you say what you WILL do, and WHEN. A time makes the promise real. B and C blame the customer or argue — a quick way to lose them.

🔧 Putting it right

Phrase 5

You want to offer to clean the bathroom again, and not charge for it. Complete it: "I can come ___ this afternoon and clean it, and there's no ___ for that."

"I can come back this afternoon and clean it, and there's no charge for that."
"Come back" = return. "No charge" = free. A clear offer with a time. Other examples: "I can fix it tomorrow", "I can send you a refund."
Phrase 6

You broke something and you want to pay for it. Say it out loud first, then check.

"I'll pay for a new one. Could you send me a photo?"
"I'll pay for…" is simple and generous. Then ask for what you need — a photo, a price, a name of the shop. Do not make the customer do more work.

✅ Checking the customer is happy

Phrase 7

You have agreed a plan. Before you finish, say all the steps back so nothing is forgotten. Complete it: "So, just to ___ — I'll clean the bathroom at two, and I'll pay for the glass."

"So, just to confirm — I'll clean the bathroom at two, and I'll pay for the glass."
The same phrase you used in Part 2. Say what you will do, and when. Then do it — a promise kept is the best answer to a complaint.
Phrase 8

The call is ending. You want to finish kindly and show you learned something. Which one?

A. "Thank you for calling me. It helps me to be better."
B. "OK, bye. I hope it doesn't happen again."
C. "Please don't tell your friends."
A — "Thank you for calling me. It helps me to be better."
You say thank you, because a customer who complains is giving you free advice. B sounds as if it is the customer's fault, and C sounds a little scary!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work for any complaint — a restaurant, a repair, a delivery. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"I'm sorry to hear that. Please tell me ___." — What other question can you ask to find out more?

"I can ___, and there's no charge." — What can you offer to put things right?

"I'll ___ before ___." — What is a promise with a time that you can keep?

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole answer out loud — "I'm sorry to hear that" → "please tell me what happened" → check the problem → "you're right, that's my mistake" → the plan with a time → "just to confirm" → thank them. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your complaint. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them.

Round 1 — you are the cleaner 🧽

A customer phones after the first visit to a small flat. Listen, say sorry once, and stay calm.

CustomerHello. I'm not very happy about yesterday. Do you have a minute?
CustomerThe kitchen floor is still dirty — there are marks everywhere. And you arrived forty minutes late.
CustomerNo, that's all. But I had to wait at home, and I missed a meeting.
CustomerSo, what are you going to do about it?
CustomerAnd next time? Will you be on time?
CustomerOK. That sounds fair.

Round 2 — now YOU are the customer 🏠

Change places. A cleaner has just looked after your office and something is wrong. Be polite — but be clear about what you want.

CleanerHello, thanks for calling. How can I help?
CleanerI'm sorry to hear that. Could you tell me a bit more about it?
CleanerI see. So the problem is… Is there anything else?
CleanerYou're right. That's my mistake. I can come back tomorrow morning, and there's no charge.
CleanerSo, just to confirm — tomorrow at nine, no charge. Is that right?
The Twist

😠 "I want my money back"

One more time — no help this round. You are the cleaner again. The customer says: "Honestly, I don't want you to come back. I just want my money back." Do not argue and do not beg. Say sorry once, ask what went wrong, and offer something fair — a refund for the visit, or a free visit with a different plan. Speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said sorry once, and I did not argue.
  • I asked questions and said the problem back.
  • I said "You're right. That's my mistake." when it was my mistake.
  • I offered a clear solution with a day and a time.
  • I confirmed the plan with "just to confirm" and said thank you.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Bigger Picture 🔭

Here is a surprising thing about complaints. Researchers who study customers often find that a person whose problem is solved quickly and kindly trusts the business MORE than a person who never had a problem at all.

Why? Because a smooth week tells the customer nothing about you. A mistake, handled well, shows exactly what kind of person you are. It is the real test.

Most customers who complain just want three things: to be listened to, to hear "sorry" once, and to know what happens next. You can give all three in two minutes.
