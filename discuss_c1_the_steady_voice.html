Two more worth having

overstate: make something sound bigger or more certain than it is. “It would be wrong to overstate what we know.”
accountable: answerable for the outcome, not only the effort. “Leaders are accountable for what their teams do.”

All the words in one place
candid (adj) — honest and direct in a way that builds trust. Example: "She was candid about what had gone wrong."
blunt (adj) — honest with no care for how it lands. Example: "His blunt reply ended the conversation."
reassure (v) — give someone real grounds to feel calmer. Example: "The figures reassured the board."
placate (v) — calm someone down without dealing with the cause. Example: "A free month won't placate angry clients."
complacent (adj) — too comfortable to notice a risk. Example: "Years of easy growth made the team complacent."
robust (adj) — strong from the start, built to take pressure. Example: "A robust plan survives a bad quarter."
resilient (adj) — able to recover after being hit. Example: "The firm proved resilient after losing its biggest client."
credibility (n) — whether people believe what you say. Example: "One inflated forecast can cost you years of credibility."
fallout (n) — the damaging consequences that spread after an event. Example: "The fallout from the announcement lasted months."
overstate (v) — make something sound bigger or more certain than it is. Example: "It would be wrong to overstate what we know."
accountable (adj) — answerable for the outcome, not only the effort. Example: "Leaders are accountable for what their teams do."
