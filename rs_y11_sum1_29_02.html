Worship, Prayer & Baptism

Liturgical vs Non-Liturgical Worship

Liturgical: Formal, structured, traditional. Set prayers, readings, hymns. Follows calendar (Advent, Lent, Easter). Anglican, Catholic, Orthodox. Emphasizes heritage and reverence.

Non-Liturgical: Contemporary, flexible, less formal. Modern songs, spontaneous prayer, dynamic preaching. Evangelical, Pentecostal. Emphasizes personal encounter.

Informal: Private, personal prayer and devotion. No set structure. Meditation, journaling, silent worship.

Prayer: Communication with God

Types: Adoration (praise), Confession (repentance), Thanksgiving, Supplication (asking).

The Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9-13): Model prayer covering worship, provision, forgiveness, protection. Recited weekly in most traditions.

Purpose: Relationship, surrender, intercession, meditation. Central spiritual discipline.

Baptism: Entry Sacrament

Infant Baptism (Paedobaptism): Child baptized; parents promise Christian upbringing. Washes away original sin. Catholic, Anglican, Orthodox practice.

Believer's Baptism (Credobaptism): Adult chooses baptism after personal conversion. Full immersion or sprinkling. Baptist, Pentecostal practice.

Symbolism: Washing (cleansing), burial/resurrection (dying to old self, new life), covenant (commitment).

Eucharist, Pilgrimage & Celebration

The Eucharist (Communion/Mass)

Practice: Bread and wine taken in memory of Last Supper. Repeats Jesus' command "Do this in remembrance of me."

Theological views:

  • Transubstantiation: Bread/wine literally become Christ's body/blood (Catholic).
  • Consubstantiation: Christ present with bread/wine, not replacing them (Lutheran).
  • Memorial: Symbolic remembrance, not literal transformation (many Protestant).

Frequency: Weekly in most churches; daily in Catholic tradition.

Pilgrimage: Lourdes, France

Marian shrine; millions visit annually. Seek healing, especially through water. Miracle cures claimed. Represents devotion and faith hope.

Pilgrimage: Iona, Scotland

Historic monastery island. Celtic spirituality. Pilgrims seek spiritual renewal, connection to Christian heritage.

Major Christian Celebrations

Christmas (December 25): Birth of Jesus. Incarnation theology. Nativity scenes, carols, family gathering.

Easter (Moveable): Resurrection of Jesus. Lent (40 days fasting/repentance) precedes. Good Friday (crucifixion) to Easter Sunday (resurrection). Most significant festival.

Pentecost (50 days after Easter): Holy Spirit's descent on disciples. Birth of Church. Speaking in tongues, empowerment.

Advent (4 weeks before Christmas): Preparation for Christmas. Emphasis on Jesus' coming. Hope and anticipation.

Church Community & Worldwide Witness

Local Church Ministry

Food banks: Redistribute surplus food, feed hungry. Acts 2:45 principle—meeting material needs.

Street pastors: Night-time pastoral presence in town centers. Prayer, listening, practical help. Breaking cycles of addiction, violence.

Other roles: Youth work, counselling, job training, housing support, hospice care. Gospel lived through action.

Persecution & Witness

Modern persecution: Occurs in China, Iran, Pakistan, Nigeria. Imprisonment, torture, death. Christians facing discrimination and violence.

Martyrdom theology: Suffering for faith honored. "Martyr" means witness. Death does not end testimony; it amplifies it.

Example: North Korea—estimated 50,000-70,000 Christians in prison camps. Underground churches. Demonstrates faith cost.

Summary: Christian Practices

Five Essential Practices

1. Worship: Liturgical and non-liturgical; gathered and personal.

2. Prayer: Adoration, confession, thanksgiving, supplication; central spiritual discipline.

3. Sacraments: Baptism (entry), Eucharist (ongoing). Visible signs of invisible grace.

4. Pilgrimage: Sacred journeys to holy places; seeking spiritual renewal.

5. Community: Worship together, serve others, witness to faith, endure persecution.

Guidance
