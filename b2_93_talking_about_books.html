Talking About Books

B2 Level · Speaking & Vocabulary for Discussing Literature

First Thoughts

Take your time with each question. Share as much as you like — the more detail, the better.

1. How often do you read books? When and where do you usually read?

2. What was the last book you finished — or the last one you started but did not finish? Why?

3. Do you prefer paper books, e-books, or audiobooks? What is good and bad about each one?

4. Has a book ever changed the way you think about something? Tell the story.

Quick warm-up choice:

  • If you could only read one book for the rest of your life, what would it be — and what would you miss about reading other books?
  • If a famous author wrote a book about your life, who would you choose as the author, and why?

Book Talk Vocabulary

Click each card to reveal the meaning. Then say the word out loud and try to use it in your own example.

protagonist The main character in a story — the person we follow.
plot twist A sudden, surprising change in the story you did not expect.
gripping So exciting that you cannot stop reading.
far-fetched Hard to believe; unrealistic. (Often used as a polite criticism.)
relatable A character or situation that feels like your own life or feelings.
to foreshadow To give a small hint early in the story about what will happen later.
dystopian Set in a frightening, unjust imagined future or society.
a page-turner A book you cannot put down — you keep turning the pages.

Now you try — speak in full sentences:

  • Choose three words from the cards. Use each one to talk about a book, film, or TV series you know well.
  • Which word from today is the most useful for you? Which one is hardest to remember? Why?
  • Can you think of a book or film with a famous plot twist? Do not say the twist — just describe how it made you feel.

Quick check: which word fits?

"The film was good, but the ending was a bit ____. There is no way a normal person could escape from prison like that."

far-fetched — meaning the story was too unrealistic to believe.

"I started it on Sunday morning and finished it the same night. It was a real ____."

page-turner — a book so good you cannot put it down.

Famous Books

Here are six well-known books from around the world. For each one, tell me: have you read it, seen the film, or heard about it? What do you think?

1. Harry Potter and the Philosopher’s Stone

J.K. Rowling · Fantasy · UK, 1997

An ordinary boy living under his cruel aunt and uncle’s staircase discovers, on his eleventh birthday, that he is a wizard — and that his parents’ deaths were not an accident.

  • Have you read any of the Harry Potter books, or only watched the films? Which do you prefer, and why?
  • Why do you think this story became so popular in so many different countries?

2. 1984

George Orwell · Dystopian fiction · UK, 1949

In a future London controlled by a single, all-watching political party, a quiet office worker begins to write down his secret thoughts — a small, dangerous act of rebellion.

  • Why do you think people still talk about this book today, almost eighty years later?
  • Do you think any part of life today feels a little dystopian? In what way?

3. The Great Gatsby

F. Scott Fitzgerald · Modern classic · USA, 1925

A mysterious, fabulously rich young man throws huge parties every weekend on Long Island, hoping that one night the woman he has loved for years will walk through the door.

  • Do you think someone can really change their whole life to win back a person from their past? Is it romantic, or is it sad?
  • Is the protagonist a hero, a fool, or something in between?

4. The Little Prince

Antoine de Saint-Exupéry · Philosophical fable · France, 1943

A pilot crashes his plane in the Sahara desert. Alone and trying to fix his engine, he meets a small, serious boy who claims to come from a tiny planet far away.

  • Many people read this book first as a child and again as an adult. Have you? Do you think the meaning changes?
  • What is one lesson from your own childhood that adults often forget?

5. The Kite Runner

Khaled Hosseini · Contemporary fiction · Afghanistan / USA, 2003

Two boys grow up like brothers in 1970s Kabul — until one cold winter day when one of them does nothing to help the other, and a friendship is broken forever.

  • Do you think it is ever possible to truly forgive someone who betrayed you as a child?
  • Some books take you to a country you have never visited. Has a book ever done that for you?

6. Murder on the Orient Express

Agatha Christie · Crime / Mystery · UK, 1934

A luxury train is stuck in deep snow somewhere in Europe. In the morning, a passenger is found dead in his cabin — and the killer is still on board.

  • Do you enjoy crime stories or mysteries? What makes a good one for you — the puzzle, the characters, or the atmosphere?
  • Some readers love a big plot twist at the end; others find it annoying. Which type are you?

Big-picture questions:

  • Of these six books, which one would you most like to read — or re-read — and why?
  • Which one would you definitely not choose? Be honest!
  • Are there any famous books from your country that you think the rest of the world should read?

Your Book Review

Now it is your turn to talk about a book you know well. Pick any book — old favourite, recent read, or even a book you disliked.

Your two-minute review should include:

Useful sentence stems

  • "The book I want to talk about is…"
  • "It is a [genre] novel set in…"
  • "The protagonist is a… who…"
  • "What I really loved about it was…"
  • "One thing I found a bit far-fetched / slow / confusing was…"
  • "I would definitely recommend it to anyone who…"
  • "Honestly, I would not recommend it to people who…"

Use the timer below. Make notes — do not write full sentences.

Planning time: 5:00

Step 2 · Deliver your review

When you are ready, present your review out loud. Aim for around two minutes — like a real book recommendation to a friend.

Follow-up questions to be ready for:

  • Was there a moment in the book that really surprised you?
  • If they made a film of this book, who should play the protagonist?
  • How did the book make you feel by the end?
  • Would you read another book by the same author? Why or why not?

Recommendations & Big Questions

Last part of the lesson — and the most fun. Recommend, argue, and reflect.

1. Recommend a book to me

Imagine I have one long flight ahead of me — about ten hours. Which book should I download, and why? Try to convince me!

2. The screen problem

Many people say they read less now because of phones, social media, and streaming. Is this true for you? What helps you actually read — or stop reading?

3. School memories

What books were you forced to read at school? Did you enjoy any of them at the time? Have your feelings changed now you are older?

4. Books vs. films

"The book is always better than the film." Do you agree? Can you think of a film that was actually better than the book?

5. One sentence challenge

Describe your all-time favourite book in one sentence — without saying the title. Make me want to read it.
