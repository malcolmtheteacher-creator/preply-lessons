Hadrian's Wall

A Roman emperor built a huge wall across Britain — and soldiers from all over the world lived on it.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The emperor builds a wall

Nearly 2,000 years ago, the Romans ruled a very big empire. Their army came to Britain and took the south. But the north was cold and wild. The people there — the Romans called them the Caledonians — fought hard, and they were never beaten. They came over the border again and again to steal animals and to fight.

In the year 122, the Roman emperor Hadrian came to Britain. He was tired of the fighting in the north. So he gave a simple order: "Build a wall." He wanted a clear line between the Romans and the people in the north.

The wall was enormous. It went across the whole top of Britain, from the sea in the east to the sea in the west — about 73 miles. It was very thick and taller than a person. There was a deep ditch in front of it, and a road behind it. Along the wall there were small forts and tall towers. Soldiers stood on top of the wall and watched the land in the north.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did Hadrian want to build a wall?

2. Where did the wall go, from one side to the other?

1. He was tired of fighting in the cold, wild north. He wanted a clear line between the Romans and the people there.

2. Across the whole top of Britain, from the sea in the east to the sea in the west — about 73 miles.

Talk about it:
  • Hadrian said, "Stop here. Build a wall." Do you think this was a good idea, or should the Romans have kept fighting? Why?

Part 2 — The soldiers on the wall

Here is a surprise. The soldiers who lived on the wall were not from Rome. They came from many different lands in the empire. Some came from Syria, some from Spain, some from near the big rivers of Europe. About 15,000 soldiers lived along the wall. After 25 years in the army, they could become Roman citizens.

Life on the wall was not only fighting. Behind the wall there were forts with warm baths, shops and small towns. Families lived there too. At one fort, called Vindolanda, people found very old letters in the ground. One letter is about a present from home: warm socks and underpants, because Britain was so cold! Another letter is from a woman. She asks her friend to come to her birthday party. It is one of the oldest letters written by a woman in the whole Roman world.

But there was danger too. The people in the north did not go away. Sometimes they came over the wall to fight and to steal. The soldiers went out through the gates to watch the land. For a soldier from a warm country, this cold, rainy place, far from home, was very hard.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did the soldiers on the wall come from?

2. What did people find at Vindolanda, and what do the letters tell us?

1. Not from Rome, but from many lands in the empire — Syria, Spain, and near the big rivers of Europe. About 15,000 of them.

2. Very old letters. They tell us about normal life: a present of warm socks and underpants, and a woman's invitation to a birthday party.

Talk about it:
  • The letters show us that these people had normal lives — parties, presents, families. Why is it special to read a letter from 2,000 years ago?

Part 3 — The wall today

The wall was a busy border for about 300 years. Soldiers lived on it, fixed it, and guarded it. But an empire cannot last forever. Rome became weaker. It had problems everywhere. So, about 1,600 years ago, the Roman army left Britain. The soldiers went away. No one guarded the wall anymore.

For hundreds of years, people took the stones from the wall. They used them to build farms, houses and churches. In many places, the great wall became just a low line of stones in the grass. But it did not disappear.

Today, Hadrian's Wall is famous. People come from all over the world to see it. Many people walk next to it, all the way from one sea to the other. When you stand on the old wall, you can think about the soldiers who lived here, so far from home, nearly 2,000 years ago.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did the Roman army leave the wall?

2. What happened to the stones of the wall after the Romans left?

1. Rome became weaker and had problems everywhere, so about 1,600 years ago the army left Britain. No one guarded the wall.

2. For hundreds of years, people took the stones to build farms, houses and churches. In many places only a low line of stones is left.

Talk about it:
  • Today, many people walk along the whole wall for fun. Why do you think people like to visit very old places?

Grammar: prepositions of place

To tell this story, we need to say where things are. Small words called prepositions do this job. Here are the important ones.

in / on / at
• in = inside something: "The soldiers lived in a fort." "Hadrian's Wall is in Britain."
• on = on top of a surface: "The guards stood on the wall."
• at = at a point or place: "They stopped people at the gate."
next to / behind / under
• next to = very near, at the side of: "The road was next to the wall."
• behind = at the back of: "The forts were behind the wall."
• under = below something: "The old letters were under the ground."
