Making Your Opinion Heard

B2 English Lesson | Expressing Feelings & Multi-word Verbs

How Do You Feel About Work?

Think about your work or studies. What do you enjoy and what do you find challenging?

Consider these emotions and situations:

Relaxed Stressed Laughing Annoyed Depressed Scared Stuck

When do you feel each of these ways?

Relaxed: When I complete a task successfully or have a break
Stressed: When there's a tight deadline or many tasks at once
Laughing: When colleagues share funny moments or jokes
Annoyed: When communication is unclear or interruptions happen
Depressed: When I don't see progress or feel undervalued
Scared: When facing something new or a difficult challenge
Stuck: When I'm repeating the same tasks without growth

Useful Phrases for Expressing How You Feel

I really enjoy... Working with my team / Learning new skills
I love... The sense of achievement / Being creative
I hate... Unnecessary meetings / Lack of recognition
It can be annoying to... Deal with unclear instructions / Wait for responses
My favourite thing about... is... My job is flexibility / The company culture
What I dislike is... Long working hours / Repetitive tasks
The only bad thing about... is... My commute is long / The pay isn't great

Multi-word Verbs: Getting Through Challenges

These verbs help describe how we handle difficult situations:

Multi-word Verbs Explained ▼

Scrape through Manage to succeed or survive with difficulty; barely pass
Get through Successfully complete or endure something difficult
Sail through Complete something easily and successfully
Go through Experience or endure something challenging
See through to the bitter end Continue with something until the very end, despite difficulties

Discussion Questions

Have you ever managed to scrape through something?

What was it and how did you feel?

Example: "I scraped through my final exams. I hadn't studied well, but I managed to pass with just enough marks."

What was the most difficult thing you had to get through?

What helped you succeed?

Example: "Getting through my job training was hard. The material was complex, but having supportive colleagues made it easier."

Have you ever sailed smoothly through something?

What made it so easy?

Example: "I sailed through my driving test because I had practiced well and felt confident."

What are some difficult situations people have to go through in life?

Difficult situations include: job loss, relationship problems, health issues, career changes, moving to a new place, learning a new skill, financial difficulties.

Is there anything you saw through till the end?

How did you feel when you finished?

Example: "I saw my university degree through to the bitter end. It was challenging, but I felt proud when I graduated."

More Useful Idioms & Expressions

These phrases help you express challenges and emotions more naturally:

Idioms for Challenges & Support ▼

Have difficulty getting to grips with something Struggle to understand or master something
Going round and round in circles Not making progress; repeating the same problems without finding a solution
Come together / Be supportive People unite and support each other during difficult times

Discussion Questions

Is there anything you have difficulty getting to grips with?

What are you struggling with?

Example: "I have difficulty getting to grips with new software at work. It takes me longer than my colleagues to master new tools."

Have you ever felt you were going round and round in circles?

What was the situation?

Example: "At work, we keep having the same meetings about the same problems without finding solutions. It feels like going round and round in circles."

Do you remember a time when people came together and were supportive of each other?

What happened?

Example: "When my colleague faced a personal crisis, the whole team came together and supported them by sharing workload and offering help. It was beautiful to see."

Expressing Your Emotions in Different Situations

In life, we experience different emotions depending on the situation. Let's explore how to talk about these feelings naturally:

Common Emotional Situations

Finishing an important exam

Relief, anxiety, confidence, exhaustion: "I felt relieved when the exam finished. Now I'm anxious waiting for results. If I prepared well, I feel confident. If not, I feel exhausted and worried."

Working a 12-hour shift

Stressed, depressed, proud, accomplished: "After a 12-hour shift, I feel physically and mentally stressed out. By the end, I feel depressed about the long hours, but also proud of my accomplishment. That sense of achievement helps."

A difficult appraisal with your boss

Scared, defensive, enlightened, motivated: "I was scared going into the appraisal, worried about criticism. But my boss was fair and constructive, so I felt enlightened about my strengths and areas to develop. It motivated me to improve."

Getting fired from a job

Shocked, depressed, angry, determined: "Being fired was a shock. I felt depressed and angry at first. But after some reflection, I felt determined to find a better opportunity and use this as a learning experience."

Only just failing a test

Frustrated, depressed, motivated to try again: "Missing a pass by such a small margin was frustrating. It depressed me, but it also motivated me. I could see I was close to success, so I prepared harder for the next attempt."

Deep Discussion: Your Experiences

These questions invite you to reflect on and discuss your real experiences:

What challenges are you currently facing in your work or studies?

How are you trying to solve them?

Think about: pressures you're experiencing, people who help or hinder you, your own coping strategies, and what support you might need. Share your honest perspective.

When was the last time you felt stressed out?

What caused it and how did you manage it?

Consider: the trigger, physical symptoms you felt, actions you took to reduce stress, whether you talked to anyone about it, and what you learned.

How do you react when you laugh out loud at work?

Is laughter something you experience often?

Reflect on: whether your workplace encourages humour, the role of laughter in team bonding, whether you feel comfortable being your authentic self, and how laughter affects productivity and wellbeing.

Tell us about a time you felt annoyed at work or school.

What made you feel that way?

Think about: unclear communication, unfair treatment, interrupted work, unreasonable deadlines, or lack of recognition. How did you express your annoyance? Did you address it?

What gives you a sense of feeling stuck in a rut?

How do you break free from it?

Consider: repetitive tasks, lack of growth, absence of new challenges, or feeling undervalued. Strategies to escape include: learning new skills, changing roles, asking for more responsibility, or seeking new opportunities.

  • It can be annoying to...

  • The only bad thing about is...

3-2-1 Reflection

3 things: Write down 3 multi-word verbs or emotion phrases you want to use more often.
You might choose: "get through," "sail through," "have difficulty getting to grips with," or "come together." Pick the ones most relevant to your life.
2 things: What are 2 challenging situations that are relevant to your life right now?
Situations might be: learning something new, working on a project deadline, managing difficult relationships, or balancing multiple responsibilities.
1 thing: What is one thing you want to improve about how you express your opinions or feelings?
You might want to: speak up more confidently, express disagreement respectfully, share feelings more openly, use more varied vocabulary, or communicate your needs clearly.
