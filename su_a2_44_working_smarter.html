Let's Start: Your Work Habits

Think about how you work and study. Answer these questions:

  • Are you a morning person or a night owl?
  • What times of day are you most productive?
  • How do you handle difficult tasks — do you do them first or last?
  • What distracts you most when you're working or studying?

What Your Answers Tell You

Structures for Giving Advice

"You should take regular breaks." (strong recommendation)

"You could try the Pomodoro technique." (suggestion)

"You need to prioritise your tasks." (necessity)

"You shouldn't check your phone every 5 minutes." (negative recommendation)

"Why don't you make a to-do list?" (friendly suggestion)

"Have you tried working in shorter bursts?" (gentle suggestion)

Delegation: Giving Tasks to Others

Delegation means giving tasks to other people. But what should you delegate?

Click each task to reveal if you should delegate it:

Writing an important report for your boss
No — This needs your personal input and your boss trusts you to do it.
Organising files and folders
Yes — Someone else can do this routine work.
Making phone calls to schedule meetings
Yes — This is a routine task that someone else can handle.
Preparing a presentation you'll deliver
No — You need to know the content and understand your own message.
Replying to routine emails
Yes — If someone knows the answers, they can reply.
Making a key decision about a project
No — You need to be responsible for important decisions.
