🎯 Techniques & Effects

IB English Analysis Toolkit

What IB Examiners Actually Want

Here's what separates a 5 from a 7 in IB English analysis:

❌ Band 5 Response

"The author uses a metaphor. There is also imagery and symbolism in the text."

→ Just naming techniques

✓ Band 7 Response

"The metaphor of darkness as a 'blanket' creates a sense of suffocation, suggesting the character's guilt is inescapable."

→ Explaining EFFECT on meaning

🔑 The Secret: Examiners don't care how many techniques you can spot. They care whether you can explain how those techniques create meaning.

The Three Levels of Analysis

Level What You Write Band
😐 Identification "There is a metaphor" 3-4
🙂 Description "The metaphor compares X to Y" 5
🤩 Analysis "The metaphor creates X effect, suggesting Y about the character/theme" 6-7

Imagery & Comparison

Metaphor

▼

What: Direct comparison (A is B)

✨ Effect: Creates vivid imagery; adds layers of meaning; makes abstract ideas concrete

"Life is a journey" — suggests progression, obstacles, destinations

Simile

▼

What: Comparison using "like" or "as"

✨ Effect: Helps readers visualize; creates connections between unlike things

"Her smile was like sunshine" — associates warmth and positivity

Personification

▼

What: Giving human qualities to non-human things

✨ Effect: Creates emotional connection; makes ideas relatable; can create threat or comfort

"The wind whispered secrets" — creates intimacy with nature

Symbolism

▼

What: Object/image representing something bigger

✨ Effect: Adds depth; unifies themes; rewards attentive readers

The green light in Gatsby — represents hope, the American Dream

🔊

Sound & Rhythm

Alliteration

▼

What: Repeated consonant sounds at start of words

✨ Effect: Creates rhythm; emphasizes words; makes phrases memorable

"Peter Piper picked" — playful, catchy, draws attention

Repetition

▼

What: Repeating words or phrases

✨ Effect: Creates emphasis; builds rhythm; reinforces key ideas

"Never, never, never give up" — intensity through repetition

⚡

Emphasis & Contrast

Juxtaposition

▼

What: Placing contrasting ideas side by side

✨ Effect: Highlights differences; creates tension; emphasizes contrast

"It was the best of times, it was the worst of times"

Hyperbole

▼

What: Deliberate exaggeration

✨ Effect: Creates humor; emphasizes emotion; makes a point dramatically

"I've told you a million times" — frustration through overstatement

Oxymoron

▼

What: Contradictory terms together

✨ Effect: Creates paradox; highlights complexity; provokes thought

"Deafening silence" — emphasizes the weight of quiet

🎭

Irony

Verbal Irony

▼

What: Saying the opposite of what you mean

✨ Effect: Creates humor; reveals character attitude; engages reader to decode

"What lovely weather" (during a storm)

Dramatic Irony

▼

What: Audience knows more than character

✨ Effect: Creates suspense; engages reader; generates tension or humor

Romeo thinks Juliet is dead (we know she's not)

Situational Irony

▼

What: Outcome opposite to what's expected

✨ Effect: Creates surprise; highlights life's unpredictability; often darkly comic

A fire station burns down

Structural Techniques

How the text is BUILT — not just what it says, but how it's arranged.

📖

Narrative Techniques

First Person Narration

▼

What: "I" narrator tells the story

✨ Effect: Creates intimacy; subjective view; reader sees through one lens

May be unreliable — we only get their version

Third Person Omniscient

▼

What: All-knowing narrator

✨ Effect: God-like perspective; can create dramatic irony; objective tone

Can show what multiple characters think/feel

Unreliable Narrator

▼

What: Narrator whose account we can't fully trust

✨ Effect: Creates mystery; forces reader to question; reveals character flaws

Often due to bias, limited knowledge, or mental state

⏱️

Time & Sequence

Flashback

▼

What: Jump to past events

✨ Effect: Provides backstory; explains motivation; creates emotional depth

Reveals why a character behaves a certain way

Foreshadowing

▼

What: Hints at future events

✨ Effect: Builds suspense; creates anticipation; rewards re-reading

Dark clouds before a tragedy

In Medias Res

▼

What: Starting in the middle of action

✨ Effect: Hooks reader immediately; creates intrigue; avoids slow setup

Opening with a crisis, explaining later

📝

Sentence Structure

Short Sentences

▼

What: Brief, punchy statements

✨ Effect: Creates tension; speeds pace; shows shock or finality

"He was dead." — blunt impact

Long Sentences

▼

What: Extended, complex syntax

✨ Effect: Slows pace; builds detail; can mimic racing thoughts

Often used for description or stream of consciousness

Sentence Fragments

▼

What: Incomplete sentences

✨ Effect: Creates impact; mimics speech; shows distress or urgency

"Gone. All of it. Just... gone."

🔄

Overall Structure

Circular Structure

▼

What: Ends where it began

✨ Effect: Creates closure; can emphasize change OR tragic stagnation

Same scene, but character has transformed

Parallel Structure

▼

What: Similar patterns repeated

✨ Effect: Creates rhythm; emphasizes comparison; satisfying to read

"She came, she saw, she conquered"

Climax

▼

What: Point of highest tension

✨ Effect: Emotional peak; turning point; maximum reader engagement

The confrontation everything has built toward

The Analysis Formula

Use this pattern every time you write about a technique:

The Effect Formula

"The author uses [TECHNIQUE], which creates [EFFECT], suggesting [DEEPER MEANING]."

Breaking It Down

Part What to Include Example
TECHNIQUE Name it + quote evidence The metaphor "blanket of dark"
EFFECT What it does to the reader creates a sense of suffocation
MEANING Connect to character/theme suggesting guilt is inescapable

Full Example

Shakespeare uses the metaphor "blanket of the dark," which creates a sense of suffocation and concealment, suggesting that Lady Macbeth sees evil as something that must be hidden from moral scrutiny — even from herself.

Power Words for Effect

Replace boring verbs like "shows" with these:

creates evokes establishes reinforces heightens underscores conveys implies suggests emphasizes highlights intensifies

For Reader Response

engages compels invites provokes unsettles draws the reader to forces us to confront
⚠️ Avoid: "This shows..." / "This demonstrates..." / "This is effective because..."

These are weak and overused. Always be specific about WHAT effect and WHY it matters.

Practice: Apply the Formula

Try analyzing these quotes using the Effect Formula.

Practice 1

"The sun smiled down on the village, unaware of the tragedy that had struck."

What technique? What effect? What meaning?

Sample Analysis:

The author uses personification ("the sun smiled") combined with juxtaposition (cheerful sun vs. tragedy), which creates a sense of cruel indifference in nature, suggesting that the universe is oblivious to human suffering — heightening the tragedy through contrast.

Practice 2

"He was gone. Just like that. Gone."

What technique? What effect? What meaning?

Sample Analysis:

The author uses short sentences and repetition of "gone," which creates a sense of abruptness and disbelief, suggesting that the narrator is struggling to process the loss — the fragmented structure mimics a mind in shock.

Practice 3

"It was a bright cold day in April, and the clocks were striking thirteen."

What technique? What effect? What meaning?

Sample Analysis:

Orwell uses juxtaposition ("bright cold") and the impossible detail of clocks striking thirteen, which creates immediate unease and wrongness, suggesting that this world operates on different rules than ours — signaling to readers that reality itself has been distorted.
