Real Travel Stories

The Best Mistake: Hiroshi from Japan

Hiroshi booked a flight to Sydney, Australia - but accidentally landed in Sydney, Nova Scotia, Canada. Instead of sunny beaches, he found a small fishing town in winter. "I was so embarrassed," he says. "But the local people were incredibly kind. A family invited me for dinner, and I ended up staying a week. Now I go back every year. It's become my favorite place in the world."

Traveling Every Country: Lexie Alford

At age 21, Lexie Alford became the youngest person to visit every country in the world - all 196 of them. What surprised her most? "The places I expected to love weren't always special. And the places I knew nothing about - like Pakistan and Iran - had the most welcoming people I've ever met. The news doesn't show you the real story."

The Psychology of Souvenirs: Dr. Sarah Chen

Travel psychologist Dr. Sarah Chen studied why we buy souvenirs. Her research found something surprising: most souvenirs stay in boxes, never looked at again. But photos of ourselves with LOCAL PEOPLE are kept and shared for decades. "We don't remember things," she explains. "We remember connections."

Think About This...

  • What do you remember most from your trips - places, food, or people?
  • Is there a place you've always wanted to visit? Why?
  • Do you think travel changes people? How?

Useful Language for Traveling

These phrases will help you in real travel situations - booking, asking for help, and making requests.

1. "I'd like to..."

Click to learn
Meaning
A polite way to say what you want. More polite than "I want..."
Form
I'd like to + verb
I'd like + noun
Pronunciation
"I'd" = /aɪd/
(sounds like "I would" but fast)

Examples in travel situations:

"I'd like to book a room for two nights, please."
"I'd like a window seat, if possible."
"I'd like to check out now."
"I'd like the chicken, please." (at a restaurant)

NOW YOU TRY!

Imagine you're at a hotel. Use "I'd like to..." to ask for:

1. A room with a view: "I'd like..."
2. A late checkout: "I'd like to..."
3. Extra towels: "I'd like..."

2. "Could you recommend...?"

Click to learn
Meaning
Asking for advice or suggestions from someone who knows more
Form
Could you recommend + noun?
Could you recommend + a good place to + verb?
Register
Polite and friendly. Use with hotel staff, tour guides, locals

Examples:

"Could you recommend a good restaurant nearby?"
"Could you recommend a good place to try local food?"
"Could you recommend any day trips from here?"
"Could you recommend somewhere quieter?"

NOW YOU TRY!

You're talking to a hotel receptionist. Ask for recommendations about:

1. A cafe for breakfast: "Could you recommend...?"
2. Things to do in the evening: "Could you recommend...?"
3. A beach that isn't too crowded: "Could you recommend...?"

3. "What's the best way to get to...?"

Click to learn
Meaning
Asking for directions or transport advice. Better than just "How do I go?"
Form
What's the best way to get to + place?
What's the best way to get to + the + place?
Tip
People usually answer with: "You can take...", "I'd recommend...", "The easiest way is..."

Examples:

"What's the best way to get to the airport from here?"
"What's the best way to get to the old town?"
"What's the best way to get to the beach?"
"What's the best way to get to the train station?"

NOW YOU TRY!

You're a tourist in a new city. Ask how to get to:

1. The main square: "What's the best way to get to...?"
2. A famous museum: "What's the best way to get to...?"
3. The nearest metro station: "What's the best way to get to...?"

Talking About Your Experiences

These phrases help you share your travel stories and talk about places you've visited (or want to visit!).

4. "Have you ever...?" / "I've never..."

Click to learn
Meaning
Talking about experiences in your life (any time until now)
Form
Have you ever + past participle?
I've/I haven't + past participle
I've never + past participle
Note
Use "been to" for visiting places
(NOT "Have you ever gone to...")

Examples:

"Have you ever been to Japan?"
"I've been to Italy three times."
"I've never tried bungee jumping."
"Have you ever missed a flight?"
"I've always wanted to visit New Zealand."

NOW YOU TRY!

Think about YOUR travel experiences. Complete these sentences honestly:

1. "I've been to..." (name 3 countries or cities)
2. "I've never..." (something you haven't done while traveling)
3. "I've always wanted to..." (a dream destination)
4. Now ask your teacher: "Have you ever...?"

5. "It was (totally) worth it"

Click to learn
Meaning
Something was difficult/expensive/hard, BUT the result was good. You're happy you did it.
Form
It was worth it
It was totally/definitely worth it
It wasn't worth it (negative)
Similar phrases
"It was worth every penny" (about money)
"It exceeded my expectations" (better than expected)

Examples:

"We waited two hours for a table, but the food was amazing. It was totally worth it."
"The hike was exhausting, but the view from the top was incredible. Definitely worth it."
"The tour was expensive, but it was worth every penny."
"I woke up at 4am to see the sunrise. It was so worth it."
"Honestly? The famous restaurant wasn't worth it. The food was okay."

NOW YOU TRY!

Think about experiences that were difficult but rewarding. Tell your teacher about:

1. A trip that was expensive but worth it: "I went to... It was expensive, but..."
2. Something that was tiring but worth it: "I remember... It was exhausting, but..."
3. Something that WASN'T worth it: "Once I... To be honest, it wasn't worth it because..."

Quick Summary - 5 Items

1. "I'd like to..." - polite requests

2. "Could you recommend...?" - asking for suggestions

3. "What's the best way to get to...?" - directions

4. "Have you ever...? / I've never..." - experiences

5. "It was worth it" - evaluating experiences
