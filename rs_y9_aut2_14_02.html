Creation Accounts Across Traditions

Every major religion has a teaching about how the world and humanity came to be. These creation stories reflect different values and worldviews. Let's explore four traditions.

Christianity

The Christian creation account comes from the Book of Genesis. There are actually two accounts in Genesis 1-2:

  • Genesis 1: A seven-day creation where God creates the heavens, Earth, plants, animals, and humanity (both male and female together) in God's image.
  • Genesis 2: A more detailed account where God creates Adam first, then creates Eve from Adam's rib.

"In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth." (Genesis 1:1)

Interpretation: Christians interpret Genesis in two main ways. Literalists believe the account is historically accurate and the world is about 6,000 years old. Liberals or progressive Christians see Genesis as poetry or metaphor expressing theological truth (that God created) rather than scientific fact.

Islam

In Islam, Allah is known as Al-Khaliq, "The Creator." The Qur'an has multiple references to creation but no single, sequential account like Genesis.

"Have those who disbelieved not considered that the heavens and the earth were joined together [as one unit of creation], and We separated them?" (Qur'an 21:30)

"And the heaven, We constructed it with might, and indeed, We are [its] expanders." (Qur'an 51:47)

Key points: Allah creates by saying "Be" (Kun). Humans are created with special dignity (Qur'an 95:4). The Qur'an assumes pre-Islamic Arabian listeners already knew the Abraham and Noah stories.

Buddhism

Buddhism does not teach that a divine creator made the universe. Instead, Buddhism sees the universe as cyclical — eternally arising and passing away in cycles.

The Unanswerable Question: The Buddha was asked about the origin of the universe and refused to answer. He called such questions avyakata — unanswerable. Why? Because such questions do not lead to enlightenment or the end of suffering.

Focus: Buddhist teaching emphasizes impermanence, suffering, and the path to liberation. The universe's ultimate origin is less important than how we live within it.

Humanism

Humanists reject the idea of divine creation. Instead, they point to natural explanations for the universe's existence and complexity.

The Scientific View: The Big Bang explains the universe's origin (not a deliberate act, but a natural event). Evolution explains life's diversity without a designer.

Worldview: The universe does not need a purpose or meaning imposed from outside. Humans create their own meaning through reason and ethics.

Essay Question: Critical Thinking

Statement: "Creation stories are outdated myths that science has replaced."

Do you agree? Write 200–300 words. Show both arguments for and against this view, then give your own reasoned position.

Structure your answer:
  • Explain what the statement means
  • Give reasons why someone might agree (the science argument)
  • Give reasons why someone might disagree (what myths provide that science doesn't)
  • State your own position with evidence
Consider: A creation story and scientific explanation can both be true at different levels. Genesis might be true as theology (God is creator) even if not literally true as history.

Check Your Knowledge

Click each question to test what you've learned.

  1. What are the two accounts of creation in Genesis, and how do they differ?
    Genesis 1 gives a seven-day creation account creating humans (male and female) together in God's image. Genesis 2 provides a more detailed narrative where Adam is created first, and Eve is created from his rib.
  2. What does the Arabic term "Al-Khaliq" mean, and in what context is it used?
    "Al-Khaliq" means "The Creator" and is one of the 99 names of Allah in Islam, emphasizing Allah as the sole creator.
  3. What is "avyakata" in Buddhist teaching?
    Avyakata means "unanswerable questions." The Buddha refused to answer metaphysical questions about the universe's origin because he said they don't lead to enlightenment or the end of suffering.
  4. How do Humanists explain the origin of the universe and life?
    Humanists explain the universe through the Big Bang and life through evolution — both natural processes that don't require a divine creator.
  5. What is the difference between a literalist and a liberal interpretation of Genesis?
    A literalist believes Genesis describes historical fact and the world is about 6,000 years old. A liberal Christian sees Genesis as poetry or metaphor expressing theological truth (God created) rather than scientific fact.
