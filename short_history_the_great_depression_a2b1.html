The Great Depression

The years when the world's money disappeared and millions of people lost their jobs.

Want more detail? Read this lesson at B2/C1 level →

Today: tell the story of the Great Depression, and talk about money, hard times and hope. Grammar: 'going to' and 'would' for the past.

Key words — match them first

Before we read, let's learn the important words. Match each word (1–8) to its meaning (A–H). Say your answers out loud — "I think 1 is D" — then check.

Words

  1. a boom
  2. to crash
  3. a bank
  4. unemployment
  5. poverty
  6. a breadline
  7. relief
  8. recovery

Meanings

  • A. getting better again after a bad time
  • B. when many people have no job
  • C. a good time when people make a lot of money
  • D. a place that keeps your money safe
  • E. help (money or food) from the government
  • F. to fall down very fast and lose all its value
  • G. a long queue of poor people waiting for free food
  • H. being very poor; having almost no money

1 = C (a boom) · 2 = F (to crash) · 3 = D (a bank) · 4 = B (unemployment)

5 = H (poverty) · 6 = G (a breadline) · 7 = E (relief) · 8 = A (recovery)

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The boom and the crash

In the 1920s, the United States was rich and happy. There were new cars, new radios, and lots of jobs. People called these years the "Roaring Twenties". Many people put their money into the stock market, where you can buy small parts of companies. The prices went up and up, and people thought they were going to get rich.

But there was a big problem. People borrowed money to buy more and more. They were sure the prices would never go down. They were wrong. In October 1929, the stock market crashed. In just a few days, the prices fell very fast. People lost huge amounts of money. The good times were over.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why were the 1920s called the "Roaring Twenties"?

2. What happened in October 1929?

1. Because the USA was rich and happy: new cars and radios, lots of jobs, and the stock market going up and up.

2. The stock market crashed. Prices fell very fast and people lost huge amounts of money.

Talk about it:
  • People were sure the good times would last forever. Why do you think people often believe a good time will never end?

Part 2 — The hard years

After the crash, people were afraid. They ran to the banks to take out their money. But the banks did not have enough money for everyone, so many banks closed. Millions of families lost all their savings.

Now there was no money. Companies closed, and people lost their jobs. Soon, about one worker in four had no job. Many families became very poor. They stood in long lines to get free bread. Some people lost their homes and built small houses from cardboard and old wood. On the farms, there was no rain for years, and big dust storms killed the crops. It was a terrible time. And it was not only in America — the problem spread to Europe and the rest of the world too.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did so many banks close?

2. What was life like for poor families in these years?

1. People were afraid and rushed to take out their money, but the banks did not have enough for everyone — so they closed.

2. Many lost their jobs and homes. They stood in long lines for free bread, and some built houses from cardboard and old wood. On the farms there was no rain and dust storms killed the crops.

Talk about it:
  • Many of these people did nothing wrong, but they lost everything. How do you think that feels?

Part 3 — The way back

In 1933, a new president started: Franklin Roosevelt. He had a plan called the "New Deal". He helped the banks, and he gave people jobs. The government paid people to build roads, bridges, schools and parks. Roosevelt also told the people not to be afraid. He said the country was going to be strong again.

The New Deal helped a lot, but it did not fix everything. Many people still had no job for years. The real end came later. When the Second World War started, the factories needed to make ships, planes and guns. Suddenly there were jobs again for everyone. At last, after more than ten years, the Great Depression was over.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did President Roosevelt do to help?

2. What finally ended the Great Depression?

1. He started the "New Deal": he helped the banks and gave people jobs, paying them to build roads, bridges, schools and parks.

2. The Second World War. The factories needed to make ships, planes and guns, so there were jobs for everyone again.

Talk about it:
  • It took a war to end the Depression, not just a good plan. What does that tell us about how hard it is to fix a big problem like this?

Grammar: 'going to' and 'would' (for the past)

In this story, people in the past thought about the future. We have two ways to say what they expected.

was/were going to + verb = a plan or hope in the past: "people thought they were going to get rich."
would + verb = something people were sure about in the past: "they were sure the prices would never go down."
We often use these when the future did not happen the way people expected.

1. Choose the right form

1. In 1929, people were sure the market (never / crash).

2. Many families thought they (be) rich one day.

3. The farmers believed the rain (come) soon.

1. would never crash (they were very sure)

2. were going to be (a hope or plan in the past)

3. was going to come (or: would come) (an expectation in the past)

2. Tell a short part of the story

Put the verbs in the right form. Then read it out loud.

In the summer, everyone was happy. People thought the good times (last) forever. Young couples were sure they (have) a good life. They did not know that in a few months the banks (close) and that many of them (lose) everything.

were going to last · would have · would close · would lose

People in the past were so sure — but the future was very different!
