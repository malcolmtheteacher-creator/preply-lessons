🚨 World's Dumbest Criminals

These are all real. Every single one.

🤦 The Selfie Burglar
In 2015, a man broke into a house in Dunnellon, Florida. He stole electronics, jewellery, and cash. Then he found the owner's phone, took a selfie, and accidentally uploaded it to the victim's social media. Police arrested him within hours.
Source: Florida Police Department, 2015
🍕 The Pizza Delivery Robber
A man in Louisiana ordered a pizza, then robbed the delivery driver when he arrived. The driver told police exactly where he'd delivered the pizza. The man was arrested at his own front door, still eating the pizza, 20 minutes later.
Source: Shreveport Police, 2017
📞 The Wrong Number
In 2018, a drug dealer in the UK accidentally texted his entire price list to a police officer's personal phone. He'd saved the number as a customer. The officer organised a "purchase" and arrested him at the meetup point.
Source: Kent Police, 2018

These stories are GOLD for practising storytelling expressions.

Expressions 1-3

Click each expression to see it in action — then try it yourself.

1. "So basically what happened was..."
Sets up a story. Signals: "I'm about to tell you something good." Use it to start.
"So basically what happened was, this guy broke into a house, stole a bunch of stuff, and then took a selfie on the victim's phone."

Now you try: Retell the pizza story using this expression.

2. "The thing is, nobody saw it coming"
Creates suspense. Signals something unexpected is about to drop.
"The thing is, nobody saw it coming — the cops already had his face because he'd literally posted it himself."

Now you try: Add this expression to your pizza story from above. What was the unexpected twist?

0 words
3. "What gets me is..."
Your personal emotional reaction. Shows you FEEL something — not just reporting facts.
"What gets me is that he was still eating the pizza when they arrested him. Like, he didn't even try to hide."

Now you try: What gets you about the drug dealer story? Give your reaction.

Expressions 4-5

Two more. Then you've got the full toolkit.

4. "You'd think [X], but actually..."
Flips expectations. The listener assumes one thing — you surprise them.
"You'd think breaking into a house would be the hard part, but actually it was taking a selfie that got him caught."

Now you try: Use this expression about the drug dealer. What would you expect vs what actually happened?

5. "It all came down to..."
Builds to the climax. Everything in the story leads to THIS one moment.
"It all came down to one stupid text message. Years of dealing, and he sends his price list to a cop."
