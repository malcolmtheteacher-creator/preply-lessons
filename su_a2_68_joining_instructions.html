Imperatives for instructions

Use imperatives in formal instructions:

From a teacher: "Please complete the assignment by Friday"
From a coach: "Arrive 15 minutes early for warm-up"
From a parent: "Remember to bring your ID and vaccination certificate"
From a boss/organizer: "Please confirm your attendance by emailing us"

Write a joining instructions email

You"re organizing a conference. Write a formal email with joining instructions.

Include:

  • A formal greeting (Dear...)
  • The venue location and how to find it
  • The date and time
  • What to bring or wear (dress code)
  • Important instructions (use imperatives: "Please...", "Remember...", "Arrive...")
  • Contact information
  • A formal closing

Discussion questions

Think about and discuss these questions:

1. Why is formal language important for joining instructions?

Think about how people feel when they receive an email. What impression does formal language make? Why might it be important for professional events?

2. When do we use imperatives in formal communication?

Compare giving instructions to a friend versus to a client or colleague. How does the language change? What imperatives are polite enough for formal situations?

3. What information is essential in joining instructions?

Imagine you"re attending an event in a new city. What would confuse you? What clear instructions would help you feel confident and prepared?

4. How do you find places in your country?

Is GPS and apps common? Do people still use paper maps or ask for directions? How would joining instructions be different in different contexts?

5. What"s the difference between "we" and "you" in formal emails?

Look back at the transformations. When do organizers use "we" versus "you"? What effect does each create?
