🔒Reveal answer

What did you learn about monotheism in Y9?

🔒Reveal answer

What does "transcendent" mean?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Transcendent means beyond or above — in Christianity, God is transcendent because He exists beyond the physical world and human limits.

What does "immanent" mean?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Immanent means within or close by — Christians believe God is immanent because He is present and active in the world and in people's lives.

God's Attributes: What Christians Believe About God

Christians believe God has specific attributes (qualities) that define His nature. These attributes are central to GCSE AQA specification:

  • Omnipotent: All-powerful; able to do anything
  • Omniscient: All-knowing; knows everything, past, present, and future
  • Omnibenevolent: Perfectly good and loving; wants the best for creation
  • Just: Fair and righteous; rewards good and punishes evil
  • Transcendent: Beyond and above creation; not limited by space or time
  • Immanent: Present and active in creation; personal and involved

God's Love and Justice in Scripture

"God is love." (1 John 4:8)

This verse is central to Christian understanding of God's nature. It means God's fundamental character is love — not just that God performs loving actions, but that love defines who God is. This supports the attribute of omnibenevolence.

"The Lord is righteous in all his ways and faithful in all he does." (Psalm 145:17)

This verse emphasises God's justice and faithfulness. God is not arbitrary or capricious; His actions are based on righteousness and moral consistency. This shows how justice and love work together in God's character.

Transcendence and Immanence: A Christian Paradox

Christians believe God is both transcendent (beyond creation, infinite, eternal) and immanent (present within creation, involved in people's lives, personal).

Transcendence: God is not limited by the laws of physics or time. God existed before creation and will exist after. God is wholly other — not human, not bounded by the material world.

Immanence: Despite being transcendent, Christians experience God as present and active. God hears prayers, guides individuals, and acts in history. God is personal and relatable.

How do both exist together? This is a mystery that theologians and believers explore. Many Christians see it as part of God's infinite nature — God can be beyond creation AND active within it, in ways our finite minds cannot fully grasp.

Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant Perspectives

While all Christian denominations believe in God's key attributes, there are some differences in emphasis:

Catholic & Orthodox: Emphasise God's transcendence and majesty. Use icons and liturgy to express awe at God's otherness. Also stress God's immanence through the incarnation (God becoming human in Jesus) and the Holy Spirit's presence in the church.

Protestant: Often emphasise personal relationship with God (immanence). Martin Luther spoke of believers having direct access to God. However, also maintain that God is wholly other and beyond human understanding (transcendence).

Theological Thinking: The Problem of Paradox

A paradox is when two seemingly opposite things are both true. Can God be all-powerful AND all-loving? If God is all-knowing, does that mean free will is impossible?

Theologians wrestle with these questions. Some argue that God's infinite nature allows both attributes to be true simultaneously — we just can't fully understand how. Others propose that God's omniscience doesn't mean God controls the future, but simply knows what will happen.

Key thinker: Thomas Aquinas (13th-century Catholic theologian) argued that God's attributes are all unified in God's essence — God is not composed of separate qualities, but is one infinite being containing all perfections.
