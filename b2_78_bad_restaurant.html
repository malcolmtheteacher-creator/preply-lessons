More Service-Related Terms

What does unsatisfactory mean?

Not satisfactory; not meeting expectations or standards; disappointing.

How would you describe food that has lost its temperature?

You might say it is lukewarm, cold, or no longer hot. If it is supposed to be hot, you would say it is not hot enough.

Polite Ways to Complain

Use these expressions to complain diplomatically:

Polite or Indirect:

  • I am afraid the food is not quite what we expected.
  • Would it be possible to have this replaced?
  • I am sorry to mention this, but the service has been rather slow.
  • Perhaps we could speak to the manager about this issue?

More Direct:

  • This meal is overcooked and unacceptable.
  • I ordered this 20 minutes ago. This is completely unacceptable.
  • The service here is terrible. We need to speak to management.
  • I am not paying for this. It is not what was promised.

Practice: Matching Situations to Language

You have waited 45 minutes for your food. Which phrase is appropriate?

More direct might be better: I ordered this meal 45 minutes ago. I am quite concerned about the delay. Or more diplomatically: I am sorry, but we have been waiting quite a long time. Could you check on our order?

Your steak arrives cold. How would you tell the server?

Politely: I am sorry, but this steak is not hot anymore. Would you be able to bring me a fresh one? Or more firmly: This steak is stone cold. This is unacceptable. I need a replacement immediately.

Customer Service Role Play

You will take one of these roles and practice complaining or responding to complaints:

Scenario A: The Broken Service

Your role: You ordered fish that arrived overcooked and dry. The server brought water that was lukewarm and the table next to you is very loud, affecting your meal.

My role: I listen to your complaint, apologize, and offer solutions (replacement, discount, or compensation).

Scenario B: The Wait

Your role: You have been waiting 50 minutes for appetizers at a restaurant that appears to be understaffed. You are frustrated because you have reservations elsewhere.

My role: I acknowledge the problem, apologize sincerely, and offer something to make up for the inconvenience (free dessert, discount, or free drink).

Scenario C: The Damaged Experience

Your role: You ordered expensive wine that arrived with a chipped glass. Your main course arrived on a lukewarm plate and tastes mediocre compared to your last visit.

My role: I show empathy, fix each issue immediately, and involve the manager if needed to preserve the relationship.

Role Play Tips

Choose a scenario and assign roles
As the customer: Start with your complaint clearly but politely
As the server or manager: Listen fully before responding; show empathy
Work toward a solution that both sides can accept
Switch roles and repeat with another scenario

Writing Practice

Write a paragraph about a bad restaurant experience you have had (real or imagined). Include:

  • What the problem was (food, service, cleanliness)
  • How you felt about it
  • What you said or did
  • How it was resolved

Key Takeaways

What is the difference between being assertive and being rude when complaining?

Being assertive means clearly stating the problem and what you need without personal attacks. Being rude involves insults, raised voice, or disrespect. Assertiveness gets results; rudeness often backfires.
