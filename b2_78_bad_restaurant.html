Identifying Formal Language

In a complaint email, use formal language. Look at these pairs and identify which is more formal.

Pair 1

a) The dish I was served was not the one I had ordered.

b) The dish was the wrong one.

Formal: The dish I was served was not the one I had ordered. (More detailed and precise)

Pair 2

a) The waiter was angry and rude.

b) I was deeply offended by the behaviour of the waiter.

Formal: I was deeply offended by the behaviour of the waiter. (Uses passive voice and more sophisticated language)

Pair 3

a) This is a case of fake advertising…

b) Contrary to your advertisement…

Formal: Contrary to your advertisement… (More sophisticated phrase)

Pair 4

a) Although we had reserved weeks in advance, we were told there was no table available.

b) I booked ages in advance for no reason.

Formal: Although we had reserved weeks in advance, we were told there was no table available. (More precise and structured)

Pair 5

a) We finally chose to leave after waiting for far too long.

b) In the end, after waiting an hour for the desserts, we decided to leave.

Formal: In the end, after waiting an hour for the desserts, we decided to leave. (Specific timing and more measured tone)

Linking Words and Phrases

Complete these sentences with the correct linking phrase.

Sentence 1

I feel I have a right to ask for an apology ____ I may take further action.

Answer: otherwise
Meaning: This indicates a consequence if no apology is given

Sentence 2

I ordered beef and was served fish, _____, it was undercooked.

Answer: to make matters worse
Meaning: This adds an additional problem on top of the first

Sentence 3

I am embarrassed about the quality of my experience and _____ will not be returning to your restaurant.

Answer: furthermore
Meaning: This adds another related consequence

Sentence 4

______ I had reserved a table a month in advance, none were available on the night.

Answer: Even though
Meaning: This contrasts the advance booking with the poor service

Sentence 5

______after waiting for over an hour for our second course, we decided to leave.

Answer: Finally
Meaning: This shows the final step after a long wait

Writing Task

Step 1: Brainstorm Problems

Think of problems that can occur in a bad restaurant experience. What would you complain about?

Example problems: slow service, cold food, wrong dish, rude staff, excessive noise, unclean facilities

Step 2: Plan Your Email

Create an email complaint with three paragraphs. Each paragraph should have:

  • A topic sentence (what the paragraph is about)
  • Supporting details or examples
  • A final sentence (conclusion or consequence)

Step 3: Draft Your Email

Use bullet points first to organize your ideas. Example structure:

Paragraph 1: The Problem

  • Waited 10 minutes to be seated
  • Restaurant was half full
  • Waited further 30 minutes for first course

Step 4: Write the Full Email

Use this format:

  • Start with "Dear Sir or Madam,"
  • Write three well-structured paragraphs
  • Use formal language and linking phrases
  • Sign off with "Yours faithfully,"

Success Criteria

Check your email:

  • ✓ Uses appropriate formal language to make complaints
  • ✓ Uses linking phrases to add ideas in sentences
  • ✓ Uses structured paragraphs with clear purpose
  • ✓ Each paragraph has topic sentence, supporting details, and conclusion
