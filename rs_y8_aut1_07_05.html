What Unites and Divides?

Compare and contrast the three Abrahamic faiths on key beliefs and practices

Retrieval: What Do You Remember?

Before we compare the three faiths, let's recall what you learned about each one. Click on each question to reveal the answer.

Abraham's role in all three faiths: Abraham is the founder figure for Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. Jews trace descent from Abraham through Isaac; Christians see Abraham as a model of faith; Muslims see Abraham (Ibrahim) as one of the greatest prophets and the father of Ishmael.
🔒
Judaism's core belief: Monotheism—belief in one God (Yahweh). The Torah is the most sacred text. Jewish practice is guided by mitzvot (commandments) and includes rituals like Sabbath observance, keeping kosher, and celebrating festivals like Passover and Hanukkah.
🔒
Christianity's core belief: Christians believe in Jesus Christ as the Son of God and Saviour. The Trinity (Father, Son, Holy Spirit) is central to most Christian theology. The Bible (Old and New Testaments) is sacred scripture. Key practices include prayer, worship, and the sacraments of Baptism and Communion.
🔒
Islam's core belief: Muslims believe in one God, Allah, and follow the teachings of the Prophet Muhammad. The Qur'an is the holy scripture, revealed directly by God. Core practices include the Five Pillars: declaration of faith, prayer five times daily, alms-giving, fasting during Ramadan, and pilgrimage to Mecca.
🔒

Quick Check

Who is the key figure in Judaism? Moses led the Exodus, but Abraham is the founding patriarch.
Why is Jesus important to Christians? Christians believe Jesus is the Son of God who died for humanity's sins.
What is the Five Pillars of Islam? The five core duties all Muslims should follow, from faith declaration to pilgrimage.
What does "Abrahamic faiths" mean? Faiths that trace their roots to Abraham and share a commitment to monotheism.

Direct Comparison: Three Traditions, One Root

Let's examine the three Abrahamic faiths side by side. What unites them? What divides them?

Beliefs About God

Christianity

God's Nature: The Trinity—one God in three persons: Father, Son (Jesus), and Holy Spirit. God is both transcendent and incarnate (present in Jesus).

God's Relationship with Humanity: God loves humanity so much that He sent Jesus to die for human sin and offer salvation.

Islam

God's Nature: Tawhid—absolute monotheism. Allah is one, indivisible, and beyond human comprehension. No partners, no division.

God's Relationship with Humanity: Allah is merciful and just. Humans are created to worship God and submit to His will (Islam = submission).

Judaism

God's Nature: Monotheism—belief in one God (Yahweh/Adonai). God is transcendent yet present in covenant relationships with the Jewish people.

God's Relationship with Humanity: God made a covenant with Abraham and renewed it with Moses. Humans are partners in fulfilling God's purposes.

Sacred Scriptures

Tradition Primary Scripture Status & Authority
Judaism Torah (first five books of Hebrew Bible); Tanakh (full Hebrew scriptures); Talmud (rabbinic interpretation) Torah is most sacred. Talmud guides interpretation and practice.
Christianity Bible: Old Testament (Hebrew scriptures) + New Testament (Gospels, Epistles, Revelation) Jesus Christ is the "Word of God." Scripture testifies to Jesus. Interpreted through church tradition.
Islam Qur'an (God's direct revelation to Muhammad); Hadith (sayings & deeds of Muhammad) Qur'an is God's literal, eternal word in Arabic. Hadith provides guidance for living.

Prophets & Revelation

Christianity

Jesus Christ is God's Son and the ultimate revelation. Old Testament prophets (Isaiah, Jeremiah) are honoured but fulfilled in Jesus. Revelation ended with the apostles.

Islam

Muhammad is the final and greatest prophet (the "Seal of the Prophets"). Earlier prophets (Abraham, Moses, Jesus) are respected but Muhammad is God's last messenger. Revelation ended with Muhammad.

Judaism

Moses is the greatest prophet. Abraham, Isaac, Jacob are patriarchs. Prophets like Isaiah and Jeremiah conveyed God's word. Rabbinic Judaism emphasizes ongoing interpretation rather than new revelation.

Prayer & Worship

Tradition Prayer Practice Central Worship Acts
Judaism Formal prayers (liturgy) in synagogue; personal prayer; blessing over daily activities. Shabbat prayers are central. Sabbath (Friday sunset–Saturday); Torah readings; High Holy Days (Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur)
Christianity Personal and communal prayer; the Lord's Prayer is fundamental. Styles vary (liturgical to spontaneous). Worship services (Sunday); Baptism and Communion (Eucharist); Christmas and Easter
Islam Salah: five daily prayers at set times (dawn, noon, afternoon, sunset, night). Face Mecca; perform ritual ablutions (wudu). Friday prayers (Jumu'ah); Hajj (pilgrimage); Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha; month of Ramadan

Dietary Laws & Food

Judaism

Kashrut (keeping kosher): Only kosher animals (split hooves, chew cud). No shellfish or pork. Separate utensils for meat and dairy. Strict rules about slaughter. Observed by observant Jews; less strictly by others.

Islam

Halal (permissible): No pork or alcohol. Animals must be slaughtered in God's name. Similar to kosher but distinct. All observant Muslims follow halal. Ramadan fasting (sunrise to sunset) is mandatory.

Christianity

Dietary law: Most Christian traditions have no dietary restrictions (Jesus declared all food clean). Historically, some Catholics fast on Fridays in Lent. Fasting is spiritual discipline, not law.

Key Insights

All Three Share: Monotheism, Abraham as a founding figure, prayer as essential practice, and a commitment to living according to God's will.

Judaism & Islam Share: Dietary laws (kosher/halal), emphasis on law and daily practice, no claim that God became human.

Christianity & Islam Share: Jesus as an important figure (though viewed differently), high regard for prophets, clear scripture and guidance.

Unique to Each: Christianity emphasizes the Trinity and Jesus as divine; Islam emphasizes Muhammad as the final prophet and the Five Pillars; Judaism emphasizes the covenant with the Jewish people and Torah interpretation through the Talmud.

Lessons 1–5 Retrieval Questions

Q1: Why is Abraham important to all three Abrahamic faiths?
A: Abraham is the founding patriarch. Jews see themselves as his descendants through Isaac. Christians view Abraham as a model of faith who trusted God. Muslims revere Abraham (Ibrahim) as a great prophet and father of Ishmael, and see Muhammad as continuing his line of prophets.
🔒
Q2: What is the main difference between Christianity's view of God and Islam's view of God?
A: Christianity teaches the Trinity—one God in three persons (Father, Son, Holy Spirit). Islam teaches strict monotheism (Tawhid)—God is one and indivisible, with no partners or division. Christians also believe God became human in Jesus; Muslims firmly reject any idea that God took human form.
🔒
Q3: Compare the role of Jesus in Christianity and Islam.
A: Christianity: Jesus is the Son of God, the Word made flesh, who died for humanity's sins and rose from the dead. He is the central figure of faith and salvation. Islam: Jesus (Isa) is a great and honoured prophet, born of the virgin Mary, but not the Son of God. Muslims reject Jesus's divinity, but respect him as a messenger.
🔒
Q4: What do Judaism and Islam have in common that Christianity does not?
A: Both Judaism and Islam emphasize the law and its observance in daily life. Both have dietary laws (kosher/halal) and daily prayer routines. Both reject the idea that God became human. Both see themselves as carrying forward Abraham's covenant.
🔒
Q5: Explain the significance of the Five Pillars in Islam.
A: The Five Pillars are the five core duties required of all Muslims: (1) Shahada (declaration of faith), (2) Salah (prayer five times daily), (3) Zakat (alms-giving), (4) Sawm (fasting during Ramadan), (5) Hajj (pilgrimage to Mecca). They form the foundation of Islamic practice and devotion.
