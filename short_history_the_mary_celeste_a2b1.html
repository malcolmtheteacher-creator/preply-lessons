The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A strange ship at sea

On 5 December 1872, a British ship called the Dei Gratia was crossing the Atlantic Ocean. The sailors saw another ship far away. Something was wrong. The ship moved in a strange way. Its sails were loose, and it did not answer their signals. The captain looked again. He knew this ship. It was the Mary Celeste, an American ship. It left New York only a few days before, on the way to Italy.

The captain sent some men in a small boat to look. What they found was very strange. The Mary Celeste was fine. It was not sinking. But there was nobody on the ship. The captain was gone. The crew was gone. All the people were gone.

There was food and water for many months. The sailors' boots, pipes and clothes were all still there. It looked like the people just walked away one minute ago. Only one thing was missing: the lifeboat. Eight people were on this ship — and now they were all gone.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What ship found the Mary Celeste, and what was strange about it?

2. What did the men find on the Mary Celeste? What was missing?

1. A British ship, the Dei Gratia. The Mary Celeste moved in a strange way, its sails were loose, and it did not answer signals.

2. They found food, water and the sailors' things, but no people. All eight people were gone. The lifeboat was missing.

Talk about it:
  • The ship was fine — it was not sinking. So why do you think all the people left it?

Part 2 — Who were they?

The people on the Mary Celeste were not strange or dangerous. The captain was Benjamin Briggs. He was a good, careful sailor with many years of experience. His wife Sarah was with him. Their little daughter Sophia was there too. She was only two years old. There were seven other sailors. They were all good sailors. They were not people who get scared easily.

The Dei Gratia took the empty ship to a port called Gibraltar. There, the police started an investigation. There was a problem. When you rescue an empty ship, you get money — a reward. So one man had a dark idea. Maybe the crew of the Dei Gratia attacked the Mary Celeste. Maybe they killed the people and made up the story, just to get the reward money.

He looked for proof. He found some marks on the ship and an old sword. But when experts looked, there was no blood and no sign of a fight. In the end, the police said the Dei Gratia crew did nothing wrong. But they still could not say what happened to the people. And the crew got only a small reward.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who was on the Mary Celeste with Captain Briggs?

2. Why did one man think the Dei Gratia crew did something bad?

1. His wife Sarah, their two-year-old daughter Sophia, and seven other sailors.

2. Because people who rescue an empty ship get reward money. He thought maybe the crew killed the people to get the money. But there was no proof, and they were found not guilty.

Talk about it:
  • The crew helped, but then people thought they did something bad. Is it fair to be a suspect just because you helped?

Part 3 — A mystery for ever

Nobody knew the answer, so people started to guess. Many years later, a young writer named Arthur Conan Doyle (later famous for Sherlock Holmes) wrote a story about a ship like this. His story was not true, but many people believed it. After that, people mixed the real story with stories of pirates, monsters and murder that never happened.

The calm ideas are more interesting. The ship carried a lot of alcohol. Maybe some gas came from it. Maybe the captain was afraid of a big fire or an explosion. Maybe he told everyone to get into the lifeboat and wait, near the ship, until it was safe. The lifeboat was tied to the ship with a rope. Maybe the rope broke. Then the little boat, with all the people, was left alone on the sea, and the Mary Celeste sailed away without them.

We will never know for sure. That is why people still talk about the Mary Celeste today, almost 150 years later. It is a perfect mystery: a normal ship, a normal family, and eight people who disappeared and were never found. The ship itself sailed for many more years. It is still the most famous "ghost ship" in the world.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did the writer Arthur Conan Doyle do?

2. What is one calm idea about what happened to the people?

1. He wrote a story about a ship like the Mary Celeste. It was not true, but many people believed it and mixed it with the real story.

2. Maybe there was gas from the alcohol, and the captain was afraid. He told everyone to get in the lifeboat. The rope broke, and the people were lost at sea.

Talk about it:
  • People still love this story after 150 years. Why do you think we like a mystery with no answer?

Grammar: Past Simple & question words

This is a story about the past, so we use the past simple. And it is a mystery, so we ask a lot of questions. Question words help us ask them.

Past simple = a finished action in the past. Many verbs add -ed: "The ship sailed from New York." Some verbs are irregular: go → went, find → found, be → was / were, leave → left.
Question words:
• What = a thing: "What did they find?"
• Why = a reason: "Why did they leave?"
• How = the way: "How did it happen?"
• Where = a place: "Where did the people go?"
Remember: in past questions we use did + the base verb (no -ed): "Where did they go?" (NOT "did they went").

1. Put the verb in the past simple

Write the past simple form of the verb in brackets.

1. The Mary Celeste (leave) New York in November.

2. The sailors (find) an empty ship.

3. All the people (be) gone.

4. The captain (take) his family on the ship.

1. left (leave → left)

2. found (find → found)

3. were (be → were, for "people")

4. took (take → took)

2. Choose the question word

Complete each question with what, why, how or where.

1. did the sailors find on the ship? (a thing)

2. did the people leave the ship? (a reason)

3. did the people go? (a place)

4. did the rope break? (the way)

1. What (a thing)

2. Why (a reason)

3. Where (a place)

4. How (the way)

3. Make the past question

Complete the question with did and the base verb in brackets. Then read it out loud.

1. Why the captain (leave) the ship?

2. What the sailors (see)?

3. Where the lifeboat (go)?

1. did … leave (Why did the captain leave the ship?)

2. did … see (What did the sailors see?)

3. did … go (Where did the lifeboat go?)

After did, always use the base verb: leave, see, go — not left, saw, went.
