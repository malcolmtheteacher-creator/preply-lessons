The Gift of the Magi

A poor young couple. Two treasures. Two secret plans for Christmas.

Today: tell the story of a husband and wife who each sold their favourite thing to buy the other a Christmas present. Grammar: because and so — the words that connect a reason and a result.

Key words — learn them, then make your guess

First, let's learn eight words. Read each word and its sentence, guess the meaning, then click Reveal to check. Say each word out loud.

1. A gift (or a present) is something you give to a person you like, for example at Christmas or on a birthday.
a gift / a present (noun) — a thing you give to someone, for example at Christmas.
2. A treasure is a thing you love very much — the most special thing you have.
a treasure (noun) — a very special thing that you love and keep.
3. When you sell something, you give it to a person and they give you money. (Past: sold.)
to sell (sold) (verb) — to give something for money.
4. A watch is a small clock. Some old watches are made of gold and stay in a family for many years.
a watch (noun) — a small clock. A gold watch can be very special in a family.
5. A watch chain is a line of small metal rings. You use it to hold a watch safely.
a chain (noun) — small metal rings joined in a line, used to hold something.
6. Combs are beautiful things for a woman's hair. You put them in long hair to hold it and make it pretty.
combs (noun) — pretty things you put in long hair.
7. When you cut hair, you make it short. (Past: cut.)
to cut (cut) (verb) — to make something shorter, for example hair.
8. A surprise is something you don't expect. It can make you very happy — or very quiet!
a surprise (noun) — something you do not expect.

Now guess the story — out loud

Before we read:
  • The story has: a poor wife with beautiful long hair · a husband with a gold watch · Christmas · two gifts · a big surprise. What do you think happens? Tell your idea in full sentences.
  • Quick question: what is the best present you can give someone when you have almost no money?

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — One dollar and eighty-seven cents

Della counted her money. One dollar and eighty-seven cents. That was all. And tomorrow was Christmas.

Della and her husband Jim were young and very poor. They lived in a small, old flat. But they had two treasures. Jim had a beautiful gold watch. It came from his father, and before that, from his grandfather. Della had wonderful long brown hair. It fell down her back like water, almost to her knees.

Della wanted to buy Jim a Christmas present — a really good present. But what could she buy with one dollar and eighty-seven cents? She sat down on the old sofa and cried.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How much money does Della have?

2. What are the two treasures?

1. One dollar and eighty-seven cents — and tomorrow is Christmas.

2. Jim's gold watch (from his father and grandfather) and Della's beautiful long hair.

Talk about it:
  • Della and Jim are poor, but they feel rich because of two things. Can a person with little money feel rich? How?

Part 2 — Della's plan

Suddenly Della had an idea. She stood in front of the mirror and let her hair down. It was beautiful. Then she put on her old brown coat and ran out of the door.

She stopped at a shop with a sign: "Madame Sofronie. We buy hair." Della went in. "Will you buy my hair?" she asked. "Twenty dollars," said Madame Sofronie. Della said yes. The woman cut off all her long hair.

Then Della was happy. She had money, so she could buy Jim's present! She looked in many shops, and at last she found the perfect thing: a simple, beautiful chain for Jim's gold watch. She paid twenty-one dollars and hurried home. Her head was now covered in small curls. "I hope Jim still likes me," she thought.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What does Della sell, and how much money does she get?

2. What present does she buy for Jim?

1. She sells her long hair for twenty dollars.

2. A beautiful chain for his gold watch.

Talk about it:
  • Della decides very fast. Is it good to decide fast, or is it better to think for a long time? When?

Part 3 — The surprise

Jim came home. He looked at Della and her short hair — and he stopped. He didn't speak. He just looked and looked. Della was afraid. "Jim, don't be angry!" she said. "I sold my hair because I wanted to buy you a beautiful present."

Jim smiled and took a small packet from his coat. "Open it," he said. Della opened it — and cried out. Inside were the combs. The beautiful combs for long hair! She had seen them in a shop window many times and loved them. But now her long hair was gone.

Della smiled through her tears. "My hair grows fast, Jim!" Then she gave him her present: the watch chain. "Do you like it? Give me your watch — let's see them together!" But Jim sat down on the sofa and smiled. "Della," he said, "let's put our presents away and keep them for some time. They are too nice to use just now. I sold the watch because I wanted to buy your combs."

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What is Jim's present for Della? Why is it a problem?

2. Why can't Jim use the chain?

1. Beautiful combs for her hair — but she sold her hair, so she can't use them now.

2. Because he sold his watch to buy the combs.

Talk about it:
  • The two presents are useless now. But is this a sad ending or a happy ending? Why?

Grammar: because & so

This whole story is about reasons and results. In English, we connect them with because and so.

because + the reason: "Della cried because she had no money." (Why did she cry? → the reason comes after because.)
so + the result: "Della had no money, so she cried." (What happened next? → the result comes after so.)
Same idea, two directions: reason because ← / → so result.

1. Choose because or so

1. Della was sad she had only $1.87.

2. She wanted money for a present, she sold her hair.

3. Jim looked at Della for a long time her hair was so short.

4. Jim had no watch now, he could not use the chain.

1. because (the reason for her sadness)

2. so (the result of wanting money)

3. because (the reason he stared)

4. so (the result of having no watch)

2. Say the same idea both ways

Finish the second sentence so it means the same as the first.

1. Della sold her hair because she loved Jim. → Della loved Jim,

2. Jim sold his watch, so he had money for the combs. → Jim had money for the combs

1. …so she sold her hair.

2. …because he sold his watch.

3. Tell a short part of the story

Write because or so. Then read the little story out loud.

Della had almost no money, she could not buy a good present. She sold her hair she wanted to buy a chain for Jim's watch. Jim loved Della very much, he sold his watch and bought the combs. The presents were useless — but the couple were happy, now they knew how much they loved each other.

so · because · so · because

Reason after because, result after so.

Going deeper — talk about the big ideas

Now take the story's ideas into the real world. Talk about each one out loud. There are no right answers — say what you think, and say why (use because!).

1 · The best gift

Some people say: "The best gifts cost nothing." Do you agree? Think of examples: time, help, a meal, a letter. Which "free" gift is the most valuable?

2 · Money and presents

In many countries, people spend a lot of money at Christmas or for New Year. Is this good or bad for families? Would a holiday with no presents at all be better or worse? Why?

3 · Wise or foolish?

Della and Jim both lost their treasures, and the presents are now useless. One friend says: "They were foolish." Another says: "They were wise." Who do you agree with? Give two reasons.

4 · Giving in your culture

How do people give gifts in your country? When? What is a "good" gift, and what is a "bad" gift? Is there a gift people give that costs nothing?
