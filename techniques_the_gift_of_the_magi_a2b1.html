The Gift of the Magi

O. Henry — a poor young couple, one day before Christmas, and a surprise ending

Ready for a harder version? read this lesson at B2/C1 level →

Read the story in three parts

Read each part slowly. Stop at the yellow box and talk about the questions. Do not read the end first — the surprise is important!

Part 1 — No money for Christmas

It was the day before Christmas. Della and Jim were young, and they loved each other very much. But they were poor. Della counted her money again. One dollar and eighty-seven cents. That was all. She wanted to buy Jim a present, but she did not have enough money. So she sat down and cried.

Della and Jim had two special things. Jim had a beautiful gold watch. It was his father's watch, and his grandfather's before that. And Della had long, beautiful hair. It fell down her back, almost to her knees. These two things made them very proud and happy.

Stop and talk:
  • Why is Della sad?
  • What are the two special things? Who owns each one?

Part 2 — Della sells her hair

Suddenly, Della had an idea. She put on her old coat and hat and ran to a shop. The shop bought and sold hair.

"Will you buy my hair?" Della asked.

"Yes. I will give you twenty dollars," said the woman.

"Cut it off, quickly!" said Della.

Now Della had twenty-one dollars, because she sold her beautiful hair. She went from shop to shop. At last she found the perfect present for Jim: a lovely chain for his gold watch. She bought it and hurried home. Now Jim could look at his watch a hundred times a day.

Stop and talk:
  • How does Della get money? What does she give up?
  • What present does she buy? Who is it for?

Part 3 — The surprise

Jim came home. He looked at Della's short hair and did not move. His face was very strange.

"Jim, darling," said Della, "I sold my hair because I wanted to buy you a present. My hair will grow again!"

Slowly, Jim took a small package from his pocket. "Open it," he said. Inside were beautiful combs — the combs that Della had wanted for a long time, for her long hair. But now her hair was gone.

Della smiled. "My hair grows fast, Jim!" Then she gave him her present. "Look! A chain for your watch. Now give me your watch — I want to see it!"

But Jim smiled and sat down. "Dell," he said, "I sold my watch, because I wanted to buy your combs."

So Della had a chain, but no watch. And Jim had combs, but no hair. They both gave up their most special thing for each other. Their presents did not work — but their love was the greatest present of all.

Now talk about the end:
  • What did Jim sell? Why?
  • The presents do not work now. So why is this a happy story?
  • Your turn: What is the best present someone gave you? Why was it special?

because & so — reason and result

This story is full of reasons and results. Two small words join them:

because tells you the reason (the "why"): Della cried because she had no money.
so tells you the result (the "what happened"): Della had no money, so she cried.

Same idea, two ways round. The reason goes after because. The result goes after so.

How the writer surprises you

The writer, O. Henry, is famous for surprise endings. Here is how he does it.

1. He tells you the two special things early

At the start, the writer tells you clearly: Jim's watch and Della's hair. He wants you to remember them. This is called foreshadowing — a small clue early in the story about what will be important later.

2. The big surprise (a twist)

Della sells her hair to buy a chain for Jim's watch. But Jim sells his watch to buy combs for Della's hair! Both gave up the exact thing the other's present needed. This surprise at the end is called a twist.

3. Happy and sad at the same time (irony)

The presents do not work — that is sad. But they show how much the couple love each other — that is happy. When the result is the opposite of what people wanted, we call it irony.

Quick check

Why does the writer tell us about the watch and the hair at the beginning?
What is the surprise (the twist) at the end?

Speaking

Task 1 · Tell the story

Tell the story in your own words. Use these words: poor, hair, watch, sell, present, love. Use because and so two or three times.

Task 2 · A present you remember

Talk about a present someone gave you, or a present you gave. What was it? Why did you choose it? Was the person happy? Use because to give your reasons.

Task 3 · What would you do?

Imagine you have very little money and you want to give a good present. What can you do? Give three ideas. Start each one with "I can…" and explain with "because…".

To finish — easy questions

  1. Do you like giving presents or getting presents more? Why?
  2. Is the best present always expensive? Why / why not?
  3. Della and Jim were poor but happy. Can money buy happiness?
  4. What is a good present for someone who has "everything"?
