🧩 The 5 Elements of Brand Identity

A complete brand is more than a name. It's a system of elements that work together.

📝
Name
Memorable, easy to spell, suggests what you do or how you're different
🎯
Tagline
A short phrase that captures your promise or positioning
🎨
Visual Identity
Logo, colours, fonts — the look that makes you recognisable
🗣️
Voice & Tone
How you communicate — formal? Friendly? Playful? Expert?
💫
Values & Story
What you stand for and why you exist

🎯 Brand Positioning: Your Unique Space

Positioning is where your brand sits in the customer's mind compared to competitors. Get it right, and you own that space.

The Positioning Statement Formula

For [target customer] who [need/want],

[Brand] is the [category] that [key benefit]

because [reason to believe].

Examples

Volvo
"For families who want peace of mind, Volvo is the car brand that offers unmatched safety because of 50+ years of safety innovation."
Oatly
"For environmentally conscious consumers who want dairy alternatives, Oatly is the oat milk that's deliciously sustainable because it uses 80% less land than dairy."

Positioning Strategies

💰
Price-Based
"The affordable option" or "The premium choice"
⭐
Quality-Based
"The best-in-class" — features, durability, excellence
🎪
Audience-Based
"Made for [specific group]" — specialization
🌍
Values-Based
"For people who care about X" — ethical, sustainable

👆 Click to see: Positioning Mistakes to Avoid

  • Trying to be everything: "For everyone who wants everything" — means nothing
  • Copying the leader: Being "like X but cheaper" rarely works
  • Features without benefits: "We have 47 features!" — so what?
  • Ignoring competition: Positioning only works relative to alternatives

💬 The Language of Branding

When presenting a brand concept — whether to Lord Sugar or a real client — you need the right vocabulary and structure.

Presenting Your Brand Concept

1. Start with the insight

"We identified that [target audience] are frustrated by..."

"There's a gap in the market for..."

2. Introduce the brand

"That's why we created [Name], which..."

"Our brand, [Name], speaks directly to..."

3. Explain the name

"The name comes from... and represents..."

"We chose this name because it suggests..."

4. Describe the visual identity

"The colour palette evokes..."

"The logo represents... and was designed to..."

5. Connect to the customer

"When our customer sees this brand, they'll feel..."

"This brand promises them..."

Key Branding Vocabulary

Target audience — The specific group you're trying to reach

Value proposition — The unique value you offer

Brand equity — The value of a brand name itself

Brand architecture — How sub-brands relate to the main brand

Differentiation — What makes you stand out

Messaging — The key points you communicate

Defending Your Brand Choices

Lord Sugar will challenge you. Be ready to explain:

  • "Why did you choose that name?" → Have a clear rationale
  • "Who exactly is this for?" → Be specific, not generic
  • "How is this different from [competitor]?" → Know your positioning
  • "Will this work internationally?" → Check for unintended meanings

🏆 The Branding Pitch

It's time to present your brand to Lord Sugar. Deliver a complete brand pitch under pressure.

📺 Your Brief

You have 3 minutes to present your complete brand concept. Cover the name, positioning, visual identity, and why it will succeed. Your teacher (as Lord Sugar) will then ask tough questions.

Pitch Checklist

  • Open with the market insight/opportunity
  • Introduce the brand name and explain its meaning
  • State the positioning clearly
  • Present the tagline and explain the promise
  • Describe the visual identity and what it evokes
  • Connect everything to the target customer
  • End with why this brand will succeed
