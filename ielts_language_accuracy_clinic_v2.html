The Article System: Making Decisions

Why This Matters: Articles are one of the most common error types in IELTS writing - and one of the easiest to fix with practice. The good news: there are clear rules. The challenge: you must practice making DECISIONS until they become automatic.
The Four Choices:
  • A/AN - First mention of ONE thing (reader does not know which specific one)
  • THE - We both know WHICH specific one (mentioned before, or there is only one)
  • NO ARTICLE - General statements about ALL of that category (plural or uncountable)
  • NO ARTICLE - Abstract concepts used generally

The Pattern:

First mention = A/AN (introducing something new)

Second mention = THE (we both know which one now)

Correct: "A researcher conducted the experiment. The researcher found interesting results."
Incorrect: "The researcher conducted the experiment. A researcher found interesting results."

Practice 2: General Categories (No Article)

education is essential for economic development.

The Pattern:

When talking about something IN GENERAL (all education, crime in general, technology as a concept) = NO ARTICLE

Correct: "[no article] Crime affects society." (crime in general)
Incorrect: "The crime affects society." (implies one specific crime)

Your IELTS Vocabulary Toolkit

The Problem: Read this Band 6 paragraph:

"Many people believe that education is important. This is because people need education to get good jobs. When people are educated, society benefits. However, some people think education is too important in society today. This is a problem for society because it creates problems for people who cannot afford it."
people (5x), society (3x), important (2x), problem (2x) - This repetition caps your Lexical Resource at Band 6. Learn alternatives and you will break through.
The 5 Most Overused Words in IELTS Essays:
  1. PEOPLE (appears 5-10 times in typical Band 6 essay)
  2. SOCIETY
  3. IMPORTANT
  4. PROBLEM
  5. GOOD/BAD

Learn 4-5 alternatives for each and you will NEVER repeat these words again.

Word 1: PEOPLE

PEOPLE
individuals citizens the public members of society the population
Remember by WHO you mean:
One person at a time? → individuals
With rights/duties? → citizens
As a mass/group? → the public or the population
"Many individuals believe that technology improves our lives."
"Citizens should have access to affordable healthcare."
"The public is increasingly concerned about climate change."

Word 2: SOCIETY

SOCIETY
the community the nation the public our world modern life
Remember by SIZE:
Local? → the community
National? → the nation
Global? → our world
About how we live now? → modern life
"This issue affects the community at every level."
"Modern life has brought many challenges."
"These changes benefit the nation as a whole."

Word 3: IMPORTANT

IMPORTANT
crucial essential vital significant fundamental
Remember by INTENSITY:
Strongest (life or death): vital, crucial, essential
Strong (really matters): significant, fundamental
Just remember the top 3: Vital, Crucial, Essential
"Education is crucial for personal development."
"Clean water is essential for human health."
"This plays a vital role in economic growth."

Word 4: PROBLEM

PROBLEM
issue challenge concern difficulty obstacle
Remember by TONE:
Neutral/formal? → issue
Something to overcome? → challenge, obstacle
Something worrying? → concern
Hard to deal with? → difficulty
"Unemployment is a major issue in many countries."
"Climate change presents a significant challenge."
"This is a growing concern for parents."
