Understanding the OTE Speaking Test

What is the OTE Speaking Module?

The Oxford Test of English Speaking module is a 15-minute computer-based test. You speak into a microphone and your answers are recorded. A trained examiner will listen and score your responses later.

The 4 Parts

1

Personal Questions

8 questions about yourself
10-20 seconds each

2

Voicemail

Leave voice messages
20s prep, 40s speaking

3

Extended Response

Longer answers on a topic
30s prep, 60s speaking

4

Photo Comparison

Compare 2 photos
30s prep, 60s speaking

What Makes a Strong vs Weak Response?

Weak Response (A2)

Question: "What do you enjoy doing in your free time?"

"I like... music. And films. Yes."

Problems:

  • Too short
  • No details
  • Doesn't use the full time

Strong Response (B1-B2)

Question: "What do you enjoy doing in your free time?"

"In my free time, I really enjoy listening to music, especially rock and indie music. I also like watching films - my favourite type is comedy because it helps me relax after a long day at work."

Why it works:

  • Answers the question directly
  • Gives specific details
  • Uses linking words
  • Fills the time naturally

The Key Difference

The difference between A2 and B2 is not about using difficult words. It's about:

  • Expanding your answers naturally
  • Adding relevant details and examples
  • Using the full time you are given
  • Connecting your ideas smoothly

Part 1: Personal Questions

What happens?

You answer 8 questions about yourself and your life. Questions 1-2 give you 10 seconds. Questions 3-8 give you 20 seconds. There is no preparation time.

Strategy: Use the E-E-E Formula

For every answer, try to include:

  1. Explain - Answer the question directly
  2. Example - Give a specific example
  3. Extra - Add one more detail

See it in action:

"Where do you come from?"
Click to see model answer

E-E-E Answer:

"I come from [city], which is in the [region] of [country]. (Explain) It's famous for its beautiful old buildings and great restaurants. (Example) I really like living there because the people are very friendly. (Extra)"

"Do you prefer to spend time alone or with friends?"
Click to see model answer

E-E-E Answer:

"Honestly, I prefer spending time with friends. (Explain) For example, every weekend I meet my best friends for coffee and we talk about everything. (Example) I find that I feel more energetic when I'm around other people. (Extra)"

Useful phrases for Part 1:

Honestly, I... To be honest... I'd say that...

For example... For instance... Like when...

I find that... The reason is... What I mean is...

Practice Activity

Answer these questions out loud. Try to speak for the full 20 seconds using E-E-E:

  1. What kind of music do you like?
  2. How do you usually spend your weekends?
  3. Do you prefer eating at home or in restaurants?

Part 2: Voicemail Messages

What happens?

You see a situation on screen and hear/read a voicemail. You have 20 seconds to prepare and 40 seconds to leave your message. You do this twice.

Strategy: The 3-Part Voicemail

Every voicemail should have:

  1. Opening - Greeting + reason for calling
  2. Main content - Answer/respond to the situation
  3. Closing - What happens next + goodbye

Example Situation:

"You want to book a table at a restaurant for your friend's birthday. Call the restaurant and leave a message. Include: date, time, number of people, and any special requests."

See model voicemail
Click to reveal

Opening: "Hi, this is [Name] calling. I'd like to book a table at your restaurant, please."

Main content: "It's for Saturday the 15th, at around 7 o'clock in the evening. There will be 6 of us. It's actually a birthday celebration for my friend, so if possible, could we have a quiet table away from the door?"

Closing: "Please call me back to confirm the booking. My number is 555-1234. Thank you very much. Bye."

Voicemail phrases:

Opening: Hi, this is [Name] calling... I'm calling because... I'm phoning about...

Requests: I'd like to... Could you please... Would it be possible to...

Closing: Please call me back at... You can reach me on... Thanks for your help. Bye.

Practice Activity

Leave a voicemail for this situation (40 seconds):

"Your English class has been cancelled. Call your classmate and leave a message telling them about the cancellation and suggesting what you could do instead."

Part 3: Extended Response

What happens?

You see a topic with 2-3 prompts to talk about. You have 30 seconds to prepare and 60 seconds to speak.

Strategy: The P-R-E Framework

Structure your answer:

  1. Point - State your main idea clearly
  2. Reason - Explain why
  3. Example - Give a specific example from your life

Use this pattern 2-3 times to fill 60 seconds.

Example Topic:

Talk about learning English

  • Why are you learning English?
  • What do you find easy or difficult?
  • How do you practise outside of class?
See model answer (60 seconds)
Click to reveal

"I'm learning English mainly for my career. (Point 1) I work in an international company, and most of our meetings are in English, so I need to communicate clearly with my colleagues. (Reason) Last month, I had to give a presentation in English, which was challenging but also really motivating. (Example)

The most difficult part for me is speaking fluently. (Point 2) I understand a lot when I read or listen, but when I try to speak, I sometimes forget words or make grammar mistakes. (Reason)

To practise, I listen to English podcasts every day. (Point 3) I especially like ones about news and business because they help me learn vocabulary I can use at work. (Reason/Example)"

Useful phrases:

Giving reasons: The main reason is... This is because... The thing is...

Adding examples: For example,... Like last week when... A good example is...

Moving on: Another thing I'd say is... Apart from that,... Also,...

Practice Activity

Speak for 60 seconds on this topic:

Talk about your hometown

  • What is special about it?
  • What can visitors do there?
  • Would you like to live there in the future?

Part 4: Photo Comparison

What happens?

You choose 2 photos from 4 options. Then you compare them and answer a question about the topic. You have 30 seconds to prepare and 60 seconds to speak.

Strategy: The S-D-O Formula

  1. Similarities - What do both photos have in common? (15 seconds)
  2. Differences - How are they different? (25 seconds)
  3. Opinion - Answer the question / give your view (20 seconds)

Example:

Imagine photos showing: (A) People exercising in a gym, (B) People exercising outdoors in a park

Question: "Which way of exercising do you think is better?"

See model comparison (60 seconds)
Click to reveal

"Looking at these two photos, I can see that both show people doing physical exercise, and in both cases the people look healthy and active. (Similarities)

However, there are some clear differences. In the first photo, the people are in a gym, using machines and weights. It looks quite modern and professional. In contrast, the second photo shows people exercising outdoors in nature - they seem to be running or doing yoga in a park. The atmosphere is more relaxed and natural. (Differences)

Personally, I think exercising outdoors is better. I believe being in nature is good for your mental health as well as your body. Fresh air and green spaces help me feel more relaxed. But of course, it depends on the weather and what type of exercise you want to do. (Opinion)"

Comparison phrases:

Similarities: Both photos show... In both pictures... They have in common that...

Differences: However,... On the other hand,... In contrast,... While the first photo..., the second...

Opinion: Personally, I think... In my opinion,... I would say that...

Practice Activity

Compare these two situations and give your opinion (60 seconds):

Photo A: People working in an office

Photo B: People working from home

Question: Which working environment do you think is more productive?

Self-Assessment Checklist

  • I answered all questions directly
  • I used the full time available
  • I gave specific examples
  • I used linking words (however, because, for example)
  • I spoke clearly and at a natural pace
  • I corrected myself when I made mistakes

Key Takeaways

The 4 Strategies

  • Part 1: E-E-E (Explain, Example, Extra)
  • Part 2: 3-Part Voicemail (Opening, Content, Closing)
  • Part 3: P-R-E (Point, Reason, Example)
  • Part 4: S-D-O (Similarities, Differences, Opinion)

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1
Answers too short

Always try to use ALL the time you are given. Short answers = lower scores.

2
No examples

General statements are weak. Specific examples from your life are strong.

3
Speaking too fast

It's better to speak clearly and naturally than to rush and make more mistakes.

4
Panicking at silence

It's OK to pause and think. Use phrases like "Let me think..." or "That's a good question..."

Buying time phrases:

Let me think about that...

That's an interesting question...

Well, I'd say that...

Hmm, I suppose...

You're ready!

Remember: The OTE tests your ability to communicate, not to be perfect. Speak naturally, give examples, and use all your time. Good luck!
