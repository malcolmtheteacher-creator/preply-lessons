Speaking: How to Agree, Disagree & Give Opinions

Agreeing with Someone

  • "I completely agree."
  • "That's a good point."
  • "You're absolutely right."
  • "I see what you mean."
  • "Exactly! That's exactly what I was thinking."
  • "I couldn't have said it better myself."

Disagreeing Politely

  • "I don't quite agree."
  • "I see your point, but..."
  • "That's not how I see it."
  • "I'd have to disagree with that."
  • "I understand what you mean, but I think..."
  • "True, but on the other hand..."

Asking for Someone's Opinion

  • "What do you think about...?"
  • "Do you agree?"
  • "What's your view on...?"
  • "How do you feel about...?"
  • "What's your take on this?"

Giving Your Opinion

  • "In my opinion..."
  • "I think that..."
  • "From my perspective..."
  • "As far as I'm concerned..."
  • "I'd say that..."
  • "It seems to me that..."

Debate Preparation

The debate question: "Is TV good for us?"

Arguments FOR (TV is Good for Us)

Educational Value

Documentaries and educational programmes provide knowledge about science, history, culture, and the world.

Entertainment & Relaxation

TV offers entertainment and helps us unwind after stressful days. It's a form of escape and enjoyment.

Social Connection

People discuss TV shows, sharing experiences and building connections with others about popular programmes.

Access to Information

News programmes keep us informed about current events and what's happening in the world.

Arguments AGAINST (TV is Bad for Us)

Physical Health Issues

Excessive TV watching leads to sedentary behaviour, obesity, eye strain, and sleep problems.

Mental Health Concerns

TV can cause addiction, reduce attention span, increase anxiety, and expose viewers to violence.

Quality of Content

Much TV content is shallow, predictable, and lacks intellectual value. It doesn't challenge viewers.

Social Impact

Heavy TV viewing reduces face-to-face interaction, affects family time, and impacts productivity.

Preparation Tip:

Choose your position and prepare 2-3 strong arguments with specific examples. Think about TV shows or situations you know that support your position.

Debate Activity

Time remaining: 5:00

How the Debate Works

  1. Preparation Phase: You and your partner review your arguments and decide who presents which point.
  2. Opening Arguments: Each side presents their main arguments (2-3 minutes).
  3. Rebuttal: The opposing side responds and challenges the arguments.
  4. Questions: Ask clarifying or challenging questions.
  5. Closing Remarks: Each side summarizes their position.

Your Role Assignment

Team FOR (TV is Good)

Your position: TV has significant benefits for education, entertainment, and society.

Your arguments might include:

  • Educational programmes teach valuable knowledge
  • Entertainment and relaxation are important for mental health
  • TV connects people and builds communities around shared viewing
  • News access keeps society informed

Team AGAINST (TV is Bad)

Your position: The negative impacts of TV on health, mental well-being, and society outweigh the benefits.

Your arguments might include:

  • TV causes physical health problems (obesity, eye strain)
  • Excessive viewing leads to addiction and mental health issues
  • Much content is of poor quality and lacks substance
  • TV reduces real-world social interaction and productivity

Debate Checklist

Debate Reflection

After the debate, think about these questions and discuss with your teacher.

Which team's arguments were stronger?

Consider the evidence, examples, and logic used by each side.

What was the most persuasive argument?

Which single argument made the strongest case? Why was it effective?

Did your opinion change during the debate?

Did you hear arguments that made you reconsider your original position?

What language helped the debate?

Which phrases for agreeing, disagreeing, or expressing opinions were most useful?

What would you do differently next time?

How could you improve your debating skills or make stronger arguments?

Personal Reflection:

In reality, is TV good or bad for you personally? What's your true opinion now, after the debate? How do you balance entertainment with other activities?

Extension:

How is TV changing with streaming services and on-demand viewing? Does this change any of your arguments? What about social media vs. traditional TV?
