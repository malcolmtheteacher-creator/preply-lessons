The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — Napoleon comes back

Napoleon was the emperor of France. For many years, his armies won battle after battle across Europe. But then he lost, and in 1814 his enemies sent him away. They exiled him to a small island called Elba. People thought the long wars were over.

But in the spring of 1815, Napoleon escaped from the island. He went back to France. The king sent soldiers to stop him — but the soldiers loved Napoleon, so they joined him instead! In a few weeks, he was the emperor of France again.

The other countries of Europe were afraid. Two big armies got ready to fight him near Brussels, in a place we now call Belgium. One army had British, Dutch and German soldiers. Their leader was the Duke of Wellington. The other army was from Prussia (part of Germany today). Its leader was the old general Blücher. Napoleon knew one thing: he must beat the two armies before they came together.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did Napoleon's enemies send him in 1814?

2. Who were the two leaders getting ready to fight Napoleon?

1. They exiled him to a small island called Elba.

2. The Duke of Wellington (British, Dutch and German army) and the general Blücher (the Prussian army).

Talk about it:
  • Napoleon came back and took power again very fast. Why do you think the soldiers wanted to follow him?

Part 2 — The long day of battle

The big battle was on 18 June 1815, near a village called Waterloo. In the night, heavy rain fell. In the morning, the ground was thick mud. Napoleon could not move his big guns in the mud, so he waited. He started the attack late, near midday. This waiting was a big mistake — but he did not know it yet.

Wellington put his army on a hill. His soldiers fought all day. Napoleon's guns fired again and again. Then his soldiers on horses attacked the hill, many times. But Wellington's men made big squares and did not run. They were tired, but they did not lose the hill.

Far away, Blücher and his Prussian soldiers were coming to help. The mud was very bad, but they did not stop. In the afternoon, the first Prussian soldiers arrived. Now Napoleon had to fight two armies at the same time. Slowly, the battle began to turn against him.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did Napoleon start the attack late?

2. Who arrived in the afternoon to help Wellington?

1. The ground was thick mud after the rain, so he could not move his big guns. He waited until near midday.

2. Blücher and his Prussian soldiers arrived to help Wellington.

Talk about it:
  • A few hours of mud helped to change the battle. Do you think luck is important in history?

Part 3 — The last attack, and the end

In the evening, Napoleon knew he did not have much time. So he tried one last big attack. He sent his best soldiers — the Imperial Guard — up the hill. These soldiers almost never lost. But Wellington's men were hiding. When the Guard came close, they stood up and fired. The Guard could not go on. They stopped, and then they went back.

The French soldiers saw this and were shocked. "The Guard is going back!" At the same time, the Prussians pushed hard on the other side. Wellington waved his army forward. Now the French army ran away in the dark. Napoleon had lost.

Many, many soldiers died that day, on both sides. It was a very sad thing. But the battle was important: it ended Napoleon's wars for ever. His enemies exiled him again — this time to Saint Helena, a small island far out in the sea. He died there six years later. After Waterloo, Europe had peace for a long time. Today, "to meet your Waterloo" means to have your last, big defeat.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened to Napoleon's best soldiers, the Imperial Guard?

2. What happened to Napoleon after the battle?

1. They attacked the hill, but Wellington's hidden men stood up and fired. The Guard stopped and went back. This shocked the French army.

2. He lost, and his enemies exiled him again — to the island of Saint Helena, far out in the sea. He died there six years later.

Talk about it:
  • Today, "to meet your Waterloo" means your last big defeat. Why do you think one battle became so famous?

Grammar: first, then, after that, finally

When you tell a story, you tell things in order. English has easy words to show the order of events.

The order words:
• First, … — the beginning, the first thing.
• Then / Next, … — the thing that comes after.
• After that, … — the thing that comes after that one.
• Finally / In the end, … — the last thing.
Example: First, Napoleon escaped from Elba. Then he became emperor again. After that, two armies got ready to fight him. Finally, he lost at Waterloo.
