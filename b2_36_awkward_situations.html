Vocabulary: Awkward Situations

Click each card to reveal the definition.

awkward Uncomfortable and embarrassing; difficult to deal with
offensive Causing displeasure or resentment; insulting
apologize To express regret or say sorry for something you did
embarrassed Feeling self-conscious or ashamed about something
uncomfortable Not feeling at ease physically or emotionally
politely In a respectful and courteous manner
excuse A reason given to explain or justify an action or behavior
colleague A person you work with at the same organization

Phrases for Making & Accepting Excuses

Making an Apology

  • I'm so sorry about...
  • I apologize for...
  • I feel terrible about...
  • I didn't mean to...
  • That was completely my fault

Making an Excuse Politely

  • I'm afraid I...
  • Unfortunately, I...
  • The thing is...
  • I wasn't able to...
  • It's just that...

Accepting an Apology

  • No worries, these things happen
  • It's absolutely fine
  • Don't worry about it
  • I understand completely
  • It's water under the bridge

Think about this:

When would you use these phrases? Think of situations in your own life where you've needed to apologize or make an excuse.

Common Awkward Situations

Read these scenarios. How would you handle each one?

Scenario 1: Forgotten Name

You've forgotten someone's name and now you have to introduce them to a visitor to your office. What do you do?

Show Suggestions
You could say: "I'm sorry, I'm having a moment—could you remind me of your name?" or "This is my colleague. I'm so embarrassed but I've completely blanked on your name!" Being honest and polite often defuses the awkwardness.

Scenario 2: Unwanted Gift

Your friend buys you a jumper you really dislike. They want you to wear it at their party next week. What do you say?

Show Suggestions
You could say: "It's so thoughtful, thank you! I'm afraid it's not quite my style, but I love that you thought of me." Or: "I really appreciate this, but I don't think it'll suit me. Can I exchange it?" Honesty with gratitude works best.

Scenario 3: Wrong Name from Boss

Your new boss keeps calling you James, but your name is John. How do you correct them?

Show Suggestions
You could say: "Actually, it's John—easy mistake though!" or "Just to clarify, my name is John." Do this early and lightly; the longer you wait, the more awkward it becomes. Your boss will appreciate the correction.

Scenario 4: Unwanted Train Companion

You get on a train and spot someone you know but don't have much in common with. They see you and sit beside you for a 30-minute journey. What do you do?

Show Suggestions
You could: Make polite conversation initially, then politely excuse yourself ("I've got to catch up on emails/reading"). It's okay to be friendly but not forced. A 30-minute conversation doesn't have to be deep—light chat is perfectly acceptable.
