🔒Reveal answer

🔒Reveal answer

🔒Reveal answer

Sawm: Fasting During Ramadan

Sawm (fasting) is the fourth pillar of Islam. During the month of Ramadan — the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar — Muslims fast from dawn (Fajr) to sunset (Maghrib) every day.

The Qur'an commands: 'O you who believe, fasting is prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may achieve taqwa (God-consciousness)' (Surah 2:183).

During the fast, Muslims abstain from: food, drink, smoking, sexual relations, and sinful behaviour (lying, backbiting, anger). The fast is broken each evening with Iftar (the evening meal) and begins each morning before dawn with Suhoor (pre-dawn meal).

Why Ramadan?

Ramadan is the month in which the Qur'an was first revealed to Prophet Muhammad through the angel Jibril. Fasting during this month honours that revelation. The Qur'an states: 'The month of Ramadan in which the Qur'an was revealed, a guidance for the people' (Surah 2:185). The last ten nights are especially significant, as they include Laylat al-Qadr (the Night of Power).

Laylat al-Qadr

The Night of Power (Laylat al-Qadr) falls within the last ten nights of Ramadan — traditionally believed to be the 27th night, though its exact date is unknown. The Qur'an says: 'The Night of Power is better than a thousand months' (Surah 97:3). Muslims spend these nights in intense prayer and worship, seeking Allah's forgiveness and blessings.

Spiritual Significance of Fasting

Sawm is not merely about physical hunger. Its purposes include:

Taqwa (God-consciousness): Fasting develops self-discipline and awareness of Allah. When you are hungry, you remember why you are fasting — for Allah's sake alone.

Empathy: Experiencing hunger and thirst creates genuine understanding of what the poor experience every day. This motivates charitable giving.

Self-discipline: Controlling physical desires during daylight strengthens the Muslim's ability to resist temptation in all areas of life.

Community: The entire ummah fasts together during the same month, breaking their fast at sunset, praying Tarawih prayers together at night. Ramadan creates powerful communal bonds.

Gratitude: Going without food and drink makes Muslims appreciate Allah's blessings — things normally taken for granted.

Exemptions from Fasting

Islam is a practical religion. The Qur'an states: 'Allah does not burden a soul beyond that it can bear' (Surah 2:286). Certain people are exempt from fasting:

Must not fast: Children before puberty, the seriously ill, menstruating women, pregnant or breastfeeding women (if health is at risk)

May delay: Travellers, temporarily ill people — they must make up missed days later

May pay Fidyah instead: The elderly, chronically ill, or those permanently unable to fast may feed a poor person for each day missed

These exemptions show that Islam values human welfare. Fasting should strengthen faith, not destroy health.

Ramadan in Practice

A typical Ramadan day: wake before dawn for Suhoor (pre-dawn meal). Pray Fajr. Go to work or school while fasting (no food, drink, or sinful behaviour). Break the fast at sunset with dates and water (following the Prophet's example), then eat Iftar. Pray Tarawih (extra night prayers, often at the mosque). Sleep. Repeat for 29-30 days.

Eid ul-Fitr

Ramadan ends with Eid ul-Fitr — the 'Festival of Breaking the Fast.' Muslims attend a special Eid prayer, give Zakat ul-Fitr (a charitable donation so everyone can celebrate), wear new clothes, visit family, and share celebratory meals. It is one of the most joyful occasions in the Islamic calendar.

12-Mark Evaluation

'Sawm is the pillar that has the greatest impact on a Muslim's character.' Evaluate this statement.

Guidance

For: Sawm develops taqwa (God-consciousness), self-discipline, and empathy — all character virtues. It is the most physically demanding pillar, requiring 29-30 days of commitment. The Qur'an explicitly links fasting to spiritual growth (2:183). No other pillar transforms daily life so completely for an entire month.

Against: Salah is performed five times every day, year-round — not just one month. Hajj is a once-in-a-lifetime journey that many describe as the most transformative spiritual experience. Zakah directly teaches generosity and detachment from wealth. The Shahadah shapes every decision and thought.

Key Terms

Sawm: Fasting from dawn to sunset during Ramadan — the fourth pillar

Ramadan: The ninth month of the Islamic calendar when fasting occurs

Suhoor: The pre-dawn meal before the fast begins

Iftar: The evening meal that breaks the fast at sunset

Laylat al-Qadr: The Night of Power — when the Qur'an was first revealed

Taqwa: God-consciousness — the primary purpose of fasting

Fidyah: Payment to feed a poor person, for those permanently unable to fast

Eid ul-Fitr: Festival of Breaking the Fast — celebrates the end of Ramadan

Tarawih: Extra prayers performed at night during Ramadan

What is the difference between Suhoor and Iftar?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Suhoor is the pre-dawn meal before the fast begins. Iftar is the evening meal that breaks the fast at sunset.

Why is Ramadan the month chosen for fasting?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Because Ramadan is the month in which the Qur'an was first revealed to Prophet Muhammad through the angel Jibril. Fasting honours that revelation.

What is Eid ul-Fitr?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The Festival of Breaking the Fast — celebrates the end of Ramadan. Muslims attend special prayers, give Zakat ul-Fitr (charity so everyone can celebrate), wear new clothes, visit family, and share celebratory meals.
