The Lies We Tell to Be Polite

C1-C2 Level | 50 minutes | 5 Key Expressions

Why Do We Lie So Much?

Research shows the average person tells 1-2 lies per day—and most of them are "polite lies"

"How are you?"
"I'm fine, thanks!" (You're actually exhausted and stressed.)

"Sorry I'm late!"
"Traffic was terrible." (You just left 10 minutes late.)

"Would you like to come to my party?"
"I'd love to, but I have plans that day." (You don't. You just don't want to go.)

Sound familiar? We're taught from childhood that lying is wrong, but we all do it constantly. Not to hurt people—quite the opposite. We lie to be kind, to avoid awkwardness, and to keep society functioning smoothly.

Psychologist Bella DePaulo from the University of California studied everyday lying and found that people tell an average of 1-2 lies per day, with most being "white lies"—small dishonest statements intended to avoid hurting someone's feelings or to smooth social interactions.

Some common examples across cultures:

  • "I'm fine" when you're not
  • "I'm on my way" when you're still getting dressed
  • "Your haircut looks great!" when it doesn't
  • "Let's do lunch sometime" when you have no intention of making plans
  • "It was so nice to meet you" when it really wasn't
  • "I'm just leaving now" when you haven't left yet

Research from the University of Massachusetts found that 60% of people can't have a 10-minute conversation without lying at least once. But here's the interesting part: these lies aren't malicious. They're social lubricant.

Philosopher Immanuel Kant argued that lying is always wrong, even to be polite. But most ethicists disagree. They argue that white lies serve an important social function—they protect feelings, maintain harmony, and allow us to live together without constant friction.

Cultural differences matter too. In some cultures (like Japan), indirect communication and "face-saving" lies are deeply embedded in social etiquette. In others (like Germany or the Netherlands), direct honesty is valued even if it's uncomfortable.

Think About It:

  • How many times today did you say "I'm fine" when you weren't?
  • When was the last time you told a white lie? Why did you do it?
  • Is it ever okay to lie to spare someone's feelings?
  • What would happen if we all started telling the complete truth all the time?

White Lies & Social Scripts

Click each expression to see how it's used

1. "To spare someone's feelings..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: To avoid hurting someone emotionally. Used to explain why we tell white lies instead of harsh truths.
Example:
"I told her the presentation was good to spare her feelings. She worked so hard on it, and honestly, the content was fine—just the delivery was nervous."
NOW YOU TRY:
Think of a time you weren't completely honest. Say: "I said [white lie] to spare someone's feelings because..."
2. "Little white lie"
Formality: Informal/Neutral
Meaning: A harmless or trivial lie, usually told to avoid hurting someone or to be polite. Not a serious deception.
Example:
"When my friend asked if I liked her new dress, I told a little white lie. It's not really my style, but it makes her happy, so what's the harm?"
NOW YOU TRY:
Complete this: "I tell little white lies when..." Give an example of when you do this.
3. "Socially acceptable"
Formality: Neutral/Formal
Meaning: Behavior that society considers appropriate or normal. Some lies are so common they're expected and accepted.
Example:
"Saying 'I'm fine' when someone asks how you are is socially acceptable, even if you're having a terrible day. Nobody expects the full truth in that situation."
NOW YOU TRY:
What polite lies are socially acceptable in your culture? Describe one using this expression.

Honesty vs. Kindness

4. "I didn't have the heart to tell them..."
Formality: Informal/Neutral
Meaning: I couldn't bring myself to tell them something unpleasant because I felt too much empathy or sympathy. Used when the truth would hurt someone you care about.
Example:
"My grandmother kept asking if I liked her cooking. I didn't have the heart to tell her it was too salty—she was so proud of making dinner for everyone."
NOW YOU TRY:
Think of a truth you avoided telling someone. Say: "I didn't have the heart to tell them that..."
5. "Brutally honest"
Formality: Informal
Meaning: Completely honest in a way that might be harsh or hurtful. The truth without any softening or politeness. Can be negative (too harsh) or positive (refreshingly direct).
Example:
"My Dutch colleague is brutally honest. If you ask 'How was my presentation?' she'll tell you exactly what was wrong—no sugar-coating. It's shocking at first, but actually quite helpful."
NOW YOU TRY:
Do you prefer people to be brutally honest with you, or would you rather hear polite lies? Explain why.

All 5 Expressions:

  • "To spare someone's feelings" — avoiding emotional hurt
  • "Little white lie" — harmless social dishonesty
  • "Socially acceptable" — behavior society considers normal
  • "I didn't have the heart to tell them..." — too empathetic to be honest
  • "Brutally honest" — truth without softening
