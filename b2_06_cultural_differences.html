Cultural Differences

Understanding behaviour in international business contexts

Opening Discussion

Let's explore cultural misunderstandings and how to navigate them.

Scenario 1: Imagine you are at an international conference. What cultural misunderstandings could occur?

Misunderstandings might include: different attitudes to punctuality, varying levels of formality in greetings, differences in eye contact customs, diverse views on personal space, varying attitudes toward gift-giving, different communication styles (direct vs. indirect), and different expectations about socialising.

International Scenarios

How would you navigate these cultural situations?

Dining and Social Situations

Scenario A: Your new American boss has invited you to her house for dinner.
In American culture, a home dinner invitation is usually casual and friendly. Bring flowers or wine as a gift. The atmosphere is typically informal. Dress smartly but not formally. Expect to arrive on time or within 15 minutes of the stated time.
Scenario B: A group of colleagues from your Delhi office is visiting next week.
In Indian business culture, showing respect is important. A bow or a slight head nod is respectful. Be aware of dietary preferences and restrictions. Hierarchical structures are significant, so acknowledge senior staff appropriately. Meetings may start late—flexibility and patience are valued.
Scenario C: The Berlin conference starts at 10:30.
In German business culture, punctuality is extremely important. Arrive at least 10 minutes early. Formal handshakes are standard. Use surnames unless invited to use first names. Business cards are exchanged formally. Meetings tend to follow strict agendas.

Business Meetings and Interactions

Scenario D: You're meeting New York colleagues at the office.
American business culture values efficiency and directness. Expect a firm handshake and informal first-name basis. Meetings are usually to-the-point. Direct eye contact shows confidence and honesty. Business is often mixed with friendliness.
Scenario E: Lunch with your British boss.
British business culture values politeness and understatement. Ask about dietary requirements beforehand. Formality is common in initial meetings but relaxes over time. Avoid discussing personal topics initially. Punctuality is important but a few minutes late is acceptable.
Scenario F: Japanese delegates are at reception now.
In Japanese business culture, respect and formality are paramount. A bow (approximately 15 degrees) is the traditional greeting. Exchange business cards with both hands and study the card respectfully. Avoid direct disagreement. Hierarchy is very important. Silence in conversation is normal and not uncomfortable.

Giving Strong and Tentative Advice

Learn to express advice at different levels of strength depending on cultural situations.

Pair Practice Activity

Work through these scenarios using the advice patterns below.

How to give tentative (softer) advice:
You might want to think about + -ing form

Example: You might want to think about giving her flowers.

Perhaps you should + base form

Example: Perhaps you should consider bowing when you meet them.

You might + base form

Example: You might think about what to wear.

How to give strong advice:
You need to + base form

Example: You need to get there on time.

Make sure (that) + clause

Example: Make sure you ask about dietary requirements.

One thing you ought never to do is + base form

Example: One thing you ought never to do is put your knife in your mouth.

Sample Answers

You might want to think about giving her some flowers.

Make sure you ask about their dietary requirements.

You need to get there on time.

It's probably not a good idea to dress very formally.

One thing you ought never to do is put your knife in your mouth.

Perhaps you should consider bowing when you meet them.

Using Stress to Emphasise Advice

Making advice stronger or weaker using word stress and intonation.

Underline the words which can be stressed to make the advice stronger

1. You might want to think about what to wear.

Stress "might" to emphasise the suggestion. This makes it stronger: "You MIGHT want to think about what to wear."

2. But remember! Make sure it's a firm handshake to show that you are interested.

Stress "remember" and "sure" strongly. This emphasises importance: "But REMEMBER! MAKE SURE it's a firm handshake to show you are interested."

3. Arriving late is one thing you ought never to do.

Stress "ought" and "never" with strong emphasis. This creates a warning tone: "Arriving late is one thing you OUGHT NEVER to do."

4. You need to be aware of the pitfalls.

Stress "need" strongly to emphasise urgency and importance: "You NEED to be aware of the pitfalls."

Key Point

Stressing certain words in advice sentences can make your advice sound more urgent, important, or emphatic. Varying your stress and intonation helps you communicate the right level of certainty and importance depending on the situation.

Cultural Presentation Task

Prepare a short presentation about behaviour in your country for international visitors.

Preparation

A colleague from another continent is visiting your country. You need to give advice on behaviour. Prepare a short presentation thinking about these key areas:

  • Greetings: How do people greet each other? (handshake, bow, cheek kiss, etc.)
  • Eating: What are important dining etiquette points?
  • Punctuality: How important is being on time?
  • Gifts: What gifts are appropriate or inappropriate?
  • Clothing: What dress codes are important in different contexts?

Preparation Time

5:00

Presentation

Present your presentation to the group. Listen to their ideas and feedback:

  • Do you agree with their observations?
  • Is there anything you would like to add?
  • Did they mention any aspect you hadn't considered?

Key Takeaways

Language Patterns for Giving Advice ▼

Tentative advice:

  • You might want to think about...
  • Perhaps you should...
  • You might...

Strong advice:

  • You need to...
  • Make sure...
  • One thing you ought never to do is...
Cultural Awareness Points ▼
  • Different cultures value punctuality differently
  • Greetings vary widely (handshakes, bows, kisses)
  • Dining etiquette is culturally specific
  • Gift-giving has different meanings across cultures
  • Formal vs. informal communication styles differ globally
  • Personal space and eye contact customs vary
  • Showing respect takes different forms in different cultures
