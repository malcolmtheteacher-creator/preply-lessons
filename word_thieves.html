Key words

Anglo-Saxon (noun) — the early form of English, also called Old English. Example: "Everyday words like day and friend come from Anglo-Saxon."
native speaker (noun) — someone who learned a language as their first language in childhood. Example: "Most people who speak English are not native speakers."
loanword (noun) — a word taken from one language and used in another. Example: "Karaoke is a Japanese loanword in English."
etymology (noun) — the history of where a word comes from. Example: "The etymology of the word window is surprisingly poetic."
mother tongue (noun) — the first language you learn as a child. Example: "Her mother tongue is Polish, but she dreams in English."
derive from (verb) — to come from something older. Example: "Many English legal terms derive from French."
conquest (noun) — taking control of a country by force. Example: "After the Norman Conquest, French became the language of power."
loot (verb) — to steal things during a war or a riot. Example: "Soldiers looted the palace and carried away its treasures."
lingua franca (noun) — a language used between people who have different first languages. Example: "English is the lingua franca of international business."
untranslatable (adjective) — impossible to express exactly in another language. Example: "Every language has a few beautiful, untranslatable words."
coin a word (verb) — to invent a new word or phrase. Example: "Nobody knows who first coined that expression."
borrow a word (verb) — to take a word from another language and use it in your own. Example: "English borrowed the word coffee by way of Arabic and Turkish."
