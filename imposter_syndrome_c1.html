The Imposter in the Room

Why successful people feel like frauds

C1 | 45-50 minutes

The Secret Doubt of Successful People

Maya Angelou wrote 11 books, won multiple awards, and was celebrated around the world. Yet she once said: "Each time I write a book, I think 'Uh oh, they're going to find out now. I've run a game on everybody, and they're going to find me out.'" Sound familiar?

84% of entrepreneurs and business owners experience imposter syndrome Kajabi Study, 2024
70% of all people will experience imposter syndrome at some point in their lives International Journal of Behavioral Science
59% of business leaders have considered leaving their job because of self-doubt NerdWallet Survey, 2022

The Paradox: More Success = More Doubt?

Barbara Corcoran

Entrepreneur, Shark Tank Investor (sold her company for $66 million)

"I still sometimes wonder if my success was just a fluke, a lucky break. But here's the secret: that imposter feeling is actually a strength. It drives me to find gaps in my knowledge. On Shark Tank, I look for entrepreneurs who question themselves - because I know they'll work twice as hard. Real imposters don't feel like imposters."

Howard Schultz

Former CEO, Starbucks

"Very few people, whether you've been in that job before or not, get into the seat and believe today that they are now qualified to be the CEO. They're not going to tell you that, but it's true."

"The beauty of imposter syndrome is you vacillate between extreme egomania and a complete feeling of: 'I'm a fraud! Oh God, they're on to me!'"

- Tina Fey, Actress and Writer

The Science Behind the Feeling

The Dunning-Kruger Paradox The more you know, the more you realise how much you don't know. Experts see the ocean of unknowns; beginners see only a puddle.
The Comparison Trap We compare our internal doubts to others' external confidence. We see their highlight reel; we feel our behind-the-scenes chaos.

Research Finding

Small Business Research, 2025

"The top triggers for imposter syndrome among business leaders are: starting a new role or business (57%) and receiving praise in front of colleagues (55%). Ironically, success itself can intensify the feeling - the higher you climb, the further you feel you could fall."

Here's the question that matters: If 84% of successful business owners feel like frauds, perhaps feeling like a fraud is actually evidence that you're doing something right?

Language Focus A

Click each expression to explore. These are sophisticated phrases for discussing confidence, self-perception, and success.

to give yourself credit (for something)

+
MEANING

To acknowledge and appreciate your own achievements; to recognise that you deserve praise for something you've done. Often used in the negative when someone fails to do this.

FORM

Subject + give + yourself/oneself + credit + (for + noun/gerund)

Common patterns: "You never give yourself credit" / "Give yourself credit for how far you've come"

REGISTER

Neutral to warm. Often used in supportive conversations, coaching, and self-help contexts. Natural in professional and personal discussions.

"You built this company from nothing. Give yourself credit for that."
"She never gives herself credit for her achievements - she always says it was luck or timing."
NOW YOU TRY

Think of something you've achieved but tend to minimise. Now, give yourself credit for it out loud. What makes this difficult to do?

to brush something off

+
MEANING

To dismiss something casually; to treat something as unimportant or not worthy of attention. Can refer to compliments, criticism, concerns, or achievements.

FORM

Subject + brush off + object OR Subject + brush + object + off

Both work: "She brushed off the compliment" = "She brushed the compliment off"

REGISTER

Informal to neutral. Very common in everyday English. Useful for describing how people respond (or fail to respond) to feedback.

"When clients praise her work, she just brushes it off and changes the subject."
"Don't brush off the warning signs. They matter."
NOW YOU TRY

What kind of feedback do you tend to brush off? Compliments? Criticism? Concerns from others? Why?

to live up to (expectations / a reputation / standards)

+
MEANING

To meet the standards or expectations that others (or you yourself) have set. Often used when discussing pressure to perform or maintain a certain level.

FORM

Subject + live up to + noun (expectations / reputation / standards / hype)

Often negative: "I'm afraid I won't live up to..." or "It didn't live up to..."

REGISTER

Universal - works in all contexts from casual to formal. Essential vocabulary for discussing performance and pressure.

"After such a successful first year, I constantly worry about living up to my own reputation."
"The film didn't live up to the hype - everyone was disappointed."
NOW YOU TRY

What expectations do you feel pressure to live up to? Are they your own expectations or someone else's?

Language Focus B

Two more expressions that perfectly capture the imposter experience.

to be your own worst critic

+
MEANING

To judge yourself more harshly than anyone else would; to be harder on yourself than others are. A defining characteristic of high achievers and people with imposter syndrome.

FORM

Subject + be + (possessive) own worst critic

"I am my own worst critic" / "She's her own worst critic" / "We're our own worst critics"

REGISTER

Neutral to slightly informal. Common in self-reflection, interviews, and discussions about personality. Shows self-awareness when used about yourself.

"Everyone loved the presentation, but I'm my own worst critic - all I could see were the mistakes."
"Stop being your own worst critic. You're doing better than you think."
NOW YOU TRY

In what area of your life are you most clearly your own worst critic? Is this helpful or harmful?

to fake it till you make it

+
MEANING

To act confident or competent even when you don't feel it, with the idea that eventually the confidence will become real. A common strategy - and a source of guilt for some.

FORM

Fake it till/until you make it (fixed expression)

Often used as advice: "Just fake it till you make it!" or as description: "I've been faking it till I make it for years."

REGISTER

Informal. Very common in business, entrepreneurship, and self-help contexts. Some see it as practical wisdom; others see it as encouragement to be inauthentic.

"In my first client meetings, I was terrified. But I learned to fake it till I made it, and eventually the confidence became real."
"I don't believe in 'fake it till you make it' - I think it just makes imposter syndrome worse."
NOW YOU TRY

What's your opinion on "fake it till you make it"? Is it good advice or dangerous advice? Have you ever done it?

Key Insights

The Paradox: Imposter syndrome is most common among high achievers. If you feel like a fraud, you're probably in good company.

The Science: The Dunning-Kruger effect explains why: the more expert you become, the more you see how much you don't know.

The Reframe: As Barbara Corcoran says - "Real imposters don't feel like imposters." Your doubt might actually be evidence of your competence.
