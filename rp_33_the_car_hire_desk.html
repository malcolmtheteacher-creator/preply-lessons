The Car-Hire Desk

You booked the car in March. Paid in full. It said so in the email. And yet here at the desk, somehow, everything is still for sale — the cover, the upgrade, the tank of fuel. Fifteen minutes between you and the road.

The Scene 🎬

The one counter in travel where the price you paid is treated as an opening offer.

Airport arrivals, holiday finally begun. The car-hire desk is the last obstacle: a queue of pale families, a rack of key fobs, and Dario, who is friendly, quick — and paid partly on what he sells you at this desk.

None of what's coming is a scam, exactly. The extra cover is real cover. The excess — the amount you'd pay yourself before insurance covers the rest — is really a thousand euros. Dario will describe that thousand euros vividly. What he won't mention is that you can decline everything, hand over the card, and drive away with exactly what you booked in March.

Two skills, then: asking what things actually cost and include, before deciding — and saying no twice, warmly, because the first no is never the end of it. Plus one habit that outranks both: walking round the car before you drive it, and getting every scratch in writing.

  1. Why do we find it so hard to say no to someone friendly? What is the friendliness for?
  2. "You'd only pay if something happened." When is peace of mind worth buying — and when is it just fear with a card machine?
YouBooked, paid, confirmation email saved on your phone. Wants the road, not a debate — but won't be rushed past a signature.
DarioCharming, quick, on commission. Every objection you raise, he's heard today already. Respects the customers who ask real questions.
First: read the conversation and watch what You does with every offer: never argues, never gets cross — just asks what it includes, what it costs, and then decides. The questions are the armour.

The Conversation 💬

The desk. Dario has your booking on screen and a laminated card of options face-down beside him.

DarioWelcome! Licence and card, please… beautiful. A week with the little Corsa, yes?
YouThat's it. I've got a booking under Weber — everything should be paid up front.
DarioAll paid, yes. Now — quick but important. The booking comes with basic cover only. If anything happens to the car, you are liable for the first one thousand euros. One small scratch in a car park — boom. For twelve euros a day, you drive away with zero excess. Complete peace of mind.
YouHold on — before anything else. What exactly does the basic cover include?
DarioCollision damage, theft, third party — the serious things. Just with the excess.
YouAnd what would I actually pay if something happened? The first thousand, and nothing above that?
DarioCorrect — the maximum you'd ever pay is the thousand. But for twelve a day—
YouTwelve a day for a week is more than eighty euros against a risk I may never meet. I'll take my chances with the standard cover, thanks.
DarioI understand. But may I say — the car parks here are a war zone. Last week a customer, one shopping trolley, seven hundred euros. For you I could do nine a day.
YouYou've talked me through it, and I'm still happy as we are.
DarioOkay! You are sure, I like it. Fuel: the tank is full. Easiest option — buy this tank from us now, bring it back empty. No stress at the airport.
YouWhat's the arrangement otherwise — full to full?
DarioFull to full, yes. There's a petrol station two minutes from the terminal.
YouThen full to full, please. I'd rather pay for the fuel I actually use.
DarioAs you wish. Sign here, initial here and here… and the car is in bay twelve.
YouTwo things before I go. The return's at six on Saturday — is there any leeway on the return time? My flight's not till ten.
DarioOfficially no. Unofficially — up to twenty-nine minutes, nobody looks. After that the system charges a day.
YouGood to know. And I'll walk round the car now — I'd like any scratches marked on the form before I take it.
DarioOf course. Take photos also — honestly, everyone should.
YouThere's one on the rear bumper, look — and a chip on the windscreen. Mark both, please. Can I get a copy of that once it's signed?
DarioPhotographed, marked, copied. You have done this before, I think.
YouOnce the expensive way. That all seems fair — where do I sign?

From memory — don't scroll back up.

  2. Dario drops his price once. From what to what — and what does You say to the second offer?
  3. What two flaws does You find on the car, and what happens to them?

The Echo 🕰️

The Greeks had camped outside Troy for ten years and taken nothing. Then one morning the beach was empty — the army gone, the ships gone, and standing at the gates a magnificent wooden horse, left as a gift. The Trojans debated. One priest, Laocoön, begged them to check it: I fear Greeks, even bearing gifts.

They didn't check. The horse was free, it was flattering, and after ten years everyone badly wanted the war to be over — which is exactly the state of mind free gifts are designed for. They rolled it through the gates themselves. That night the soldiers hidden inside opened Troy from within, and the city that ten years of siege couldn't take was finished by an upgrade nobody had questioned.

Three thousand years on, the horse still turns up wherever things are signed in a hurry — bigger, shinier, free, and needing only your yes. Laocoön's advice still stands, and at a hire desk it costs one question: what does this gift include?
