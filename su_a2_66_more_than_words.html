Who Looks Confident?

Look at these descriptions of two people giving a presentation. Which one seems more confident?

Person A

Slouches at the front. Looks down at notes the whole time. Speaks very quietly. Doesn't smile. Stands still in one spot. Rarely moves hands.

Person B

Stands straight. Makes eye contact with the audience. Speaks clearly and loudly enough to hear. Smiles naturally. Moves around the stage. Uses hand gestures to explain ideas.

Person B looks more confident. Body language, eye contact, voice, and movement all show confidence. These things matter more than we think!

The 4 Ps of Vocal Delivery

These four things control how powerful your voice sounds:

1. Power (Volume)

Speak loud enough for everyone to hear, but not so loud that you shout. A confident speaker uses their full voice without being aggressive.

2. Pause (Silence)

Don't be afraid of silence! Pauses let your audience think about what you said. They also give you time to breathe and stay calm. Pauses feel longer to you than they do to your listener.

3. Pace (Speed)

Nervous speakers often talk too fast. Slow down. Speaking at a natural pace helps people understand you. Mix it up — speed up for exciting ideas, slow down for important points.

4. Pitch (Tone)

Don't speak in a flat, boring voice. Vary your pitch to show emotion and keep people interested. Questions go UP at the end. Statements come DOWN.

Stress Shift Exercise

Say this sentence out loud 4 times. Each time, stress (emphasize) a different word. Notice how the meaning changes:

I didn't eat your cake.
Meaning: Someone else ate it, not me.
I didn't eat your cake.
Meaning: I did something else with it instead.
I didn't eat your cake.
Meaning: I did something else with it (like destroyed it).
I didn't eat your cake.
Meaning: I ate someone else"s cake, not yours.

Practice: Voice Only & Gestures Only

Try these two exercises:

Exercise 1: Gestures Only

Say this sentence using only hand gestures and facial expressions. No voice. Can your partner understand you?

"The weather was hot, so I drank a cold drink."

Exercise 2: Voice Only

Now describe the same situation using only your voice. Keep your body still — no gestures. What happens?

The best presenters use both! Gestures show energy and help people understand. Voice carries meaning and emotion. Together, they make your message clear and memorable.

Plan Your Presentation

You have 1 minute to talk about your job. Plan what you will say, and think about how you will use your body and voice to look confident.

Step 1: Write Your Main Points

What 3 or 4 main ideas will you cover? Write them here:

Step 2: Plan Your Body Language

Posture: Will you sit or stand? How will you position your body?

Eye Contact & Gestures: When will you move? What will you use your hands to show?

Step 3: Plan Your Vocal Delivery

Pace: Where will you slow down? (Important points to emphasize)

Pauses: Where will you take pauses to let your meaning sink in?

Pitch & Volume: How will you show energy? Will you raise your voice for important ideas?

Step 4: Practice & Record

Now give your 1-minute presentation. Use your plan. If possible, record yourself so you can watch it and check:

  • Do you look confident?
  • Is your body language natural?
  • Can people understand you?
  • Does your voice show energy?

Think & Discuss

These questions help you think about non-verbal communication and how it affects your message:

1. Your Confident Image

Think about someone you know who seems confident when they speak. What do they do? How do they stand, look, and sound?

2. Body Language & Meaning

Can your audience understand your message if your body language disagrees with your words? For example, what if you say "I love my job" but you look bored?

3. Nervous Habits

What do you do when you are nervous? Do you talk too fast? Avoid eye contact? Forget to move? How can you fix these habits?

4. Your Biggest Challenge

Is it posture? Eye contact? Voice variety? Gestures? What is the hardest part for you?

5. Practice & Feedback

After you give your job presentation, ask your partner: "What did I do well? What could I improve?" Listen carefully to their feedback.

Remember

Non-verbal communication is just as important as what you say. Confident body language and vocal delivery make your message stronger. Practice these skills, and you will improve.
