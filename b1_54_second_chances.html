Some Numbers

Research from around the world tells us some interesting things about change:

66 days average time to form a new habit (University College London, 2009)
70% of people who get support and education after making mistakes don't repeat them (2026 meta-analysis)
5x people are five times more likely to change with help from others than alone

Three Real Stories

Read each story. Pay attention to the words in purple — you'll need them later.

Marcus — London, UK

Marcus grew up on a difficult housing estate in south London. He dropped out of school at 15 and got into trouble with the police several times. At 19, he spent six months in a young offenders' institution.

But a mentor there encouraged him to try cooking. He discovered he loved it. After he got out, he enrolled in a catering college. Today, at 34, Marcus runs a restaurant in Brixton and employs other young people who have had difficult starts. He says: "Someone believed in me when I didn't believe in myself. That's what changed everything."

SPEAK: Without looking at the text — tell the story of Marcus in your own words. What happened to him? What changed?

Ana — São Paulo, Brazil

Ana was a successful lawyer, but she was deeply unhappy. She suffered from anxiety and depression for years, and she relied on alcohol to cope. She lost her job, her apartment, and most of her friends.

At her lowest point, she reached out to a free counselling service. With the help of a therapist, she slowly rebuilt her life. She went back to university to study psychology. Now she works as a counsellor herself, helping people with addiction. She has been sober for seven years.

SPEAK: Tell Ana's story from memory. What was her life like before? What helped her change?

Tomás — Medellín, Colombia

Tomás used to be part of a street gang. He saw no future for himself. At 17, a teacher at a community centre offered him a place in an art programme. He didn't want to go, but he had nothing else to do.

Art became his way of understanding his feelings. He learned to express anger and sadness through painting instead of violence. Today, Tomás is 28 and has turned his life around. He teaches art to teenagers in difficult neighbourhoods and has had exhibitions in galleries across Colombia.

SPEAK: Tell Tomás's story. Then: What do Marcus, Ana, and Tomás have in common? What helped all three of them?

What Do These Stories Tell Us?

SPEAK: Look at the three stories together. What factors helped these people change? Can you find at least three things they have in common?
Show Ideas
Common factors: Someone believed in them (a mentor, a therapist, a teacher). They found a purpose or passion (cooking, psychology, art). They got practical support (education, counselling, a programme). Change didn't happen overnight — it took time and effort.

Key Vocabulary

Click each card to see the meaning. Then try to make a sentence with it.

to drop out (of) To leave school or a programme before finishing — "He dropped out of school at 15."
to get into trouble To do something wrong and have problems because of it — "She got into trouble at work."
to reach out (to) To ask someone for help — "He reached out to a counsellor when things got bad."
to rebuild your life To start again after a difficult time — "She slowly rebuilt her life after losing everything."
to turn your life around To completely change your life from bad to good — "He has turned his life around."
to rely on To need and depend on something or someone — "She relied on alcohol to cope."
to enrol (in) To officially join a course or programme — "He enrolled in a catering college."
to suffer from To experience something painful for a long time — "She suffered from anxiety for years."

Grammar in the Stories

The stories use two important structures to talk about change. Can you spot the difference?

Past Simple — for finished actions and stories

"He dropped out of school at 15."

"She lost her job and her apartment."

"A teacher offered him a place in an art programme."

We use past simple when the action is finished and we know when it happened.

Present Perfect — for life changes connected to now

"She has been sober for seven years." (= she is still sober now)

"He has turned his life around." (= his life is different now)

"He has had exhibitions in galleries." (= from the past until now)

We use present perfect when the change started in the past and is still true now.

SPEAK: Complete these sentences about the people from the stories. Use past simple or present perfect:

1. Marcus _______ (spend) six months in a young offenders' institution.
2. Ana _______ (be) sober for seven years.
3. Tomás _______ (use to) be part of a street gang.
4. Marcus now _______ (run) a restaurant in London.
5. Ana _______ (go back) to university to study psychology.
Check Answers
1. Marcus spent six months... (finished past action)
2. Ana has been sober for seven years. (still true now)
3. Tomás used to be part of a street gang. (past habit, no longer true)
4. Marcus now runs a restaurant. (present simple — his current situation)
5. Ana went back to university. (finished past action)

Discussion Cards

Choose 3 cards. For each one, speak for at least one minute. Give your opinion and explain why.

1

Support vs Punishment

When someone makes a serious mistake, what helps them change more — punishment or support? Think about the stories you read. What worked for Marcus, Ana, and Tomás?

2

The Role of Other People

The research says people are 5 times more likely to change with help. Why do you think other people are so important? Can anyone really change completely alone?

3

Art, Education, or Therapy?

Marcus found cooking. Ana found therapy. Tomás found art. Which do you think is most powerful for helping people change — learning a new skill, talking to a professional, or creative expression?

4

Trust

If someone you know has lied to you or let you down in the past, but says they've changed — how do you know if you can trust them? What would you need to see?

5

Children vs Adults

Is it easier to change when you're young or when you're older? Why? At what age is it hardest to change your behaviour?

6

Society's Responsibility

Should society help people who have made serious mistakes to rebuild their lives? Or should people be responsible for fixing their own problems? Where is the line?
