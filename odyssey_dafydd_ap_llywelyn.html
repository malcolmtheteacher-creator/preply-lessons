The words for today's story

1. Gruffydd was his elder half-brother.
a half-brother (n) — a brother with one shared parent.
2. Gruffydd was imprisoned.
to imprison (v) — to put in prison.
3. He made a rope from sheets.
a rope (n) — a thick cord.
4. His claim was recognised.
to recognise (v) — to accept officially.
5. He was held as a hostage.
a hostage (n) — a person held to make someone keep a promise.
6. Dafydd had no heir.
an heir (n) — a person who inherits.
7. He had to surrender land.
to surrender (v) — to give up.
8. He used the title "Prince of Wales".
a title (n) — a name showing rank.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The chosen son

Dafydd was the son of Llywelyn the Great and Joan. Although his half-brother Gruffydd was older, Llywelyn chose Dafydd as heir, partly because his mother was royal. When Llywelyn died in 1240, Dafydd was recognised by Henry III — but Gruffydd was imprisoned by Dafydd.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why was Dafydd chosen?

2. What happened to Gruffydd?

1. Partly because his mother was King John's daughter.

2. He was imprisoned.

Discuss:
  • Is it fair to choose a younger son?

Part 2 — The Tower

In 1241 Henry III invaded and forced Dafydd to hand over Gruffydd. Gruffydd was taken to the Tower of London. On 1 March 1244 he tried to escape by climbing down a rope made of knotted sheets. It broke, and he fell to his death.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where was Gruffydd taken?

2. How did he die?

1. To the Tower of London.

2. His rope of sheets broke during an escape.

Discuss:
  • Why would Henry want Gruffydd?

Part 3 — Prince of Wales

Freed of his rival, Dafydd rebelled and styled himself "Prince of Wales" — the first Welsh ruler known to use the title. He even asked the Pope for support. But he died in February 1246, without children, and his nephew Llywelyn ap Gruffydd — son of the brother who fell — took over.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What title did Dafydd use?

2. Who succeeded him?

1. Prince of Wales.

2. His nephew Llywelyn ap Gruffydd.

Discuss:
  • Why might a title matter so much?

Grammar — The Passive Voice

Historians often care more about what was done than who did it — or they simply do not know who did it. That is the job of the passive.

How it works:
• Form: be + past participle: "The king was crowned at Westminster."
• Add by only when the doer matters: "The castle was built by Norman masons."
• Reporting passives hedge a legend: "He is said to have…", "It was believed that…"

1. Make it passive

Complete each sentence about Dafydd ap Llywelyn. Use the word in brackets.

1. Dafydd (choose) as heir.

2. Gruffydd (imprison) by his brother.

3. Gruffydd (take) to the Tower.

1. was chosen

2. was imprisoned

3. was taken

2. Choose the right passive form

1. The rope (make) from sheets.

2. Dafydd's claim (recognise) by Henry in 1240.

3. The title (use) by him for the first time.

1. was made

2. was recognised

3. was used

Going deeper

Sibling rivalry repeatedly weakened Welsh rulers, giving English kings the chance to divide and rule.

Worth noticing: Gruffydd's son became Llywelyn ap Gruffydd, "the Last", the next lesson.
Learning from the past — for today: Rivalry within a team helps your competitors. When colleagues compete instead of cooperate, an outsider usually wins. Settle internal disagreements before facing outside challenges.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Was Dafydd right to imprison Gruffydd?
  • Why do titles matter?
  • When has internal rivalry hurt your team?
