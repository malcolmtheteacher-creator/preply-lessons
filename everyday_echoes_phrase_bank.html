Your Phrase Toolkit

Every phrase you meet in the role plays, gathered in one place and sorted not by situation but by what it does — so when you need to ask, soften, complain or thank, you know exactly where to reach.

How to use it. Don't read it like a list — raid it. Before your next real phone call, email or awkward conversation, find the job you need doing below, borrow the phrase, and say it out loud until it feels like yours. The same handful of moves — asking gently, complaining without a fight, standing your ground warmly — works in almost any situation in English.

🚪 Announcing yourself

Sounding assured and organised from your very first sentence.

"I've got a reservation under the name of…"
The confident, standard way to say who you are and that you've booked — a hotel, a restaurant, a table, a slot.
Checking into a Hotel
"And while you've got my details up…"
Slips in an extra request while the other person is already looking at your booking — so it feels like no trouble at all.

🌟 Asking for a favour

Getting a "yes" by making it easy — and generous — to give one.

"I don't suppose there's any chance of…, is there?"
The least pushy request in English. It half-expects a no, which makes refusing painless and saying yes feel kind.

"…if it's not too much trouble."
Hangs an easy exit onto any request — which, oddly, makes people more willing to help. Tack it on the end.

"Would it be at all possible to…?"
One of the most useful frames in the language. Fits anything: store our bags, move the meeting, get a quieter table.

🌧️ Opening a complaint — gently

Naming the problem so people help you, instead of getting defensive.

"There seems to be a bit of a problem with…"
"Seems to be" describes rather than accuses; "a bit of a problem" downplays it. Understatement gets you help, not a fight.

"The … doesn't seem to be working."
Reports a fault while leaving a polite sliver of doubt — maybe it's you, maybe the switch — so no finger is pointed.

"Sorry to trouble you again, but I'm afraid there's a problem with…"
Acknowledges you're interrupting and cushions the bad news, so they help gladly rather than bracing for a row.

🤝 Pressing gently — standing your ground

Insisting you're reasonable while you push for what's fair.

"I don't mean to be difficult, but…"
Pre-empts the fear that you're a nightmare, so what follows reads as fair, not a tantrum. Keeps them on your side.

💛 Closing warmly

The goodbye that makes the other person glad they helped you.

"You've handled it so graciously — I've half a mind to…"
Praises the person, not just the result, and "I've half a mind to…" is a lovely warm way to say you're genuinely tempted.
