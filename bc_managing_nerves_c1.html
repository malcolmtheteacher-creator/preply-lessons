Stress Reduction Techniques

When you feel nervous or panicked during a speech, use these techniques to calm down.

Deep Breathing

Slow, controlled breathing calms your nervous system. Try breathing in for 4 counts, holding for 4, and exhaling for 4.

Reframing Your Beliefs

Instead of "I'm nervous," say "I'm excited." Replace fear with anticipation. Nervousness and excitement feel similar—choose a positive interpretation.

Positive Visualization

Before your speech, close your eyes and imagine yourself delivering confidently. See the audience smiling and engaged. Picture success.

Manage "Flight or Fight"

Your body releases stress hormones when nervous. Acknowledge this physical response as your body preparing to perform, not a sign of danger.

Use Your Space

Move around purposefully. Physical movement helps burn nervous energy and makes you feel more grounded and present.

Audience Connection

Remember your audience wants you to succeed. Make eye contact, smile, and connect with individuals. This shifts focus from your anxiety to your message.

Your Calm-Down Plan

Choose three techniques that appeal to you. Practice them before your next presentation so they feel natural when you need them.

Eight Key Rehearsal Techniques

Full preparation significantly reduces anxiety. Here are the eight techniques matched to their descriptions:

Technique What It Means
Rehearse Practice your speech using the same format as speech day (cue cards, notes, scripted, or by memory)
Gestures Video yourself with your phone to assess your body language. Break it down: hand movement, emphasizing, balancing
Visuals Practice giving the speech with any technology you plan to use. Dress in what you'll wear on speech day
Environment Try to practice in the actual venue. Imitate the lighting and the technology you'll use
Feedback Ask yourself specific questions: What do I like most/least? What's missing? How's the start? How's my body language?
Timing Practice to a clock. If you go over time, identify which area needs reducing and practice again
Vocals Record yourself giving the speech a few times. Measure vocal improvement: volume, intonation, pausing, stress
Keep Going On speech day, if you make mistakes, aim to fix them the next time without stopping

Which techniques are most important?

All of them matter, but start with:

  1. Rehearse — Know your material inside out
  2. Timing — Stay within limits to feel in control
  3. Environment — Practice where you'll perform if possible

Speaking Task: Your Preparation Strategy

Time Remaining
10:00

Your Task

Speak for 10 minutes about how you manage nerves before public speaking. Use specific techniques and examples from your experience.

Structure Your Talk

  1. Your Experience (2 min): Describe how you feel before giving a speech. Where does your fear come from?
  2. Stress Reduction (3 min): Choose two stress-reduction techniques and explain how they help you. Give examples.
  3. Rehearsal Strategies (3 min): Talk about which rehearsal techniques you use or plan to use. Why are they effective?
  4. Your Action Plan (2 min): Describe what you will do differently for your next speech.

Discussion Questions to Help You

  • Are you afraid of speaking in front of other people in a public setting?
  • What definitely doesn't work to calm your nerves?
  • Do you think famous speakers feel nervous? Why or why not?
  • Describe the most nerve-wracking experience in your life so far.
