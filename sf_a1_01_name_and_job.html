Grammar: I'm / Are you…?

In a first conversation you say who you are. English uses the verb be: am and are.

I'm = I am. Use it for your name, your country and your job: "I'm Sara." "I'm from Mexico." "I'm a nurse."
Are you…? = a question. Answer Yes, I am or No, I'm not: "Are you from Italy?" — "No, I'm not. I'm from Poland."
Question words: "What's your name?" (What's = What is) · "Where are you from?" · "What do you do?" (= your job)
a or an? a nurse, a driver — but an engineer, an office. Use an before a, e, i, o, u.
