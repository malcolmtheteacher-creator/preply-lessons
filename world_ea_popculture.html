The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. The K-pop fandom organises events all over the world.
fandom (n) — a community of fans of a person, group or show. Example: “The K-pop fandom organises events all over the world.”
2. Many people said the Korean Wave was just a craze.
craze (n) — something that is very popular for a short time. Example: “Many people said the Korean Wave was just a craze.”
3. Entertainment is now one of South Korea's most important exports.
export (n) — something that is sold to other countries. Example: “Entertainment is now one of South Korea's most important exports.”
4. Many viewers learned to enjoy films with subtitles.
subtitle (n) — words at the bottom of a screen that show what people say in a film. Example: “Many viewers learned to enjoy films with subtitles.”
5. Streaming helped films cross borders quickly.
streaming (n) — playing music or video over the internet, without downloading it. Example: “Streaming helped films cross borders quickly.”
6. K-pop trainees practise singing and dancing for years.
trainee (n) — a person who is learning a job by doing it. Example: “K-pop trainees practise singing and dancing for years.”
7. Manga covers every genre, from sport to cooking.
genre (n) — a type of music, film or writing. Example: “Manga covers every genre, from sport to cooking.”
8. Pop culture gives a country soft power.
soft power (n) — the ability of a country to influence others through culture rather than force. Example: “Pop culture gives a country soft power.”

The stereotype: “East Asian pop culture is just cute cartoons and K-pop for teenagers — a passing craze.” Let's find out what's really true.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The Korean Wave

In the late 1990s Korean dramas and pop music became popular in China, Japan and Southeast Asia, and Chinese media called it hallyu — the Korean Wave. South Korea's government and companies invested in culture after the 1997 financial crisis. In 2012 “Gangnam Style” became the first YouTube video to reach a billion views. In 2020 BTS's “Dynamite” reached number one on the US Billboard Hot 100, and Parasite became the first non-English-language film to win the Oscar for Best Picture.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What does hallyu mean?

2. Which film won Best Picture in 2020?

1. The Korean Wave.

2. Parasite.

Discuss:
  • Why might a government invest in popular culture?

Part 2 — Anime and manga

Japan's anime and manga are enjoyed by all ages, and manga covers every subject from sport and cooking to business and history. Studio Ghibli's Spirited Away won the Oscar for Best Animated Feature in 2003, and Hayao Miyazaki won it again in 2024 with The Boy and the Heron. Many fans learned some Japanese just to understand their favourite series.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Which genres does manga cover?

2. Which Ghibli film won an Oscar in 2003?

1. Every genre, from sport and cooking to business and history.

2. Spirited Away.

Discuss:
  • Which cartoon or comic did you love as a child, and why?

Part 3 — Beyond the craze

Chinese online novels, television dramas and video games such as Genshin Impact and Black Myth: Wukong now reach worldwide audiences. The K-pop training system, in which young people practise for years, has also been criticised for long hours and heavy pressure. So “just a craze for teenagers” does not describe it: the audiences are of every age and nationality, and the industry has strengths and serious problems like any other.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

2. What has been criticised about the K-pop training system?

1. Online novels, television dramas and video games.

2. The long hours and heavy pressure.

Discuss:
  • What might make a trend last for decades instead of a few months?

Grammar — Past Modals of Deduction — Must Have, Can't Have, Might Have

A stereotype and the reality rarely match. This lesson's grammar gives you the structure to describe the difference.

How it works:
Deduction about the past (be + past participle):
• must have + past participle: “It must have spread through social media.” (almost sure)
• can't / couldn't have + past participle: “It can't have been just a craze, because it lasted for decades.” (almost sure it is not true)
• might / may / could have + past participle: “The government support might have helped.” (possible)

1. Choose the right form

Complete each sentence about this topic.

1. The Korean Wave (spread) through social media, because it grew so fast.

2. It (be) just a craze, because it has lasted for decades.

3. Government investment (help), but we can't be sure.

1. must have spread

2. can't have been (or couldn't have been)

3. might have helped (or may / could have helped)

2. Now try these

1. Many fans (learn) Korean to understand the songs, so the language must have become more popular.

2. Parasite (win) the Oscar without good reviews — it must have impressed critics.

3. Streaming services (play) a part in the spread of anime, but there were other reasons.

1. must have learned

2. can't have won (or couldn't have won)

3. might have played (or may / could have played)

Going deeper

Pop culture spreads along roads that other industries build. Cheap, fast internet, social media and streaming services let a song or series cross borders in a day. Subtitles and fan translations helped even more. At the same time, a powerful idea is at work: people everywhere enjoy stories about love, friendship and growing up, and they enjoy them even more when the setting is unfamiliar and the quality is high.

Worth noticing: Deduction about the past: must have + past participle (almost sure), can't / couldn't have (almost sure it is not true), might / may / could have (possible).
For today — practical cross-cultural tips: When you meet someone who loves a series, singer or game from another country, ask what they like about it and what they have learned from it. It is a friendly way to start a conversation, and fans are usually generous teachers of their language.
