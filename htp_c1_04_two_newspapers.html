Talking about bias

  • It's framed as …
  • The paper plays down …
  • It plays up …
  • There's no mention of …
  • Yours makes it sound as if …

Hedging

  • It seems to suggest that …
  • Arguably, …
  • I'd say it leans towards …
  • To be fair, mine does say …

Listen to how it sounds

AMine says the library will close in June. What does yours say? BSame date, but mine says eleven jobs will go. Is there anything about jobs in yours? AThere's no mention of jobs at all. Mine plays that down completely. BAnd in mine, the mayor “admitted” visitor numbers had fallen. That's quite a loaded word. AMine puts it more neutrally. She just “said” they'd fallen. BSo yours is framed as a sad but sensible decision, and mine as a failure.

What does the verb tell you?

These verbs are not in the lists above. Decide what each one suggests about the writer's view.

Key words

slant (noun) — a way of presenting information that favours one side. Example: "Both reports had the same facts but a very different slant."
bias (noun) — an unfair preference for one side or view over another. Example: "Readers complained about the paper's bias against the council."
loaded (adjective) — carrying strong feelings or a hidden judgement. Example: "Calling it a fiasco is a very loaded way to describe a cancelled event."
neutral (adjective) — not supporting either side. Example: "A good headline should sound neutral."
downplay (verb) — make something seem less important than it really is. Example: "The company tried to downplay the delays."
frame (verb) — present something in a particular way so that people see it like that. Example: "The story was framed as a victory for local people."
omit (verb) — leave something out, on purpose or by mistake. Example: "The report omits any mention of the cost."
allege (verb) — say that something is true without giving proof. Example: "Residents allege that the council ignored their petition."
sensationalise (verb) — present a story so that it seems more shocking than it really is. Example: "Some papers sensationalised the cancellation."
