Today's aim: Today we're learning to explain why things get hot and cold, so you can say what happens and why.

Words you'll need

hot (adj) — with a high temperature. Example: "The soup is too hot to eat."
cold (adj) — with a low temperature. Example: "Ice is very cold."
warm (adj) — a little hot, in a pleasant way. Example: "The room is warm."
heat (n) — the energy that makes things hot. Example: "Heat moves from the fire to your hands."
temperature (n) — how hot or cold something is. Example: "The temperature of boiling water is 100 degrees."
thermometer (n) — a tool for measuring temperature. Example: "The doctor used a thermometer."
melt (v) — to change from solid to liquid because of heat. Example: "The ice cream melts in the sun."
freeze (v) — to change from liquid to solid because of cold. Example: "Water freezes at zero degrees."
boil (v) — to heat a liquid until it bubbles. Example: "I boil water for tea."
lid (n) — a cover for a cup, pan or box. Example: "Put a lid on the pan."

A question to start

You make a hot cup of tea. You forget about it and come back half an hour later.

Say it aloud: What has happened to the tea? Where did the heat go?

The tea is cold. The heat did not disappear: it moved from the hot tea into the cooler air and the cup. Heat always moves from hot things to cold things, never the other way.

Heat and temperature

🌡️ Temperature

Temperature tells us how hot or cold something is. We measure it with a thermometer.

➡️ Heat moves

Heat always moves from something hot to something cold. A hot cup warms your hands. Your hands do not warm the cup.

🧣 Keeping warm

Some things slow the heat down. A lid, a coat and a blanket all help to keep heat in.

What you just saw

1 · Heat moves out
The hot tea gives its heat to the cooler air around it.
2 · A lid helps
A lid slows the heat down, so the tea stays hot longer.
3 · A flask helps most
A flask almost stops the heat from leaving.

The heat does not disappear. It moves into the air.

Language for explaining

Facts: the present simple

Heat moves from hot things to cold things.
Ice melts when it is warm.

Giving a reason: because and so

The tea is cold because the heat moved into the air.
The heat moved into the air, so the tea is cold.

because + the reason  ·  so + the result

Comparing: -er than

A thermos is better than a lid.
Boiling water is hotter than warm water.

Why is a flask good for tea?

A thermos flask keeps tea hot for many hours. It has two walls with empty space between them. Heat cannot move easily across the empty space, so the tea stays hot.

1 · Hot tea 🍵
The tea is very hot. The heat wants to move out.
2 · Two walls 🥛
The empty space between the walls stops the heat.
3 · Still hot ☕
After many hours, the tea is still warm.
