Definitions

  • NOMA: Non-Overlapping Magisteria — the idea that science and religion answer different questions and don't conflict
  • Teleological: Designed with a purpose; assuming nature has a goal or end-point (e.g., evolution directed toward humans)
  • Cosmological: Relating to the origin or structure of the universe
  • Theistic Evolution: The belief that evolution happened, but God guided or created the process
  • Creationism: The belief that the universe and life were created by God as described in religious texts, not through evolution
  • i'jaz: The Islamic concept that the Qur'an is a miracle — its beauty and inimitability prove it comes from Allah
  • Avyakata: In Buddhism, the "unanswerable questions" — metaphysical questions the Buddha left open, saying they distract from ending suffering
  • Miracle: An event that appears to break natural laws and is attributed to divine power
  • Contingency Miracle: An event with a natural explanation but religious significance due to timing or context
  • Natural Law: The principle that the universe operates according to consistent, discoverable rules (gravity, thermodynamics, etc.)
  • Big Bang: The scientific model that the universe began from an extremely hot, dense state about 13.8 billion years ago
  • Genesis: The first book of the Bible, containing creation accounts in Judaism and Christianity
  • Al-Khaliq: "The Creator" — one of Allah's 99 names in Islam, emphasizing Allah's role as creator of all existence
  • Consciousness: Subjective awareness and experience — the quality of being sentient or aware
  • Anicca: In Buddhism, the doctrine of impermanence — the idea that all things are in constant flux and nothing is permanent

Review Questions

Click each question to reveal the answer. Review across all 5 lessons.

What are the 4 models of the science-religion relationship?
NOMA (non-overlapping magisteria), Conflict (they directly oppose), Dialogue (they can engage and enrich each other), and Integration (they can be harmonized into a coherent worldview).
Compare creation accounts from Christianity, Islam, and Buddhism.
Christianity and Islam: God/Allah creates the universe and life with purpose. Buddhism: The universe is eternal, without a creator. Focus is on understanding suffering, not origins.
Explain how Christians, Muslims, and Buddhists respond to evolution.
Christians: Many accept evolution as compatible with faith (theistic evolution); some reject it (creationism). Muslims: Many accept evolution; Qur'an and evolution can coexist. Buddhists: Evolution is compatible with Buddhist teachings on impermanence and no permanent self.
What is Hume's argument against miracles?
A miracle is a violation of natural law, and it is always more rational to believe the testimony is false than to believe a miracle occurred, because natural laws are more reliable than any testimony.
What questions can science not answer?
Why the universe exists, what gives life meaning, what is morally right, what happens after death, what is consciousness, whether there is purpose to existence.

Extended Written Assessment

Question

"Science and religion give different but equally valuable answers to life's big questions." Do you agree?

Write 300–400 words. Refer to at least two religious traditions and at least two scientific concepts in your answer.

Things to consider:

  • What kinds of questions does each address?
  • Are answers from both "equally valuable"? Why or why not?
  • Use specific examples: e.g., evolution and theistic evolution, miracles and natural law, meaning and the hard problem of consciousness
  • Address potential conflicts: Do they ever contradict?
  • Conclude with your own reasoned position
