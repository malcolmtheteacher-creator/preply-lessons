Explanation Moves

Use these 5 strategies to explain any word:

Move 1: Function — What does it do?
"It's something you use to..." or "You use it for..." or "It helps you..."
Example: "A pencil? It's something you use to write with. You can erase it."
Move 2: Category — What group does it belong to?
"It's a type of..." or "It's a kind of..." or "It's a category of..."
Example: "A penguin? It's a type of bird. It can't fly, but it lives in very cold places."
Move 3: Location — Where do you find it?
"You usually see this in..." or "You find it at..." or "It's common in..."
Example: "A library? You find books there. People go there to read or study quietly."
Move 4: Opposite — What is it NOT?
"It's the opposite of..." or "It's not like..." or "The opposite would be..."
Example: "Cheap? It's the opposite of expensive. You pay very little money."
Move 5: Example — Give a specific example
"For example..." or "Like..." or "You might..."
Example: "Transport? Cars, buses, trains — things that move people from one place to another."

Remember: You can use one move or combine several. Mix and match to explain anything!
