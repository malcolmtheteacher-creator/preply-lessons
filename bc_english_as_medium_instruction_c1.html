The EMI Debate: Key Arguments

Arguments in Favor of EMI:

  • Global alignment: EMI helps countries align with English-speaking partners and integrate into the global economy
  • Language development: Students develop strong English language skills while studying academic subjects
  • Career opportunities: Graduates with strong English are more competitive in international job markets
  • Access to resources: Most academic research and international materials are in English

Arguments Against EMI:

  • Cognitive load: Students may struggle to learn complex subjects in a non-native language
  • Equity issues: Students from different language backgrounds may have unequal educational experiences
  • Loss of local identity: Over-reliance on English may threaten local languages and cultures
  • Teacher preparation: Teachers may not be sufficiently trained to teach academic content in English

Comprehension Questions

1. What is one advantage of EMI mentioned in the arguments?

Possible answers: Global alignment with English-speaking countries, language development, career opportunities, or access to international academic resources.

2. What is the "cognitive load" argument against EMI?

The argument that students may struggle to learn complex subjects when taught in a non-native language, as they have to process both the language and the academic content.

3. How might EMI affect educational equity?

Students from different language backgrounds may have unequal educational experiences. Those with stronger English skills may have an advantage, while others may fall behind.

Phrases for Formal Debate

Use these phrases when expressing your views in a formal debate.

The way I see it A polite way to introduce your personal opinion
The truth of the matter is Emphasizing what you believe to be the essential fact
There's no denying that Acknowledging an undeniable fact before presenting your view
I have to say Introducing a strong personal opinion or conviction
In my opinion A standard phrase for stating your personal view
I would argue that Presenting a carefully reasoned position
It seems to me A softer way of presenting an opinion while inviting discussion
From my perspective Introducing your viewpoint based on your experience or background

Practice with Debate Phrases

Complete these sentences using the debate phrases above.

1. "_____ _____ _____ _____, EMI helps students align with international academic standards."

Options: "The way I see it" / "In my opinion" / "I would argue that"

Any of these options would work: "The way I see it," "In my opinion," or "I would argue that." Each introduces the opinion in a slightly different way.

2. "_____ _____ _____ _____ that not all students benefit equally from EMI."

Options: "There's no denying" / "I have to say" / "The truth of the matter is"

All three work here: "There's no denying that," "I have to say," or "The truth of the matter is." Each emphasizes the seriousness of your point.

3. "_____ _____, teachers need substantial professional development to teach content in English."

Options: "It seems to me" / "From my perspective" / "I would argue"

"It seems to me" or "From my perspective" are the best fits here, as they invite the listener to consider your viewpoint.

Reflection Questions

After the debate, what did you find most challenging?

Common challenges include: staying objective while defending a position you might not personally hold, using formal debate language under pressure, responding quickly to opposing arguments, and acknowledging valid points from the other side while maintaining your position.

What is your genuine opinion about EMI?

Consider: What was your own educational experience? Do you see benefits and drawbacks in your own country's education system? How might EMI have affected your learning? What does the evidence suggest about student outcomes? What role should your native language play in education?

How might EMI implementation differ based on context?

Context matters greatly: In countries where English is widely spoken, EMI may be easier to implement. In countries with lower English proficiency, it may require significant teacher training. The level of education (primary vs. tertiary) affects implementation. Economic resources also play a role in whether schools can support EMI effectively.
