The Three Parts of an Introduction

A strong academic introduction has three essential components that work together to draw the reader in and set up your argument.

1. Hook (Opening Statement)

A surprising fact, question, or statement that captures attention. It makes the reader want to continue.

2. Background Information

Context about the topic. Explains why this topic is important and relevant to your reader.

3. Thesis Statement

Your main argument or point. Usually 1-2 sentences. Clear and specific about what the essay will discuss.

Example Introduction

Hook: "Every year, millions of students struggle with the transition to university life, experiencing stress and anxiety that affects their academic performance."

Background: "This phenomenon has become increasingly common as universities expand and become more competitive. Research shows that first-year students face unique challenges in adapting to new environments, increased academic demands, and social pressures."

Thesis: "To address this issue, universities should implement comprehensive support programs that combine academic tutoring, mental health services, and peer mentoring to help students adjust successfully."

Writing Effective Hooks

Types of Hooks

  • Surprising Fact: "Did you know that 40% of Internet traffic is now video-based?"
  • Question: "What if you could learn a new language in just six months?"
  • Quotation: "As Einstein once said, 'Education is not the learning of facts, but training the mind.'"
  • Problem Statement: "Climate change threatens global food security, affecting billions of people."
  • Statistics: "Urban populations are growing at a rate of 1.8% annually worldwide."

What NOT to Do

  • Don't start with "In this essay, I will discuss..."
  • Don't use general statements like "Education is important"
  • Don't be too casual or use slang
  • Don't make claims you can't support in your essay

Analysing Introductions

Read each introduction and identify the three parts. Which is most effective?

Example 1

"Social media has transformed how we communicate. Many people use Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok every day. Some use these platforms for business, others for entertainment. This essay will discuss different uses of social media."

Which element is missing or weak?

The hook is weak (too general). The thesis is also unclear—what specific point is this essay making about social media use? A stronger version would have a surprising fact/question and a clearer argument.

Example 2

"Did you know that students who take regular breaks score 25% higher on exams? The pressure to study continuously is increasing in schools worldwide. However, recent research proves that taking strategic breaks actually improves memory retention and focus. This essay argues that educational institutions should redesign study schedules to include mandatory break periods."

What makes this a strong introduction?

It has all three elements: Hook (question with statistic), Background (explains the problem context), and Thesis (clear argument about what should happen). The hook is specific and interesting, context is provided, and the argument is clear.

Example 3

"Technology is everywhere. People use computers, phones, and tablets. In this essay, I will discuss the role of technology in education."

What needs to improve?

Everything needs improvement. The hook is too general, background information is vague, and the thesis doesn't make a clear argument. Rewrite with: a specific fact/question, relevant context about tech in education, and a clear stance (does tech help? harm? in what way?).

Example 4

"Online learning grew from 2% to 44% of all course enrollments between 2012 and 2022. Universities shifted to digital platforms during recent global disruptions. Yet many educators remain uncertain whether virtual education provides equivalent learning outcomes to traditional classrooms. This essay examines evidence for and against the effectiveness of online education."

Identify each element and evaluate them.

Hook: Statistics (strong and relevant). Background: Explains why this matters (timely, clear context). Thesis: The essay will examine evidence—balanced approach. This is well-structured with specific, credible information that engages the reader.
