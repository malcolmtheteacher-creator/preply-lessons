📚 Lesson 27: Past Perfect Continuous

Explaining Duration Before a Past Moment - Detective Stories & Excuses

🕵️ The Mystery at the Office

The Scene: When the police arrived at the office at 9:00 AM, they found the safe open and empty. The security footage showed that someone had entered the building at 6:30 AM.

Detective Sarah's Questions:

  • How long had the thief been in the building before anyone arrived?
  • What had they been doing all that time?
  • Why were they so exhausted when they left?

To answer these questions, we need the Past Perfect Continuous!

💡 Why Do We Need This Tense?

The Past Perfect Continuous helps us explain:

  • ✅ How long something was in progress before another past action
  • ✅ Why someone was tired, angry, happy, etc. in the past
  • ✅ The cause of a past result or situation
  • ✅ Background activities that explain what happened

🎯 Today's Speaking Goals

By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to:

  • Explain why things happened in the past
  • Tell detective-style stories with background information
  • Make excuses and explain situations naturally
  • Answer "How long...?" questions about the past

🔍 Grammar Discovery

📖 The Formula

had + been + verb-ing

🎬 Click each sentence to discover its function!

"I had been working for 12 hours when my boss finally let me go home."

→ Shows how long the action continued before the boss let me leave

"She was exhausted because she had been running for an hour."

→ Explains WHY she was exhausted

"The streets were wet. It had been raining all morning."

→ Shows the cause of a past result (wet streets)

"When I called her, she had been crying. Her voice was shaky."

→ Gives background information to explain the situation

💬 Free Practice - Life Stories

🎯 Instructions

Discuss these questions with your teacher. Use the past perfect continuous to explain the situations!

1. A Time You Were Exhausted

Think about:

  • When did this happen?
  • What had you been doing before you felt exhausted?
  • How long had you been doing it?
  • What did you do after that?

Example: "Last month, I was completely exhausted because I had been studying for my exams for 3 days straight without sleeping properly..."

2. Explaining a Big Achievement

Think about:

  • What did you achieve?
  • What had you been doing to prepare?
  • How long had you been working on it?
  • How did you feel when you finally succeeded?

Example: "When I passed my driving test, I was so happy! I had been practicing for 6 months, and I had been taking lessons twice a week..."

3. A Mysterious Situation

Tell a story:

Describe a time when you saw someone in an unusual condition (tired, happy, angry, dirty, wet, etc.) and had to guess what had been happening to them.

Example: "I met my friend on the street and she looked really happy. I asked her why, and she said she had been playing with puppies at a rescue center all afternoon..."

4. Before vs. After

Compare:

  • Before you started learning English seriously, what had you been doing? (studying alone, watching movies, etc.)
  • Before you got your current job/started university, what had you been doing to prepare?
  • Before you moved to your current home, where had you been living?

5. Making Excuses Game

Scenario: You arrive very late to an important meeting. Your boss asks, "Where have you been?!"

Create the most creative/funny excuse using past perfect continuous:

  • "I'm so sorry! I had been helping a cat stuck in a tree..."
  • "I apologize! I had been following the wrong person because I thought they were you..."
  • Create your own!

🎭 Role-Play Scenarios

Scenario 1: The Detective Investigation

Detective (Teacher/Student)

Your job: Question the suspect about what they had been doing before the crime happened.

Questions to ask:

  • "Where had you been living before you moved to this city?"
  • "What had you been doing between 6 PM and 8 PM?"
  • "How long had you been working at that company?"
  • "Why were you so tired when we found you?"

Suspect (Student/Teacher)

Your job: Answer the detective's questions using past perfect continuous to explain your actions.

Possible answers:

  • "I had been living in Tokyo for 5 years before I moved here..."
  • "I had been working in my home office all evening..."
  • "I was tired because I had been jogging in the park..."

Scenario 2: The Job Interview

Interviewer (Teacher/Student)

Your job: Ask about the candidate's preparation and background.

Questions to ask:

  • "What had you been doing before you applied for this position?"
  • "How long had you been studying this field?"
  • "What skills had you been developing in your previous role?"

Job Candidate (Student/Teacher)

Your job: Impress the interviewer by explaining your preparation and experience.

Example answers:

  • "Before I applied, I had been working as a junior developer for 3 years..."
  • "I had been taking online courses in AI and machine learning..."
  • "I had been building my portfolio and practicing coding every day..."

Scenario 3: The Excuse Master

Boss/Teacher/Parent (One person)

Your job: You're angry because the other person is late/didn't do their work/broke something.

Start with:

  • "Why are you 2 hours late?!"
  • "Why didn't you finish the report?"
  • "Why is my car damaged?!"

Late Person/Employee/Child (Other person)

Your job: Make creative excuses using past perfect continuous!

Be creative:

  • "I'm sorry! I had been helping an old lady who..."
  • "I apologize! I had been dealing with..."
  • "I know it looks bad, but I had been..."

✅ Lesson Review

📖 Grammar Summary

Past Perfect Continuous:

had + been + verb-ing

Use it for:

  • Duration before a past moment
  • Explaining causes of past results
  • Background activities
  • Making excuses and explanations

💬 Key Phrases

  • "I had been working for..."
  • "She had been studying since..."
  • "They had been living there for..."
  • "We had been waiting all day..."
  • "How long had you been...?"
  • "Why were you tired? I had been..."

🎯 Common Time Expressions

  • for 3 hours/2 days/5 months
  • since 9 AM/Monday/January
  • all morning/day/week
  • the whole time/day/evening
  • How long...

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • ❌ I was working for 5 hours
    ✅ I had been working for 5 hours
  • ❌ She has been studying when I called
    ✅ She had been studying when I called
  • ❌ They had worked all day (focus on duration)
    ✅ They had been working all day
