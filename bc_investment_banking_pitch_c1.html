Pushing Back on an Unrealistic Deadline

C1 Level · Investment Banking · Client Management

Investment Banking · Dewey 332.6

First Thoughts

A repeat client wants a pitch book, valuation model, and preliminary fairness opinion by Monday morning. It is Thursday afternoon. The analysts on your team are already running three other live deals.

You cannot simply say no — this client generates seven-figure fees. But if you agree, the quality will suffer, the team will burn out, and you will be the one in the MD’s office explaining why the model has errors.

Take a moment with each question below. Share as much detail as you like — specific examples, what you would actually say, what tripped you up last time.

1. When was the last time a client asked you for something you knew was unrealistic? What did you say in the first ten seconds?

2. Where is the line between stretching a team and breaking a team? How do senior bankers you respect spot it?

3. What is the real cost of saying yes to something you cannot deliver well — beyond the immediate deliverable?

4. Some bankers believe the client should never hear the word ‘no’. Do you agree? What do they say instead?

Quick opener — pick whichever you prefer:

  • You have sixty seconds before the client calls back to discuss timing. What is your opening sentence?
  • An MD once said: “The most expensive word in banking is the word yes said too quickly.” What did they mean?

Key Language

Useful chunks of language for this conversation. Read each phrase aloud and think about when you would actually reach for it.

Opening — acknowledging before pushing back

  • “I hear you on the urgency, and I want to deliver for you — let me just be straight with you about what is realistic.”
  • “Before I commit to a date, can I walk you through what is sitting on the team right now?”
  • “I want to get this right for you, which means I need to be honest about the timing rather than promise something we would struggle to hold.”

Framing the constraint

  • “To get to the quality bar your board will expect, we need a realistic runway — not a heroic one.”
  • “The valuation I could give you by Monday and the valuation I would want to put my name on are two different documents.”
  • “I would rather under-promise on timing and over-deliver on quality than the other way round.”

Offering a structured alternative

  • “What I can commit to is a staggered delivery — the preliminary view Monday, the full pack on Wednesday.”
  • “There are essentially three options here, and I would like your steer on which fits best with your board meeting.”
  • “If Monday is genuinely non-negotiable, then we need to have a conversation about what comes off the scope, not just what gets added.”

Closing without losing the client

  • “I appreciate you being flexible on this — it lets us give you work that will actually stand up in the room.”
  • “My commitment to you is: a clean preliminary view by Monday, a full pack by Wednesday, no surprises between now and then.”
  • “Thank you for trusting us with this. Let me come back within the hour with a concrete re-plan.”

The Scenario

The situation: You are a Vice President in M&A. A long-standing client — a PE-backed industrials business — has an unscheduled board meeting on Tuesday morning and wants a pitch book, a full valuation model, and a preliminary fairness opinion by Monday at 08:00. It is now Thursday 16:30. Your team is already covering two live deals at critical stages. The MD is travelling and reachable only by phone.

Your task: Call the CFO now. By the end of the call, they need to understand what is possible by Monday, what is not, and what you propose instead — without feeling you have let them down.

Step 1 · Plan your response

Use the timer. Make notes — do not write full sentences. Think about what you will say first.

Step 2 · Deliver

When you are ready, role-play the conversation out loud. Be ready to handle the follow-up questions below.

Follow-up questions to handle:

  • Goldman would have this ready by Monday. Why can’t you?
  • If I push my board meeting to Wednesday, can you guarantee everything by then?
  • Can you at least get me the headline valuation number by Friday evening, even if it is preliminary?
  • I need to tell my CEO something tonight. What is the one line you want me to use?
