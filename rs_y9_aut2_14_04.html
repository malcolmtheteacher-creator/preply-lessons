Darwin's key idea about evolution was that organisms with traits best suited to their environment are more likely to survive and pass on those traits — this is called natural selection.
Theistic Evolution is the view that evolution happened, but that God guided or created the process — combining religious faith with acceptance of evolutionary science.
The Catholic Church's official position is that evolution is compatible with Christian faith, as long as we believe God is the ultimate creator of human souls.
Buddhist view: Most Buddhists accept evolution as a description of how life develops, and see it as neutral to Buddhist teachings about suffering and enlightenment.

Miracles

What is a Miracle?

An event that appears to break natural laws and is attributed to divine power.

David Hume's Argument Against Miracles

The philosopher David Hume defined a miracle as "a violation of natural law." He argued that it is always more rational to believe the testimony is false than to believe a miracle occurred. Why? Because we have reliable evidence that natural laws exist, but we have never reliably witnessed the violation of natural law.

Christian Miracles

  • Jesus' miracles: Healing the sick, walking on water, raising Lazarus from the dead
  • The resurrection: Jesus rising from the dead after crucifixion — the foundation of Christian faith
  • Lourdes healings: In 1858, the Virgin Mary reportedly appeared to a girl in France. Thousands have reported miraculous healings at the shrine, though the Church carefully investigates each claim

Islamic Miracles

  • The Qur'an as miracle: Muslims believe the Qur'an itself is a miracle (i'jaz) — its beauty, structure, and inimitability prove it comes from Allah, not from Muhammad
  • The Night Journey: Muslims believe Muhammad was taken from Mecca to Jerusalem and then to heaven in a single night
  • Splitting of the moon: The Qur'an records a miracle where Allah split the moon, a sign for disbelievers

R.F. Holland's "Contingency Miracles"

Philosopher R.F. Holland proposed a different way to think about miracles. A contingency miracle is an event that has a natural explanation, but its timing or context gives it religious significance.

Example: A young child is on railway tracks. A train is approaching. The child's parent prays desperately. The train stops — but only because the driver used the emergency brakes. Scientifically, nothing impossible happened. Yet for the parent, the coincidence of timing feels miraculous.

Science and Miracles: A Question

Are miracles just events we don't yet understand? Or are they genuinely supernatural — outside the laws of nature? Can something be scientifically inexplicable and still be a miracle?
