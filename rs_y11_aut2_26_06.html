Recall: Key Concepts from Unit 26

You have 10 minutes to test your knowledge of all topics covered in this unit. These questions cover Hajj, Jihad, and Islamic festivals.

Define Hajj and explain why it is important in Islam.

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Hajj is the pilgrimage to Makkah, the fifth pillar of Islam. It is obligatory once in the lifetime of able Muslims. It represents submission to Allah, spiritual cleansing, community unity, and following the example of Prophet Abraham.

What is Ihram and what restrictions apply during this state?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Ihram is a sacred state of ritual purity. Restrictions: no cutting hair/nails, no perfume, no sexual relations, no hunting, no arguing. Male pilgrims wear white seamless garments to symbolise equality before Allah.

Describe Tawaf and Sa'i. What do they commemorate?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Tawaf: walking counter-clockwise around the Ka'bah 7 times. Sa'i: running between Safa and Marwa 7 times, commemorating Hagar's search for water. Both demonstrate devotion and perseverance.

Why is standing at Mount Arafat on the 9th Dhu al-Hijjah essential to Hajj?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Arafat is the spiritual climax where pilgrims stand in prayer and seek forgiveness. Prophet said "There is no Hajj without Arafat." Missing this invalidates the entire pilgrimage.

What is Qurbani and how is the meat distributed?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Qurbani is animal sacrifice commemorating Prophet Abraham's obedience. Meat distribution: one-third to family, one-third to friends/relatives, one-third to the poor.

Define Greater Jihad and give examples.

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Greater Jihad is the spiritual struggle against one's desires, weaknesses, and temptations. Examples: resisting greed, controlling anger, developing moral character, studying Islam, choosing forgiveness over revenge.

What is Lesser Jihad and what conditions must be met for just war?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Lesser Jihad is armed defence. Conditions: defensive (not aggressive), authorised by legitimate authority, proportionate force, civilians protected, ethical conduct, no breaking agreements.

Explain the religious significance of Id-ul-Adha.

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Id-ul-Adha celebrates Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Ismail, showing obedience to Allah. Themes: faith, divine mercy (Allah substituted a ram), charity (meat shared with poor), community.

What does Id-ul-Fitr celebrate and what is Zakat ul-Fitr?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Id-ul-Fitr celebrates the end of Ramadan. Zakat ul-Fitr is obligatory charity given before Eid prayers to ensure the poor can celebrate with dignity.

How do Sunni and Shi'a Muslims understand Ashura differently?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Sunni: Ashura commemorates Prophet Moses's deliverance across the Red Sea; optional fasting is encouraged. Shi'a: Ashura mourns Husayn's martyrdom at Karbala (680 CE); major commemoration with passion plays and processions.

Who was Husayn and why is he significant in Shi'a Islam?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Husayn ibn Ali was the grandson of Prophet Muhammad. He was killed at the Battle of Karbala (680 CE). For Shi'a Muslims, he represents resistance to tyranny and injustice.

What is Ta'ziyeh and what role does it play in Ashura?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Ta'ziyeh are passion plays dramatising Husayn's life and martyrdom. They are central to Shi'a Ashura commemorations, allowing the community to re-enact and reflect on the tragedy.

Unit 26 Summary: Islam Practices Part 2

The Five Pillars and Beyond

This unit explored two pillars of Islam (Hajj and Jihad — the struggle) and major festivals tied to Islamic history and practice:

  • Hajj: The pilgrimage, demonstrating equality, submission, and spiritual transformation
  • Jihad: The struggle — both spiritual (Greater Jihad) and physical (Lesser Jihad with strict ethical conditions)
  • Id-ul-Adha: Festival celebrating Abraham's obedience and emphasising sacrifice and charity
  • Id-ul-Fitr: Joyful celebration of spiritual achievement after Ramadan fasting
  • Ashura: Reflection (Sunni) or mourning (Shi'a) revealing diversity within Islam

Key Themes

  • Obedience: To Allah's commands and the example of the prophets
  • Community: Muslims united across nationality, wealth, and gender
  • Justice: Fighting against injustice and oppression (Lesser Jihad) and within the self (Greater Jihad)
  • Charity: Care for the poor and needy in festivals and practices
  • Diversity: Different Islamic schools (Sunni, Shi'a, etc.) interpret practices differently

Exam Focus Areas

When answering exam questions about Unit 26, ensure you:

  • Use accurate Islamic terminology and date the events correctly
  • Explain the spiritual significance of practices, not just procedures
  • Include Qur'anic references where relevant
  • Acknowledge different perspectives (Sunni/Shi'a) without bias
  • Link practices to core Islamic values (submission, community, justice, charity)

Quick-Fire Test: Multiple Concepts

Test your understanding with these rapid-fire questions. Click each card to reveal the answer.

In which month does Hajj take place?

Answer: Dhu al-Hijjah (12th month of Islamic calendar), specifically 8th-13th.

What is the spiritual climax of Hajj?

Answer: Standing at Mount Arafat on the 9th Dhu al-Hijjah.

Which type of jihad is the struggle against temptation?

Answer: Greater Jihad (Jihad al-Nafs).

What was the Battle of Karbala and when did it occur?

Answer: The battle in 680 CE where Husayn ibn Ali was killed, mourned annually by Shi'a Muslims on Ashura.

What animal was substituted for Ismail in the Qur'anic account?

Answer: A ram (provided by Allah).

How many times is the Ka'bah circumambulated in Tawaf?

Answer: Seven times counter-clockwise.

What is the condition for a Lesser Jihad to be just?

Answer: Defensive (not aggressive), authorised by legitimate authority, proportionate, civilians protected.

What is Zakat ul-Fitr and when is it given?

Answer: Obligatory charity given before Eid prayers on Id-ul-Fitr to help the poor celebrate.

Extended Writing Task: 12-Mark Exam Question

Question: Hajj is the fifth pillar of Islam. Evaluate the extent to which the rituals and practices of Hajj reflect the core values of Islam. (12 marks)

Task: Write a response to this question in the textarea below. Aim for 300-400 words. Consider:

  • What are the core values of Islam? (submission, community, justice, charity)
  • How do specific Hajj rituals (Ihram, Tawaf, Sa'i, Arafat, Qurbani) reflect these values?
  • Are there any aspects of Hajj that do NOT clearly reflect these values?
  • What is your conclusion about the extent to which Hajj embodies Islamic values?
Words: 0/400

Sample Answer Structure (Not to be copied)

  • Introduction (1-2 sentences): State that Hajj is a pillar designed to embody Islamic values and your overall assessment
  • Body Paragraph 1 — Ihram & Tawaf (3-4 sentences): How white garments show equality and Tawaf shows devotion
  • Body Paragraph 2 — Arafat & Charity (3-4 sentences): How standing at Arafat demonstrates submission and Qurbani shows charity
  • Body Paragraph 3 — Potential limitations (2-3 sentences): If anything, mention the physical danger or cost
  • Conclusion (1-2 sentences): Overall, Hajj powerfully reflects Islamic values
  • Target: Use Qur'anic references, explain "why" not just "what," evaluate rather than describe
