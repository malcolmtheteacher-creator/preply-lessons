Getting a Pay Rise

B2 Level English • Workplace Negotiations

What you will learn

Participate in a workplace negotiation to discuss a pay rise. Learn how to accept and reject proposals, express your position tentatively, and find common ground.

Let us get started

Think about this scenario

You have been with your company for five years. Your performance has been excellent, but your salary has only increased by 3% over the last year, while inflation was at 8%. You want to ask for a pay rise and improved benefits. What would you ask for?

What percentage pay rise would you request?

What three benefits would improve your work life?

Discussion Questions

Answer these questions. Think about your own work situation, or imagine one.

1. Have you ever negotiated for a pay rise or benefits?

Example: Yes, I asked for flexible hours when my children started school.

2. What makes a negotiation successful?

Example: Clear evidence of your value, reasonable requests, and willingness to compromise.

3. How do you feel when asking for something you want?

Example: A bit nervous, but confident if I have prepared my reasons.

Negotiation Language

Learn these phrases for accepting and rejecting proposals. Notice how we use tentative language to sound diplomatic and professional.

Accepting a Proposal

ACCEPTING
That sounds like a fair offer. I can accept that.
Direct agreement with enthusiasm
ACCEPTING WITH RESERVATIONS
I might be willing to consider that if you can offer paid overtime.
Acceptance, but with a condition attached
ACCEPTING PARTIALLY
I am not unhappy with that, though I was hoping for something better.
Tepid agreement while expressing a preference

Raising Objections

OBJECTION
I am afraid there might be a slight problem with that.
Polite way to express concern
OBJECTION
Looking at our budget, your request is somewhat unrealistic.
Explain why a request cannot be met
OBJECTION
It is unlikely that we will be introducing profit sharing in the near future.
Polite refusal of a proposal

Refusing an Offer

REFUSAL
I am not sure I can accept that.
Express doubt about an offer
REFUSAL
We probably won't be able to accommodate that.
Say something is not possible
REFUSAL WITH ALTERNATIVE
That appears to be much less than I originally requested. Could we discuss an alternative?
Refuse but keep the door open for negotiation

Tentative Language

In negotiations, we use tentative words and phrases to sound diplomatic. We slow down and lower our intonation when speaking these words.

Key tentative words:

might • could • somewhat • slightly • probably • perhaps • I am afraid • looking at • I am not sure • I could conceivably • appears to be

These words show respect and willingness to listen, even when you disagree.

Common Benefits and Perks

Read about these benefits. Which five are most important to you? Which would your employer likely accept?

Salary-Related Benefits

  • Pay increase: A percentage rise in annual salary (often 3-8%)
  • End-of-year bonus: Extra payment based on company performance
  • Paid overtime: Extra money for hours worked beyond standard week
  • Lunch allowance: Money towards daily meals or subsidized company lunch

Time-Related Benefits

  • Flexible hours: Ability to start and finish work at different times
  • Remote working: Work from home some or all of the week
  • Extra holiday: Additional vacation days beyond the legal minimum
  • Maternity leave: Paid time off for new parents

Long-Term and Protection Benefits

  • Pension scheme: Company contributes to your retirement savings
  • Health insurance: Private medical coverage for you and family
  • Life insurance: Financial protection if something happens to you
  • Professional development: Training courses and certifications paid by employer

Other Perks

  • Company car: Vehicle provided or car allowance
  • Gym membership: Free or subsidized fitness club access
  • Profit sharing: Share in company profits when they are good
  • Stock options: Opportunity to buy company shares at discount

Prepare for Your Negotiation

You are going to prepare a role play negotiation. Read both role cards and prepare your arguments.

Scenario: Senior Web Designer and Company Owner

Role A: Senior Web Designer (You are the employee)

Your background:

  • You have been with the company for 5 years
  • Your performance reviews have always been excellent
  • No performance issues in your entire time here
  • You have taken on more responsibilities this year

Your situation:

  • Last year inflation was 8%, but your salary only increased 3%
  • You now have more responsibility than when you started
  • Your most recent performance review was outstanding

What you are going to ask for:

  • A pay rise (what percentage?)
  • Two additional benefits (which ones?)

Role B: Company Owner (If someone is playing this part)

Your background:

  • The employee has done excellent work over 5 years
  • You would like to reward them
  • However, you must be careful about what you agree to

Your constraints:

  • Last year the company made only a small loss
  • All employees only received a 3% rise, even though inflation was 8%
  • You cannot afford a large salary increase

What you are prepared to offer:

  • A small salary increase (what percentage?)
  • Two perks as recognition of good work (which ones?)

Prepare Your Arguments

If you are the employee (Role A), think about:

  • What specific achievements prove your value to the company?
  • What new responsibilities do you have?
  • What salary increase is realistic given inflation?
  • Which benefits would you most like to have?

If you are the owner (Role B), think about:

  • What percentage increase can the company afford?
  • Which perks cost little but are valuable to employees?
  • How will you acknowledge their good work?
  • What will you refuse and why?

Role Play Practice

Now conduct your negotiation. Remember: use tentative language, be respectful, and try to find a solution that works for both sides.

What to cover in your conversation:

Key topics to discuss:

  • The impact of inflation on salary
  • Your new responsibilities and achievements
  • What pay rise or benefits you are asking for
  • What the employer can reasonably offer
  • A compromise that both sides can accept

Remember to:

  • Accepting proposals: "I might be willing to consider that if…", "I could accept that, provided that…"
  • Raising objections: "I am afraid that might be a problem because…", "Looking at the numbers, that appears…"
  • Refusing offers: "I am not sure I can accept that. What if instead we…?"
  • Finding compromise: "Could we perhaps agree on…?", "Would it be possible to…?"
