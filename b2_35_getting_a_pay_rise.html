Tentative Language & Stress Patterns

Use tentative words and stress to sound polite and open to negotiation.

Tentative Words to Emphasize

  • might — "I might be willing to consider..."
  • could — "We could possibly..."
  • probably — "That"s probably not realistic."
  • slightly — "There"s a slightly different approach..."
  • somewhat — "Your request is somewhat ambitious."
  • appears — "That appears to be less than expected."
  • unlikely — "It"s unlikely we can accommodate that."

Examples with Natural Intonation

Show Examples

Tentative: "I"m afraid there might be a slight problem with that."

Tentative: "Looking at our budget, your request is somewhat unrealistic."

Tentative: "I"m not sure I can accept that."

Tentative: "We probably won"t be able to accommodate that."

Tentative: "I might be willing to consider it if you can offer paid overtime."

Tentative: "It is unlikely that we will introduce profit sharing soon."

Tentative: "That appears to be much less than I originally requested."

Tentative: "I could conceivably accept that, if you can provide a company car."

Key point: When you stress tentative words (might, slight, unlikely, appears), you slow down and lower your intonation. This makes you sound professional, polite, and willing to negotiate rather than demanding.

Preparing Your Role-play

You will negotiate a pay rise between a Senior Web Designer and a company owner.

Role Card: Senior Web Designer

Show Details

Your situation: You"ve been with the company for 5 years with no performance issues. You"re preparing to negotiate a better package.

You want to ask for:

  • A pay increase (decide how much: 5%, 10%, 15%?)
  • Additional benefits/perks (choose 2-3 from the list)

Your reasons:

  • You joined 5 years ago (loyalty)
  • Last year inflation was 8%, but you only got 3% (unfair)
  • You have more responsibilities now (give examples)
  • Your recent performance review was outstanding (give details)

Your priorities: What matters most to you? The money? The benefits? Flexible hours?

Role Card: Company Owner

Show Details

Your situation: The employee is good and deserves recognition, but money is tight.

Your constraints:

  • Last year, the company made a small loss
  • All employees only received 3% raise (including inflation at 8%)
  • You"re willing to give a small increase (decide: 3%, 5%, 7%?), but with conditions

Your plan:

  • Offer a modest salary increase
  • Offer 2 perks to acknowledge their good work (choose which ones)
  • Maybe tie future increases to company performance

Your goal: Keep the employee happy without bankrupting the company.

Now It"s Your Turn

Before You Start

Prepare your role card by answering the questions:

  • If you"re the Designer: What specific increase do you want? What 2-3 benefits matter most to you?
  • If you"re the Owner: What increase can you realistically offer? Which 2 perks will you propose?
Preparation time: 3:00

Negotiation Checklist

  • Designer: Present your case with specific numbers and reasons. Use negotiation language to handle rejection.
  • Owner: Use tentative language. Acknowledge their value while explaining your limits.
  • Both: Try to reach a compromise. Be willing to negotiate on different parts of the package.

Reflection Questions (after role-play):

1. Have you ever asked for a pay rise or negotiated for something you want? Did you get it?

2. Do you feel a pay increase should automatically happen each year?

3. What"s your reaction if you don"t get what you want? How do you deal with that emotion?

4. Would you like to change your negotiating style? If yes, how? What would you do or not do differently?

Key Takeaways

  • Use data (performance, inflation, loyalty) to support your case
  • Be specific about what you want
  • Listen to the other person"s constraints
  • Be willing to negotiate on multiple benefits, not just salary
  • Use tentative language to stay professional and open
  • It"s OK to disagree and come back later with a new proposal
