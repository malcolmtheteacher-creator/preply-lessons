Breaking the Ice

Click each card to flip and see the definition. These are key words you'll use when meeting people in social and business situations.

Small Talk Light, casual conversation about topics like weather or work — used to build comfort before deeper discussion.
Networking Meeting and building relationships with professionals, often for career or business opportunities.
Rapport A feeling of connection and mutual understanding between people — the goal of good conversation.
Icebreaker A question or comment that starts a conversation and makes people feel comfortable talking together.
Mingling Moving around a social event, talking to different people in casual, relaxed conversation.
Colleague A person you work with at the same company or in the same profession.

Starting Conversations

In different social settings, you need different conversation starters. Choose the right level of formality for each situation.

Conversation Starters by Setting

At a Wedding Reception

Setting: Casual, celebratory, lots of strangers.

The opener:

Click to Reveal
"Aren't the flowers lovely? Do you know the bride or groom?" or "Have you traveled far to be here?"

At a Work Dinner

Setting: Professional but friendly, colleagues and new people.

The opener:

Click to Reveal
"How do you know the host?" or "What's your role in the company?" or "Have you tried the soup yet?"

At a Conference Coffee Break

Setting: Professional, people with shared interests in the field.

The opener:

Click to Reveal
"Which sessions have you attended so far?" or "Are you enjoying the conference?" or "What's your take on the morning's presentation?"

Waiting for a Meeting to Start

Setting: Business, brief time window, 1-to-1 or small group.

The opener:

Click to Reveal
"How was your morning?" or "First time visiting this office?" or "Is this your first time working with the team?"

Matching Formality Levels

Casual / Social
  • "I'm mad about travel. Where have you been recently?"
  • "I really love good coffee — do you come to these events often?"
  • "I'm not really into networking events, but this one's different!"
Professional / Business
  • "I understand you work in marketing — I'd love to hear about your projects."
  • "What brings you to the conference today?"
  • "I've been meaning to connect with someone from your department."

Showing Interest — Phrases to Use

To show genuine interest:

  • "I'm crazy about [topic] — tell me more about what you do."
  • "I really love meeting people from different backgrounds."
  • "I could spend hours talking about [their interest] — what's your take?"
  • "I'm not really into [something], but I'm fascinated by [their expertise]."

Saying Goodbye

In professional settings, it's important to end conversations gracefully and leave a good impression.

Farewell Phrases by Category

Exchanging Details
  • "Let me give you my business card."
  • "I'd love to stay in touch — do you have a card?"
  • "Let's exchange contact information."
  • "I'll find you on LinkedIn."
Wrapping Up the Conversation
  • "It's been lovely chatting to you."
  • "I've really enjoyed our conversation."
  • "Thanks so much for sharing your insights."
  • "I'm glad we finally got to meet."
Future Plans
  • "Let's catch up again later."
  • "I hope to see you at the next event."
  • "I'll reach out next week to arrange a coffee."
  • "Let's definitely stay in contact."

Scenario: Pick Your Setting

Scenario 1: Coffee Break at a Conference

You're: At a tech conference. Standing by the coffee station. Someone approaches and we start chatting.

Your task: Greet them, find out what sessions they've been to, share your interests, exchange details, and say goodbye warmly.

Scenario 2: A Work Dinner

You're: At a company dinner event. You see someone you don't know sitting near you.

Your task: Start a conversation, find common ground, show genuine interest in their role, offer your business card, and wrap up gracefully.

Scenario 3: A Wedding Reception

You're: At a friend's wedding. You bump into someone you don't know during a break.

Your task: Comment on the event, ask how they know the bride or groom, share a bit about yourself, and end on a warm, memorable note.
