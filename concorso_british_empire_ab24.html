Jigsaw Cards — content

One card per letter (A–E). Each home group of 5 has one student per card. Print 5 sets. Dyslexic student's card: images + bullets, colour-coded, 14pt font minimum.

Card A
Political Impact
  • India gained independence in 1947 after 200 years of British rule
  • Over 50 countries became independent between 1945–1980 (decolonisation)
  • Many countries kept British-style parliaments and legal systems
  • Key figure: Mahatma Gandhi — non-violent resistance movement
Card B
Economic Impact
  • Britain extracted raw materials from colonies: cotton, rubber, spices, gold
  • The transatlantic slave trade was central to British wealth (abolished 1833)
  • Colonies were forced to buy British manufactured goods
  • The Industrial Revolution was partly funded by colonial profits
Card C
Cultural Impact
  • English is now spoken by 1.5 billion people — partly due to empire
  • British culture spread globally: cricket, legal systems, education models
  • Post-colonial literature challenges the empire's narrative (e.g. Chinua Achebe)
  • Many place names, currencies, and institutions still reflect British influence
Card D
The Commonwealth Today
  • 56 countries voluntarily remain in the Commonwealth of Nations
  • Members share trade agreements, educational exchanges, and diplomatic ties
  • The British monarch is the symbolic head
  • Debated: genuine partnership, or echo of imperial power?
Card E
Resistance & Legacy
  • Colonised people resisted throughout: Mau Mau uprising (Kenya), Indian National Congress, ANC (South Africa)
  • The reparations debate: should Britain pay compensation for slavery and colonialism?
  • Rhodes Must Fall / BLM movements have reignited this debate
  • The empire's legacy is deeply contested — development vs. exploitation

My methodology choices

Presenting your methodology — key phrases (click to copy)

I chose PPP combined with CLIL because this topic requires students to develop both historical content knowledge and the English language skills to discuss it — the two are inseparable at this level.
This approach aligns with Krashen's Input Hypothesis — the adapted text provides comprehensible input at i+1, slightly above current competence but supported by visual vocabulary.
The production stage reflects Swain's Output Hypothesis — students must produce language to teach their peers and express opinions, which forces them to notice gaps in their knowledge.
The jigsaw technique reflects Vygotsky's ZPD — in the expert phase students co-construct meaning; in the home phase they teach peers, consolidating their own understanding in the process.
The lesson follows the CEFR action-oriented approach — the jigsaw creates a genuine information gap: each student holds unique knowledge the group needs, making communication purposeful and authentic.
To address the needs of the dyslexic student, I have provided colour-coded materials, a word bank, and an image-supported card — reducing decoding load without reducing cognitive challenge.
ICT is integrated through the IWB and optional Padlet, which enhances engagement, provides authentic visual input, and offers a digital alternative for written output.
