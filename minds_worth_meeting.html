A Conversation Series · C1

Minds Worth Meeting

Ten people from around the world, each carrying one big, arguable idea — and ten conversations built so that you do almost all of the talking.

About this series

Most lessons hand you a topic. These hand you a person — a writer, a scientist, a philosopher, a poet — and the single idea that made them impossible to ignore. The point is not to memorise a biography. It is to think out loud, in English, about something genuinely worth arguing over.

Every lesson follows the same five-part shape: you share what you already know, you meet the big idea in plain language, you pick up the vocabulary and the speculating-and-disagreeing phrases a C1 speaker needs, you wrestle with three timed thought-experiment speaking tasks, and you get the last word. From first minute to last, the talking is yours.

Take them in any order. Each one stands alone.

Minds Worth Meeting · 01
Stanisław Lem
The hardest question in science fiction: what if we met a mind so alien we could never understand it — and only ever saw ourselves reflected back?
Origin · Poland · Science Fiction
Minds Worth Meeting · 02
Hannah Arendt
“The banality of evil” — her unsettling claim that catastrophe is carried out not by monsters but by ordinary people who simply stop thinking.
Origin · Germany · Political Philosophy
Minds Worth Meeting · 03
Italo Calvino
Lightness as a way of telling the truth — how play, fantasy and structure can carry more weight than the heaviest realism.
Origin · Italy · Literature
Minds Worth Meeting · 04
Marie Curie
What obsession costs and what it achieves — the first woman through a door that was built to keep her out, and the price she paid for going through it.
Origin · Poland / France · Science
Minds Worth Meeting · 05
Albert Camus
The absurd — the gap between our hunger for meaning and a silent universe — and the strange freedom of choosing to live fully without false hope.
Origin · Algeria / France · Philosophy
Minds Worth Meeting · 06
Ada Lovelace
A century before the computer, she saw that a machine might do far more than calculate — it might one day create. Imagination as the engine of technology.
Origin · England · Mathematics
Minds Worth Meeting · 07
Ibn Khaldun
Why empires rise and rot: asabiyyah, the social glue that lets a group seize power — and then quietly dissolves over a few comfortable generations.
Origin · Tunisia · History & Sociology
Minds Worth Meeting · 08
Rumi
Longing as a path to truth — the cut reed crying for the reed-bed, and the dissolving of the self into something far larger than itself.
Origin · Persia (Balkh → Konya) · Poetry & Mysticism
Minds Worth Meeting · 09
Naguib Mahfouz
A single Cairo alley holding the whole weight of a nation — ordinary lives where tradition pulls against modernity and faith against change.

Minds Worth Meeting · 10

One honest woman’s voice claiming the right to speak as a whole human being — rebirth, freedom, and the real cost of refusing to be silent.
Origin · Iran · Poetry & Film
