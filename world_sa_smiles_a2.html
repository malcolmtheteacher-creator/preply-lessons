Land of Smiles: Is Every Smile the Same?

Thailand is called the “Land of Smiles”. But a smile can mean many things.

Today: you will learn what smiles mean in Thailand, and use the present continuous.
Talk first:
  • Why do people smile?
  • Do you smile at strangers?
  • Can a smile mean “sorry”?

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. She gave me a big smile.
smile (n) — a happy look on the face. Example: “She gave me a big smile.”
2. A smile can mean “I'm sorry”.
sorry (adj) — feeling bad about something. Example: “A smile can mean “I'm sorry”.”
3. Stay calm and smile.
calm (adj) — quiet and not angry. Example: “Stay calm and smile.”
4. Do not be angry in public.
angry (adj) — very cross. Example: “Do not be angry in public.”
5. A smile can hide a problem.
problem (n) — something difficult. Example: “A smile can hide a problem.”
6. Every visitor notices the smiles.
visitor (n) — a person who goes to a place for a short time. Example: “Every visitor notices the smiles.”
Talk it through:
  • Which word is new for you? Say a sentence with it.
The stereotype: “Thai people smile because they are always happy.”

The story

Part 1 — The nickname

Thailand is called the “Land of Smiles”. Visitors see friendly smiles in shops, markets and hotels. Smiles are very common in Thailand. But the nickname does not mean that every person is always happy.

Quick check:

1. What is Thailand called?

2. Are all Thai people always happy?

1. The “Land of Smiles”.

2. No.

Discuss:
  • Does your country have a nickname?

Part 2 — Many smiles

A Thai smile can mean many things. It can mean “thank you”, “sorry” or “excuse me”. It can mean “I do not agree, but I will not argue”. Sometimes it means “I am embarrassed”. Thai people also say “jai yen”, a cool heart. It means staying calm.

Quick check:

1. What does “jai yen” mean?

2. Can a smile mean “sorry”?

1. A cool heart, staying calm.

2. Yes.

Discuss:
  • When do you smile?

Part 3 — Read the smile

Look at the situation. A smile can hide a problem. It can also show that a person is embarrassed. In Thailand, it is not good to shout in public. A visitor who is calm and polite usually has a better result than a person who is angry.

Quick check:

1. What is not good in public in Thailand?

2. What works better than anger?

1. Shouting.

2. Being calm and polite.

Discuss:
  • What do you do when you are angry?

Grammar — present continuous

am / is / are + verb-ing = something happening now.
“She is smiling.” “They are talking.”
Negative: “He isn't shouting.” Question: “Is she smiling?”

1. Choose the word

1. She (smile) at the visitor now.

2. The people (talk) in the market.

3. I (not shout). I am calm.

1. is smiling

2. are talking

3. am not shouting

2. Make your own

1. At the moment I am ______.

2. My friend is ______ now.

3. They aren't ______.

1. ...studying English. / ...sitting at home.

2. ...working. / ...having lunch.

3. ...smiling. / ...shouting.
