Lesson 5 — Transform

IELTS Task 1 Comparing Data · B2 · Lesson 2 of 3 (Set 2)

Today: no tables, no word bank. Cold recall first, then surgery on real broken sentences, then a full pie chart. You will struggle — that's how it sticks.

Cold recall — flip each comparison

Ten quick transforms from Lesson 4. Type your answer, click Check. Accept close variants (as long as the grammar is right).

1. Flip it: "Spain was higher than Portugal."
Portugal was lower than Spain.
2. Flip it: "Tokyo had twice as many visitors as Osaka."
Osaka had half as many visitors as Tokyo.
3. Which proportion phrase = 25%? (2–3 words)
a quarter of (or one in four / one quarter)
4. Which proportion phrase = 75%? (2–3 words)
three-quarters of (or three in four / the majority)
5. Modifier for tiny gap (1–2%)? (1 word)
slightly (or marginally)
6. Modifier for big gap (e.g. 60% vs 15%)? (1 word)
far (or much)
7. Transform to superlative: "Reading was lower than every other activity."
Reading was the lowest activity. (Or: Reading had the least time of all.)
8. "Less / fewer" — which goes with students? (1 word)
fewer (students = countable; less goes with uncountable like money / time)
9. Transform to multiple form: "Sales in Tokyo were three times higher than in Osaka." — flip it.
Sales in Osaka were a third of those in Tokyo. (Or: only one-third as high as Tokyo.)
10. Transform to proportion: "50% of teenagers play sport."
Half of teenagers play sport. (Or: One in two teenagers plays sport.)

📊 Fact 3: Coffee sales — London £60m, Bristol £58m, Leeds £35m, Hull £20m. Write three about London vs Bristol (close gap).
A comparative:
B superlative:
C close-gap phrasing:

A. London was only slightly higher than Bristol.

Sentence surgery — fix the broken comparisons

These are the exact mistakes B2 students make every single time. Fix each one. Reveal the answer to check.

❌ "Bristol is more higher than Hull."
Clue: "higher" is already comparative.
✅ Bristol is much higher than Hull. (Drop "more"; you can add "much" or "far" for emphasis.)
❌ "London had the most highest sales."
Clue: superlative is one word, not two.
✅ London had the highest sales. (Don't double up — pick "highest" OR "the most expensive", never both.)
❌ "Dogs were twice more popular than cats."
Clue: "twice" doesn't combine with "more than".
✅ Dogs were twice as popular as cats. (The pattern is: twice / three times AS [adjective] AS.)
❌ "There were less students in 2020 than in 2010."
Clue: countable noun.
✅ There were fewer students in 2020 than in 2010. (fewer + countable; less + uncountable like time / money.)
❌ "Cats are most popular than fish."
Clue: comparing only two — superlative is wrong.
✅ Cats are more popular than fish. (Two things → comparative. Use superlative only when you compare three or more.)
❌ "A quarter of the people preferred tea, while three-quarter preferred coffee."
Clue: only one of these proportion words is correct.
✅ A quarter of the people preferred tea, while three-quarters preferred coffee. (When more than one quarter, use the plural three-quarters.)
❌ "Spain is bigger as Portugal in tourist numbers."
Clue: wrong word after "bigger".
✅ Spain is bigger than Portugal in tourist numbers. (Comparatives use than, never as. as…as is for equal things: "as big as".)
❌ "London is most highest, after that comes Bristol."
Clue: two superlative errors AND awkward linking.
✅ London had the highest sales, followed by Bristol. (Use followed by to introduce the second-place item — it's the cleanest linker for ranking.)

Free write — your own description

Look at the same pie chart from Tab 4. Write 4–6 sentences describing it. Rules:

  • Use at least one comparative (higher than / lower than / more popular than)
  • Use at least one superlative (the most / the highest / the lowest)
  • Use at least one multiple or proportion (twice as / a quarter of / accounted for)
  • Use at least one size modifier (far, slightly, considerably)

Model:

Of the five commuting methods, driving was the most popular, accounting for 40% of journeys. The train came second, used by almost a third of workers. The bus was considerably less common, at only 15%, while cycling and walking together made up just 15% of all commutes. Walking was the least popular method overall, used by only one in twenty workers — far fewer than any other category.

Notice the mix: superlative ("the most", "the least"), comparative ("less common"), proportion ("a third", "made up", "one in twenty"), size modifier ("considerably", "far fewer").
