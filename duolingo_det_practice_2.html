Level up from Lesson 1

Lesson 1 gave you the tasks and the golden rule. Lesson 2 sharpens them, refreshes every practice, and adds the two big tasks you have not drilled yet: Interactive Reading and Interactive Listening.
Still the golden rule: NEVER STOP — fill every second, every line. Now add one thing: reach a little higher.
How to turn a pass into a strong score: join your ideas with linking words (however, because, for example, on the other hand); use a range of tenses; and replace tired words — instead of good / nice / very, try useful, impressive, extremely, genuinely. Small upgrades, big difference.
Remember the nerves plan: a simple structure for every task means you are never staring at a blank. Every tab here gives you one to lean on.
Warm up (60 seconds)

Describe your town or city to someone who has never been there. Keep talking — buildings, food, weather, what to do — until the timer ends. Try to slip in two linking words.

01:00

Read Aloud — longer, smoother

Same task, harder sentences. It scores pronunciation and fluency. Now work on linking — letting words flow into each other — and stressing the important words.
How to win it: read it silently first and find the key words to stress. Let small words link (“arrive at” → “arrive-vat”). Keep an even pace, finish your endings (-ed, -s), and don't let your voice fall away at the end.

Read each one aloud — twice

READ ALOUD
Despite the heavy traffic, we managed to arrive at the airport with time to spare.
READ ALOUD
The committee agreed that the proposal deserved further discussion next week.
READ ALOUD
Scientists believe that the discovery could change the way we treat the disease.
READ ALOUD
If the train is delayed again, I will seriously consider driving instead.
READ ALOUD
Learning a language takes patience, but the rewards are absolutely worth it.
Watch: the -ed endings (managed, agreed, deserved, delayed) — say them fully. The th in the, that, further, worth, they. Stress the content words (heavy, airport, proposal, discovery, patience) and let the small words link between them.
Fluency lap (45 seconds)

Read all five, one after another, smoothly and calmly — like a newsreader who is never in a hurry.

Interactive Reading

A real test task you have not drilled yet: you read a short passage, then answer several small questions about it — filling gaps, choosing a title, and finding what is true. Read once for the general idea, then again for detail.
Many cities are now encouraging people to cycle to work. New bike lanes make the roads safer, and some companies even offer a small payment to staff who leave their cars at home. Supporters say cycling reduces traffic and pollution and keeps people healthier. Critics, however, point out that cycling is difficult in bad weather and that not everyone lives close enough to their workplace. Even so, the number of cyclists in many cities has risen sharply over the last few years.

Complete the sentence (from the passage)

1. New bike lanes make the roads ___.

2. Some companies pay staff who leave their ___ at home.

3. Critics say cycling is difficult in bad ___.

Interactive Listening

The other task you have not drilled: a conversation where you choose the best reply, then sum up what happened. Here, your teacher reads the other person's lines aloud; you pick the best response, then speak a short summary.
The situation: you telephone a language school to ask about an evening course. For each line you hear, choose the reply that is polite, clear and moves the conversation forward.
School: “Good morning, Riverside Language School. How can I help you?”
Your best reply:
School: “Of course. Which language are you interested in?”
Your best reply:
School: “We have a class on Tuesday evenings. Would that suit you?”
Your best reply:
Now sum it up (60 seconds)

In your own words, say what the phone call was about, what you asked for, and what you decided. Full sentences, no stopping.

01:00

Describe & Speak

A new photo, and new speaking topics. Same rescue frames: for the photo, general → details → a guess; for a topic, your answer → two reasons → one example.

Write about the photo (at least three sentences), then read it back:

One good version: “This is a busy outdoor market, probably in the morning. In the picture, there are several stalls full of colourful fruit and vegetables, and a lot of shoppers walking between them. A seller on the left is weighing something for a customer. It looks lively and a little crowded — people are probably buying food for the week.”
Speak about the photo (90 seconds)

Say your description out loud, then keep going: the colours, the sounds, the smells, what you would buy. Do not stop until the timer ends.

The Writing Sample

Your writing appears in the score report, so accuracy matters — tenses, articles, agreement. Structure it, then leave 30 seconds to check. A new prompt this time.
The plan: 1) State your opinion in one clear sentence. 2) Reason one + an example. 3) Reason two + an example. 4) One-sentence conclusion. Join them with linking words. Simple and correct beats long and broken.
WRITE · 5 min
Some people think children should start learning a foreign language at primary school. Others think it is better to wait until they are older. What is your opinion?
05:00

Check your own writing before you finish

  • Every verb is in the right tense (past for finished time, present perfect for since/for).
  • Singular/plural agree: a child learns, children learn.
  • Articles are there: a, an, the where needed.
  • I used at least two linking words (however, because, for example).
  • Opinion, two reasons with examples, a conclusion — and I filled the space.
