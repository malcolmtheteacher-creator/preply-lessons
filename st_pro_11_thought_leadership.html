Your Thought Leadership Narrative

Strategic Storytelling

The Throughline

The single theme that connects everything you've done, even the detours.

Think and write

If someone followed your career from the outside — no inside knowledge — what pattern would they see? What would they get wrong about your journey?

Today: We're learning how to articulate a professional throughline — the narrative that makes your path make sense.

Your CV is a list of jobs. Your throughline is the story of why that list makes sense. It's the theme that runs underneath the titles and dates. And when you can articulate it, something shifts. Your opinions stop sounding random and start sounding inevitable.

Thought leadership isn't about having the most opinions. It's about having a narrative that makes your opinions inevitable — that shows where they come from and why they matter. That narrative is built on your throughline.

Most people don't have one. They have a career. But they don't have a career story. Today we fix that.

The Narrative Resume

Two concepts: the throughline and the signature story.

1. The Throughline

A single sentence that captures the theme connecting your career. Not "I've worked in tech, education, and nonprofits" (that's a CV). But "I've spent 20 years learning that systems fail when they forget the humans inside them — and building tools to fix that."

Notice: that throughline makes the job changes make sense. Law, then tech, then education aren't random. They're chapters of the same story.

2. The Signature Stories

You need 2-3 go-to stories from your career that you can deploy in any context (keynote, podcast, interview, networking). Each one illuminates a different facet of your throughline. Each one is told using the Defining Moment method (Lesson 2) so it lands with precision.

Example: The Throughline

Career: lawyer → startup CTO → director of education at nonprofit

CV explanation: "Career changer. Worked in three different industries."

Throughline: "I've spent 20 years learning that systems fail when they forget the humans inside them — whether that's a legal system ignoring real people, a technical infrastructure ignoring user behavior, or an education system ignoring how humans actually learn."

Now the three jobs aren't random career changes. They're chapters of the same story.

Find Your Throughline

The exercise. Step by step.

Step 1: List your key career moments

Not every job. The 5-7 moments that actually changed something — a breakthrough, a pivot, a failure that taught you something, a role that unexpectedly opened a door. Write them down.

Step 2: Find the thread

Look at those moments. What's the pattern? What keeps showing up? Is it a problem you keep trying to solve? A type of person you keep helping? A kind of system you keep building or fixing? That's your thread.

Step 3: Write your throughline

In one sentence, capture the theme. Start with something like: "I've spent my career..."

Step 4: Test it

Does your throughline explain the detours? Does it explain why you left one field and went to another? Does it point forward — to the kind of work you want to do next? If yes, you've got it. If no, try again.

Step 5: Build your signature stories

You need 2-3 stories that illustrate your throughline. For each, use the Defining Moment method (Lesson 2): Isolate the moment, name what changed inside you, show before and after, make it visible, connect it outward.

Signature Story 1

A moment from your career that shows one facet of your throughline. What was the moment? What changed?

Signature Story 2

A different moment that shows a different facet. Or deepens the first one.

The Public Narrative

Deliver your throughline + one signature story in a professional context.

Choose your context and deliver

Pick two of the three scenarios below and deliver your full thought leadership narrative for each — throughline + one signature story. You're choosing the context that feels most real for you.

Option A: Opening a Keynote (3 min)

You're opening a conference or event. You have 3 minutes to establish who you are and why your thinking matters. Use your throughline and one signature story.

Option B: Podcast Interview (2 min)

The host asks: "Tell me your story." You have about 2 minutes before they ask a follow-up question. Deliver your throughline and one signature story.

Map your throughline onto the four-quadrant empathy map. Who cares about your narrative? What do they know, fear, want, and need to do? Does your throughline speak to them?

Final Review

1. What's the difference between a CV and a throughline?

2. What makes a signature story? How many do you need?

3. Why does thought leadership need narrative, not just opinions?

1. A CV is a list of jobs and accomplishments. A throughline is the single theme that makes the list make sense — the story of why your career has followed the path it has.

2. A signature story is a moment from your career (shaped using the Defining Moment method) that illuminates one aspect of your throughline. You need 2-3 so you can deploy different stories in different contexts without repeating yourself.

3. Because opinions sound like preferences. Narrative makes them sound like conclusions you've reached after experiencing something. They stop sounding random and start sounding inevitable.
