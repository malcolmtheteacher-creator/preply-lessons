Can doubt make us calmer and happier than certainty?

Your friends are arguing about a new diet. One says it's a miracle; the other says it's dangerous. Both have articles, studies and experts on their side. Do you have to choose a team? More than two thousand years ago, a Greek philosopher said that the wisest and calmest answer is: "I can't say."

Today: You'll meet Pyrrho, the first great sceptic, so you can express doubt politely in English and explain why he thought suspending judgement could bring peace of mind.

Meet Pyrrho

Read one part at a time. After each part, close your eyes and answer the question before you check.

I

From painter to traveller

Pyrrho was born around 365 BC in Elis, in southern Greece. He started his working life as a painter, not a philosopher. Then he joined Alexander the Great's army on its famous journey east, all the way to India. There, according to ancient writers, he met wise men who lived very simply, some of them almost naked. The Greeks called them the 'naked wise men'. These meetings may have changed the way Pyrrho saw the world.

Where did Pyrrho travel, and who did he meet there?

With Alexander the Great's army to India, where he met wise men who lived very simply.
II

The calm pig

Pyrrho decided that we can never be completely sure how things really are. Our senses can trick us, and for every argument there seems to be an equally strong argument against it. So, he said, the wise thing is to stop judging. A famous story says that once, on a ship in a terrible storm, everyone panicked except one passenger: a pig, calmly eating. Pyrrho pointed at it and said that a wise person should be just as calm.

Why did Pyrrho point at the pig during the storm?

Because it stayed calm and kept eating while everyone panicked. He said a wise person should be just as calm.
III

Jokes and honours

Pyrrho's enemies told funny stories about him: that he didn't trust his senses, so his friends had to save him from carts and cliffs. These were probably jokes. In fact, he lived a quiet, simple life back in Elis. According to an ancient biographer, his city respected him so much that it made him its high priest. He wrote nothing, but his student Timon spread his ideas. Pyrrho died around 275 BC, aged about 90.

Are the stories about Pyrrho walking into carts and towards cliffs true?

Probably not. They were most likely jokes told by his enemies. He actually lived a quiet life and was respected in his city.
Talk: Pyrrho thought a calm pig was a good example for humans. Is it really better to be calm than to care? When is it good to worry?

Key words

Each word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means. Then reveal and compare.

1. When her son said he had finished all his homework in five minutes, she was sceptical and asked to see it.
sceptical (adj) — not easily believing that something is true, and wanting proof first. Example: "I'm sceptical about adverts that promise you'll lose ten kilos in a week."
2. He spoke with total certainty, as if there was no chance at all that he could be wrong.
certainty (n) — the state of being completely sure about something. Example: "Nobody can say with certainty what the world will look like in fifty years."
3. The police found no evidence against him: no fingerprints, no witnesses, nothing.
evidence (n) — facts or signs that show whether something is true. Example: "There is strong evidence that regular exercise improves your mood."
4. She only reads news from one website, so her view of the story is quite biased.
biased (adj) — unfairly preferring one person, side or idea to another. Example: "The referee was accused of being biased towards the home team."
5. Even after we showed him the map, he insisted that we were going the right way.
insist (v) — to say firmly that something is true or must happen, even when others disagree. Example: "He insisted that he had locked the door, but it was wide open."
6. After the noisy city, the lake felt so tranquil: no cars, no voices, just birds.
tranquil (adj) — calm, quiet and peaceful. Example: "We stayed in a tranquil village in the mountains, far from the traffic."
7. Our perception of colour depends on the light: the same shirt can look blue indoors and grey outside.
perception (n) — the way you notice or understand something through your senses or your mind. Example: "Being tired can change your perception of time."

The idea: Suspending judgement

1
A claimSomeone says that something is true.
2
BalanceFind the arguments for it and against it.
3
SuspendIf they seem equally strong, don't decide: 'I can't say.'
4
CalmFree from the fight to be right, you find peace.

See it in action

Here is the idea in action. One friend says, "Coffee is bad for you." Another says, "No, coffee is good for you." You look it up: some studies say one thing, some say the other, and the experts disagree. Pyrrho would not choose a side. He would say, "It seems this way to some people and that way to others. I can't say which is true." He might still drink his coffee if he felt like it, but without worrying about who was right.

Your turn

Now you: explain Pyrrho's idea to a friend who gets stressed by every argument online. Speak for about a minute. Use the starters if they help.

Pyrrho believed that we can never...For every argument, there is...So instead of choosing a side, he...He thought this would bring...
Pyrrho believed that we can never be completely sure how things really are. For every argument, there is usually an equally strong argument on the other side. So instead of choosing a side, he suspended judgement and said, 'I can't say.' He thought this would bring peace of mind, because you no longer have to fight to be right.

Thought experiment: The certainty machine

Imagine a machine that can make you completely certain about the answer to one big question, for example, what the meaning of life is. But you don't know in advance which answer it will give you, and after using it you can never doubt that answer again.

Connect

Compare

Socrates and Pyrrho: Socrates said he knew that he knew nothing, but he kept asking questions to get closer to the truth. Pyrrho said we can't know, so we should stop judging and stay calm. Which kind of doubt seems more useful to you?
Socrates' doubt is a starting point: it makes you search harder. Pyrrho's doubt is a resting place: it lets you stop the search and find peace. One is about finding the truth, the other about finding calm.
Epicurus and Pyrrho: Epicurus and Pyrrho both wanted a calm mind, free from worry. Epicurus found it through simple pleasures and friends; Pyrrho found it through doubt. Which route sounds more realistic to you?
Epicurus says: understand the world, stop fearing death and the gods, and enjoy simple things. Pyrrho says: stop trying to be certain about the world, and it won't disturb you. Both aim at peace of mind, but Epicurus has firm beliefs and Pyrrho avoids them.

Remember earlier thinkers

No looking back. Answer from memory first, then check.

From Marcus Aurelius

What did Marcus Aurelius mean by the inner citadel?

That a calm mind is like a fortress: events can't get in unless our own judgement lets them. It isn't things that upset us, but our judgements about them.
From Epicurus

According to Epicurus, what is the secret of a happy life?

A calm mind and a body without pain: want only simple things that are easy to get, and enjoy them with friends.
From Socrates

Why did the oracle call Socrates the wisest man in Athens?

Because he knew that he knew nothing, while others believed they knew things they didn't.

With Alexander the Great's army to India, where he met wise men who lived very simply.

That a wise person should stay as calm as the pig, which kept eating while everyone else panicked.

Not easily believing something, and wanting proof first. For example, being sceptical about an advert for a miracle diet.
