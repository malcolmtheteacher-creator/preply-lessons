Today's Question

Some people believe that governments should not provide international aid to other countries when they have unemployment and homelessness in their own country.

To what extent do you agree or disagree?

(Based on a question from IELTS Academic 2024)

Remember: This IS an opinion essay. You MUST state your position clearly in the introduction and maintain it throughout. Do not sit on the fence.

Model Essay: Band 8

Notice the PARTIALLY AGREE position: the essay acknowledges that domestic problems are important but argues international aid should continue. Pay attention to the opinion language and how counter-arguments are handled.

INTROWhile it is true that many governments face serious domestic challenges such as unemployment and homelessness, I believe that completely withdrawing international aid would be misguided and morally indefensible. This essay argues that governments can and should pursue both objectives simultaneously.
BODY 1The primary argument for maintaining international aid is the moral imperative to alleviate human suffering across borders. Humanitarian organisations such as the WHO have demonstrated that preventive international health programmes reduce the incidence of infectious disease outbreaks that would later cost far more to address domestically. Furthermore, instability in developing regions often creates refugee crises that place additional burdens on wealthy nations. Strategic aid investments today prevent costlier humanitarian interventions tomorrow, making the initial expenditure economically prudent.
BODY 2Admittedly, governments must address homelessness and unemployment within their borders; these are urgent social imperatives. However, the premise of the question presents a false dichotomy. Scandinavian countries such as Denmark and Norway demonstrate that nations can maintain robust welfare systems AND substantial international aid budgets simultaneously. It would be short-sighted to believe that governments can only solve one problem at a time; indeed, strategic fiscal planning and progressive taxation can fund both.
CONCLUSIONIn conclusion, while domestic hardship demands attention, I believe that abandoning international aid would be both ineffective and morally indefensible. A balanced approach that addresses home concerns whilst maintaining global responsibility is both possible and necessary.

Opinion language patterns used:

"While it is true that... I believe that..." — acknowledges one side but states clear position

"The primary argument for... is..." — introduces main point with authority

"Admittedly,... However,..." — concedes a point then rebuts it

"It would be short-sighted to..." — dismisses opposite view

"...both possible and necessary" — concludes with balanced optimism

Evidence patterns:

Real examples: WHO, Scandinavian countries (Denmark, Norway)

Cause-effect: "preventive aid → fewer domestic costs later"

Logic: "false dichotomy", "short-sighted", "fiscally possible"

Write Your Introduction

Question

Should governments prioritise international aid or focus only on domestic unemployment and homelessness? To what extent do you agree or disagree?

Introduction frame (adapt for this topic):

While it is true that [acknowledge one side: domestic problems], I believe that [your clear position: maintain aid / cut aid / balance both]. [Signal the direction: argue that governments should... / demonstrate that...].
Paraphrasing challenge: Do NOT write "international aid" and "unemployment and homelessness" directly from the question. Try: "global development programmes", "humanitarian assistance", "joblessness and housing poverty", "domestic welfare priorities."

Write Body 1: Your Main Argument

Why should we continue/cut aid? What is your strongest reason? Explain it with detail and evidence.

Opinion language toolkit:

[The primary / A compelling / The strongest] argument for [your position] is that [your reason].
[This / Such programmes / These interventions] have [demonstrated / proven / shown] that [evidence or consequence].
[Organisations such as / Research from / Evidence shows that] [specific example].
Avoid vague arguments! "Aid is important" ✗ — "International health programmes prevent disease outbreaks that would later cost developed nations far more to address" ✓

Write Body 2: Counter + Rebuttal OR Second Argument

You can either: (1) Concede the opposite view, then counter it, OR (2) State a second argument for your position. Choose one approach.

Option A: Concede + Rebuttal (RECOMMENDED)

Admittedly, / It is true that [acknowledge the opposite view: domestic problems matter].
However, / Nevertheless, [rebuttal: explain why this doesn't mean we should cut aid].
[It would be short-sighted / Naive / A false dichotomy] to [dismiss the opposite argument].

Option B: Second Argument

Furthermore, / Additionally, [your second reason for maintaining/cutting aid].
[Evidence shows / Countries such as] [concrete example that supports your second argument].
Recommended: Use Option A (Concede + Rebuttal) because it shows the examiner you understand both sides — a hallmark of Band 8.
