The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. His claim to the throne was weak.
a claim (n) — a right you say you have.
2. He married Elizabeth of York to unite the families.
to unite (v) — to join together.
3. Pretenders claimed to be lost princes.
a pretender (n) — someone claiming a title falsely.
4. He filled the royal treasury.
a treasury (n) — the place where money is kept.
5. He was frugal.
frugal (adj) — careful with money.
6. Nobles had to pay fines.
a fine (n) — money paid as punishment.
7. He made nobles sign bonds.
a bond (n) — a written promise to pay if you break a rule.
8. He worked to suppress noble private armies.
to suppress (v) — to stop by force.
9. He founded the Tudor dynasty.
a dynasty (n) — a series of rulers from one family.
10. The Tudor rose became an emblem.
an emblem (n) — a symbol.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — From exile to king

Henry Tudor, a Lancastrian with a weak claim, spent fourteen years in exile in Brittany and France. In 1485 he landed in Wales and defeated Richard III at Bosworth.

In 1486 he married Elizabeth of York, Edward IV's daughter, uniting the two houses. The Tudor rose combined the red and the white.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did Henry spend his exile?

2. Why marry Elizabeth of York?

1. Brittany and France.

2. To unite Lancaster and York.

Discuss:
  • Why are symbols like the Tudor rose powerful?

Part 2 — Pretenders

Two young men claimed to be Yorkist heirs. Lambert Simnel, who pretended to be the Earl of Warwick, was defeated in 1487 and given a job in the royal kitchens. Perkin Warbeck claimed to be Richard, younger of the princes; he was captured and executed in 1499.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened to Lambert Simnel?

2. What happened to Perkin Warbeck?

1. He was given a job in the royal kitchens.

2. He was captured and executed in 1499.

Discuss:
  • Why did Henry spare Simnel but not Warbeck?

Part 3 — Money

Henry checked the accounts personally, initialling pages. He used fines and bonds to control nobles. By his death in 1509 he left a large fortune and a secure throne.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did Henry control nobles?

2. What did he leave?

1. With fines and bonds.

2. A large fortune and a secure throne.

Discuss:
  • Is a careful ruler a boring one?

Grammar — Relative Clauses

To pack a lot of information into one sentence — who someone was, where something happened — we use relative clauses.

How it works:
• who for people, which for things, where for places, whose for possession.
• Non-defining clauses add extra information with commas: "Edward, who was deeply religious, rebuilt Westminster Abbey."
• Defining clauses identify which one — no commas.

1. Choose who, which, where or whose

Complete each sentence about Henry VII. Use the word in brackets.

1. Henry, claim was weak, won at Bosworth.

2. Elizabeth of York, father was Edward IV, became queen.

3. The Tudor rose is the emblem united the roses.

1. whose

2. whose

3. that / which

2. Join the sentences

1. Simnel was spared. He was a boy. (who)

2. Henry checked the accounts. He initialled them. (which)

3. Brittany is where he lived in exile. (where)

1. Simnel, who was a boy, was spared.

2. Henry checked the accounts, which he initialled.

3. Brittany is the place where he lived in exile.

Going deeper

Henry VII is less famous than his son, but he ended the Wars of the Roses and built a stable state. He also sponsored John Cabot's voyage to North America in 1497.

Worth noticing: many of his account books with his initials survive.
Learning from the past — for today: Careful habits build security. Checking your accounts, paying debts and avoiding risky spending may be boring, but they create stability.
Dig into it — talk these through:
