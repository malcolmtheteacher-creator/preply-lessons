Signposting and Presentation Language

These phrases help organize and present data clearly:

Checking Understanding

Is that clear? / Does that make sense?
Any questions so far?

Referring to Research

Research shows / studies indicate that...
According to recent data...

Organizing Your Presentation

First / Firstly, let's start by...
Secondly / Next / Moving on to...
Finally / To conclude / In summary...

Referring to Data in a Graph

As you can see in the graph...
The data shows that...
This demonstrates / illustrates...

Pronunciation: Signposting Intonation

In presentations, signposting phrases have distinctive intonation patterns:

Rising-Falling Intonation ↑↘

Use this pattern for introductory signposting phrases:

OK, so, welcome to week 3 of our course.

First, let's start by looking at some data.

In fact, the global catch actually trebled.

However, there is always a risk of disease.

Firstly, increased demand for shark fin soup.

Secondly, increased persecution of sharks.

Practice Tip

When you use signposting phrases, start on a higher pitch and drop down at the end. This makes your presentation clearer and more professional.

Discussion Topics

1. What is Environmental Science?

What do you know about this field? What modules do you think an Environmental Sciences degree covers?

2. Local Conservation

Do you know of any environmental conservation projects in your area or country?

3. Endangered Species

Are there endangered species in your country? Why are they at risk?

4. Teaching About Conservation

How important is it to teach children about endangered species and conservation?

5. Humanity and Species

What do you think the growing list of endangered species says about humankind?

Data Presentation Task

Prepare a short presentation describing environmental data using proper signposting language.

Step 1: Select Your Data

Choose an environmental science dataset you find interesting (marine conservation, species population changes, pollution levels, habitat loss, etc.)

Step 2: Plan Your Presentation Structure

Use this structure with signposting language:

Introduction: "OK, so today I want to look at..."

Data Description: "As you can see in the graph / The data shows that..."

Analysis: "This demonstrates / illustrates..."

Conclusion: "In summary / Finally..."
