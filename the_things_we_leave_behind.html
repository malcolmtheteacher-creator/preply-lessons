Marcus from Seattle

Marcus, a taxi driver, found a worn teddy bear in his cab. Most people would throw it away. Instead, he posted it online. Within hours, a mother called in tears - her daughter had carried that bear since birth. It had survived three house moves, two countries, and a hospital stay. "It's just stuffing and fabric," she said. "But to her, it's everything." A complete stranger had understood what it meant.

Elena from Prague

Elena works in the lost property office at Prague's main train station. "People lose practical things - laptops, keys, glasses. But it's the personal things they call back about, crying. Last month, a man left his father's watch. It was worth maybe 50 euros. He offered me 500 to find it. Some objects have sentimental value that you can't put a price on."

Collocations A

Click each card to reveal the meaning, then try using it yourself.

sentimental value

Click to reveal
Meaning
The emotional importance of an object, separate from its financial worth. Something has sentimental value when it connects us to memories, people, or moments.
Form
have/hold sentimental value (for someone)
something of sentimental value
Note: "sentimental" only pairs with "value" in this fixed phrase
Pronunciation & Register
/ˌsen.tɪˈmen.təl ˈvæl.juː/ - Stress on "MEN" and "VAL". Neutral register - used in conversation and formal contexts.
"This ring isn't expensive, but it has huge sentimental value - my grandmother wore it on her wedding day."

NOW YOU TRY:

Tell me about something you own that has sentimental value. What memories does it hold?

a lucky escape

Click to reveal
Meaning
When you avoid something bad by chance or good fortune. Often used when you nearly lost something, nearly had an accident, or nearly made a serious mistake.
Form
have a lucky escape
That was a lucky escape!
Note: "escape" pairs with "lucky," "narrow," or "miraculous" but NOT "fortunate escape"
Pronunciation & Register
/ə ˈlʌk.i ɪˈskeɪp/ - Informal/conversational. Often said with relief or surprise.
"I left my laptop on the train, but someone handed it in. That was a lucky escape!"

NOW YOU TRY:

Describe a time you had a lucky escape - with losing something, an accident, or a bad decision you almost made.

a lasting impression

Click to reveal
Meaning
An effect or memory that stays with you for a long time. Can be positive or negative. Often used about people, places, experiences, or moments.
Form
make/leave a lasting impression (on someone)
something made a lasting impression
Note: "lasting" pairs naturally with "impression," "impact," "effect," "memory"
Pronunciation & Register
/ə ˈlɑː.stɪŋ ɪmˈpreʃ.ən/ - Slightly formal. Often used when reflecting on meaningful experiences.
"The kindness of that stranger who returned my wallet left a lasting impression on me."

NOW YOU TRY:

What's something from your past - a place, person, or moment - that made a lasting impression on you? Why did it stay with you?

Collocations B

Two more collocations to complete your set of five.

a personal touch

Click to reveal
Meaning
Something individual and human that makes an experience feel special, warm, or customized. The opposite of generic or impersonal.
Form
add/give a personal touch
something with a personal touch
something lacks a personal touch
Note: Often used in hospitality, gifts, communication
Pronunciation & Register
/ə ˈpɜː.sən.əl tʌtʃ/ - Neutral to slightly warm. Common in service industries and when talking about thoughtfulness.
"The hotel left a handwritten welcome note in my room - I loved that personal touch."

NOW YOU TRY:

When has someone added a personal touch to something - a gift, a service, a message? What made it feel special?

a complete stranger

Click to reveal
Meaning
Someone you have never met before and know nothing about. "Complete" emphasizes that there is zero connection or familiarity.
Form
a complete/total/perfect stranger
Note: We say "complete stranger" NOT "full stranger" or "absolute stranger"
Often used with verbs like: helped, trusted, talked to, gave to
Pronunciation & Register
/ə kəmˈpliːt ˈstreɪn.dʒər/ - Neutral. Often used when expressing surprise that someone unknown did something kind (or unexpected).
"A complete stranger noticed I'd dropped my wallet and ran after me to return it."

NOW YOU TRY:

Tell me about a time a complete stranger helped you, surprised you, or made your day better. What happened?
