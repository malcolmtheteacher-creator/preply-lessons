Haggling at a Market

You see a beautiful carpet at a busy market. You like it — but the price is high. Now you talk, you smile, and you try to pay less.

The Scene 🎬

You are on holiday. Today you are in a big, busy market. There are lamps, spices, and lots of people. On one stall, you see a beautiful carpet. The colours are lovely. You stop and look.

In this market, the first price is not the real price. You can talk, and the price can go down. This is called "haggling" — you ask for a lower price, in a friendly way. It is normal here. It is like a small, happy game.

The seller sees your face. He smiles. He knows you like the carpet! Your job is easy: say you like it, ask the price, ask for less — and be ready to say "no, thank you" and walk away. That is your best trick.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like markets? What do you like to buy — food, clothes, or small gifts?
  2. In your country, can you ask for a lower price? Or is the price always fixed?

Who's who

You
The Buyer 🧳

You like the carpet, but you do not want to pay a lot. You are friendly. You smile. And you are ready to walk away if the price stays high.

The other person
The Seller 🧵

He is friendly and he loves to talk. He always starts with a high price. But he wants to sell — so the price can go down.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each, then change. Find the phrases from your toolkit. Listen for the friendly words, and watch the price go down.

SellerHello, hello! Come, look. This carpet is very beautiful. Do you like it?
YouYes, it's beautiful. Where is it from?
SellerMy family makes them, in the mountains. This one took three months! Touch it — it is very soft.
YouIt's lovely. How much is this one?
SellerFor you, my friend, a special price. One hundred.
YouOh! That's too much for me. It is beautiful, but one hundred is a lot of money.
SellerOK, OK. You like it, I can see. Tell me — how much do you want to pay?
YouCan you make it cheaper? Can I give you fifty?
SellerFifty? Oh, no, no! My family works so hard. Eighty. Eighty is a good price.
YouHmm. Eighty is still a lot. I think I'll leave it. Thank you.
SellerWait, wait! Don't go. OK — for you, seventy. Seventy is my best price.
YouSeventy… OK. It's a deal! Thank you very much.
SellerThank you, my friend! You will love it. Have a good day!

From memory — don't look up!

1. Before the price, the buyer asks one question about the carpet. What does the buyer ask?

2. The seller starts at one hundred. What is the last price — the price the buyer pays?

3. The buyer says "I think I'll leave it." Then the price goes down again. Why is "I'll leave it" a good thing to say?

1. The buyer asks where the carpet is from ("Where is it from?").
2. Seventy. (The price goes 100 → 80 → 70.)
3. When you look ready to leave, the seller is afraid you will go with no carpet — so he gives you a better, lower price to make you stay. Being ready to walk away is your strongest move.

The Echo 🕰️

A long, long time ago, there was a very long road. It was called the Silk Road. It went from China all the way to Europe. On this road, people carried silk, spices, gold, and lots of other beautiful things.

People from many countries met on this road. They did not speak the same language. But they could still buy and sell — with a smile, a price, and their hands. They haggled, just like you did today.

So when you haggle for a carpet, you do a very old thing. People have done it for two thousand years. You are part of a very long story!

Two questions to talk about

  1. On the Silk Road, people did not speak the same language, but they could still trade. How can two people buy and sell with almost no words?
  2. Today, many shops have fixed prices and you can buy with one click. Is that better than haggling? Or do we lose something fun?
