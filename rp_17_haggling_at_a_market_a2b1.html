Haggling at a Market

You see a beautiful carpet at a busy market. You like it — but the price is high. Now you talk, you smile, and you try to pay less.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

You are on holiday. Today you are in a big, busy market. There are lamps, spices, and lots of people. On one stall, you see a beautiful carpet. The colours are lovely. You stop and look.

In this market, the first price is not the real price. You can talk, and the price can go down. This is called "haggling" — you ask for a lower price, in a friendly way. It is normal here. It is like a small, happy game.

The seller sees your face. He smiles. He knows you like the carpet! Your job is easy: say you like it, ask the price, ask for less — and be ready to say "no, thank you" and walk away. That is your best trick.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like markets? What do you like to buy — food, clothes, or small gifts?
  2. In your country, can you ask for a lower price? Or is the price always fixed?

Who's who

You
The Buyer 🧳

You like the carpet, but you do not want to pay a lot. You are friendly. You smile. And you are ready to walk away if the price stays high.

The other person
The Seller 🧵

He is friendly and he loves to talk. He always starts with a high price. But he wants to sell — so the price can go down.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the friendly words — beautiful, thank you, a special price. It is OK to speak slowly. It is OK to say "no, thank you" and walk away.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the friendly words, and watch the price go down.

SellerHello, hello! Come, look. This carpet is very beautiful. Do you like it?
YouYes, it's beautiful. Where is it from?
SellerMy family makes them, in the mountains. This one took three months! Touch it — it is very soft.
YouIt's lovely. How much is this one?
SellerFor you, my friend, a special price. One hundred.
YouOh! That's too much for me. It is beautiful, but one hundred is a lot of money.
SellerOK, OK. You like it, I can see. Tell me — how much do you want to pay?
YouCan you make it cheaper? Can I give you fifty?
SellerFifty? Oh, no, no! My family works so hard. Eighty. Eighty is a good price.
YouHmm. Eighty is still a lot. I think I'll leave it. Thank you.
SellerWait, wait! Don't go. OK — for you, seventy. Seventy is my best price.
YouSeventy… OK. It's a deal! Thank you very much.
SellerThank you, my friend! You will love it. Have a good day!

From memory — don't look up!

1. Before the price, the buyer asks one question about the carpet. What does the buyer ask?

2. The seller starts at one hundred. What is the last price — the price the buyer pays?

3. The buyer says "I think I'll leave it." Then the price goes down again. Why is "I'll leave it" a good thing to say?

1. The buyer asks where the carpet is from ("Where is it from?").
2. Seventy. (The price goes 100 → 80 → 70.)
3. When you look ready to leave, the seller is afraid you will go with no carpet — so he gives you a better, lower price to make you stay. Being ready to walk away is your strongest move.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use in any market. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it with a smile. Haggling is friendly here — it is not a fight. A warm voice and a smile work best. It is always OK to ask for a lower price, and it is always OK to say "no, thank you" and walk away.

😍 Say you like it

Phrase 1

You stop at the stall and you love the carpet. You want to say a nice thing about it. Which sentence is warm and friendly?

A. "I need this! How much? Quick!"
B. "This is beautiful."
C. "Is this the cheap one?"
B — "This is beautiful."
A nice, simple thing to say. It makes the seller happy — and a happy seller gives a better price. A shows you want it too much (bad!). C sounds cold. Also good: "I really like this." "The colours are lovely."
Phrase 2

You want the seller to talk about the carpet — where it comes from. Ask a short, friendly question. Say it out loud first, then check.

"Where is it from?"
A short, easy question. Sellers love to talk about their things, and a talking seller is a friendly seller. Also try: "Who made it?" "Is it handmade?"

💰 Ask the price

Phrase 3

Now you want to know the price. Put the words in order:

ishow muchthis?
"How much is this?"
The easy, normal way to ask a price. You can also say: "How much is it?" or "How much does it cost?"
Phrase 4

The seller says a high number. You want to say it is too high — but stay friendly, no "no". Complete it: "That's too ___ for me."

"That's too much for me."
"Too much" = more money than you want to pay. It is soft and polite — you do not say "no", you just say it is a lot. Add: "It's beautiful, but it's too much for me."

🤝 Ask for a lower price

Phrase 5

You want the seller to drop the price. Which question is friendly and clear?

A. "Your price is bad. Change it."
B. "Can you make it cheaper?"
C. "Give me a low price now."
B — "Can you make it cheaper?"
"Can you…?" is soft and polite. "Cheaper" = a lower price. A and C sound like an order, and people help you less when you are rude. Also good: "Can you do a better price?"
Phrase 6

Now you say your own price — a lower number. Put the words in order:

give youcan Ififty?
"Can I give you fifty?"
Start low! The seller started high, so you start low, and you meet in the middle. Also good: "I can pay fifty." "How about fifty?"

🚶 Ready to walk away

Phrase 7

The price is still too high. Your best move now is to look ready to leave — this makes the price go down. Which is the calm, friendly way to do it?

A. "I think I'll leave it. Thank you."
B. "This is a bad shop! Goodbye!"
C. "No! Never! Too expensive!"
A — "I think I'll leave it. Thank you."
This is your strongest move. You are calm and kind, but you start to go. The seller does not want to lose the sale, so he often says a better price! Keep it warm so he calls you back. Also: "Maybe next time. Thank you."
Phrase 8

The seller gives you a good price. You are happy and you want to say yes! What do you say? Say it out loud first, then check.

"OK, it's a deal! Thank you very much."
"It's a deal!" is a happy way to say yes and finish. Shake hands and smile. Also good: "OK, I'll take it!" "Yes, thank you, I'll buy it."

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in markets, shops, and online too. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"How much is ___?" — Think of a thing you want to buy. What is it?

"Can you make it cheaper?" — Where could you say this? Write the place and the thing.

"Can I give you ___?" — Choose your own lower price. What number do you offer?

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole thing out loud — say you like it → ask the price → say it's too much → ask for cheaper → offer a price → walk away → say "it's a deal!". When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your market. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Role-play timer: 3:00

Round 1 — you are the buyer 🧳

The seller speaks — you answer. Be friendly, smile, and remember: it is OK to walk away.

SellerHello, hello! Come, look. This carpet is the best in the market. Do you like it?
SellerMy family makes them! Three months of work. So — do you want it?
SellerFor you, a special price — one hundred! Almost free, my friend.
SellerOK, OK. Tell me — how much do you want to pay?
SellerSo low! No, no. Eighty. Eighty is a very good price.
SellerWait, wait! OK — seventy. Seventy, my best price, my friend!

Round 2 — now YOU are the seller 🧵

Change places. It is your stall and your carpet. Start with a HIGH price, love your carpet, and come down slowly. Have fun!

BuyerThis is beautiful. Where is it from?
BuyerIt's lovely. How much is this one?
BuyerOh! That's too much for me.
BuyerCan you make it cheaper? Can I give you fifty?
BuyerHmm. That's still a lot. I think I'll leave it. Thank you.
BuyerOK, it's a deal! Thank you very much.
The Twist

🌩️ Only fifty in your pocket

One more time — no script now. You love the carpet, but you have only fifty in your pocket. You have no card. The seller wants seventy. What do you do? You can say, "I only have fifty, sorry." You can ask for a smaller carpet. Or you can walk away and try again. Stay friendly and smile. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said a nice thing about the carpet first.
  • I asked the price clearly ("How much is this?").
  • I asked for a lower price in a friendly way.
  • I looked ready to walk away ("I think I'll leave it").
  • I stayed friendly and smiled the whole time.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

A long, long time ago, there was a very long road. It was called the Silk Road. It went from China all the way to Europe. On this road, people carried silk, spices, gold, and lots of other beautiful things.

People from many countries met on this road. They did not speak the same language. But they could still buy and sell — with a smile, a price, and their hands. They haggled, just like you did today.

So when you haggle for a carpet, you do a very old thing. People have done it for two thousand years. You are part of a very long story!

Two questions to talk about

  2. Today, many shops have fixed prices and you can buy with one click. Is that better than haggling? Or do we lose something fun?
