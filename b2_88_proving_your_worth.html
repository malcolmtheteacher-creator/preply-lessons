Language for Presenting Your Case

When you need to convince someone that you are qualified for a role, these phrases help you sound confident and professional.

Talking about experience

"I have extensive experience in [area] — over [X] years."

"Throughout my career, I have consistently demonstrated [skill/quality]."

"My track record shows that I can deliver results at a senior level."

Talking about qualifications

"My qualification is equivalent to [local standard] and has been formally recognised by [body]."

"I completed my training at [institution], which is accredited by [organisation]."

"I have undergone additional training in [area] to meet the requirements."

Expressing frustration professionally

"I understand the process, but I feel my experience should speak for itself."

"It can be disheartening when you have to re-prove something you have already achieved."

"I am fully committed to completing whatever steps are needed, but I do find it frustrating."

Situation 1

You are in a meeting with HR. They ask why you think you deserve the promotion.

a) "I have extensive experience in this area — over seven years."

b) "I think I am probably quite good at this."

c) "Nobody else could do this job as well as me."

a) — Professional, specific, and backed by evidence. (b) is too vague and weak; (c) is arrogant.

Situation 2

You have been told your foreign qualification needs to be validated. You want to express that you find it frustrating without being unprofessional.

a) "This is ridiculous. I already did this years ago."

b) "I am fully committed to completing the steps, but I do find it frustrating."

c) "Fine, whatever you say."

b) — Shows commitment while being honest about feelings. (a) is too aggressive; (c) is passive and dismissive.
