The Nature of God

Christianity teaches that God has three key attributes:

  • Omnipotent: All-powerful; able to do anything
  • Omnibenevolent: All-loving; wholly good; desires the best for creation
  • Omniscient: All-knowing; knows past, present, and future

Scripture (John 1:1-3, NRSV): "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things came into being through him, and without him not one thing came into being."

This passage shows that God (the Word) existed before creation and is responsible for all that exists.

The Trinity: Father, Son, Holy Spirit

Christians believe in one God who exists as three persons:

  • Father: Creator and sustainer of the universe; God as lawgiver and judge
  • Son: Jesus Christ; God incarnate (God in human form); redeemer of humanity
  • Holy Spirit: God's presence in the world; guides believers; gives spiritual gifts; empowers the Church

The Nicene Creed (325 AD): A statement of Christian belief affirming the Trinity and defining who Jesus is. It says Jesus is "of one Being with the Father" — fully God, not a created being.

The Trinity is difficult to explain — Christians often use analogies like water (liquid, solid, gas) or a three-leaf clover.

Creation & Incarnation

Creation (Genesis 1): God created the universe from nothing in six days, and pronounced creation "good." God rested on the seventh day (Sabbath).

The Problem of Sin: Humans chose to disobey God (original sin), breaking the perfect relationship between God and creation. Sin separated humans from God.

Incarnation: God became human in Jesus Christ to restore the relationship between God and humanity. Jesus showed people who God is by his teachings, miracles, and sacrificial love.

Crucifixion: Jesus died on the cross as a payment for human sin. Christians see this as the ultimate act of God's love — dying to save the ones he loves.

Resurrection: Jesus rose from the dead after three days, proving his victory over sin and death. This gives Christians hope in eternal life and forgiveness.

Salvation: The process of being saved from sin and its consequences. Through faith in Jesus, Christians believe they are forgiven and restored to relationship with God.

Christian Beliefs in Practice

How beliefs shape what Christians do:

  • Belief in God's love → Practice of prayer: Christians pray because they believe God is personal and listens
  • Belief in resurrection → Practice of celebrating Easter: Easter remembers Jesus' victory over death
  • Belief in the Holy Spirit → Practice of worship & praise: Christians worship to experience God's presence and power
  • Belief in salvation → Practice of Communion/Eucharist: Remembering Jesus' death and receiving forgiveness
  • Belief in the Trinity → Practice of baptism "in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit": Initiation into the Christian faith

In Y10–11, you'll study these practices in depth. For now, recognise: Christian beliefs aren't just intellectual — they shape how believers live, worship, and treat others.
