Christian Teachings on Marriage

The Purpose and Sacrament of Marriage

In Christianity, marriage is understood as:

  • A covenant with God — not just a legal contract, but a sacred bond blessed by God
  • Procreation and unity — "it is not good for man to be alone" (Genesis 2:18)
  • Mutual support and love — Ephesians 5:25 calls husbands to love wives as Christ loves the Church

Christian Marriage Vows (Traditional)

"To have and to hold from this day forward, for better, for worse, for richer, for poorer, in sickness and in health, to love and to cherish, till death do us part."

This reflects the lifelong, unconditional commitment expected in Christian marriage.

Islamic Teachings on Marriage (Nikkah)

Nikkah: The Marriage Contract

In Islam, marriage is a contractual agreement (Nikkah) witnessed by the community. Key elements:

  • Ijab and Qabul — offer and acceptance between bride and groom (or representatives)
  • Mahr — a gift from groom to bride, signifying his commitment and her financial security
  • Witnesses — marriage is public, witnessed by the community
  • Purpose — Surah 30:21 — "He created for you mates from among yourselves that you may dwell in tranquility with them"

Cohabitation and Pre-marital Relationships

Christian Views on Cohabitation

Traditional/Catholic position: Sex outside marriage is sinful (premarital cohabitation is viewed as living in sin). 1 Corinthians 6:18 — "Flee from sexual immorality."

Progressive Protestant position: While some Protestants maintain traditional views, others see cohabitation as acceptable if there is genuine commitment and intention to marry. Emphasis shifts from legal status to relational commitment.

Islamic Views on Cohabitation

Traditional Islamic position: Cohabitation outside marriage is forbidden (Zina — illicit sexual relations). Surah 17:32 — "Do not approach unlawful sexual intercourse. Indeed, it is ever an immorality and, as a way, evil."

Islam emphasises marriage as the only legitimate framework for sexual relationships. However, modern Muslim families increasingly face questions about dating and engagement periods.

Key Difference: Permanence vs. Conditioned Contract

Christianity: Emphasises the permanence of marriage — the ideal is lifelong union; divorce is a failure and tragedy.

Islam: Sees marriage as a contract that can be dissolved, though divorce is discouraged and regulated to protect both parties.

Family Roles and Authority

Christian Teachings on Family Structure

Traditional Christian view (often complementarian): Ephesians 5:22-24 — wives submit to husbands; Ephesians 5:25 — husbands love wives sacrificially. This creates a hierarchy with husband as "head of the household."

Progressive Christian view (egalitarian): Emphasises Galatians 3:28 — "There is neither male nor female... in Christ Jesus." Equal partnership and mutual submission (Ephesians 5:21) is the ideal.

Parental authority: Children are to "honour father and mother" (Exodus 20:12); parents have responsibility to discipline and educate children in faith.

Islamic Teachings on Family Structure

Traditional Islamic teaching: Surah 4:34 — "Men are guardians over women... those [women] whose righteousness you fear." This is often interpreted as male leadership in family decisions, though Islamic law protects women's property and inheritance rights.

Complementarian interpretation: Different roles (not unequal value) — men bear financial responsibility; women manage household and childcare (though women can work).

Modern Muslim families: Many Muslims reinterpret Surah 4:34, emphasising "consultation" (Shura) and equal partnership while maintaining traditional gender roles in specific areas.

Parental authority: Children owe parents respect and obedience; Surah 17:23 — "Your Lord has decreed... that you be kind to parents."

Guidance

Answer the following questions to check your knowledge. Aim for 2-3 sentences per answer.

What does "Nikkah" mean in Islamic law?

Answer: Nikkah is the Islamic marriage contract, an agreement between bride and groom witnessed by the community. It includes ijab (offer), qabul (acceptance), and the Mahr (gift from groom to bride).

Name one Christian scripture about marriage and explain its meaning.

Answer: Genesis 2:18 — "It is not good for man to be alone" — emphasises that humans are created for companionship and relationship. Or Ephesians 5:25 — husbands should love wives as Christ loves the Church — emphasises sacrificial, unconditional love.

What is the Catholic position on divorce?

Answer: Catholicism teaches that marriage is indissoluble (cannot be dissolved). Divorce is not permitted; only annulment (declaring the marriage was never valid from the start) is possible.

How does Islam view divorce (Talaq)?

Answer: Divorce is legally permissible but morally disliked. The Hadith states: "Of all things permitted, divorce is the most disliked by Allah." There is a waiting period (Iddah) for potential reconciliation.

What is the main difference between Christian and Islamic views on marriage permanence?

Answer: Christianity emphasises the permanence and sacramental nature of marriage as a covenant with God. Islam views marriage as a contract that can be dissolved, though it is discouraged.

Key Takeaways

  • Christian marriage is a covenant with God, ideally lifelong, emphasising spiritual union and procreation.
  • Islamic Nikkah is a contract with specified conditions, including Mahr and witnesses, offering legal protections to both parties.
  • Cohabitation is rejected in traditional Christianity and Islam, though modern interpretations vary.
  • Divorce is disallowed (Catholicism) or permitted but discouraged (Protestantism, Islam), reflecting different views on marriage as permanent covenant vs. contract.
  • Family roles vary within both traditions: traditional complementarianism vs. progressive egalitarianism.
  • Both traditions emphasise commitment, mutual care, and the importance of family as a social and spiritual unit.
