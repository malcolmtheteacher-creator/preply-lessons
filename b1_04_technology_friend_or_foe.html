Technology: Friend or Foe?

Let's Get Started

Discussion Question

Do you agree or disagree with these statements about technology?

  • 1. The Internet has opened up new opportunities for everyone.
  • 2. With the technology now available, anyone can take a professional-looking photograph.
  • 3. Children under the age of 12 should not have portable devices such as tablets and smartphones.
  • 4. The more technology advances, the lazier we become and the more impatient we become.
  • 5. Advances in technology have freed us from daily chores and left us more time for what's important to us in life.
  • 6. New technology is not only more efficient; it's becoming more and more stylish.
  • 7. Technology has had a negative effect on the environment.
  • 8. E-books and film and music downloads are very handy, but people will always want to read real books, listen to live music, and spend time at the cinema.

Key Vocabulary

Common Terms When Describing Technology
Quality & Features
• high/low quality - the standard or level of excellence
• user-friendly - easy to use and understand
• intuitive design - naturally easy to understand without explanation
• lightweight - not heavy; easy to carry
• functional - designed to be practical and useful
• stylish - fashionable and attractive in appearance
Performance & Capability
• powerful zoom - ability to magnify images significantly
• screen resolution - clarity and sharpness of the display
• downloadable apps - programs you can obtain and install
• battery life - how long the device works before needing recharge
• processing speed - how quickly a device performs tasks

Phrases for Discussion & Debate

Use these phrases to participate effectively in a debate about technology.

• For instance, ... - to give a specific example
• Just look at ... - to draw attention to evidence
• In answer to that, I'd say ... - to directly respond to a point
• Let's get back to (what I wanted to say) ... - to refocus the discussion
• Take (me) for example, ... - to use yourself as an illustration
• What about ...? - to raise a new point for consideration
• Exactly! - to show strong agreement
• I agree to a point. - to partially agree with some reservations
• I can't agree. - to clearly disagree
• I understand, but you have to remember ... - to acknowledge their point before presenting a counter-argument
• Yes, that's very true, but think about ... - to validate their point while offering an alternative perspective
• Yes, you're absolutely right, but ... - to agree strongly before presenting a different angle
• (Helen's) got a point. - to acknowledge someone else's good argument
• Oh come on! - to express skepticism or disagreement casually
• I'm sorry to interrupt, but ... - to politely break in with your point
• Can I just say something? - to ask permission to contribute
• I'd just like to add something here ... - to contribute an additional point
• You need to consider ... - to suggest an important factor they've overlooked

"I agree to a point, but smartphones can also help children learn and stay connected with their families."

Great Debate: Technology Statements

Choose a side (for or against) and prepare your arguments using the vocabulary and phrases you've learned.

Debate Topics

1. The risk of having a social networking account outweighs the benefits.
2. Tablets and handheld phones will soon replace laptops and personal computers.
3. Internet access should not be allowed to anyone; people must sign up and clarify why they would want to use it.
4. E-books will soon completely replace printed books.
5. Labour-saving technology is making us overweight and lazy.
6. Teenagers play too many video games. While some games are educational, it remains as a source of negative learned behaviour for minors.
7. We've become too reliant on technology; people rarely speak to each other when they meet. Instead, they look down at their phones.
8. Upgrades in technology are simply designed to make us spend more money, not to make our lives better.

Review & Reflection

After the Debate

Answer these questions to reflect on the debate:

1. Which side 'won' the debate? Why do you think this was?

Consider: Did one side use better examples? Were their arguments clearer? Did they use debate language more effectively?

2. Which side really reflects your own opinion best?

You don't have to choose the side you defended. Be honest about what you actually believe.

3. What did you find most interesting about the debate?

Was it a particular argument? A piece of vocabulary? Something someone else said?

4. What do you need more practice with?

Speaking fluently? Understanding debate language? Coming up with examples?
