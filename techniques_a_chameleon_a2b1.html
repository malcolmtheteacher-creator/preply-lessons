A Chameleon

Chekhov's story of a policeman, a small white dog, and an opinion that changes again and again.

Today: you'll read about a policeman who changes his mind five times in ten minutes — and you'll learn strong adjectives like tiny, huge and boiling, and which words go before them.
Warm up — before we read. Talk about these questions out loud. There are no wrong answers.
Discuss:
  • Are people sometimes nicer to rich or important people? Give an example.
  • Have you ever changed your opinion very quickly? Why?
  • A chameleon is an animal that changes colour. What do you think a "chameleon" person is like?

Now — the words for today's story

Ten words open the story. Do the four short activities. Say your answer out loud first, then click Reveal.

A · What does the word mean here?

1. Everyone ran to see what was happening. Soon there were thirty people standing around the dog. A big crowd was there.
a crowd (n) — a large group of people in one place. Adjective: crowded — "The market was very crowded."
2. The dog was small and afraid. It was shaking all over. It was trembling.
to tremble (v) — to shake a little because you are afraid, cold or ill. "Her hands were trembling."
3. The man showed everyone his finger. There was blood on it. The dog bit him!
to bite (v) — bite, bit, bitten: to cut something with your teeth. Noun: a bite — "a dog bite".

B · Which word means…?

Three words: owner · general · stray. Say the right word, then reveal.

1. Which word means the person a thing or an animal belongs to?
an owner (n) — from the verb to own. "Who is the owner of this dog?"
2. Which word means a dog or cat with no home and no owner?
a stray (n), stray (adj) — "a stray dog", "There are many strays in the city."
3. Which word means a very important officer in the army?
a general (n) — a top army officer. In this story, everyone is afraid of the general. (Don't mix it up with the adjective general = not special.)

C · Choose the meaning — a / b / c

1. "Destroy the dog! And the owner must pay a fine!" A fine is…
  (a) money you pay as a punishment   (b) a present   (c) a kind of prison
(a) — a fine (n) / to fine (v): "He got a fine for parking there." Different from I'm fine (= I'm OK).
2. The crowd laughed at Khryukin, and his face went red. To laugh at someone is to…
  (a) laugh with them because you are friends   (b) laugh to make them feel stupid   (c) tell them a joke
(b) — laugh at someone = make fun of them. Laugh with someone = enjoy a joke together.

D · Word families & word partners

1. The policeman says: "I've changed my ___." Which word completes it — head, mind or brain? And what's the noun from to decide?
change your mind = have a new opinion or decision. decide → a decision: "to make a decision" (not do a decision).
2. Complete with on or off: "It's hot — take your coat ___!" · "It's cold — put your coat ___!"
take your coat off · put your coat on. Watch the policeman's coat in the story — it tells you what he is thinking!

Now use them — out loud

Talk before we read:
  • Use bite, owner, general and fine: what do you think happens in this story?
  • Which words are about people? Which are about the dog? Say them in two groups.

The story, in three parts

This is a famous Russian story, told here in simple English. Read each part. Then answer the questions from memory, and talk about one question.

Part 1 — The bitten finger

A policeman called Ochumelov is walking across the market square. He is wearing a new coat. Behind him walks a young constable. Everything is quiet. The shops are open, but there are no people in the square. It is a quiet, sleepy day, and nothing is happening. Ochumelov is an important man here, and he knows it.

Suddenly someone shouts: "You bit me, you terrible animal!" A small white dog runs out of a yard. A man runs after it, falls down and catches it by the back legs. In a minute, a big crowd is there.

The man is Khryukin, a goldsmith. He is holding up his finger, and there is blood on it. "This dog bit me for no reason!" he says. "I work with my hands. I can't work for a week! Somebody must pay me!" The little dog sits in the middle of the crowd. It is trembling.

"I'll stop this!" says Ochumelov. "The owner will pay a fine, and the dog will be destroyed. Whose dog is it?" Somebody in the crowd says, "I think it's the general's dog."

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened to Khryukin?

2. What does Ochumelov say first about the dog and its owner?

1. A small white dog bit his finger. He wants money because he can't work.

2. The owner will pay a fine, and the dog will be destroyed.

Discuss:
  • Do you feel sorry for Khryukin or for the dog? Why?

Part 2 — Hot, then cold

"The general's dog? Oh!" says Ochumelov. "Take my coat off, please. It's boiling today!" He looks at Khryukin. "This is a tiny dog, and you are a huge man. How could it bite you? I think you cut your finger on a nail, and now you want money!"

"He put a cigarette in the dog's face for a joke," says a man in the crowd, "so the dog bit him." "That's a lie!" shouts Khryukin. "And my brother is a policeman, you know!" "Be quiet!" says Ochumelov.

"No," says the constable. "This isn't the general's dog. His dogs are big and expensive." "Really?" says Ochumelov. "Yes, I thought so. Look at it — it's a dirty, ugly street dog! Khryukin, you are right. This dog must be destroyed!"

"But maybe it is the general's," says the constable. "I saw a dog like this at his house." "Oh!" says Ochumelov. "Put my coat on, please. It's freezing! Take the dog to the general's house. It's a lovely, valuable dog. And you, Khryukin — put your stupid finger down. It's your fault!"

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why does Ochumelov take his coat off — and why does he put it on again?

2. How many times does his opinion about the dog change in this part?

1. He says it's hot, then cold — but really he is nervous because the dog might belong to the general.

2. Three times: it's a tiny dog (general's) → an ugly street dog (not the general's) → a lovely dog (maybe the general's).

Discuss:
  • Is the weather really changing? What is Ochumelov really feeling?

Part 3 — The cook

Then the general's cook, Prokhor, walks past. Everybody in the crowd turns to look at him. "Prokhor! Is this your dog?" "No," says Prokhor. "We never had a dog like that." "Then it's a stray!" says Ochumelov. "Destroy it!"

"It's not ours," Prokhor says, "because it belongs to the general's brother. He arrived yesterday." Ochumelov smiles a huge smile. "His brother! How wonderful! What a sweet little dog! It bit this man's finger — ha ha! Clever little dog!"

Prokhor takes the dog home. The crowd laughs at Khryukin. "I'll get you later!" Ochumelov says to Khryukin. Then he puts his coat around him and walks away across the square, as if nothing happened.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who is the real owner of the dog?

2. Who do the people laugh at at the end?

1. The general's brother.

2. Khryukin — not the policeman.

Discuss:
  • Why doesn't anyone laugh at Ochumelov?

Grammar — strong adjectives: tiny, huge, boiling

Ochumelov uses strong adjectives when he wants to show strong feelings: the dog is tiny, the day is boiling, then freezing. Strong adjectives mean "very + adjective".

Normal → strong:
• very small → tiny  ·  very big → huge
• very hot → boiling  ·  very cold → freezing
• very good → wonderful  ·  very bad → terrible
• very dirty → filthy  ·  very angry → furious
Which word goes before them?
• Normal adjectives: very — very small, very hot.
• Strong adjectives: absolutely or really — absolutely tiny, really boiling.
• Don't say very tiny or very boiling — tiny already means "very small"!

1. Normal → strong

Write the strong adjective.

1. The dog was very small. → It was .

2. "Take my coat off — it's very hot!" → "It's !"

3. Khryukin was a very big man. → He was .

1. tiny   2. boiling   3. huge

2. very or absolutely?

Write very or absolutely.

1. "Put my coat on — it's freezing!"

2. The dog was afraid.

3. Khryukin was furious.

1. absolutely   2. very   3. absolutely

Really is fine in all three!

3. Tell the story — complete the paragraph

Write a strong adjective in each gap. Then read the paragraph aloud.

First, Ochumelov said the dog was (very small) and Khryukin was (very big). He said it was (very hot) and took his coat off. Then he said the dog was (very dirty) and (very bad). Then he said it was (very cold) and put his coat on again. At the end, he said the dog was (very good)!

tiny · huge · boiling · filthy · terrible · freezing · wonderful

Seven strong adjectives — and they show you how much his feelings change!

Going deeper

Anton Chekhov wrote this story in 1884. He was 24 years old and was just becoming a doctor. He wrote many short, funny stories for magazines to earn money for his family. Today Russian people still call a person who always changes their opinion "a chameleon".

The heart of it. Look at the coat. Chekhov never says "Ochumelov is afraid of the general". Instead, Ochumelov is suddenly hot and takes his coat off, then suddenly cold and puts it on again. The coat shows us his feelings. Look at the pattern too: every time somebody says something new about the owner, Ochumelov changes his opinion — and he always has a "good reason". And the general? He is never in the story. Nobody sees him. But everybody is afraid of him.
Talk it through:
  • Why does Chekhov use the coat? Is it better than saying "he was afraid"?
  • Khryukin maybe put a cigarette in the dog's face. Is he a good man or a bad man?
  • The general is never in the story. Why is he so important?
  • Is Ochumelov a bad person, or just afraid of losing his job?

Task A · Tell the story (3–4 minutes)

Tell A Chameleon in your own words. Follow the steps: the quiet square → the bitten finger → "pay a fine!" → "the general's dog?" — coat off → "an ugly street dog" → "maybe it is the general's" — coat on → the cook → "the general's brother!" → the crowd laughs.

Use at least four strong adjectives and at least five key words (crowd, tremble, bite, owner, stray, general, fine, laugh at, change your mind, take off / put on).

Task B · Bad man or afraid man? (3–4 minutes)

Say what you think and give three reasons. Is Ochumelov a bad man? Or is he only afraid of the general and of losing his job? What should a good policeman do in this situation?

Then change sides! Speak for one minute for the other opinion.

Task C · Important people (3–4 minutes)

Talk about how people treat important people.

No idea? Choose one of these:

  • Describe your town twice: first with nice strong adjectives, then with bad ones. Your partner chooses which is true.
  • Tell a story about a time someone was treated better because they were rich or famous.
  • Describe a pet you know — use three strong adjectives.
  • Is it OK to change your mind? When is it good, and when is it bad?
  • Are there many stray dogs or cats in your country? What happens to them?
  • Do you know a "chameleon" person? Describe them (no names!).

To finish — the big questions

  1. Why does Ochumelov's opinion change so many times?
  2. At the end, he walks away. Do you think he feels bad? Why or why not?
  3. Do people still behave like this today? Give an example.
