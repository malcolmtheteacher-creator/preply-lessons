The Happy Prince

A story by Oscar Wilde about a gold statue and a little bird who help the poor.

Today: read a famous story by Oscar Wilde about a gold statue and a little bird who give things to poor people. Talk about kindness and helping others. Grammar: who and which, and the past simple.
Warm up — talk first. Answer these questions out loud, in one or two easy sentences. There are no wrong answers.
Before we read:
  • When did you last help someone? What did you do?
  • Is it good to give things to people who have less than you? Why?
  • What is more important: to be beautiful, or to be kind?

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The statue and the bird

High above the city, on a tall stone column, stood a statue. It was the statue of the Happy Prince. The statue was covered in gold, and it was very beautiful. It had two blue jewels for eyes, and one big red jewel on its sword. Everyone in the city looked up and said, "How beautiful he is!"

One night, a little bird — a swallow — stopped to sleep at the statue's feet. All his friends were already far away, in warm Egypt. Then a drop of water fell on the bird. He looked up. The eyes of the Happy Prince were full of tears, and the tears ran down his gold face.

"Why are you crying?" asked the swallow. The Prince said, "When I was alive, I lived in a big house. I never saw poor people, so I was always happy. But now I stand up here, and I can see all the sad and poor people in my city. I cannot stop crying." Far away, he saw a poor woman and her little boy. The boy was ill, and the woman had no money for medicine.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was the statue made of, and what did it have for eyes?

2. Why was the Happy Prince crying?

1. It was covered in gold. It had two blue jewels for eyes and one big red jewel on its sword.

2. Because now he stands high above the city and can see all the poor and sad people. When he was alive, he never saw them.

Talk about it:
  • The Prince only saw poor people when he was up high. Do we sometimes not see the people near us? Why?

Part 2 — Giving things away

"Little swallow," said the Prince, "please take the red jewel from my sword and give it to that poor woman." The swallow wanted to fly to warm Egypt, but he felt sorry for the Prince. So he took the red jewel across the city and put it next to the sleeping woman. Then he moved his wings over the sick boy, and the boy felt better and slept well.

Every night, the Prince saw more poor people. He asked the swallow to help, and the little bird stayed. The Prince gave one blue eye to a young writer who was cold and hungry. He gave the other blue eye to a poor girl in the street. Now the Prince could not see, so the swallow said, "I will stay with you always," and he did not fly away.

Then the swallow flew over the city. He saw rich people in warm houses and poor people outside in the cold. So he took the gold off the Prince, piece by piece, and gave it to the poor. The children had food now, and they laughed and played. But the statue was not beautiful any more. It was grey and old and shabby.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did the Prince give to the poor woman first?

2. Why did the swallow decide to stay with the Prince?

1. He gave her the big red jewel from his sword.

2. The Prince gave away both blue eyes, so he could not see. The swallow did not want to leave a blind friend, so he stayed.

Talk about it:
  • The swallow wanted to go to Egypt, but he stayed to help. Was this a good choice? What would you do?

Part 3 — The two friends

Winter came, and it was very cold. The little swallow was cold too, but he did not want to leave the Prince. He loved him now. He used his last strength to fly up and say goodbye. "Goodbye, dear Prince," he said. The Prince said, "I am glad you are going to warm Egypt at last." "No," said the swallow, "I am going far away." He kissed the Prince and fell down. At that moment, something broke inside the statue. The Prince's heart, which was made of grey metal, broke in two.

The next morning, the mayor and the town men walked below. "Look how old and ugly the statue is!" they said. "The red jewel is gone, the eyes are gone, and there is no gold." So they took the statue down. They put it in a big fire, but the broken heart did not melt. They threw it away, next to the little dead bird.

Then God spoke to one of his angels. "Bring me the two best things in this city," he said. The angel brought the broken heart and the little bird. "You have chosen well," said God. "This little bird will sing in my garden for ever, and the Happy Prince will live with me." So the two friends, who gave everything away, were the richest of all in the end.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened to the swallow on the cold night?

2. What did God say about the broken heart and the little bird?

1. The swallow was too cold and too tired. He kissed the Prince, said goodbye, and fell down. At that moment the Prince's heart broke in two.

2. He said they were the two best things in the city. The bird will sing in his garden for ever, and the Prince will live with him.

Talk about it:
  • The town men said the statue was ugly, but God said it was the best thing in the city. Who was right? Why?

Grammar: who / which + past simple

This story tells us about people and things. We can add more information with two easy words: who for people and which for things. And because the story happened in the past, we use the past simple.

who = for a person: "The swallow helped a boy who was ill." "The Prince, who stood high above the city, could see the poor people."
which = for a thing or an animal: "The jewel which was on his sword was red." "The heart, which was made of metal, broke in two."
Past simple: for finished actions in the past. Many verbs add -ed (help → helped, look → looked). But many common verbs are irregular: give → gave, take → took, stand → stood, fly → flew, see → saw, break → broke.

Going deeper

You know the story now. Let's think about what it means. It is a simple story, but the questions are big ones.

Why people still love this story. Oscar Wilde wrote The Happy Prince a long time ago, in 1888, but people still read it today. At the start, the statue is beautiful and full of gold. At the end, it is old and grey — but it is kind. The town men say it is worthless, but in the story it is the most precious thing in the city. The story is sad, but it is also warm: it says that kind people are the real "rich" people.
Talk it through:
  • The name. The statue is called the "Happy" Prince, but we see him cry a lot. Is he really happy? When?
  • Beautiful or kind? At the start the statue is beautiful. At the end it is kind but ugly. Which statue do you like more? Why?
  • The ending. The swallow and the Prince come to a quiet end, but God says they are the best things in the city. Is the ending sad, happy, or both? Why do you think Oscar Wilde ended it like this?
  • What about you? The swallow could fly to warm Egypt and live. But he stayed to help. Would you stay, or would you go? Why?

Speak

Do all three tasks out loud. Use the timer to keep talking.

Useful language: use who for people ("the bird who stayed") and which for things ("the jewel which was red"). Use the past simple for the story ("he gave", "the swallow flew"). To give an opinion: "I think…", "In my opinion…", "For me…".

Task B · Helping other people (3 minutes)

The Prince and the bird gave everything to help poor people. Is this a good thing to do? Do you like to help other people? How do you help — for example, family, friends, or people in need? Give one example.

Task C · Being kind (3 minutes)

Talk about kindness in your own life. Use who and which and the past simple.

Not sure what to talk about? Choose one:

  • A person who helped you one day. What did they do?
  • A time when you helped someone. How did you feel?
  • A gift which was small but very special to you.
  • A person who gives a lot to other people — in your family, or someone famous.
  • A thing which is not expensive, but which is important to you.
  • A place which needs more help. Who lives there?
