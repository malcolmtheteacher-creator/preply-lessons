The Story of Abraham

Abraham is a figure whose importance stretches across three of the world's major religions. He is not a modern historical figure — he appears in religious texts written over 2,000 years ago — but his story has shaped billions of lives. Let's trace his journey through time and through the eyes of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.

Who Was Abraham?

Abraham (originally called Abram) was born in ancient Mesopotamia, in what is now Iraq, around 2000 BCE. According to the Bible and the Quran, he was chosen by God to become the father of a great nation. This choice, and his unwavering faith, makes him central to three world religions today.

Key Events in Abraham's Life

🕎 Judaism: Abraham the Father

In Judaism, Abraham is honoured as the founder of the Jewish people. The story begins when God calls to Abram: "Leave your country, your people, and your father's household and go to the land I will show you. I will make you into a great nation." This is the call to go to Canaan, the Promised Land (modern-day Israel/Palestine).

✝️ Christianity: Abraham the Model of Faith

Christians see Abraham as the model of faith and obedience. His willingness to trust God completely, even when asked to sacrifice his son Isaac, shows a faith so strong that he is called "the father of the faithful." For Christians, this foreshadows Jesus's sacrifice.

☪️ Islam: Abraham the Friend of God

In Islam, Abraham (Ibrahim) is revered as one of the greatest prophets. Muslims honour him particularly for his willingness to sacrifice his son Ishmael for God. Muslims believe that Abraham's legacy continues through Ishmael, who became the ancestor of the Arab people and eventually the prophet Muhammad.

The Key Events

The Call (c. 2000 BCE)
God calls Abram to leave his home and journey to an unknown land. He is promised descendants and a great nation.
The Journey to Canaan
Abraham travels to what is now Israel/Palestine. He is promised this land as an inheritance for his descendants.
The Covenant (Genesis 17)
God makes a formal agreement with Abraham. He will be the father of many nations, and all his male descendants must be circumcised as a sign of the covenant.
Isaac's Birth
Abraham's wife Sarah gives birth to Isaac when Abraham is very old. Isaac is the child of the promise and becomes the focus of the covenant.
The Sacrifice (Genesis 22)
God asks Abraham to sacrifice his beloved son Isaac. Abraham prepares to obey, but God stops him at the last moment and provides a ram as a sacrifice instead. This tests Abraham's faith and obedience.
Ishmael and the Arab Nations
Abraham also has a son Ishmael with Hagar (Sarah's servant). According to Islamic tradition, Ishmael becomes the ancestor of the Arab peoples and, eventually, the prophet Muhammad.

Why Abraham Matters to All Three Religions

Abraham is called the "Father of Three Faiths" because:

  • Judaism traces its origins directly to Abraham and his covenant with God. Jews are sometimes called the "children of Abraham."
  • Christianity sees Abraham as the ancestor of Jesus (through David), and as the model of faith for all believers.
  • Islam recognizes Abraham as one of the most important prophets (Khalil Allah, "the Friend of God") and the ancestor of Muhammad through Ishmael.

All three religions trace their spiritual roots back to Abraham's original covenant with God. This shared connection is why they are called the Abrahamic faiths.

Does Sharing a Common Ancestor Make These Religions More Similar Than Different?

This is a challenging question for scholars and believers. Abraham is a shared spiritual ancestor, but Judaism, Christianity, and Islam have developed very different beliefs and practices over 2,000 years. Let's explore both sides of this debate before forming your own view.

✓ Yes, They're More Similar

Arguments for:

  • All three believe in one God
  • All honour Abraham as a founding figure
  • All have a covenant or sacred agreement with God
  • All have prophets and sacred texts
  • Many believers find common ground and respect across the faiths

✗ No, They're More Different

Arguments against:

  • Different beliefs about who God is (Trinity in Christianity)
  • Different ideas about prophets (Jesus as God's son, Muhammad as last prophet)
  • Different sacred texts (Torah, Bible, Quran)
  • Different laws and practices (Sabbath, dietary rules)
  • 2,000 years of separate development creates major differences

Your Response:

Which argument do you find more convincing? Write a response that includes:

  • Your position (similarities matter more / differences matter more / both equally)
  • At least 2 reasons to support your view
  • Recognition of the other side's strongest point

Remember:

There is no "right answer" to this question. Historians, theologians, and believers themselves disagree. What matters is that your response shows careful thinking and understanding of the issues.

Lesson Review

Before you finish, test your understanding of today's key content with these retrieval questions.

1. What covenant did God make with Abraham?

🔒 Try to answer from memory first!

Answer: God promised to make Abraham the father of a great nation and give his descendants the land of Canaan. In return, Abraham and his descendants had to be circumcised as a sign of the covenant.

2. Name Abraham's two sons and explain their significance to the three faiths

🔒 Try to answer from memory first!

Answer: Isaac is the child of Abraham and Sarah, promised by God. He is important to Judaism and Christianity. Ishmael is Abraham's son with Hagar. In Islam, he is seen as the ancestor of Muhammad and the Arab peoples.

3. Why did the sacrifice of Isaac matter to each religion?

🔒 Try to answer from memory first!

Answer: Judaism: It showed Abraham's absolute faith and obedience to God. Christianity: It prefigures Jesus's sacrifice. Islam: When Abraham was willing to sacrifice Ishmael (not Isaac in Islamic tradition), he showed perfect submission to God's will.

4. Why is Abraham called "the Father of Three Faiths"?

🔒 Try to answer from memory first!

Answer: Because Judaism, Christianity, and Islam all trace their spiritual roots back to Abraham and the covenant God made with him. He is the shared founder figure of all three Abrahamic faiths.

5. What is the promised land in Abraham's story?

🔒 Try to answer from memory first!

Answer: Canaan, which is modern-day Israel and Palestine. God promised Abraham that his descendants would inherit this land.
