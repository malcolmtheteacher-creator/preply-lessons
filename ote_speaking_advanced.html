B2 vs C1: What's the Difference?

Solid B2 Response

"What are the advantages of remote work?"

"I think the main advantage is flexibility. People can work from home and save time on commuting. Also, they can organise their day better. However, some people find it difficult to concentrate at home."

Characteristics: Clear, organised, uses linking words, good vocabulary

Strong C1 Response

"What are the advantages of remote work?"

"Remote work offers several compelling advantages. Perhaps the most significant is the autonomy it provides - employees can tailor their working environment to optimise productivity. There's also the elimination of commuting, which not only saves time but reduces stress and environmental impact. That said, it's worth acknowledging that remote work isn't universally beneficial; those who thrive on face-to-face collaboration may find it isolating."

Characteristics: Sophisticated vocabulary, nuanced perspective, natural hedging, complex structures

C1 Target

What examiners look for at C1

  • Sophisticated linking: "That said...", "Having said that...", "On balance..."
  • Hedging and nuance: "It could be argued that...", "To some extent..."
  • Abstract discussion: Ability to discuss hypotheticals and complex ideas
  • Self-correction: Natural reformulation without disrupting flow

Part 1: Interview Questions

Format

8 questions on personal topics and opinions. Questions 1-2: 10 seconds. Questions 3-8: 20 seconds. No preparation time.

Strategy: Position + Justification + Development

For 20-second responses, aim for:

  1. Position - State your answer/opinion clearly
  2. Justification - Give a reason or explanation
  3. Development - Add depth: example, consequence, or contrast

Practice Questions

"Do you think university education is essential for career success?"

Model (C1):

"Not necessarily, though it depends on the field. (Position) While certain professions like medicine or law require formal qualifications, many successful entrepreneurs and tech professionals have built careers without degrees. (Justification) That said, university does offer valuable networking opportunities and develops critical thinking skills that are hard to acquire elsewhere. (Development)"

"What role should governments play in addressing climate change?"
Click to see model answer

Model (C1):

"Governments should take the lead, in my view. (Position) They have the regulatory power to enforce emissions standards and incentivise renewable energy adoption. (Justification) However, meaningful progress requires international cooperation - no single government can solve a global problem alone. (Development)"

C1 Phrases for Part 1

Positioning: Not necessarily, though... It depends on... In my view... I'd argue that...

Developing: That said,... Having said that,... On balance,... What's more,...

Nuancing: To some extent... It's worth noting that... This isn't to say that...

Part 2: Professional Voicemail

Format

Leave voicemail messages in professional/semi-formal contexts. 30 seconds preparation, 45 seconds to speak. Two tasks.

Strategy: GRIP Framework

  1. Greeting - Professional opening with your name
  2. Reason - State why you're calling immediately
  3. Information - Provide all required details
  4. Prompt - Clear call to action + contact details

Example Task

Situation

You're a project manager. A client meeting scheduled for next week needs to be rescheduled due to a conflict. Leave a voicemail for the client explaining the situation and proposing alternative times.

See model voicemail
Click to reveal

G: "Good morning, this is [Name] from [Company] calling for Mr Peterson."

R: "I'm calling regarding our meeting scheduled for Tuesday the 23rd. Unfortunately, an unavoidable conflict has arisen and I need to request a rescheduling."

I: "I'm available on Wednesday afternoon or Thursday morning if either works for you. Alternatively, I could arrange for my colleague Sarah to take the meeting as planned if you'd prefer."

P: "Please let me know your preference at your earliest convenience. You can reach me on 0207 555 1234 or by email. I apologise for any inconvenience. Thank you."

Professional voicemail language

Opening: This is [Name] calling from/regarding... I'm calling in connection with...

Explaining issues: Unfortunately, an issue has arisen... I regret to inform you that... Due to unforeseen circumstances...

Offering solutions: I'd like to propose... As an alternative,... Would it be possible to...

Closing: Please let me know at your earliest convenience. I look forward to hearing from you.

Practice Task

You applied for a job and received an interview invitation, but you have a scheduling conflict. Leave a voicemail asking to reschedule and suggesting alternative times.

Part 3: Extended Monologue

Format

Discuss a topic in depth using 3-4 prompts. 45 seconds preparation, 90 seconds to speak.

Strategy: Use the 90 Seconds Wisely

  • 0-15s: Introduction - frame the topic, signal your approach
  • 15-60s: Main points - address 2-3 prompts with development
  • 60-90s: Synthesis - bring ideas together, conclude thoughtfully

Example Topic

The Future of Work

  • How is technology changing the workplace?
  • What skills will be most valuable in the future?
  • How should education adapt to prepare young people?
See model response (90 seconds)
Click to reveal

Introduction: "The future of work is a topic that affects us all, and I think we're already seeing significant shifts driven by technology and globalisation."

Main point 1: "Technology is fundamentally reshaping how we work. Automation and AI are taking over routine tasks, while remote collaboration tools have made location increasingly irrelevant. The pandemic accelerated these trends dramatically - what might have taken a decade happened in months."

Main point 2: "In terms of valuable skills, I'd say adaptability is paramount. Technical skills become obsolete quickly, but the ability to learn, problem-solve, and collaborate remains constant. Emotional intelligence is also increasingly valued as machines handle more analytical work."

Synthesis: "This has implications for education - we need to move away from memorisation towards critical thinking and creativity. Ultimately, I believe the future belongs to those who can combine human skills with technological fluency."

Structuring extended speech

Introduction: This is a topic that... There are several aspects to consider here...

Developing: In terms of... When it comes to... This has implications for...

Examples: Take [X] for instance... A case in point is...

Concluding: Ultimately,... On balance,... Looking ahead,...

  • Why is work-life balance important?
  • What challenges do people face achieving it?
  • What can employers do to support employees?

Part 4: Responding to Opinions

Format

You hear/read a statement expressing an opinion. You must respond, agreeing or disagreeing with justification. 30 seconds preparation, 60 seconds to speak.

Strategy: Engage, Then Develop

  1. Acknowledge - Show you understand the position
  2. State your position - Agree, disagree, or partially agree
  3. Justify - Explain your reasoning with evidence
  4. Extend - Consider implications or alternative views

Example Statement

"Universities should focus on preparing students for employment rather than teaching academic theory."

See model response
Click to reveal

Acknowledge: "I can see the logic behind this view - there's certainly pressure on graduates to be 'job-ready'."

Position: "However, I'd argue this represents a false dichotomy."

Justify: "Academic theory and practical skills aren't mutually exclusive. In fact, theoretical understanding often underpins practical application. A doctor needs both anatomical knowledge and clinical skills; an engineer needs mathematical theory and hands-on experience."

Extend: "Moreover, if universities become purely vocational, we risk losing the critical thinking and intellectual curiosity that drive innovation. Perhaps the solution lies in better integration of theory and practice rather than prioritising one over the other."

Engaging with opinions

Acknowledging: I can see the logic behind... There's certainly merit to... It's understandable that...

Disagreeing politely: However, I'd argue that... That said, I'm not entirely convinced... While I see the point, I'd suggest...

Partial agreement: To an extent, yes, but... There's some truth to this, however...

Practice Statement

"Social media has done more harm than good for society."

Respond in 60 seconds, showing you understand the position before giving your view.

Part 5: Audio Summary

Format

Listen to a short audio (lecture excerpt, discussion, news item). Then summarise the main points. Listen once/twice, then 60 seconds to speak. This tests mediation skills.

Strategy: Listen for Structure

While listening, note:

  • Topic - What is being discussed?
  • Main argument/position - What's the speaker's stance?
  • Key supporting points - 2-3 main reasons or examples
  • Conclusion/implication - What's the takeaway?
C1 Skill

Mediation Language

Summaries require reporting what others said - use appropriate language:

  • The speaker argues that...
  • According to the discussion,...
  • The main point raised was...
  • It was suggested that...

Example Summary Task

Audio topic: The gig economy

Imagine you heard a 2-minute discussion about the growth of gig work (Uber, freelancing, etc.) and its impact on workers.

See model summary
Click to reveal

"The discussion focused on the rise of the gig economy and its implications for workers."

"The speaker highlighted that while gig work offers flexibility and autonomy, it often lacks the security and benefits of traditional employment. Key concerns raised included unpredictable income, no sick pay or pension contributions, and limited legal protections."

"However, it was also noted that for some workers, particularly those balancing other commitments, the flexibility outweighs these drawbacks."

"The speaker concluded by suggesting that regulation needs to evolve to protect gig workers while preserving the benefits of this new working model."

Summary language

Introducing: The discussion focused on... The speaker addressed...

Reporting: It was argued that... The main point was... Key concerns included...

Contrasting views: However, it was also noted... On the other hand,...

Concluding: The speaker concluded by... The overall message was...

What Distinguishes C1 from B2

1
Lexical sophistication

B2 uses clear, appropriate vocabulary. C1 uses precise, nuanced vocabulary with natural collocations and less common expressions.

2
Grammatical complexity

B2 uses complex sentences correctly. C1 uses a wide range of structures flexibly, including conditionals, passives, and modals for nuance.

3
Discourse management

B2 organises ideas clearly. C1 creates cohesive extended discourse with sophisticated linking and natural topic development.

4
Flexibility and precision

B2 communicates effectively. C1 reformulates, clarifies, and adjusts to context with apparent ease.

Top Tips

Final Recommendations

  • Don't over-prepare phrases - Memorised chunks sound unnatural
  • Embrace complexity - Show you can handle nuance and ambiguity
  • Self-correct naturally - This demonstrates awareness and control
  • Use the full time - Underdeveloped answers limit your score potential
  • Stay calm - Hesitation is natural; recovery is what matters
