Edexcel A-level English Language · Paper 2 · C1/C2

Child Language Decoded

Six lessons that turn the strangest exam paper in English — one 45-mark essay about a talking toddler — into a subject you can read like a linguist and write like a top-level candidate.

How this series works. Paper 2 (9EN0/02) gives you 75 minutes, one transcript of a small child talking with an adult, and one 45-mark question. These six lessons build everything that essay needs, in order: how to read the data, the sound patterns, the grammar-in-progress, the five famous theories, the adult's hidden teaching moves, and finally the full essay itself — with model answers to compare against at every step. Work through them in sequence; each lesson reaches back and tests what came before.

Lesson 1

Reading Child Data

The exam's shape, the transcript symbols, the phonetic slashes — and the Golden Rule that separates passing answers from failing ones: development, never deficit.

I can: read any child-language transcript and describe it in exam voice.
Lesson 2

How Children Build the Sound System

Five patterns explain almost every child pronunciation: substitution, cluster reduction, weak syllable deletion, elision, reduplication — plus the paradox of the perfect dinosaur name.

I can: name the pattern behind any /phonetic/ form and write a phonology paragraph.
Lesson 3

Words, Meanings and Grammar Under Construction

The four developmental stages, stretchy word meanings, and the brilliant logic of "I goed" — why a child's errors are the best evidence of intelligence in the whole subject.

I can: place a child's stage from evidence and analyse virtuous errors properly.
Lesson 4

The Five Theories

Skinner, Chomsky, Bruner, Halliday, Tomasello — each with the data that supports it AND the data that challenges it, plus the theory courtroom where you argue every side.

I can: weave theories into analysis instead of bolting them on.
Lesson 5

The Adult's Half of the Conversation

Six scaffolding moves every parent makes without knowing — modelling, expansion, reformulation and more — and the child's own conversation skills that answer the exam's question.

I can: analyse both halves of a transcript, not just the child's lines.
Lesson 6

Building the 45-Mark Answer

The 10-minute annotation method, the five-part paragraph engine, weak-versus-strong model answers, and a timed flight on fresh data with a full feature key to mark yourself against.

I can: plan and write a complete Paper 2 essay in 75 minutes.
