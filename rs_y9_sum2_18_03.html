Tawhid: The Oneness of God

Tawhid (Arabic: توحيد) means absolute monotheism — the belief that there is only one God (Allah) and nothing is to be worshipped but Allah.

It's the central principle of Islam and appears in the Shahada: "There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the messenger of Allah."

Key differences from Christianity:

  • Islam emphasizes God's strict oneness — no Trinity, no incarnation
  • Jesus is respected as a great prophet, not as God or Son of God
  • God is entirely transcendent (beyond human understanding) but merciful and just

The Six Articles of Faith (Sunni)

Sunni Muslims affirm six key beliefs:

  1. Tawhid: Oneness of God; absolute monotheism
  2. Angels (Malaikah): Spiritual beings created from light who serve God; chief angel Jibril (Gabriel) delivered the Qur'an to Muhammad
  3. Holy Books: Allah revealed scriptures to multiple prophets: Torah (to Moses), Psalms (to David), Gospel (to Jesus), Qur'an (to Muhammad). The Qur'an is the final and perfect revelation
  4. Prophets & Messengers: Allah sent prophets throughout history (Abraham, Moses, Jesus, Muhammad, etc.). Muhammad is the final prophet ("Seal of the Prophets")
  5. Day of Judgment (Yawm ad-Din): All people will be resurrected and judged by Allah for their deeds. Believers enter Paradise; those who reject truth face punishment
  6. Divine Decree (Al-Qadr): Allah has complete knowledge of all that will happen (predestination), yet humans have free will and responsibility

The Qur'an & Sunnah

The Qur'an: Believed by Muslims to be the literal word of Allah, revealed orally to Muhammad over 23 years. Written in classical Arabic. 114 chapters (surahs); 6,236 verses (ayahs).

Topics in the Qur'an: God's nature, prophets, law, ethics, family, commerce, warfare, forgiveness, mercy.

The Sunnah: The teachings and practices of Prophet Muhammad, recorded in collections called Hadith. The Sunnah shows how to apply Qur'anic principles in daily life.

Sharia (Islamic Law): Derived from four sources: Qur'an, Sunnah, Ijma (consensus), Qiyas (analogy). It governs all aspects of life: personal, family, business, crime, governance.

Predestination (Al-Qadr) & Human Responsibility

A key Islamic belief that puzzles non-Muslims: How can Allah know the future and predetermine all things, yet humans have free will and are responsible for their choices?

Islamic scholars explain: Allah's knowledge is different from human foreknowledge. Allah is outside time, so Allah's knowing what you'll choose doesn't make the choice unfree. It's like a video recorder watching a movie you've already made — the knowledge doesn't make you less responsible.

Humans are fully responsible for their actions (Qur'an 2:286: "Allah does not burden any person with more than they can bear").

Islamic Beliefs in Practice

How the Five Pillars express Islamic belief:

  • Shahada (Declaration): "There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the messenger." Belief expressed verbally and lived daily
  • Salah (Prayer): Five daily prayers show submission to Allah and keep believers in constant remembrance of God
  • Zakat (Charity): Giving 2.5% of wealth to the poor purifies wealth and expresses belief that all belongs to Allah
  • Sawm (Fasting in Ramadan): Refraining from food/drink during daylight develops self-discipline and empathy for the poor
  • Hajj (Pilgrimage): All Muslims who are able should visit Makkah once in their lifetime, expressing unity and submission
