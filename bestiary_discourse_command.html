The Authority Escalation

These five move from directness to closure: be direct → state the bottom line → make a request → make a proposal → summarise. When it's time to land the plane, these are your instruments.

When to Use Each One
Dingo — "Let me be direct..." A signal that you're about to cut through politeness and say what needs saying. Use when the conversation has been too diplomatic and a clear statement is needed. The phrase itself gives you permission to be blunt.
Boar — "The bottom line is..." Strips away context and gives the conclusion. Use when decision-makers need the headline, not the analysis. Perfect for closing a presentation, ending a debate, or responding to "so what does this mean?"
Newt — "What I need from you is..." A direct, respectful request. Use when you need a specific action from someone. The phrase is powerful because it's personal ("I need") and targeted ("from you"). It creates accountability without aggression.
Hedgehog — "Here's what I propose..." Puts a concrete plan on the table. Use when the discussion needs a decision and you're ready to offer one. This phrase says: "I've listened to everything and here's my recommendation."
Sunbird — "To summarise the key takeaways..." Closes the loop. Distils an entire discussion into its essential points. Use at the end of meetings, presentations, or negotiations. The person who summarises is the person who controls the narrative.
