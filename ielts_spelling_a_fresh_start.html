IELTS Spelling Story · B1/B2 · 50 min

A Fresh Start Abroad

A short story built from the words IELTS candidates most often misspell. Read it — then prove you can spell them.

Read the story

Every word in bold is one that IELTS candidates very often spell wrong. Read carefully and photograph each one in your mind — you'll have to spell them from memory in the next tab.

Maya had always dreamed of studying abroad. When the opportunity finally came — a place at a university in a foreign country — she was definitely nervous, but excited beyond words.

The hardest part, right at the beginning, was finding accommodation. She searched online until well past midnight, and it was necessary to be careful: some advertisements were clearly designed to cheat or embarrass foreign students. At last a colleague from her course recommended a small flat next to a beautiful park. Maya could hardly believe her luck.

The government paperwork was a nightmare. She had to register her address immediately, and the queue at the office stretched out of the door. To her surprise, the official was patient and shared knowledge she badly needed. "It is a privilege to help students who are serious," he said with a smile.

University life was not what she had imagined. She wanted to achieve excellent results, and she knew that success would not come without discipline. The environment was friendly but fiercely competitive. By February she felt calm and independent, and the adventure that had once terrified her had become, quite unexpectedly, the best decision of her life.

Why these words catch people out

Five traps explain almost every spelling mistake in the story.

1 · Double letters where you least expect them

accommodation (double c and double m), necessary (one c, double s — "one collar, two sleeves"), recommend (one c, double m), embarrass (double r, double s), beginning (double n), address (double d, double s), success (double c, double s).

2 · Silent letters

government (the silent n: govern-ment), environment (silent n: environ-ment), foreign (silent g), February (the first r people drop), discipline (the silent c: dis-c-ipline).

3 · The wrong vowel

definately → definitely (think "finite"). seperate → separate ("there's a rat in separate"). independant → independent (‑ent). untill → until (one l).

4 · "i before e… except after c" (and the rebels)

Follows the rule: believe, achieve. Breaks it: foreign, and the classic exception weird. After c → ei: receive, deceive.

5 · Letters that hide a sound

privilege (no "d" — not "priviledge"), surprise (the first r: sur‑prise), knowledge (silent k, ends ‑dge), queue (just the q… then "ueue"), colleague (‑eague), beautiful (b‑e‑a‑u).

Now use them — and one last test

Speaking

Tell the story of a time you (or someone you know) started something new — a job, a course, a move to a new city. Use at least six of the bold words from the story. Spell three of them out loud, letter by letter, as you go.

Writing (IELTS practice)

Write 3–4 sentences answering: "Some people think studying or working abroad is the best way to grow. Do you agree?" You must correctly spell: opportunity, environment, knowledge, necessary, achieve. Then check each one in the test tab.
