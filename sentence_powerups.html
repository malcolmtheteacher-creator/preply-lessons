Sentence Power-Ups

Transform Band 6 sentences into Band 8 sentences

Prerequisite: You should already understand simple, compound, complex, and compound-complex sentences. This lesson builds on that foundation.

The Band 6 vs Band 8 Gap

Both paragraphs below say the same thing. Both are grammatically correct. But one scores Band 6 and one scores Band 8. Can you see why?

Band 6

Many people use social media every day. This causes problems. People feel anxious. They compare themselves to others. Governments should do something about this. They could make laws to protect young people.

Band 8

The widespread use of social media has led to increasing levels of anxiety, particularly among young people who constantly compare themselves to others. Were governments to introduce regulations protecting minors, this growing mental health crisis could be significantly reduced.

What's Different?

The Band 8 version uses specific grammatical structures that signal sophistication to examiners:

Structure Band 6 Version Band 8 Version
Nominalization Many people use social media The widespread use of social media
Complex noun phrase This causes problems increasing levels of anxiety
Relative clause They compare themselves young people who constantly compare themselves
Inversion Governments should do something Were governments to introduce regulations

The Official Band Descriptors

Here's what IELTS examiners are actually looking for:

Band Grammatical Range & Accuracy
6 Uses a mix of simple and complex sentences; errors occur but rarely reduce communication
7 Uses a variety of complex structures; frequent error-free sentences
8 Uses a wide range of structures with very occasional errors
9 Full flexibility and accuracy; rare "slips" only
The key phrase is "wide range." You don't need perfect grammar - you need VARIETY. This lesson teaches you 5 specific structures that most Band 6 writers never use.

The 5 Power-Ups

In this lesson, you'll learn to transform ordinary sentences using:

1. Nominalization

Turn verbs into nouns to sound more academic

They implemented the policy → The implementation of the policy...

2. Inversion

Reverse word order for emphasis and sophistication

It has never been... → Never has it been...

3. Advanced Conditionals

Conditionals without "if" for formal writing

If governments acted... → Were governments to act...

4. Complex Noun Phrases

Pack more information into fewer words

costs that are rising rapidly → rapidly rising costs

5. Emphasis Structures

Cleft sentences and participles for highlighting key points

Education is the key → What is crucial is education

Nominalization: The #1 Academic Transformation

Nominalization means turning verbs (actions) or adjectives (descriptions) into nouns (things). This is the single most important transformation for academic writing because it:

  • Makes writing sound more formal and objective
  • Removes the need for personal subjects (I, you, we)
  • Allows you to pack more information into each sentence
VERB → NOUN
The government introduced new laws → The introduction of new laws...

Common Transformations

Verb Noun Example
introduce introduction The introduction of new technology...
implement implementation The implementation of this policy...
develop development The development of infrastructure...
increase increase The increase in population...
reduce reduction The reduction of emissions...
improve improvement The improvement in living standards...
decide decision The decision to invest...
fail failure The failure to act...

Adjective → Noun

Adjective Noun Example
important importance The importance of education...
able ability The ability to communicate...
available availability The availability of resources...
possible possibility The possibility of failure...

Practice: Transform These Sentences

1. The government decided to ban plastic bags.

Model answer:

The decision to ban plastic bags...

Or: The government's decision to ban plastic bags...

2. Technology has developed rapidly in recent years.

Model answer:

The rapid development of technology in recent years...

Notice how "rapidly" (adverb) becomes "rapid" (adjective) before the noun.

3. Students fail because they don't prepare properly.

Model answer:

Student failure is often due to inadequate preparation.

Or: The failure to prepare properly leads to poor results.

4. The number of people who use public transport has increased.

Model answer:

There has been an increase in public transport usage.

Or: The increase in public transport usage...

5. We need to be able to communicate effectively in the workplace.

Model answer:

The ability to communicate effectively is essential in the workplace.

Or: Effective communication is essential in the workplace.

IELTS Task 1 Bonus: Describing Trends

Nominalization is especially useful for Task 1 graphs:

Sales increased dramatically from 2010 to 2015.
There was a dramatic increase in sales from 2010 to 2015.
The number of tourists decreased slightly.
There was a slight decrease in the number of tourists.
Prices fluctuated significantly during this period.
There was significant fluctuation in prices during this period.
Using BOTH patterns ("X increased" AND "There was an increase in X") in your Task 1 shows grammatical range!

Inversion: Instant Sophistication

Inversion means putting the verb before the subject (like a question, but it's not a question). Very few Band 6 writers attempt this - which is exactly why you should.

NEGATIVE ADVERB + AUXILIARY + SUBJECT + VERB
It has never been so important → Never has it been so important
Use inversion 1-2 times per essay maximum. More than that sounds unnatural and forced.

The Key Patterns

Pattern 1: Never / Rarely / Seldom

It has never been more important to protect the environment.
Never has it been more important to protect the environment.
Governments rarely take swift action on climate change.
Rarely do governments take swift action on climate change.
We seldom see such dramatic changes in policy.
Seldom do we see such dramatic changes in policy.

Pattern 2: Not only... but also

Citizens should not only reduce car use, but they should also save electricity.
Not only should citizens reduce car use, but they should also save electricity.
This policy did not only reduce pollution, but it also created jobs.
Not only did this policy reduce pollution, but it also created jobs.

Pattern 3: Only + time/condition

We will see real change only when governments take action.
Only when governments take action will we see real change.
The problem can be solved only by working together.
Only by working together can the problem be solved.

Pattern 4: Little

People little realise the impact of their daily choices.
Little do people realise the impact of their daily choices.

Practice: Create Inverted Sentences

1. Education has never been more accessible than it is today.

Model answer:

Never has education been more accessible than it is today.

2. Technology not only connects people but also creates new opportunities.

Model answer:

Not only does technology connect people, but it also creates new opportunities.

3. The government will address the housing crisis only when the situation becomes critical.

Model answer:

Only when the situation becomes critical will the government address the housing crisis.

4. People rarely consider the long-term consequences of their decisions.

Model answer:

Rarely do people consider the long-term consequences of their decisions.

5. We can solve this problem only by investing in education.

Model answer:

Only by investing in education can we solve this problem.

Best Essay Types for Inversion

Opinion Essays (Agree/Disagree)

Use inversion to make strong claims:

  • Never has it been more important to...
  • Rarely do governments consider...
  • Only by [action] can [result] be achieved.

Problem/Solution Essays

Use inversion to emphasize solutions:

  • Only when [condition] will [result] occur.
  • Not only would this reduce [problem], but it would also...

Advanced Conditionals: Beyond "If"

You already know the basic conditionals (If + past simple, would + verb). But Band 8 writers use conditionals without "if" - this immediately signals formal, academic English.

WERE + SUBJECT + TO + VERB
If governments invested more... → Were governments to invest more...

The Three Patterns

Pattern 1: Were + subject + to + verb (Second Conditional)

Use for hypothetical present/future situations:

If the government invested in renewable energy, pollution would decrease.
Were the government to invest in renewable energy, pollution would decrease.
If companies reduced their emissions, air quality would improve.
Were companies to reduce their emissions, air quality would improve.
If this policy were implemented, unemployment would fall.
Were this policy to be implemented, unemployment would fall.

Pattern 2: Had + subject + past participle (Third Conditional)

Use for hypothetical past situations:

If the government had acted earlier, the crisis could have been avoided.
Had the government acted earlier, the crisis could have been avoided.
If they had invested in infrastructure, the economy would have grown faster.
Had they invested in infrastructure, the economy would have grown faster.

Pattern 3: Should + subject + verb (First Conditional - formal)

Use for possible future situations (more formal than "if"):

If the situation changes, we will need to adapt.
Should the situation change, we will need to adapt.
If you need further information, please contact us.
Should you need further information, please contact us.

Bonus: If + Adjective (Shortened Conditionals)

These compact forms are elegant and efficient:

If it is possible, governments should invest in public transport.
If possible, governments should invest in public transport.
If it is necessary, stricter regulations should be introduced.
If necessary, stricter regulations should be introduced.
When it is appropriate, flexible working should be offered.
Where appropriate, flexible working should be offered.

Practice: Transform These Conditionals

1. If schools taught financial literacy, young people would be better prepared for adult life.

Model answer:

Were schools to teach financial literacy, young people would be better prepared for adult life.

2. If the government had built more affordable housing, fewer people would be homeless today.

Model answer:

Had the government built more affordable housing, fewer people would be homeless today.

3. If companies offered remote working options, employee satisfaction would increase.

Model answer:

Were companies to offer remote working options, employee satisfaction would increase.

4. If it is possible, public transport should be made free for students.

Model answer:

If possible, public transport should be made free for students.

5. If this problem gets worse, immediate action will be required.

Model answer:

Should this problem get worse, immediate action will be required.

Best Essay Types for Advanced Conditionals

Problem/Solution Essays

Perfect for proposing solutions:

  • Were governments to implement stricter regulations, this problem could be significantly reduced.
  • Should this trend continue, urgent action will be required.

Opinion Essays

Strong for making predictions:

  • Were this policy to be adopted, the benefits would be substantial.
  • Had governments acted earlier, the current crisis could have been avoided.

Complex Noun Phrases: Packing Power

A complex noun phrase puts information BEFORE and AFTER the main noun. This lets you say more with fewer words - essential for the 250-word limit in Task 2.

[MODIFIERS] + NOUN + [QUALIFIERS]
costs → the rapidly increasing costs of living in major cities

Building Blocks

Before the Noun (Pre-modification)

Type Example
Adjective significant increase
Adverb + Adjective rapidly increasing costs
Noun as modifier government policy
Participle (-ing/-ed) growing concern / increased funding

After the Noun (Post-modification)

Type Example
Prepositional phrase the cost of living
Relative clause people who work remotely
Reduced relative (-ing) students studying abroad
Reduced relative (-ed) methods used by teachers
To-infinitive the ability to communicate

Transformation Examples

People who live in cities often face pollution. The pollution is increasing rapidly.
Urban residents often face rapidly increasing pollution levels.
Students who come from poor families struggle to afford university.
Students from disadvantaged backgrounds struggle to afford university.
Companies are using technology more. This technology was developed recently.
The growing use of recently developed technology by companies...
People who are concerned about their health often exercise regularly.
Health-conscious individuals often exercise regularly.
There are methods that teachers use. These methods are effective.
The effective methods used by teachers...

Practice: Build Complex Noun Phrases

1. People who work from home often feel isolated. This is becoming more common.

Model answer:

The growing number of remote workers often experience feelings of isolation.

Or: Employees working from home often feel increasingly isolated.

2. Young people use social media. They use it excessively. This has negative effects on their mental health.

Model answer:

The excessive use of social media by young people has negative effects on their mental health.

Or: Excessive social media use among young people negatively impacts mental health.

3. Houses in the city centre are expensive. The prices are rising quickly.

Model answer:

The rapidly rising cost of city-centre housing...

Or: Rapidly increasing property prices in urban centres...

4. Teachers use technology in the classroom. This technology is new. Some teachers find it difficult.

Model answer:

The integration of new technology in the classroom presents challenges for some teachers.

Or: Some teachers struggle with the implementation of recently introduced classroom technology.

5. People are living longer. This creates problems for healthcare systems.

Model answer:

Increasing life expectancy creates significant challenges for healthcare systems.

Or: The growing elderly population places increasing pressure on healthcare systems.

Best Essay Types for Complex Noun Phrases

Advantages/Disadvantages Essays

Perfect for introducing topics efficiently:

  • The growing reliance on technology in education...
  • The increasingly common practice of working from home...
  • The widespread availability of online learning resources...

All Essay Types - Topic Sentences

Strong openings for body paragraphs:

  • The main disadvantage of [complex noun phrase] is...
  • One significant benefit of [complex noun phrase] is...

Emphasis Structures: Cleft Sentences & Participles

These structures help you highlight the most important part of your argument. Use them strategically - not constantly.

Use cleft sentences sparingly - 1-2 per essay maximum. Overuse makes your writing sound formulaic, and IELTS examiners will notice.

Cleft Sentences: "It is... that" and "What... is"

Pattern 1: It is/was [X] that...

Emphasizes WHO or WHAT did something:

Governments have the responsibility to address climate change.
It is governments that have the responsibility to address climate change.
Education transforms lives.
It is education that transforms lives.

Pattern 2: What [subject] [verb] is...

Emphasizes WHAT needs to happen:

Governments need to invest more in renewable energy.
What governments need to do is invest more in renewable energy.
Many people don't consider the long-term consequences.
What many people fail to consider is the long-term consequences.
Children need love and stability, not material possessions.
What children truly need is love and stability, not material possessions.

When to Use Cleft Sentences

Good uses:
• Introducing your main argument in Discussion essays
• Emphasizing a key counterargument you want to address
• Drawing attention to a solution in Problem/Solution essays

Avoid: Using "It is often argued that..." or "What is important is..." in every paragraph

Participle Clauses: Compact Cause and Effect

Participle clauses (-ing and -ed forms) let you show relationships between ideas elegantly:

Present Participle (-ing) - Active meaning

Because they face rising costs, many families struggle to make ends meet.
Facing rising costs, many families struggle to make ends meet.
When we consider the evidence, it is clear that action is needed.
Considering the evidence, it is clear that action is needed.

Past Participle (-ed) - Passive meaning

Young people are faced with increasing competition in the job market.
Faced with increasing competition, young people struggle to find employment.
The policy was implemented in 2020 and has since reduced emissions by 20%.
Implemented in 2020, the policy has since reduced emissions by 20%.

Perfect Participle (Having + past participle) - Completed action first

After I considered both sides of the argument, I believe that...
Having considered both sides of the argument, I believe that...
Governments have failed to act for decades, so the problem has worsened.
Having failed to act for decades, governments now face a much larger problem.

Practice: Use Emphasis Structures

1. Parents have the most influence on children's development.

Model answer:

It is parents that have the most influence on children's development.

2. The government needs to provide more funding for healthcare.

Model answer:

What the government needs to do is provide more funding for healthcare.

3. Young people face high unemployment and are struggling to buy homes.

Model answer:

Facing high unemployment, young people are struggling to buy homes.

4. I have considered the advantages and disadvantages, and I believe the benefits outweigh the drawbacks.

Model answer:

Having considered the advantages and disadvantages, I believe the benefits outweigh the drawbacks.

5. Many people don't realise that small daily choices have a significant environmental impact.

Model answer:

What many people fail to realise is that small daily choices have a significant environmental impact.

Best Essay Types for Emphasis Structures

Discussion Essays (Both Views)

Useful for presenting contrasting views:

  • What supporters of this view argue is that...
  • What critics often overlook, however, is...
  • Having considered both perspectives, I believe...

Problem/Solution Essays

Participles work well for cause-effect:

  • Faced with rising costs, many families...
  • Lacking proper support, young people often...

Two-Part Question Essays

"Why is this happening? Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"

Question 1 (Causes/Reasons)

  • Nominalization: The increase in X is due to...
  • Complex NP: The main reason for this trend is...

Question 2 (Your view)

  • Participle: Considering these factors...
  • Conditional: Were this trend to continue...
Sample phrases for Two-Part Essays:
  • The primary reason for [trend] is the increasing [factor].
  • Considering these developments, I believe that...
  • Were this trend to continue unchecked, [consequence] would be inevitable.

Quick Reference: Structure Limits

Remember: variety matters, but so does naturalness. Here's how often to use each structure:

Structure Recommended Use Risk of Overuse
Nominalization 3-5 times per essay Low - sounds natural
Complex noun phrases 3-5 times per essay Low - sounds natural
Inversion 1-2 times per essay Medium - can sound forced
Advanced conditionals 1-2 times per essay Low - very formal
Cleft sentences 1-2 times per essay HIGH - easily spotted as formulaic
Participle clauses 2-3 times per essay Low - sounds natural

Your Action Plan

  1. Master nominalization first - it's the most useful and hardest to overuse
  2. Add one inversion to your next practice essay
  3. Try one "Were... to" conditional in a problem/solution context
  4. Build complex noun phrases for your topic sentences
  5. Use cleft sentences sparingly - one per essay maximum
Final tip: Don't try to use ALL of these in one essay. Pick 3-4 structures that fit naturally with your topic. Quality over quantity!
