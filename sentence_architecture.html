Sentence Architecture

Master the Building Blocks of Academic Writing

The Foundation: Simple Sentences

Every sentence you'll ever write starts here. Master the simple sentence, and everything else becomes easy.

SUBJECT + VERB (+ OBJECT/COMPLEMENT)
One independent clause. One complete thought.
SUBJECT
VERB
OBJECT

Key Rule

A simple sentence has ONE independent clause. It can be short or long, but it expresses only ONE complete idea.

Short simple sentence
The cat sleeps.
Longer simple sentence (still ONE clause)
The old grey cat on the red sofa sleeps peacefully in the afternoon sun.

Compound Sentences: Joining Equals

A compound sentence joins two or more independent clauses (complete thoughts) using coordinating conjunctions.

INDEPENDENT CLAUSE + FANBOYS + INDEPENDENT CLAUSE
Two complete thoughts joined as equals

The FANBOYS

For
= because
And
+ addition
Nor
= negative and
But
= contrast
Or
= alternative
Yet
= surprising contrast
So
= result

Punctuation Rule: Use a COMMA before the FANBOYS conjunction when joining two independent clauses.

Example
The rain stopped, so we went outside.
Example
I wanted coffee, but the cafe was closed.

Complex Sentences: Main + Dependent

A complex sentence has one independent clause and at least one dependent clause. The dependent clause cannot stand alone - it needs the main clause.

INDEPENDENT CLAUSE + DEPENDENT CLAUSE
or
DEPENDENT CLAUSE + , + INDEPENDENT CLAUSE

Subordinating Conjunctions

These words START dependent clauses:

because although even though while when whenever if unless since after before until as as soon as once whereas wherever though

Punctuation Rule: Use a COMMA after the dependent clause ONLY when it comes FIRST.

Dependent clause FIRST = comma needed
Because it was raining, we stayed inside.
Dependent clause SECOND = no comma
We stayed inside because it was raining.

Compound-Complex: The Full Package

This is the most sophisticated sentence type. It combines features of both compound and complex sentences.

2+ INDEPENDENT CLAUSES + 1+ DEPENDENT CLAUSE
Multiple complete thoughts + subordination = Band 8 variety
Example 1
Although I was tired, I finished my homework, and I went to bed early.
Dependent: Although I was tired
Independent 1: I finished my homework
and
Independent 2: I went to bed early
Example 2
The team worked overtime, but they missed the deadline because the project was too complex.
Example 3
When the storm hit, the power went out, so we lit candles.

Sentence Surgery: Transform and Improve

Real IELTS skill isn't just knowing sentence types - it's knowing when and how to use variety. Let's practice transforming sentences.

Exercise 1: Simple to Complex

Transform these simple sentences into complex sentences by adding a dependent clause.

Simple: I missed the bus.

Possible complex sentences:

I missed the bus because I woke up late.

Although I ran to the stop, I missed the bus.

I missed the bus even though I left on time.

Simple: She got promoted.

Possible complex sentences:

She got promoted because she worked harder than everyone else.

After she completed the project, she got promoted.

Since she joined the company, she has been promoted twice.

Simple: The city invested in public transport.

Possible complex sentences:

The city invested in public transport because traffic had become unbearable.

When the new government took power, the city invested in public transport.

Exercise 2: Two Simples to One Compound

Simple 1: The concert was sold out.

Simple 2: We watched it online.

The concert was sold out, so we watched it online.

Simple 1: The project was behind schedule.

Simple 2: The team worked weekends.

The project was behind schedule, so the team worked weekends.

The project was behind schedule, and the team worked weekends.

Exercise 3: Improve This Paragraph

This paragraph uses only simple sentences. Rewrite it with better variety.

Original (all simple):

Many people use social media daily. They check their phones constantly. This affects their productivity. Some companies ban phones at work. Employees become more focused. The policy is controversial.

Model answer:

Because many people use social media daily and check their phones constantly, their productivity is often affected. Although the policy is controversial, some companies have banned phones at work, and they report that employees become more focused as a result.

(2 sentences instead of 6, using complex and compound-complex structures)

Original (all simple):

Cities are growing rapidly. Housing prices are increasing. Young people cannot afford homes. Governments are building more apartments. The problem continues. New solutions are needed.

Model answer:

As cities grow rapidly, housing prices are increasing, and young people can no longer afford homes. Although governments are building more apartments, the problem continues, so new solutions are urgently needed.

Exercise 4: Multiple Transformations

Take these two simple sentences and write them THREE different ways.

Sentence A: The exam was difficult.

Sentence B: I passed.

Model answers:

Compound: The exam was difficult, but I passed.

Complex: Although the exam was difficult, I passed.

Compound-complex: Although the exam was difficult, I passed, and I'm proud of my result.

2+ IC + 1+ DC

Punctuation Rules Summary

Compound (always comma before FANBOYS)
I was tired, but I kept working.
Complex - dependent FIRST (comma after)
Although I was tired, I kept working.
Complex - dependent SECOND (no comma)
I kept working although I was tired.
