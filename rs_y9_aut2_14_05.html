The Limits of Science

What Science CAN Do

  • Explain how the physical world works
  • Make predictions based on evidence
  • Test hypotheses through observation and experiment
  • Build models of how nature operates

What Science CANNOT Answer

  • Why does the universe exist at all? (Why is there something rather than nothing?)
  • What is the meaning of life?
  • What makes something morally right or wrong?
  • What happens after death?
  • Is there purpose to existence?

These are not failures of science. Science simply operates in a different domain. It explains the how but not the why in a deeper sense.

Ludwig Wittgenstein on the Limits of Knowledge

The philosopher Ludwig Wittgenstein wrote: "Even when all possible scientific questions have been answered, the problems of life remain completely untouched."

This suggests that science, however complete, cannot address the most fundamental human concerns: meaning, purpose, ethics, and mortality.

The "Hard Problem" of Consciousness

Science can map brain activity. We can see which neurons fire when you see the colour red. But science cannot yet explain why you experience redness — why consciousness feels like something from the inside.

This gap between what we observe and what we experience is called the "hard problem of consciousness." It points to something science alone might not fully explain.

Religious Answers to Meaning Questions

Christianity

Life's purpose is relationship with God. Humans are created in God's image, and our deepest meaning comes from loving God and loving others.

Islam

Life's purpose is submission to Allah (Islam) and preparation for the afterlife (akhirah). Our actions in this life determine our fate after death.

Buddhism

Life's purpose is to end suffering through enlightenment (nirvana). The goal is to escape the cycle of suffering and rebirth (samsara) by understanding the nature of existence (the Four Noble Truths).

The Humanist Perspective

Humanists believe that meaning is not discovered in the universe; it is created by humans. We give our own lives purpose through:

  • Relationships and love
  • Creative expression
  • Contribution to others and society
  • Personal growth and learning

For humanists, this doesn't make life meaningless — it makes it our responsibility to create meaning.

Which Questions Matter Most?

Select the questions you think are most important. Click to choose, then explain why.

Why is there something rather than nothing?

Why does the universe exist at all? What caused it to begin?

What is consciousness?

Why do we experience the world subjectively? How does brain activity create awareness?

Is there life after death?

What happens to consciousness when the body dies? Is there an afterlife?

What makes life meaningful?

Is there objective purpose to existence, or do we create our own meaning?

What is morally right?

Are moral values discovered (objective) or invented (subjective)?

Why is there suffering?

If God is all-good and all-powerful, why does suffering exist? (The Problem of Evil)

What is love?

Is love reducible to chemistry and biology, or is it something more transcendent?

Is there free will?

Do we freely choose our actions, or are our choices determined by physics and chemistry?
