What Science Can and Cannot Do

The Scope of Science

Science excels at "How?" questions about physical world. But struggles with "Why?" questions:

  • Why do I exist? Science explains reproduction, not purpose.
  • What is beautiful? Science measures brain response, not beauty.
  • What is right/wrong? Science describes behavior, not morality.
  • What happens after death? Science has no data from afterlife.

Science requires testable, measurable evidence. Meaning, purpose, supernatural are outside science.

Five Big Questions Science Struggles With

1. Consciousness: Science maps brain activity but cannot explain subjective experience (qualia).

2. Why anything exists: Big Bang explains origin, not why something rather than nothing.

3. Morality: Science describes moral behavior; cannot tell you what you should do.

4. Life meaning: Science explains how life works, not what makes it meaningful.

5. Beyond matter: Science studies physical universe; cannot test for supernatural/spiritual.

Where Science Ends and Philosophy Begins

The "God of the Gaps" Problem

Danger: assuming "science can't explain X, so God did it." This is flawed because:

  • Science advances; gaps shrink over time.
  • It misuses both science and religion.

Better approach: Science explains mechanisms ("how"). Religion addresses meaning ("why"). Different questions.

NOMA (Gould)

Science and religion serve different purposes: science=facts; religion=values. They don't overlap, so cannot conflict.

Humanism

Science is best tool for truth. Meaning comes from human flourishing, not supernatural. No need for God.

Can Science Answer It?
