Phrases for Summarising

These phrases help you tell a story or explain an article clearly.

Opening a Summary

This article is about... / The writer describes how... / The author talks about how...

Explaining the Main Point

In essence, ... / Overall, this is about... / On the whole, ...

Adding Details

What he did was... / He felt that... / He reported that...

Concluding

What it boils down to is... / He concludes that... / In the end ...

Pausing Naturally in Summaries

Pauses (//) help listeners follow your summary. Mark where you would pause:

This article is about Mark Boyle, a business and economic student who, concerned about the state of the world, decided he wanted to change his lifestyle. What he did was live without money for over two years.

He talks about how by making our own products we can learn to respect them. He describes how he lived in a donated caravan and did volunteer work to pay the rent. For fuel, he used wood from the woods and he travelled on foot or by bike.

The thing was, he wrote a book about his experience that was quite popular. He found that what it boils down to is friendship—not money—is what provides security. In the end, he realised that he was never happier than when he was living without money.

Three Money-Free Business Models

1. Leila (Berlin): Library for Borrowing

Show Details
How it works: Members lend items to the shop and can borrow anything from 2,000+ items. Members include skis, toys, coffee machines, and power tools.

Cost: No contracts, run on trust. Members can donate €2-3 per month to help with rent.

Success factor: Common sense and community trust.

2. The Merit Shop (San Francisco): Talent for Goods

Show Details
How it works: Instead of money, you pay with your talent. Sing a song, draw a picture, or show a skill and receive goods in return.

Model: Customers upload videos of themselves. The Merit Shop selects talents and matches them with items (cereal for singing, sunglasses for drawing, etc.).

Goal: Make people smile and show that friendship matters more than money.

3. TEM Exchange (Volos, Greece): Virtual Currency

Show Details
How it works: A bartering system using virtual credit. You sell goods or services and get paid in TEM. Members can use TEM to buy from other members.

Context: Created during Greece's economic crisis so people could exchange goods and services without using euros.

Status: 800+ members and growing. Can work alongside traditional currency (part euro, part TEM).

Discussion & Reflection

Reflection Questions (think and discuss with your teacher):

1. Do you think you could live for a year with no money? What would be the biggest challenge?

2. Out of the three initiatives in this lesson (Leila, Merit Shop, TEM), which one appeals to you most? Why?

3. Do you ever barter when buying something in a market or shop? Give an example.

4. How often do you borrow from or lend things to friends and family? What kinds of things?

5. What do you pay membership fees for? Do you think it's worth it?
