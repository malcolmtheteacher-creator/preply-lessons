plummeted (also accepts plunged)
2. Became almost 3× bigger (verb phrase, 2 words)

3. Stopped changing after a rise or fall (2 words)

4. Moved up and down irregularly (1 word)

5. Very small, almost unnoticeable change (adverb)

Build It — Verb × Adverb × Frame

Here are 6 mini data points. For each, build a sentence. Pick a verb, an adverb, and a frame from your memory. Then reveal a model answer.

Data set — "Internet use by age group, 2000 vs 2020"

• Under-25s: 40% → 98%
• 25–49: 30% → 95%
• 50–64: 10% → 80%
• Over-65s: 2% → 55%
• Overall: 25% → 85%

1. Over-65s

Internet use among the over-65s rose dramatically, climbing from just 2% in 2000 to 55% by 2020.

2. Under-25s (they nearly reached the ceiling)

Usage among under-25s more than doubled, surging from 40% to 98%.

3. 50–64 (use "nearly" + multiple)

The 50–64 age group saw use rise sharply from 10% to 80% — an eightfold increase.

4. 25–49 (use a rising verb + adverb)

Among 25–49-year-olds, internet use climbed steadily from 30% to 95%.

5. Overall trend (one sentence summarising the whole)

Overall, internet use rose significantly across every age group, more than tripling from 25% to 85%.

6. Contrast — use Frame 5 ("While X…, Y…")

While under-25s nearly reached full saturation, the over-65s — though rising fast — remained the group with the lowest usage.

Mini-Paragraph — 150 words, 4 frames minimum

Using the data below, write a short Task 1 response. You must use at least four of the six sentence frames and at least three verbs from your vault.

Data — "Household spending, UK, 1990 vs 2020 (% of budget)"

• Housing: 18% → 32%
• Food: 22% → 11%
• Transport: 14% → 14%
• Leisure: 8% → 18%
• Other: 38% → 25%

Model (148 words)

The chart shows how UK household spending shifted between 1990 and 2020. Overall, housing and leisure costs rose significantly, while food spending nearly halved.

The share of income spent on housing climbed sharply, going from 18% to 32% — almost doubling over the three decades. Leisure spending more than doubled, surging from 8% to 18%. In contrast, the proportion spent on food plummeted, falling from 22% to just 11% — a drop of around a half. While housing and leisure increased, 'other' categories declined steadily from 38% to 25%. Meanwhile, transport spending remained relatively stable at 14% across the whole period.

Frames used: F1 (climbed from…to), F2 (more than doubled, surging…), F3 (falling from…to), F5 (While X…, Y…), F6 (Meanwhile, Z remained stable at…).

Error Surgery — Fix and diagnose

Six broken sentences. For each: (a) spot the error, (b) write the fix, (c) name the rule. Then reveal.

1. "Car use has increased by almost a triple."

Fix: Car use nearly tripled.
Rule: Use the verb (double/triple), not a noun phrase ("a triple").

2. "All types of transport has decreased."

Fix: All types of transport have decreased.
Rule: Plural subject (types) → plural verb (have). Subject-verb agreement.

3. "The amount of trips has risen."

Fix: The number of trips has risen.
Rule: Use number for countable (trips, cars, people). Amount is for uncountables (water, money, time).

4. "Walking has decreased around half."

Fix: Walking has decreased by around half.
Rule: We say "decreased by X" to give the size of the fall.

5. "Cars has become from least used to most used."

Fix: The car has changed from being the least-used to the most-used mode.
Rule: Use Frame 4 — "changed from being X to Y", not "become from X to Y".

6. "Bus use has dropped slightly of 2 million trips."

Fix: Bus use has dropped slightly, by 2 million trips.
Rule: Size of a change → "by X". Not "of X".
