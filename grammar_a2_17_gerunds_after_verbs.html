Check your idea CLICK
Because this isn't about right now — it's about what Ben always likes and dislikes. After feeling words like love, hate, enjoy, English uses -ing to name the activity itself, not to say it's happening this second. That's a new job for the -ing ending you already know from "I am baking".

Saying What You Like: "I love cooking"

For feelings about an activity — like, love, hate, enjoy, prefer, don't mind.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday opinions:

  • "I love reading before bed."
  • "She hates waiting in queues."
  • "We don't mind walking to work."

Before you check: what comes right after love / hates / don't mind in each one?

Check the pattern CLICK
like / love / hate / enjoy / prefer / don't mind + verb-ing. Never "to" here — the -ing form names the activity, almost like a noun.
like / love / hate / enjoy / prefer / don't mind + verb-ing

From the verb to the activity

"swim" (the verb)
→
"I love swimming." (the activity)

Once a feeling word like love, hate or enjoy comes before it, the verb changes into its -ing form.

Getting Active: "We go swimming"

For activities and sports — especially ones with movement.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday activities:

  • "We go swimming every Saturday."
  • "They went shopping yesterday afternoon."
  • "Let's go running before breakfast."

What changes between the three sentences, and what stays the same? Say it before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
go changes with the tense (go / goes / went / going to go), but the activity after it always stays -ing: swimming, shopping, running.
go / goes / went / going to go + verb-ing (activity)
Common ones you'll hear all the time: go swimming, go shopping, go running, go dancing, go cycling, go sightseeing.

Activities & Wishes: "Swimming is..." / "I'd like to..."

Using -ing as the subject of a sentence, and the difference between a habit and a wish.

Spot the pattern

  • "Swimming is great exercise."
  • "Cooking for a big group relaxes me."
  • "Walking to work saves money."

What is the subject — the "doer" — of each sentence? Say it before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
The activity itself — swimming, cooking, walking — is the subject. The -ing form works just like a noun, and it's singular: "Swimming IS", never "Swimming ARE".
verb-ing (as the subject) + singular verb

From an everyday habit to the subject of a sentence

"I read every night."
→
"Reading relaxes me."
Careful with two similar phrases: "I like swimming." is a general habit — true most of the time. "I'd like to swim today." is a specific wish — just this once, right now. "Would like" is always followed by to + verb, never -ing.
