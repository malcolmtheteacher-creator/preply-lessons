Now vs Usually

Present simple — habits, facts, routines: I work from home. Water boils at 100°.
Present continuous — now, or temporarily around now: I am working this week. It is raining.
State verbs (know, like, want, need, understand, believe, mean, own) stay simple: I understand, never “am understanding.”

Finished vs Connected

Past simple — a finished time (yesterday, last week, in 2019, ago): I saw her yesterday.
Present perfect — time not finished, or it still matters now (ever, never, just, already, yet, since, for): I have lived here for years.
Golden rule: never a finished-time word with the present perfect. Not “I have seen him yesterday” → I saw him yesterday.

Telling a Story

Past continuous — the background, in progress: I was cooking…
Past simple — the event that happens: …when the phone rang.
Past perfect — an earlier past, before another past moment: When I arrived, the train had already left.

Talking Ahead

will — a decision now, a prediction, an offer: I'm tired — I 'll go to bed.
going to — a plan, or evidence you can see: Look at those clouds — it 's going to rain.
present continuous — a fixed arrangement: I 'm meeting Anna at seven.
Trap: after when, if, as soon as, before, after, use a present tense — never “will.” I'll call you when I arrive.

Before you finish

  • I never put a finished-time word (yesterday, in 2019, ago) with the present perfect.
  • I use since / for with the present perfect, not the present simple.
  • I keep state verbs (know, like, want, understand) out of the continuous.
  • After when / if / as soon as, I use a present tense, not “will.”
  • I use had + past participle for the earlier of two past actions.
