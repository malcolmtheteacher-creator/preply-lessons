Back Home: Reunions & Catching Up

B1+ Level · Past Perfect, "I Wish" & Telling Longer Stories

The Arrival

The first hour back home is always strange. Familiar but different. Everyone is older. Some things have stayed exactly the same. Others have completely changed.

Read & Notice

Read this short paragraph. Look at the verbs in bold.

"By the time I got to my parents' house, the whole family had already arrived. My aunt had cooked everything I loved as a child. My cousin had grown taller than me. The dog I remembered had died the year before, but they had got a new one. Everything had moved on without me — and yet, in some strange way, nothing had really changed."

1. What is the main moment? (When did the speaker arrive?)

"I got to my parents' house" — this is the past simple, the main moment.

2. What had happened BEFORE that moment?

The family had arrived. The aunt had cooked. The cousin had grown. The dog had died. They had got a new one. — all of these happened before the speaker arrived.

3. Which tense is used for the earlier events?

Past perfect: had + past participle. We use it to talk about a past inside the past.

By the Time…

The past perfect is your storytelling superpower. It lets you talk about two times in the past — what happened, and what had already happened before that.

The Pattern

Past Perfect = had + past participle

+  She had eaten before I arrived.

−  She hadn't eaten when I arrived.

?  Had she eaten when you arrived?

"By the time we got there, everyone had gone to bed."
"I didn't recognise the place — they had painted all the houses."
"When she opened the door, I realised she had been crying."
"He had never seen snow before that day."

"I Wish…"

When we miss something, or want something to be different, we use "I wish". Going home brings out lots of these feelings.

Three Patterns

For the present (something now is not how I want it)

I wish + past simple

"I wish I lived closer to my family."

"I wish I had more time at home."

For the past (a regret about something already done)

I wish + past perfect

"I wish I had visited last summer."

"I wish I hadn't left so quickly last time."

For other people (something annoying they keep doing)

I wish + would + verb

"I wish my brother would call me more often."

Spot the Mistake

1. "I wish I can stay longer."

could — "I wish I could stay longer." After I wish for the present, use the past form: could, not can.

2. "I wish I didn't say that to her last year."

hadn't said — "I wish I hadn't said that to her last year." For past regrets, use past perfect.

3. "I wish my parents will visit me one day."

would — "I wish my parents would visit me one day."

Catching Up

When you meet someone after a long time, you have a special set of questions and phrases. They feel warm. They open doors.

Useful Phrases

"It's been ages! How have you been?"
"You haven't changed a bit!"
"Tell me everything — what have you been up to?"
"Last time I saw you, you were…"
"I can't believe how much you've grown / changed!"
"Whatever happened to… (your old friend / that old shop / your dream of…)?"
"Are you still in touch with…?"
"We have so much to catch up on!"

Mini Dialogue

A: "Wow! It's been ages! How have you been?"

B: "I know, it's been what — three years? You haven't changed a bit!"

A: "Neither have you! Tell me everything — what have you been up to?"

B: "Oh, where do I start? Last time I saw you, I had just moved away. So much has happened since then."

A: "Whatever happened to that little café you used to work in?"

B: "It closed down! Can you believe it? They turned it into a phone shop."

Which past tense does Speaker B use to set the scene?

"I had just moved away" — past perfect, because it happened before the previous meeting.

Roleplay — Bumping Into Someone

Imagine you've just bumped into an old friend on the street in your hometown. You haven't seen them for five years. The teacher will play the friend.

Use as many of these as you can:

  • "It's been ages!"
  • "Last time I saw you…"
  • "What have you been up to?"
  • "Whatever happened to…?"
  • "You haven't changed a bit!"
Roleplay time: 3:00

A Longer Story

Now let's put everything together. You're going to tell a longer story — a memory of one specific time you went home.

Story Structure (5 Parts)

1. Set the scene. When? Where? Who with?

"It was the summer of…" / "I'll never forget the time…"

2. Background. What had happened before? Why was this trip special?

"I hadn't been home for…" / "I had been planning this trip for months."

3. The main event. What happened?

"As soon as I arrived…" / "The strangest moment was when…"

4. The feeling. How did you feel? What surprised you?

"I couldn't believe…" / "I realised I had really missed…"

5. Looking back. What do you remember most? Any regrets or wishes?

"Looking back…" / "I wish I had…" / "I'll always remember…"

Speaking task: Tell your story in 3 minutes. Try to use:
  • At least one used to sentence
  • At least one past perfect (had + past participle)
  • At least one "I wish" sentence
  • Past simple + past continuous together
Story time: 3:00

Discussion

Final questions. Try to give the longest answer you can.

Conversation Questions

1. Is there a person you wish you had stayed in touch with?

Who? Why did you lose contact? Would you ever try to reconnect?

2. What's the biggest change you've noticed in your family over the years?

People? Traditions? How they treat you?

3. Has anyone ever surprised you when you went back home?

Someone who had completely changed? Or someone you had almost forgotten?

4. What's harder — leaving home, or coming back?

Why do you think so?

5. If you could go back to one moment in your life and live it again, which would it be?

Not to change it — just to feel it again.

6. There's a saying: "Home is not a place, it's a feeling." Do you agree?

What makes somewhere feel like home, for you?
