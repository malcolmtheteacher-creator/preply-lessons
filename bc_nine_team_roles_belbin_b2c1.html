What Makes a Great Team?

"A team is not a bunch of people with job titles, but a congregation of individuals, each of whom has a role which is understood by other members. Members of a team seek out certain roles and they perform most effectively in the ones that are most natural to them."
— Dr Meredith Belbin

Who is Dr. Belbin?

Dr. Meredith Belbin is a British researcher and management theorist who studied team dynamics for over 9 years. His research led to the identification of nine distinct team roles that people naturally adopt.

Key Insight

Belbin discovered that the most successful teams weren't necessarily those with the smartest individuals, but those with the right balance of roles. Teams that had too many of the same type often underperformed.

Quick Reflection

Think about a team you've been part of (at work, school, or in a hobby). Was there a good balance of different personalities and skills? What happened when there wasn't?

Belbin's Nine Team Roles

The roles are grouped into three categories. Click each role to learn more:

🔴 Action-Oriented Roles

Shaper Action
Challenging, dynamic, thrives on pressure. Has drive to overcome obstacles.

They bring: Energy, urgency, and the drive to get things moving.

Key phrase: "Let's make this happen!"

⚠️ May be prone to provocation
Implementer Action
Practical, reliable, efficient. Turns ideas into actions and organises work.

They bring: Structure and the ability to make things happen practically.

Key phrase: "Here's how we do it."

⚠️ Is somewhat inflexible
Completer Finisher Action
Painstaking, conscientious, anxious. Searches out errors.

They bring: Attention to detail and high standards.

Key phrase: "Let me check that again."

⚠️ Is inclined to worry unnecessarily

🟢 People-Oriented Roles

Co-ordinator People
Mature, confident, identifies talent. Clarifies goals. Delegates effectively.

They bring: Leadership and the ability to bring out the best in others.

Key phrase: "Who would be best for this?"

⚠️ Can often be seen as manipulative
Team Worker People
Co-operative, perceptive and diplomatic. Listens and averts friction.

They bring: Harmony and support for other team members.

Key phrase: "How can I help?"

⚠️ Might become indecisive when unpopular decisions need to be made
Resource Investigator People
Outgoing, enthusiastic, communicative. Explores opportunities, develops contacts.

They bring: External connections and new opportunities.

Key phrase: "I know someone who can help!"

⚠️ Loses interest when enthusiasm has passed

🔵 Thinking-Oriented Roles

Plant Thinking
Creative, imaginative, free-thinking. Generates ideas and solves problems.

They bring: Innovation and creative solutions.

Key phrase: "What if we tried...?"

⚠️ Could be forgetful and too preoccupied to communicate effectively
Monitor Evaluator Thinking
Sober, strategic and discerning. Sees all options and judges accurately.

They bring: Critical analysis and logical evaluation.

Key phrase: "Let's look at this objectively."

⚠️ Can lack drive and ability to inspire others
Specialist Thinking
Single-minded, dedicated. Provides knowledge and skills in rare supply.

They bring: Deep expertise in a specific area.

Key phrase: "In my professional experience..."

⚠️ May have a tendency to dwell on technicalities

Describing Tendencies

When describing people's typical behaviours (including weaknesses), we use these structures to show it's a tendency, not an absolute. Click each one to learn more:

could be + adjective
Shows a possible characteristic that might appear sometimes
"She could be forgetful when under pressure."
is inclined to + verb
Indicates a natural tendency or habit
"He is inclined to worry about small details."
might become + adjective
Shows a possible change under certain conditions
"She might become indecisive when facing difficult choices."
may be prone to + noun/-ing
Suggests vulnerability to a particular weakness
"He may be prone to provocation in stressful meetings."
may have a tendency to + verb
Describes a pattern of behaviour
"She may have a tendency to dwell on technical details."
