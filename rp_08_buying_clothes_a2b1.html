Buying Clothes

You want a new jacket. It is a little too small. And you have an old jumper to bring back. Time to shop — and be polite.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is Saturday. You are in a clothes shop. You have two jobs today. First, you want a new jacket. There is a friend's wedding soon, and you need something nice.

You see a jacket you like. But there is a problem — this one is too small for you. You want to ask for a bigger size, and you want to try it on before you buy it. Second job: you have a jumper in your bag. You bought it here last week, but it is not really you. You want to bring it back.

The shop assistant is friendly. They want to help you — but they also want to sell you more things! So you must be polite, but also say "no, thank you" when you need to.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like buying clothes? Do you go to shops, or do you buy online? Why?
  2. Do you try clothes on before you buy them? Have you ever bought something and then taken it back?

Who's who

You
The Shopper 🧥

Friendly and polite. You want a jacket that fits, and you want to bring back an old jumper — with no stress.

The other person
The Shop Assistant 🛍️

Kind and helpful. They can find your size and take the jumper back — but they will also try to sell you more!

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at how the shopper asks for a bigger size, tries the jacket on, and says "no, thank you" in a nice way. It is OK to speak slowly. You can ask them to say it again.

From memory — don't look up!

1. The shopper likes a jacket, but there is a problem. What is the problem, and what do they ask for?

2. The assistant tries to sell one more thing. What is it, and what does the shopper say?

3. The shopper wants to return the jumper. What does the assistant ask for? And how do they fix it at the end?

1. The jacket is too small. So the shopper asks for it in a bigger size — and the assistant finds a large one.
2. The assistant tries to sell a shirt too ("with the jacket"). The shopper says "No, thank you — just the jacket today."
3. The assistant asks for the receipt. Because the shopper is buying the jacket, the assistant takes the jumper's price off the total — an easy fix for both.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use in any clothes shop. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it with a smile. A friendly voice and the words please and thank you do a lot. Short sentences are fine. And it is OK to say "no, thank you" — you do not have to buy more.

🚪 Looking around

Phrase 1

You walk in. The assistant comes to you fast and asks, "Can I help you?" You want to look first, on your own — but you want to be nice. Which answer is polite?

A. "No."
B. "Please go away."
C. "I'm just looking, thank you."
C — "I'm just looking, thank you."
"I'm just looking" tells them you are not ready to buy yet, but you are still friendly. A and B sound cold or rude. You can also say: "I'm just having a look, thanks."

👕 Sizes and the fitting room

Phrase 2

You like a jacket, but this one is too small for you. You want a bigger one. Complete it: "Do you have this in a bigger ___?"

"Do you have this in a bigger size?"
"in a bigger size" is how you ask for a larger one. For a smaller one, say "a smaller size". You can also say: "Have you got this in a bigger size?"
Phrase 3

You want to put the jacket on before you buy it, so you can see if it fits. Put the words in order:

trycan Iit on?
"Can I try it on?"
We "try on" clothes to see if they fit. You try it on in the fitting room (or "changing room"). "Can I…?" makes it a polite question.
Phrase 4

You are in the fitting room. You put the jacket on, but you can't close it — it is not big enough for you. Complete it: "This one is too ___."

"This one is too small."
"too small" or "too big" tells them the problem fast. You can also say "It doesn't fit." Other useful words: "too tight" (small) and "too loose" (big).

🪞 Deciding

Phrase 5

The bigger jacket is good. It fits well. You want to buy it. Which sounds natural and friendly?

A. "I will purchase this item."
B. "I'll take it, thanks."
C. "Give me this one."
B — "I'll take it, thanks."
"I'll take it" is the normal, easy way to say "I want to buy this". A is too formal, like a robot. C sounds like an order, not a request.
Phrase 6

Different day, different shop. Nothing is right for you today. You want to leave, but stay friendly, so they are happy if you come back. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"I'll leave it, thank you. Maybe next time."
"I'll leave it" means "not today, thank you". It is soft and polite — you buy nothing, but nobody is upset. You can also say: "I'll think about it, thanks."

🔁 Taking something back

Phrase 7

Now the other job: a jumper you bought last week. You want to bring it back to the shop. Complete the opener: "Hello, I would like to ___ this jumper, please."

"I would like to return this jumper, please."
To "return" something means to bring it back to the shop. Say why, gently: "It doesn't fit" or "It's not really me." Nobody has to feel bad.
Phrase 8

You want the return to be quick and easy. Before they ask, you show the little paper that proves you bought it here. What do you say, and what do you show? (Say it out loud first.)

"Here is the receipt."
The receipt is the little paper that shows you paid. Show it before they ask — then everything is fast and easy. You can add: "The tags are still on, and I haven't worn it."

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in every shop, not just for clothes. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"Do you have this in a bigger/smaller size?" — Think of a thing you want in another size or colour. What do you ask?

"Can I try it on?" — What is one thing you always try on before you buy it?

"I would like to return this, please." — Think of a thing you bought but did not want. What do you say in the shop?

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — look around → ask for a bigger size → try it on → say what is wrong → decide → then bring back the old jumper. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your shop. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the shopper 🧥

The Twist

👗 No bigger size

One more round — no script this time. You find a jacket you love, but this one is too small. You ask for a bigger size… and the assistant says: "I'm sorry, this is the last one. We have no bigger size." What do you say? Stay friendly! Ask something else — Can they check another shop? Can they order one for you? Is it 20% off if you take this one? Start the timer and talk it through. Stay the kind of customer they want to help.

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said "I'm just looking" in a friendly way.
  • I asked for a bigger size, and asked to try it on.
  • I said "no, thank you" to the extra shirt — and stayed polite.
  • I asked to return the jumper, and showed the receipt.
  • I said "thank you" warmly at the end.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

Five hundred years ago, in the time of the Tudors, you could not just buy any clothes you liked. There were laws about clothes. Only the king and queen could wear purple silk. Only rich, important people could wear red and fine velvet. Everyone else wore simple wool.

Your clothes told people who you were. They showed if you were rich or poor, important or not. You could not choose. And you could not change it.

Today it is very different. You can try a jacket on, buy it or not, and bring an old jumper back — all in one afternoon. A Tudor person could only dream of that!
