Hot Takes: The Apology Tour

You're in trouble. Time to face the press.

The set-up

You've taken a hot take in real life — or someone has dug one up — and now you're paying for it. In each scenario, you'll deliver a short opening statement, then face four sharp questions. You don't know the questions in advance.

1. The non-apology apology

"I'm sorry if anyone was offended." This shifts the blame onto the offended. Modern audiences hate it. Avoid it — but recognise when it slips out.

2. The full apology

Three ingredients: name what you did, name who was hurt, say what you'll do differently. Skip any one and it sounds hollow.

3. The bridge

"Yes, that happened. And here is the wider context that doesn't excuse it but does explain it." Acknowledge first, then add. Never lead with the "but".

4. The hedge

"At the time, given what was known…" / "I'm not in a position to comment on that ongoing matter, but…" / "We are actively reviewing…" Buys time without lying.

5. Owning it

Sometimes the strongest move is the simplest: "I was wrong. There is no good defence. Here is what I'm going to do." Used well, it disarms even hostile rooms.
