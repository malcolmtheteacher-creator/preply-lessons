A Noisy Neighbour

It is late, and the music next door is loud. You have work in the morning. Time to knock — and ask nicely, without a fight.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is night — after eleven o'clock. You are in bed. You have work early tomorrow. But you can't sleep. Your neighbour is playing loud music, and you can hear it through the wall.

You are tired, and a little angry. But you don't want a fight. This person lives next to you, and you will see them every day. So you want two things: some quiet, and to still be friends.

You get up, put on a jumper, and take a big breath. You walk to their door. You knock. Now — be calm, be friendly, and ask for what you need.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you have neighbours? Are they quiet or loud? Do you know them well?
  2. When someone makes you angry, do you say something, or stay quiet? Why?

Who's who

You
The Neighbour 🚪

Tired, but kind. You want to sleep — but you also want to keep the peace. You will knock, smile, and ask nicely.

The other person
The One With the Music 🎧

Not a bad person — they just did not know. A little worried at first, but kind and helpful, if you are kind too.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. See how the person stays friendly. They never say "you are too loud!" They talk about the wall, not the person. That is the trick.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the friendly, polite words.

NeighbourHello? Can I help you?
YouHi! Sorry to knock so late. I'm your neighbour — I live next door, in flat 12.
NeighbourOh, hello! Nice to meet you. Is everything OK?
YouYes, everything is fine. I don't want to make a problem. But I can hear your music through the wall.
NeighbourOh no. Is it too loud? It's not very late…
YouIn the day it's fine. But at night it's hard. The wall is very thin, so I can hear everything.
NeighbourI'm sorry. I didn't know.
YouIt's OK, don't worry. The problem is, I get up early — at six. So at night I need to sleep.
NeighbourYes, of course. What can I do?
YouCan you turn the music down a little at night? That's all. In the day it's fine.
NeighbourYes, no problem. And I can move the speakers away from the wall too.

From memory — don't look up!

1. What does the guest say first, before they talk about the music?

2. Why is the night hard for the guest? (There are two reasons.)

3. At the end, the neighbour offers to do two things. What are they?

1. They say who they are, in a friendly way: "I'm your neighbour, I live next door, in flat 12." They say hello first, before the problem.
2. The guest gets up early (at six), so they need to sleep at night. And the wall is very thin, so they can hear everything.
3. The neighbour will turn the music down at night, and move the speakers away from the wall.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases to ask a neighbour for quiet — in a kind, friendly way. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it nicely. You want quiet — but you also want to stay friends. So smile, speak slowly, and be kind. Talk about the wall and the noise, not "you". It is OK to say "sorry" and "please" a lot.

🚪 Knocking softly

Phrase 1

The door opens. First, say who you are — be friendly. Complete it: "Hi, I'm your neighbour. I live ___ door."

"Hi, I'm your neighbour. I live next door."
Say who you are first, before the problem. It makes you a friendly neighbour, not a stranger with a complaint. "Next door" means the home right beside yours.
Phrase 2

You knocked late at night. Start in a soft, kind way. Which one is best?

A. "Do you know how late it is?"
B. "Sorry to knock so late."
C. "You are too loud!"
B — "Sorry to knock so late."
Saying "sorry" first makes the other person relaxed and happy to help. A and C sound angry — and then people don't want to help you.

🗣️ Say the problem

Phrase 3

Now say the problem — but talk about the wall, not the person. Complete it: "I can hear the music through the ___."

"I can hear the music through the wall."
The wall is thin, so it is the wall's "fault", not the person's. When you blame the wall, there is nothing to fight about. Everyone stays calm.
Phrase 4

You want to say the noise is a problem, but with no fight. Which sentence is kinder?

A. "You play music too loud all the time."
B. "The wall is very thin, so I can hear everything."
B — "The wall is very thin, so I can hear everything."
B blames the thin wall, so there is nothing to fight about. A points a finger ("you… too loud") and starts an argument.

🤝 Ask for a change

Phrase 5

Give a real reason — say why the night is hard for you. Complete it: "I get up early, so at night I need to ___."

"I get up early, so at night I need to sleep."
A real reason (an early start, you need to sleep) helps people say "yes". Use it for other things too: "I have a test tomorrow, so I need to study."
Phrase 6

Now ask for the change — small and polite. Put the words in order:

turnitdowna little
"Can you turn it down a little?"
"Turn it down" means make it quieter. "A little" makes it a small, easy thing to ask — so it is easy to say "yes". You can also say: "Can you turn the music down?"

💛 Keep it friendly

Phrase 7

You want to be fair too. Offer them the same: if you are ever too loud, they can tell you. Say it out loud first, then check.

"If we are ever too loud, please tell us."
This makes it fair for both people. You are neighbours, not enemies. You can also say: "Just knock on our door any time."
Phrase 8

The neighbour says sorry and agrees. You want to leave as friends. Which one is best?

A. "OK. Just don't do it again."
B. "Thank you, that's very kind. Good night!"
B — "Thank you, that's very kind. Good night!"
B is warm and leaves you as friends. A sounds cold and angry — and remember, you still live next door tomorrow!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work for any small, polite ask — with neighbours, friends, anyone. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"Hi, I'm your neighbour. I live ___." — Who lives next to you? How would you say hello?

"I can hear ___ through the wall." — What noise can you hear at home? (music, a TV, a dog…)

"Can you turn it down a little?" — Think of a small thing you need to ask someone. What is it?

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — knock → say who you are → say the problem (blame the wall) → give your reason → ask for quiet → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your wall, your night. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Role-play timer: 3:00

Round 1 — you are the one knocking 🚪

Their door opens — you answer. Be friendly: talk about the wall, not "you". Use sorry, please, and thank you.

NeighbourHello? Can I help you?
NeighbourOh, hello. Is something wrong?
NeighbourOh. It's not very late, though, is it?
NeighbourOK. What do you want me to do?
NeighbourYes, no problem. I'm sorry.

Round 2 — now YOU are the one with the music 🎧

Change places. Someone knocks about YOUR music. It is easy to get angry — don't. Say sorry, and be the kind neighbour you would want. Think of your own fix.

NeighbourHi, sorry to knock so late. I'm your neighbour, from flat 12.
NeighbourI can hear your music through the wall at night.
NeighbourI get up early, so I really need to sleep.
NeighbourCan we find a way to fix this?
NeighbourThank you, that's very kind.
The Twist

🌙 It happens again

Two nights later, the music is loud again. You knock one more time. You are very tired now — but stay calm and friendly. Don't get angry. Say that it happened again, and ask nicely one more time. Maybe ask for a clear rule you can both keep — like "music off after ten on work nights". Try to leave as friends. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said who I was, in a friendly way.
  • I talked about the wall and the noise, not "you".
  • I gave a real reason (I get up early).
  • I asked for a small change, politely.
  • I said thank you and stayed friendly.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

Long ago in Rome, there was an emperor named Nero. One day, a big fire burned many people's homes. But Nero was not sad. He took the land, and he built a huge golden palace just for himself — with a big lake, in the place where people used to live.

Nero was the worst kind of neighbour. He did not care about anyone else. He only thought about himself.

You are the opposite of Nero. You knock, you smile, and you ask nicely. You care about the people next to you. That is what makes a good neighbour — and a good person.

Two questions to talk about

  1. Nero never had to say sorry to anyone. Is it easy to be kind when you have a lot of power? Why, or why not?
  2. A good neighbour gives a little, so everyone is happy. When is it good to say "yes, no problem" — and when should you say "no"?
