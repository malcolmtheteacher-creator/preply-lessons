What is C2 Proficiency?

C2 Proficiency (formerly known as CPE - Cambridge Proficiency in English) is the highest level Cambridge English qualification. It proves you can communicate with the fluency and sophistication of a highly competent English speaker.

Important: Is CPE Right for You?

CPE is designed for candidates who are already at a very advanced level. If you are currently at B2 or lower C1, we recommend starting with CAE (C1 Advanced) first. Many candidates find CPE significantly more challenging than they expected.

CAE vs CPE: What is the Difference?

C1 Advanced (CAE)

  • Texts are challenging but manageable
  • Vocabulary is advanced but practical
  • Can make some errors and still pass
  • Good for university admission, professional use

C2 Proficiency (CPE)

  • Texts are literary, academic, abstract
  • Vocabulary includes rare words, nuanced meanings
  • Demands near-native precision
  • For teaching English, academic careers, prestige

The Scoring System

CPE uses the Cambridge English Scale (80-230 points):

Score Grade CEFR Level
220-230 Grade A C2 (exceptional)
213-219 Grade B C2
200-212 Grade C C2
180-199 Level C1 C1 certificate issued

Silver Lining

Even if you do not reach C2, scoring 180+ gives you a C1 certificate - equivalent to passing CAE! So attempting CPE is not "wasted" if you fall slightly short.

The Four Papers

CPE has the same structure as CAE, but everything is significantly harder.

Paper 1: Reading & Use of English

7 parts, 53 questions

1 hour 30 mins

40% of total score

Paper 2: Writing

2 tasks

1 hour 30 mins

20% of total score

Paper 3: Listening

4 parts, 30 questions

~40 mins

20% of total score

Paper 4: Speaking

3 parts, with partner

~16 mins

20% of total score

Key Differences from CAE

Paper CAE CPE
Reading & UoE 8 parts 7 parts (but harder texts)
Writing length 220-260 words 240-280 words (Part 1), 280-320 (Part 2)
Listening Hear twice Part 2: Hear ONCE only
Speaking 4 parts, 15 mins 3 parts, 16 mins (longer turns)

The CPE Listening Trap

In Part 2 of Listening, you hear the recording only once. This catches many candidates off guard. You must be ready to catch information on the first listen.

Sample Tasks - See the Difficulty

Let us look at examples that show why CPE is considered elite level.

Reading & Use of English - Part 1 (Multiple Choice Cloze)

Choose the best word to complete the sentence.

The artist's work has been criticized for its lack of originality, yet it continues to command high prices at auction.

A) harshly    B) severely    C) widely    D) strongly

Answer: C) widely

All options could potentially work grammatically, but "widely criticized" is the correct collocation meaning "criticized by many people." This tests nuanced collocational knowledge.

Reading & Use of English - Part 4 (Key Word Transformation)

Complete with 3-8 words including the key word.

Original: I am sure he did not realize the consequences of his actions.

Key word: LITTLE

He his actions would have.

Answer: He little realized what consequences his actions would have.

This tests inversion with negative adverbs ("Little did he realize...") and complex restructuring. Notice the 3-8 word range vs CAE's 2-5.

Writing - Part 2 (Article)

280-320 words required. Notice the sophistication expected.

Task: A literary magazine has asked readers to contribute articles on the following topic:

"In an age of instant digital communication, the art of letter writing has become obsolete."

Write an article for the magazine giving your views on this statement.

Examiners want:

  • Sophisticated vocabulary ("epistolary tradition," "tangible artifact")
  • Complex sentence structures with variety
  • Engaging style appropriate for a literary magazine
  • Nuanced argumentation (not just "I agree/disagree")
  • Cultural and literary references where appropriate

Speaking - Part 2 (Collaborative Task)

4 minutes of discussion with your partner.

Task: Here are some factors that might influence a person's sense of identity, and a question for you to discuss.

Factors: cultural heritage / career / relationships / beliefs / place of residence

Question: How significant is each of these in shaping a person's sense of identity?

Key strategy: You must engage with abstract concepts, show sophisticated reasoning, and demonstrate excellent interaction skills. Phrases like "That raises an interesting point about..." and "To build on what you said..." are essential.

The Big Decision

Choose CAE If...

  • You are currently at B2 or early C1
  • C1 certificate meets your needs
  • You have 3-6 months to prepare
  • You want a more achievable first goal

Choose CPE If...

  • You passed CAE with Grade A or B
  • You specifically need C2 certification
  • You have 6-12 months to prepare
  • You read English literature regularly

There is No Shame in Starting with CAE

Many successful CPE candidates took CAE first. Getting a strong CAE result builds confidence, proves your level, and gives you a certificate while you continue preparing for CPE. It is a strategic path, not a step backward.
