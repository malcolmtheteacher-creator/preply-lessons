What is marriage in Christian teaching?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Permanent covenant between man and woman. Two purposes: unitive (bonding) and procreative (children). Sacrament in Catholic teaching; lifelong commitment. Mark 10:9: "What God has joined, let no one separate."

What is Nikah in Islamic teaching?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Marriage contract (Ijab and Qabul). Legal binding agreement requiring consent from both parties. Half of faith (Hadith). Not a sacrament but divinely ordained institution for companionship and procreation.

What is the sanctity of life and how do religions justify it?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Human life is sacred and inviolable. Christianity: imago Dei (image of God, Genesis 1:27). Islam: created by Allah (Quran 15:29). Foundation for opposition to abortion, euthanasia, capital punishment.

When does Islamic law permit abortion?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Before 120 days of pregnancy (ensoulment per Quran 23:14). Most scholars allow on health/wellbeing grounds. After 120 days: only to save mother life. Shows balance between fetal and maternal rights.

What is Just War theory and its key criteria?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Christian framework evaluating whether war is morally justified. Criteria: right authority, just cause, right intention, last resort, reasonable success, proportionality, discrimination (protect civilians).

What is Jihad in Islamic teaching?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Two types: greater jihad (inner spiritual struggle against ego/sin), lesser jihad (armed defense of faith). Lesser jihad justified only under specific conditions: legitimate authority, just cause, last resort, protect non-combatants.

What are the four purposes of punishment?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Retribution (offender deserves punishment), Deterrence (prevent future crime), Reform (rehabilitation), Protection (incapacitate dangerous offender). Different purposes emphasize different goals of justice.

What is Qisas and Diyah in Islamic law?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Qisas: retaliatory justice (life for life). Diyah: blood money/compensation. Victim family may choose Diyah instead of Qisas. Combines justice with mercy and allows for forgiveness.

What do religions teach about forgiveness?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Christian: Lord Prayer asks forgiveness; Jesus commands forgive "seventy times seven" (unlimited). Islamic: Surah 42:40 - forgiveness superior to retaliation. Both emphasize restoration of relationships.

What are common errors in exam answers?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Only one perspective (need two), no examples, vague language, poor spelling of key terms (Muhammad, Quran, Shariah), not addressing specific question, insufficient detail on high-mark questions, too much on low-mark questions.

What is environmental stewardship and how do religions frame it?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Humans care for creation as God trustees. Christianity: dominion = responsible management (Genesis 2:15). Islam: Khalifah (trustee) role (Quran 2:30). Both condemn exploitation; stewardship is moral duty.

Theme A: Relationships & Families

Core topics: Marriage (sacrament vs Nikah), divorce (indissoluble vs Talaq), contraception (Humanae Vitae vs Islamic permission), sexual ethics (zina), gender roles (Ephesians 5 vs Quran 4:34), gender equality.

Key teaching points: Christian emphasis on lifelong commitment and procreation. Islamic emphasis on consent, mutual rights, and flexibility. Modern discussions of LGBTQ+, remarriage, spousal equality.

Theme B: Religion & Life

Core topics: Creation (Genesis 1-2 vs Quranic account), sanctity of life (imago Dei), abortion (conception vs 120 days), euthanasia (doctrine of double effect), afterlife (heaven/hell vs Akhirah/Barzakh), environmental ethics (stewardship vs Khalifah).

Key teaching points: Both traditions emphasize life as sacred but differ on when it begins. Christianity increasingly opposes capital punishment. Environmental crisis as moral/spiritual issue. Varying perspectives on withdrawal of treatment.

Theme D: Religion, Peace & Conflict

Core topics: Just War theory (Aquinas criteria), Holy War/Jihad (greater and lesser), pacifism (Quakers), WMDs, terrorism, refugees, peace organizations (Christian Aid, Islamic Relief).

Key teaching points: Both traditions attempt ethical frameworks for conflict. Protection of non-combatants (civilians) critical. Pacifism offers alternative to Just War. Extremism misuses religious concepts. Peace building through development and justice.

Theme E: Religion, Crime & Punishment

Core topics: Causes of crime, four purposes of punishment (retribution, deterrence, reform, protection), capital punishment (Catholic abolition), Qisas and Diyah, forgiveness (Lord Prayer, Surah 42:40), Shariah law (Hudud and Tazir).

Key teaching points: Religious perspectives emphasize moral development and second chances. Modern trend toward rehabilitation over retribution. Reconciliation theology (Truth and Reconciliation Commission). Islamic law offers flexibility through Diyah and judge discretion in Tazir.

Christian Perspective Summary

Distinctive emphases: Sanctity of life (imago Dei), forgiveness and redemption, love command, stewardship of creation, evolution toward mercy (less retribution, more reform).

Key figures: Jesus (Sermon on Mount), Pope Francis (ecological crisis), Bishop Tutu (reconciliation), Augustine and Aquinas (just war).

Modern tensions: LGBTQ+ inclusion, women ordination, capital punishment abolition, environmental response, contraception acceptance.

Islamic Perspective Summary

Distinctive emphases: Tawhid (God unity and authority), Khalifah (trusteeship), justice and mercy in balance, community harmony, divine law (Shariah) as complete system.

Key sources: Quran, Sunnah (prophetic tradition), Ijma (scholarly consensus), Qiyas (analogy). Allows for flexibility through Ijtihad (interpretation).

Modern diversity: Reformist vs traditionalist readings, varying applications of Shariah, women rights debates, terrorism condemnation by mainstream scholars.

4-Mark Explanation Question

Q3. Explain why contraception is controversial in Christianity. [4 marks]

Model answer (4 marks):

"The Roman Catholic Church teaches contraception is immoral based on Humanae Vitae (1968) [teaching identified - 1 mark]. This is because contraception separates the procreative (child-bearing) purpose from the unitive (bonding) purpose of sexual relations [reason explained - 1 mark]. The Church argues that sexual relations within marriage should be open to procreation and contraception artificially closes this possibility [example - 1 mark]. However, many modern Catholics use contraception and some Protestant churches permit it, showing the teaching is controversial and not universally accepted in practice [significance - 1 mark]."

6-Mark Comparison Question

Q4. Compare Christian and Islamic teaching on abortion. [6 marks]

Model structure: Catholic Christianity teaches absolute opposition from conception; theologically, life is sacred as imago Dei. Islam permits abortion before 120 days (ensoulment) if mother health at risk or severe abnormality. Christianity emphasizes sanctity from conception; Islam balances fetal potential with maternal wellbeing. Both traditions value life highly but differ on ensoulment and circumstances. [Each perspective 2-3 marks, comparison 1 mark = 6 marks total]

12-Mark Evaluation Question

Q5. "Religious belief in the sanctity of life makes it impossible for believers to support capital punishment." Evaluate this statement using Christian and Islamic perspectives. [12 marks]

Mark allocation: For statement (3-4 marks): traditional Christian and Islamic teaching allowed capital punishment for murder (Qisas). Supporting argument with explanation and example. Against statement (3-4 marks): modern religious opposition (Pope John Paul II, Evangelium Vitae; many Islamic scholars question Qisas). Analysis (2-3 marks): sanctity of life principle applied differently (redemption possible vs retributive justice). Conclusion (1-2 marks): sanctity of life alone does NOT determine stance; interpretation and context matter.

Example 12-Mark Answer (Full Retrieval Model)

Question: "The sanctity of life principle prevents believers from accepting abortion in all circumstances." Evaluate using Christian and Islamic perspectives. [12 marks]

Model Answer [Extract]: "Christian teaching emphasizes that human life is sacred, made in God image (imago Dei, Genesis 1:27). The Catholic Church therefore teaches that abortion is always immoral, even when mother life is at risk (Evangelium Vitae 1995). However, many Protestant theologians argue that the sanctity of life must be balanced with the mother right to life and health. For example, in cases where continuation of pregnancy threatens mother life or in extreme fetal abnormality, some Christians permit abortion despite sanctity principle. This shows that sanctity of life alone does NOT automatically prevent all abortion." [For argument: ~3-4 marks. Against: ~3-4 marks. Analysis: shows theological reasoning and values. Conclusion: nuanced view. Total: 12 marks]

4. Spelling religious terms wrongly (Qu'ran, Muhammad, Sharia)

5. Not directly addressing the question asked

6. Repetition without new analysis

7. Poor grammar and punctuation

8. Too much on 1-2 mark questions, too little on 12-mark
