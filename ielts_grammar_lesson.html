Subject-Verb Agreement

The Rule: Singular subjects take singular verbs. Plural subjects take plural verbs.
He walks. They walk. The student studies. The students study.

Tricky Words - These Are ALL Singular!

These words LOOK plural but need SINGULAR verbs:

  • Everyone - Everyone has a phone. (NOT "have")
  • Everybody - Everybody wants success. (NOT "want")
  • Someone - Someone is calling. (NOT "are")
  • Nobody - Nobody knows the answer. (NOT "know")
  • Each - Each student has a book. (NOT "have")
  • Every - Every country has problems. (NOT "have")

The "Of" Trap

Don't be fooled by words between the subject and verb!

"The cost of living expenses are high."

"The cost of living expenses is high."

The subject is "cost" (singular), not "expenses"!

Articles: A, An, The, or Nothing

A / AN

Use for:

  • First mention of something
  • One of many (not specific)
  • Jobs: "She is a teacher."

"I saw a movie."

"He is an engineer."

THE

Use for:

  • Specific thing (we both know which)
  • Second mention
  • Only one exists

"The movie was great."

"The sun is hot."

NO ARTICLE

Use for:

  • General concepts
  • Plural things in general
  • Most country names

"Education is important."

"People need jobs."

THE #1 IELTS Article Mistake

Using "the" with general concepts:

"The education is important."

"Education is important."

"The technology has changed our lives."

"Technology has changed our lives."

"The money cannot buy happiness."

"Money cannot buy happiness."

A or AN?

Use AN before vowel SOUNDS (not just vowel letters):

  • an apple, an example, an idea, an hour (silent h)
  • a university (sounds like "yoo"), a European (sounds like "yoo")
  • an honest person (silent h)

Fixing Run-on Sentences

What is a Run-on Sentence?

Two complete sentences joined together without proper punctuation or a connector.

"Many students study abroad it improves their careers."

This is TWO thoughts with nothing connecting them!

4 Ways to Fix Run-on Sentences

1. Use a Period

Make two separate sentences.

"Many students study abroad. It improves their careers."

2. Comma + FANBOYS

For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So

"Many students study abroad, and it improves their careers."

3. Semicolon

For closely related ideas.

"Many students study abroad; it improves their careers."

4. Subordinating Conjunction

Because, although, while, if, when

"Many students study abroad because it improves their careers."

Practice: Fix These Run-on Sentences

Click each sentence to see possible corrections.

1. "Cities are crowded many people still prefer them."
Possible fixes:
- Cities are crowded, but many people still prefer them.
- Although cities are crowded, many people still prefer them.
- Cities are crowded; however, many people still prefer them.
2. "Technology is useful it can be dangerous."
Possible fixes:
- Technology is useful, but it can be dangerous.
- Although technology is useful, it can be dangerous.
- Technology is useful. However, it can be dangerous.
3. "Students need money many take loans."
Possible fixes:
- Students need money, so many take loans.
- Because students need money, many take loans.
- Students need money; therefore, many take loans.
4. "Exercise is healthy people should do it daily."
Possible fixes:
- Exercise is healthy, so people should do it daily.
- Exercise is healthy. People should do it daily.
- Because exercise is healthy, people should do it daily.
5. "Learning English is difficult it opens many opportunities."
Possible fixes:
- Learning English is difficult, but it opens many opportunities.
- Although learning English is difficult, it opens many opportunities.
- Learning English is difficult; however, it opens many opportunities.
6. "The internet is everywhere we cannot live without it."
Possible fixes:
- The internet is everywhere, and we cannot live without it.
- The internet is everywhere; we cannot live without it.
- The internet is so common that we cannot live without it.

Using Connectors Correctly

ADDING (+ similar idea)

and, also, furthermore, moreover, in addition

"Education improves careers. Furthermore, it builds confidence."

CONTRASTING (opposite idea)

but, however, although, while, despite

"Cities are exciting. However, they can be stressful."

CAUSE/EFFECT (reason/result)

because, so, therefore, as a result, consequently

"She studied hard. Therefore, she passed."

THE BIGGEST CONNECTOR MISTAKE

Using two connectors that do the same job:

"Although it is expensive, BUT many people buy it."

Both "although" and "but" show contrast - you can only use ONE!

"Although it is expensive, many people buy it."

"It is expensive, but many people buy it."

More Double Connector Errors

"Because I was tired, so I went home." - WRONG

"Because I was tired, I went home." - CORRECT

"I was tired, so I went home." - CORRECT

"Despite it was raining, we went out." - WRONG

"Despite the rain, we went out." - CORRECT (despite + noun)

"Although it was raining, we went out." - CORRECT (although + clause)

What You've Learned:

  • Subject-Verb Agreement: Everyone/each/every = singular verb
