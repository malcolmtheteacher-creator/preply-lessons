Understanding Mission and Evangelism

What are Mission and Evangelism?

Mission: The Church's overarching calling to spread the Gospel and serve people in Jesus's name. Mission includes both evangelism and social action.

Evangelism: Sharing the Christian faith with others and inviting them to become Christians. This is a key part of mission.

For centuries, Christians have believed they are called to spread their faith. This comes from Jesus's final command.

Biblical Foundation

The Great Commission (Matthew 28:19-20):

"Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them... teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you."

Acts 1:8: "You will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon you... and you will be my witnesses to the ends of the earth."

These passages form the theological basis for Christian mission.

Evangelism vs. Social Action

Evangelism: Direct Conversion

Traditional evangelism focuses on converting people to Christianity.

  • Sharing personal testimony
  • Preaching and revivals
  • Door-to-door witnessing
  • Alpha Courses (introduction to Christianity)
  • Mission trips and overseas work

Evangelical churches particularly emphasize evangelism as a core practice.

Social Action: Broader Mission

Many Christians emphasize that mission includes social justice and practical help.

  • Working against poverty and injustice
  • Environmental action
  • Community development
  • Medical and educational missions
  • Advocating for human rights

Example: Christian Aid works to address root causes of poverty globally.

Key Distinction

Proselytizing means pressuring or manipulating people into converting. Evangelism is respectfully sharing faith and inviting belief.

Most Christian churches distinguish between respectful evangelism (which they affirm) and aggressive proselytizing (which many reject).

Mission in Practice

The Alpha Course

Alpha is an 11-week introduction to Christianity, run by thousands of churches worldwide. Participants attend dinners, watch videos, and discuss Christian faith in small groups.

Why it works: Creates a welcoming, non-threatening space. Low pressure. Allows people to ask questions.

Criticism: Some argue it represents a subtle form of evangelism targeting those in vulnerable situations (e.g., addiction recovery).

Christian perspective: Proponents see Alpha as honest evangelism—clearly Christian, but open and inclusive.

Missionary Work Globally

Thousands of Christian missionaries work in Africa, Asia, and Latin America. They combine:

  • Evangelism and church planting
  • Medical and educational services
  • Community development projects

Growth impact: Christianity is growing fastest in Africa and Asia, partly through missionary work.

Ethical questions: Do missionaries impose Western values? Do they respect local cultures? How do we balance evangelism and cultural respect?

Social Media Evangelism

Modern churches use YouTube, Instagram, and TikTok to share faith:

  • Testimonies and personal stories
  • Bible teaching and reflections
  • Discussion and community-building

Advantage: Reaches young people who don't attend church buildings.

Challenge: How do we balance authentic witness with performative social media culture?

Evangelism in a Multi-Faith Society

The tension: Should Christians actively try to convert people from other faiths?

Traditional view: Yes—Christianity is true, so sharing it is loving and necessary.

Pluralist view: No—all faiths have validity. Evangelism can be disrespectful and colonial.

Middle ground: Christians can respectfully share their faith without dismissing others' beliefs. Interfaith dialogue is possible.

Evaluate: Should Christians Try to Convert Others?

Essay question (12 marks): "Christians should not try to convert others." Evaluate this statement.

Essay Guidance

What to include:

  • Arguments FOR active evangelism (biblical mandate, love of neighbor, truth claims)
  • Arguments AGAINST (cultural disrespect, imperialism, multi-faith respect)
  • Distinction between evangelism and proselytizing
  • Reference to different Christian approaches (Evangelical vs. Catholic vs. pluralist)
  • Examples from Alpha, missionary work, or social media
  • A balanced conclusion

Structure: Introduction → Point 1 + evidence → Point 2 + evidence → Counterargument → Conclusion

Key Statements to Consider

  • "Jesus commanded us to go and make disciples. Evangelism is a core Christian practice."
  • "Evangelism in a multi-faith society is disrespectful and implies that other faiths are false."
  • "There's a difference between respectfully sharing your faith and aggressively trying to convert people."
  • "If you truly believe Christianity is true and saving, how can you not share it?"
