Christian Baptism and Confirmation

YEAR 8 · UNIT 3: RITES OF PASSAGE

What You'll Learn

You will explore Christian baptism (infant and believer's baptism) and confirmation, understanding the symbolism of water and the transition from childhood to adult commitment in Christianity.

Think: Water as Symbol

Reflection:

Why do many religions use water in their rituals? What does water symbolise? Think about cleansing, beginning anew, washing away, entering new life.

Encounter: Baptism

Baptism is a Christian sacrament (a sacred rite believed to convey grace) in which a person is immersed in, or has water sprinkled on, their head. The word "baptism" comes from Greek meaning "to dip" or "to immerse".

Two Types of Baptism

Infant Baptism

Parents bring their baby to church to be baptised. The church community welcomes the child into the Christian faith. Water is sprinkled on the child's forehead. Parents and godparents promise to raise the child in the faith.

Believer's Baptism

An older child or adult who has decided to follow Christ chooses baptism. They're typically fully immersed in water, symbolising death to their old life and resurrection to new life in Christ.

The Symbolism of Water

Water in baptism symbolises several things:

  • Cleansing: Washing away sin and guilt
  • Rebirth: Emerging from water as a new person, born into the faith
  • Death and Resurrection: Being submerged as a symbolic death to the old self; emerging as resurrection to new life
  • Journey: The waters of baptism are a journey into the Christian life
Theological meaning: In baptism, the person is united with Christ's death and resurrection. Their sins are forgiven, and they're incorporated into the Body of Christ (the Church).

Explore: Confirmation

Confirmation is a Christian sacrament in which a baptised person (usually a teenager) publicly affirms their Christian faith and receives the Holy Spirit (in Christian belief) to strengthen them for their Christian life. Confirmation marks the transition from childhood to adult commitment.

The Confirmation Rite

In confirmation, usually overseen by a bishop, the person is anointed with sacred oil (chrism) on the forehead. The bishop or priest says something like: "Be sealed with the Holy Spirit." The person may also receive a new name or reaffirm their baptismal vows.

Stages of Christian Initiation

Baptism (Separation)

The child/person is separated from secular life and welcomed into the Church. Water symbolises cleansing and rebirth.

Confirmation (Incorporation)

The adolescent/adult affirms their faith and publicly commits to the Christian life. Anointing with oil strengthens them for their journey ahead.

Theological understanding: Baptism initiates a person into the Church; confirmation is the person's mature affirmation that they choose to live as a Christian. Together, they constitute full initiation into Christian faith.

Evaluate: Ritual and Faith

The Power of Baptism and Confirmation

For Christians, baptism and confirmation are not just social ceremonies but sacraments—actions through which God's grace works. The rituals express and enact theological truths: cleansing from sin, death and resurrection with Christ, the gift of the Holy Spirit, and commitment to Christian discipleship.

Your reflection:

Do you think ritual actions can actually change a person spiritually, or are they mainly symbolic? Why does Christianity place such importance on these ceremonies?

Review: Key Concepts

Baptism

A Christian sacrament in which a person is immersed in or sprinkled with water, symbolising cleansing, rebirth, and incorporation into the Church. Can be performed on infants or believing adults.

Confirmation

A Christian sacrament in which a baptised person (usually a teenager) publicly affirms their faith and receives the Holy Spirit. Marks the transition from childhood baptism to adult commitment.

Water Symbolism

Water symbolises cleansing from sin, rebirth, death and resurrection with Christ, and the journey into Christian life.

Central insight: Baptism and confirmation are Christian rites of passage that mark both incorporation into the church and personal commitment to the Christian faith. They transform the person's spiritual and social identity.
