First Day at Work

New building, new faces, new coffee machine you don't understand yet. Time to walk in like you belong.

The Scene 🎬

It's 8:47 on a Monday morning and you're standing in a lobby you've only seen in a job advert, holding a lanyard that still smells of the printer. This is it: day one of the new job. You got here twenty minutes early because the thought of being late made you feel slightly sick.

Nobody expects you to know anything yet — but everybody's forming a first impression, whether they mean to or not. The trick today isn't to look like an expert. It's to seem warm, willing and easy to work with, and to ask for the help you genuinely need without sounding like you're drowning on your first morning.

Heading over to meet you: a colleague who's been here years, remembers exactly how their own first day felt, and has been asked to help you settle in. Get this right, and by lunchtime you'll have an ally.

Warm up — talk for a minute or two

  1. Think of a first day you'll never forget — a job, a school, a country. What made it go well, or go horribly? What did you wish someone had told you beforehand?
  2. On a first day, would you rather ask "silly" questions straight away and risk looking clueless, or quietly work things out yourself and risk getting them wrong? Which is really the braver choice?

Who's who

You
The New Starter 🪪

Nervous but hiding it well. Keen to make a good impression, useful rather than helpless, and to get through day one without breaking anything — including the coffee machine.

The other voice
The Colleague 🤝

Been here years, seen a dozen first days, quietly kind. Knows every unwritten rule, every shortcut, and exactly which questions you're too shy to ask.

Next: pick up the phrases you'll need in the next tab, then watch them at work in a real conversation. Don't just notice what the new starter asks — watch how they ask for help so it sounds keen, not lost. That's the whole game today.

When you're the new starter

👋 Introduce yourself
"I don't think we've met. I'm Sam, I've just joined the team."
"I'm still finding my feet."
🙏 Ask for help
"I don't suppose you could point me in the right direction?"
"Could you talk me through how it works?"
🔍 Show you tried first
"I've had a good look, but I can't for the life of me find the stationery. Any idea where it lives?"
"I think I've worked out the printer, but not the coffee machine."
😊 Share how it is going
"So far so good. Everyone's been really welcoming, to be honest."
"It's a lot to take in, but I'm enjoying it."
💬 Show interest
"How long have you been here, then?"
"What do you do on the team?"
🌟 Offer and thank
"Is there anything I can take off your plate?"
"I'll let you get on. Thanks so much for taking me under your wing today."

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one role each, then swap. Spot as many phrases from your toolkit as you can. Listen for how the new starter turns every "I don't know" into a warm, confident question.

ColleagueYou must be our new starter! I'm Priya — I sit just across from you. How was the journey in? Not too grim, I hope?
YouHi Priya, lovely to meet you — I'm Sam. The journey was fine, honestly; I left ridiculously early because I was terrified of being late on day one. I don't think I've ever been so punctual in my life.
ColleagueHa — everyone does that on the first day, then never again. Come on, I'll walk you over to your desk. They've put you right next to the good coffee machine, so you've already won at life.
YouThat's the dream. I'll warn you now — I'm still finding my feet, so do bear with me if I ask a hundred questions before lunch.
ColleagueAsk away, honestly — I'd much rather that than have you quietly panicking over there. Right, this is you. Laptop's on the desk; IT set your login up this morning.
YouBrilliant. Actually — while you're here — I don't suppose you could point me in the right direction for logging in? I've got a password but no idea what I'm meant to open first.
ColleagueCourse. Click that blue icon, pop your password in, and it'll walk you through the rest. If it throws a wobbly, give IT a shout — fourth floor, and surprisingly friendly for IT.
YouNoted. And — sorry to be a pain — could you talk me through how the coffee machine works before I humiliate myself in front of the whole floor?
ColleagueNow that's a sensible priority. It's temperamental: you have to hold the button, not press it. Trade secret. Oh — speaking of which, a few of us grab lunch around one, if you fancy joining?
YouI'd love that, thank you — saves me eating a sad sandwich alone at my desk on day one. How long have you been here yourself, then?
ColleagueSix years, believe it or not. Started right where you're sitting, funnily enough. It grows on you. Heads up — Dan, the manager, likes a quick hello with new people around eleven. Nothing scary.
YouGood to know — thanks for the warning. In the meantime, is there anything I can take off your plate this morning? I'd honestly rather be useful than sit here looking lost.
ColleagueKeen! I like it. Tell you what — have a read through the shared handbook for now, and I'll grab you the second there's something real to get your teeth into. Don't burn out before lunch.
YouDeal. Well — thank you so much for taking me under your wing today, Priya. I was dreading walking in this morning, and you've made it completely painless.
ColleagueThat's what I'm here for. Welcome aboard — and remember: hold the button, don't press it. That's the only rule that really matters around here.

From memory — no peeking back up!

1. Sam admits to two small nerves in the first few lines — turning up far too early and being braced for a hundred questions. How does Sam say these things so that they sound charming and self-aware rather than anxious?

2. Priya volunteers two "heads up" warnings that Sam never actually asks for. What are they — and what do they tell you about the kind of colleague Priya is?

3. Near the end, Sam offers to help rather than only asking for help. Why is that a clever move on a first morning — and how does it change the impression Sam leaves?

1. Sam owns the nerves out loud and makes a joke of them — "I don't think I've ever been so punctual in my life", and warning Priya about "a hundred questions" with a smile. Naming your own nervousness lightly is disarming; it reads as honest and easy-going rather than helpless.
2. The coffee machine trick (hold the button, don't press it) and Dan the manager's eleven o'clock hello. Offering useful information nobody asked for is exactly what a warm, generous colleague does — she's smoothing Sam's path before Sam even hits the bumps.
3. Asking "is there anything I can take off your plate?" flips Sam from a burden into a helper. On day one, willingness matters more than skill — it signals initiative and good will, and it leaves Priya thinking "this one's going to be easy to work with".

The Echo 🕰️

Around 210 BC, the first emperor of China was so anxious about his own big new start — the one into the afterlife — that he had an entire army built to report for duty alongside him. Around 8,000 life-size soldiers in clay, each with a different face, buried in silent ranks near the city of Xi'an: the greatest first-day preparation in history.

Every one of them a new recruit, standing to attention, waiting for orders that never came — because they were only rediscovered in 1974, by farmers digging a well. Two thousand years, and still turning up on time.

You've got it easier. No clay army, no eternity — just a login, a lunch invitation, and a coffee machine to master. And unlike the emperor's soldiers, at least someone's shown you where the button is.

Two bigger questions

  1. The emperor prepared for the afterlife with 8,000 soldiers, yet you can't prepare for a first day much beyond turning up early and being pleasant. Why is it that some of the most important "first days" are the ones we can't really rehearse for?
  2. Every terracotta soldier has a different face, as if each mattered as an individual — yet they were built to serve one man forever. When you join a new workplace, how do you stay yourself while fitting into someone else's ranks?
