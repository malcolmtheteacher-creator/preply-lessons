Credibility Characteristics

These qualities demonstrate credibility in professional contexts. Click to flip:

authenticity being genuine and true to yourself

decisiveness ability to make clear decisions confidently
expertise advanced knowledge or skill in a field
honesty telling the truth and being transparent
humility modesty; acknowledging what you don't know
integrity moral principles; doing the right thing
responsibility being accountable for your actions
sincerity genuine honesty in your words and actions
trustworthiness reliability; being someone others can depend on

Understanding Your Reputation

To build credibility, you need to understand how others perceive you. Consider these reflection questions:

Self-Assessment

1. Who are your key stakeholders? Supervisors, colleagues, clients, team members, mentors, peers in your industry

2. How often do you communicate with them and how? Regular meetings, email, presentations, one-on-ones, group discussions, social media, networking events

3. What words have they used to describe you as a person? Thoughtful, energetic, reliable, creative, calm, organized, collaborative, visionary

4. What additional tasks do people ask you to do? Leading projects, mentoring, presenting, problem-solving, strategic planning, training others

The SPARK Strategy

Use these five techniques to raise your profile and strengthen your credibility with stakeholders:

S - Speak Up

Contribute meaningfully to discussions and meetings. Ask thoughtful questions. Share your perspective when you have valuable input. Be confident but not dominating.

Tips: Prepare talking points. Listen actively before speaking. Make eye contact. Use positive body language.

P - Promote Yourself

Share your achievements and accomplishments appropriately. Let others know about your success without seeming boastful. Update your stakeholders on completed projects.

Tips: Use email updates. Reference past successes in relevant contexts. Accept compliments gracefully. Document your work.

A - Ask

Seek opportunities to take on more responsibility. Ask for feedback to improve. Request to be involved in important decisions or projects. Show your ambition appropriately.

Tips: Ask for stretch assignments. Request mentoring. Seek feedback regularly. Show genuine interest in learning.

R - Respond

Follow through on commitments. Be reliable and dependable. Respond promptly to requests and communications. Deliver quality work consistently.

Tips: Meet deadlines. Communicate proactively. Admit mistakes and fix them. Keep stakeholders informed.

K - Know When to Contribute

Understand context and timing. Contribute when your input adds value. Recognize when to lead and when to support. Show discretion and judgment.

Tips: Read the room. Ask yourself if your input is needed. Consider the broader context. Show strategic thinking.
