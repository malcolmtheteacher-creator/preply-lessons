Key words

jettison (verb) — throw something out of a boat or plane to make it lighter. Example: "The crew had to jettison half the cargo to stay afloat."
overboard (adverb) — over the side of a boat and into the water. Example: "A wave washed two boxes overboard during the night."
expendable (adjective) — not needed enough to keep; able to be given up. Example: "In the end we agreed that the suitcase was expendable."
indispensable (adjective) — so important that you cannot manage without it. Example: "Fresh water is indispensable on any long voyage."
tarpaulin (noun) — a large sheet of strong waterproof material. Example: "They stretched a tarpaulin over the boat to keep the sun off."
flare (noun) — a stick that burns with a very bright light, used to call for help. Example: "She lit a flare as soon as she saw the ship."
drift (verb) — be carried slowly by water or wind, without control. Example: "With no engine, the boat began to drift away from the coast."
ration (verb) — allow only a small, fixed amount of something because there is not much of it. Example: "We had to ration the water to one cup each a day."
hindsight (noun) — understanding of a situation that comes only after it has happened. Example: "With hindsight, throwing away the rope was a mistake."
stranded (adjective) — stuck in a place with no way of leaving. Example: "The storm left hundreds of passengers stranded on the island."
