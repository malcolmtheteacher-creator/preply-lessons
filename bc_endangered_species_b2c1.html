Endangered Species

Explore the world's vulnerable wildlife and argue for conservation

Let's Start

What endangered species do you know? Think about animals you've seen in documentaries or news stories.
What threats do animals face today? Consider habitat loss, climate change, hunting, and pollution.
Can we reverse extinction? What role can humans play in saving species?

Phrases for Persuasion

Persuasive language helps you make a compelling argument. Look at these examples:

  • 1. Create vivid imagery: "The majestic snow leopard roams through the harshest winter known to man..."
  • 2. Highlight distinctive qualities: "The shy, retiring orangutan is recognizable by its characteristic colouring, high intelligence and endearing features."
  • 3. Appeal to emotions: "The gentle giant of the sub-tropical rainforests faces extinction on an unprecedented scale."
  • 4. Explain the threat: "It faces a two-fold threat, primarily from illegal and unsustainable logging..."
  • 5. Call for action: "Are you willing to stand idly by and watch the dramatic decline of both these species?"
  • 6. Empower your audience: "Only with your help can this be halted."
  • 7. Make the request specific: "We urgently need donations to fund the ongoing conservation projects."
  • 8. End with a clear call: "Please call... to preserve these animals for future generations."

Your Task: Advocate for a Species

Your school is adopting one endangered species as its conservation focus. Your job is to argue in favour of your chosen animal. This means you need to convince others that your species is the best choice.

Criteria for Adoption

Before you choose, consider these factors:

Conservation Potential
  • Does this species have a realistic chance of recovery with support?
  • Are there active conservation projects already underway?
Educational Value
  • Does this species teach us about broader environmental issues?
  • Is it interesting and engaging for your school community?
Feasibility
  • Can you fundraise for this animal's conservation?
  • Are there organizations already working to protect it?

Preparation Framework

Use this structure to build your argument:

1. Introduction & Emotion
  • Introduce your species with a vivid, memorable opening
  • Describe its endearing features and iconic status
2. Current Situation
  • Explain its conservation status (critically endangered, endangered, vulnerable)
  • Detail the specific threats it faces (habitat loss, hunting, climate change, etc.)
3. Why It Matters
  • Connect it to larger environmental issues your school cares about
  • Explain what we learn from protecting this species
4. Call to Action
  • Make a direct appeal using persuasive language
  • Explain exactly what your school can do to help

Your Presentation

When you're ready, present your argument to the group. Remember:

Speak, don't read. Use notes, but deliver with confidence and passion.
Use the collocations. Refer back to Tab 2 if you need vocabulary.
Tell a story. Make your audience care about this animal emotionally.
Be specific. Include facts, numbers, and concrete examples.

After All Presentations

The group will vote on which species your school should adopt. Vote based on which argument was most convincing and which species you think deserves your school's support.
