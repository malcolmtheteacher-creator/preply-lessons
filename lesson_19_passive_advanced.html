📰 The Power of Passive in News

When BBC reporter Sarah Chen covers crime stories, she almost never says "Police caught the thief." Instead, she says "The suspect has been apprehended." Why? Because passive voice:

  • Sounds more professional and neutral
  • Focuses on WHAT happened, not WHO did it
  • Is perfect when we don't know who did something
  • Makes information sound more authoritative

📚 Advanced Passive Structures

🎯 Three New Passive Patterns

In Lesson 18, you learned basic passive. Today, let's level up with more sophisticated structures used in news and formal English!

Structure Example When to Use
Modal + be + past participle The thief could be arrested tonight. Possibility, ability, obligation
Present Perfect Passive Three suspects have been questioned. Recent events, results
It is + past participle + that It is believed that he fled the country. Rumors, reports, opinions

Modal Passive Form Example

🗣️ 5 Essential News & Formal Expressions

👆 Click each card to reveal details and practice!

1. "It is believed that..." / "...is believed to be..."
Formality: Formal (news/reports)
Use this when reporting what people think or suspect, without saying who exactly thinks it. Perfect for rumors, theories, and unconfirmed information.
🔹 "It is believed that the painting was stolen by insiders."
🔹 "The suspect is believed to be armed and dangerous."
NOW YOU TRY: Think of a famous unsolved mystery (Bermuda Triangle, Loch Ness, etc.). Report one theory using "It is believed that..."
2. "...has been confirmed/announced/reported"
Formality: Formal (official statements)
Use these present perfect passive forms for recent official news or developments. Very common in news bulletins.
🔹 "It has been confirmed that the CEO will resign."
🔹 "A new policy has been announced by the government."
NOW YOU TRY: Create a "breaking news" announcement about something at your school/workplace using one of these verbs.
3. "...must/should/can be + past participle"
Formality: Neutral to Formal
Modal passives express rules, advice, and possibilities without saying who should do the action. Common in instructions and formal situations.
🔹 "All evidence must be handled with gloves."
🔹 "Suspects should be considered innocent until proven guilty."
NOW YOU TRY: What rules MUST be followed in your country? Tell your teacher 3 rules using "must be..."
4. "...is said to be..." / "...are thought to be..."
Formality: Formal (journalism)
Another way to report opinions or beliefs without stating who holds them. The structure is: Subject + is/are + said/thought/known/reported + to + verb.
🔹 "The diamond is said to be worth $50 million."
🔹 "The hackers are thought to be based in Eastern Europe."
NOW YOU TRY: Think of a famous person. What are they "said to be" or "known to be"? (e.g., "Elon Musk is said to work 100 hours a week.")
5. "...have/has not been found/recovered/solved"
Formality: Neutral to Formal
Perfect passive negative - used when something hasn't happened yet, often for ongoing investigations or missing items.
🔹 "The stolen artwork has not been recovered."
🔹 "The murder has never been solved."
NOW YOU TRY: Think of something famous that "has never been found" or "has not been explained" (Amelia Earhart, Malaysia Airlines MH370, etc.)
