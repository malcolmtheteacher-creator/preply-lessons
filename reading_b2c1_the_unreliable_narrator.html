Key words

narrator (noun) — the person or voice that tells a story. Example: "The narrator of the novel is a ten-year-old boy."
unreliable (adjective) — not able to be trusted or believed. Example: "He is an unreliable witness: his story changes every time."
lodger (noun) — a person who pays to live in a room in somebody else's house. Example: "She took in a lodger to help pay the bills."
account (noun) — a spoken or written description of something that happened. Example: "The two drivers gave very different accounts of the accident."
insist (verb) — say firmly that something is true, especially when others doubt it. Example: "She continued to insist that she had been at home all evening."
contradict (verb) — say or show the opposite of something, so that both cannot be true. Example: "The muddy boots contradict her story about staying indoors."
imply (verb) — suggest that something is true without saying it directly. Example: "The empty hanger seems to imply that a coat has been taken."
infer (verb) — reach an opinion from the facts you have, although nobody has told you directly. Example: "From the two glasses we can infer that she was not alone."
conceal (verb) — hide something, or keep it secret. Example: "He tried to conceal the letter under a pile of books."
evidence (noun) — facts or objects that help to show what is true. Example: "There was no evidence that anybody had slept in the bed."
