Ordering Food

A2 English Lesson | Restaurant & Food Vocabulary

Welcome to the Restaurant!

Let's start with a question:

Do you like trying new food when you travel? Why or why not?

Some people love discovering new cuisines because it's exciting and gives them a real taste of the culture. Others prefer familiar food because they know what to expect. Both approaches are valid!

Explore Different Cuisines

Here are some international dishes you might order at a restaurant. Do you know any of these?

Sushi (Japanese) - Rice and fish, often rolled with seaweed
Satay (Indonesian/Thai) - Grilled meat on skewers with peanut sauce
Pho (Vietnamese) - A noodle soup with broth and meat
Gado Gado (Indonesian) - Mixed vegetables with peanut sauce
Dim Sum (Chinese) - Small dishes served in baskets or carts
Pajeon (Korean) - Vegetable or seafood pancakes

Which of these would you order if you were on holiday?

You might choose based on what you already know you like, or be brave and try something completely new! In a restaurant, asking the server for help is always an option.

Saying Prices Clearly

Pronunciation Focus: Stress in Prices

When you say a price in English, the first number usually gets the stress. Listen carefully to how each price is pronounced:

£1.75 = "One pound SEVenty-five" (stress on "sev")
£7.90 = "Seven pound NINety" (stress on "nin")
£10.70 = "Ten pound SEVenty" (stress on "sev")
£3.85 = "Three pound EIghty-five" (stress on "ei")
£4.20 = "Four pound TWENty" (stress on "twen")
£7.35 = "Seven pound THIRty-five" (stress on "thir")

Try It Out

Can you say these prices aloud? Remember: stress the first number of the decimal part.

Prepare for Role Play

You'll role play ordering food in a restaurant. There are two roles: Customer and Server. Here's how to prepare:

If You're the Customer:

Customer Preparation

Read the Server's questions below and write down your answers. Use words and phrases from this lesson!

  • "What can I get you?" → Use: "I'd like..."
  • "Anything else?" → Use: "Um... I'll have..."
  • "Any drinks?" → Use: "Yes... / No..."
  • "So that's ...?" → Confirm: "Yes, that's right" / "No, I'll have..."

Practice Questions You Might Ask:

  • "Do you have...?"
  • "How much is a/an...?"
  • "Excuse me, could I have another...?"
  • "Could I have the...?"

If You're the Server:

Server Preparation

Prepare answers to these customer questions. Use words and phrases from this lesson!

  • "Do you have...?" → Answer: "Yes, we have... / No, sorry we don't."
  • "How much is a/an...?" → Answer: "That's £... / The ... is £..."
  • "Excuse me, could I have another...?" → Answer: "Of course! / Sure thing!"
  • "Could I have the...?" → Answer: "Absolutely! / Coming right up!"

Example Answers You Might Give:

  • "We have sushi, satay, and pho."
  • "The fish is £12.50 and the sushi is £14.99."
  • "Would you like a drink with that?"

Your Turn to Order!

How the Role Play Works

You will have a conversation where one of you is the Customer and one is the Server. Then you'll swap roles so you both get to practice both parts.

Use the language you've learned from this lesson. Don't worry about being perfect — focus on communication!

Quick Language Reference

Server says:

  • "What can I get you?"
  • "Anything else?"
  • "Any drinks?"
  • "So that's...?"

Customer says:

  • "I'd like..."
  • "I'll have..."
  • "Yes... / No, that's all."
  • "Yes, that's right."
  • "Do you have...?"
  • "How much is...?"
  • "Could I have...?"

Tips for Success

  • Use the language from this lesson, but feel free to add your own ideas
  • Speak clearly, especially when saying prices
  • If you get stuck, it's okay to ask for help or repeat yourself
  • Have fun with it! A real restaurant conversation is natural and relaxed
  • After the first role play, swap roles and do it again

Key Phrases to Remember

"I'd like the..." — Use this to order food
"How much is a/an...?" — Ask about prices
"Could I have...?" — Polite way to ask for something
"Anything else?" — The server asks this to see if you want more
"That's all, thanks!" — Use this to say you're finished ordering
