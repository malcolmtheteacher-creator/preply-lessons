Below are key facts and concepts from Lessons 1-5. Try to recall before revealing the answer.

What is the purpose of marriage in Christian theology?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Christian marriage is a covenant with God emphasising spiritual unity, procreation, and mutual sacrificial love. It is ideally lifelong and sacramental.

What are the key components of Islamic marriage (Nikkah)?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Ijab (offer) and Qabul (acceptance), Mahr (gift from groom), witnesses from the community, and mutual consent.

What does Galatians 3:28 say about gender?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: "There is neither male nor female... in Christ Jesus." This is cited by egalitarian Christians to argue for gender equality.

What does Surah 4:34 teach about men and women?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: "Men are guardians (Qawwamun) over women." Traditional interpretation: male authority. Progressive: applies to specific contexts; requires consultation and kindness.

What is Imago Dei?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: "Image of God" (Genesis 1:27) — humans are made in God's image and have unique spiritual dignity and worth.

What is the concept of ensoulment in Islam?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The moment when the soul (Ruh) enters the human body. Islamic scholars traditionally place this at 120 days gestation, affecting views on abortion.

What is the Catholic position on contraception?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The Catholic Church forbids artificial contraception because every sexual act must remain open to procreation. Only Natural Family Planning (rhythm method) is permitted.

What does Islam teach about divorce (Talaq)?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Divorce is legally permissible but morally disliked. Hadith: "Of all things permitted, divorce is the most disliked by Allah." There is a waiting period (Iddah) for reconciliation.

What do Christians mean by "theistic evolution"?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The belief that God created life through the evolutionary process. God is the Creator; evolution is the mechanism. This allows faith and science to coexist.

What is the Christian concept of Heaven?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Heaven is eternal communion with God; a state/place of perfect peace, joy, and God's presence—the reward for the righteous.

What is Jannah in Islamic theology?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Jannah (Paradise) is the Islamic concept of Heaven—reward for the righteous, described with gardens, rivers, spiritual pleasures, and closeness to Allah.

What is the Catholic teaching on euthanasia?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Active euthanasia is forbidden (only God determines death). However, palliative care and withdrawal of futile treatment are permissible.

Content Review: Key Themes

Theme A: Relationships and Families (Lessons 1-3)

Marriage Teachings

Christian: Covenant with God; permanent; procreative and unitive.

Islamic: Contract (Nikkah); witnessed; regulated; more permissive on dissolution.

Sexuality and Contraception

Christian: Sex within marriage only; Catholic tradition forbids contraception; Protestants accept it.

Islamic: Sex within marriage; mutual pleasure recognised; contraception generally permitted with consent.

Homosexuality

Traditional: Condemned in both religions based on scripture.

Progressive: Both traditions have reinterpreters who emphasise love, consent, and inclusion.

Gender and Women's Roles

Complementarianism: Equal value but different roles (traditional in both traditions).

Egalitarianism: Full equality and shared leadership (progressive position in both).

Ordination: Catholic/Orthodox: No female priests. Many Protestants: Ordain women fully.

Theme B: Religion and Life (Lessons 4-5)

Creation and Origins

Christian: Genesis accounts (literal or metaphorical); Imago Dei; humans created in God's image.

Islamic: Surah 21:30, 15:28-29; humans as Khalifah (vicegerent); Allah creates directly.

Science: Theistic evolution widely accepted; Big Bang compatible with creation theology.

Abortion

Catholic: Life begins at conception; abortion always wrong.

Progressive Protestant: Personhood develops; early abortion may be permissible.

Islamic: Before 120 days (ensoulment): more permissible. After: forbidden except to save mother's life.

Euthanasia and End-of-Life Care

Both traditions: Active euthanasia forbidden. Palliative care permitted. Withdrawal of futile treatment increasingly accepted.

Afterlife

Christian: Heaven, Hell, Judgment; Resurrection of body; (Catholic: Purgatory).

Islamic: Jannah, Jahannam, Barzakh, Yawm al-Qiyamah (Day of Judgment).

Test Questions: Apply Your Knowledge

Section A: Knowledge and Comprehension (Short answers)

Answer each question in 1-2 sentences, using specific references to scripture or Islamic sources.

Q1: According to Islamic teaching, what is the purpose of Nikkah (marriage)?

Answer: Nikkah is a contractual marriage creating a legal bond for companionship, procreation, and spiritual partnership. Surah 30:21 describes it as creating "tranquility" between spouses.

Q2: Explain the key difference between Christian marriage as a "covenant" and Islamic marriage as a "contract."

Answer: Christian covenant emphasises an eternal, spiritual bond with God as witness and sacred permanence. Islamic Nikkah is a legal contract with specified conditions, potentially terminable by Talaq (divorce).

Q3: What do progressive Christians argue about the creation accounts in Genesis?

Answer: Progressive Christians argue Genesis is theological (teaching God's creative power and human Imago Dei), not scientific; the "days" may represent ages; evolution could be God's mechanism.

Q4: What is the Islamic concept of ensoulment, and why is it important for abortion ethics?

Answer: Ensoulment is when Allah breathes the soul (Ruh) into the body at 120 days. This divides the pregnancy into stages: before ensoulment, abortion is more permissible; after, it is forbidden except for mother's life.

Q5: Compare Catholic and Protestant Christian views on contraception.

Answer: Catholicism forbids artificial contraception; every act must be open to procreation. Protestants generally accept contraception as responsible family planning and stewardship of resources.

Section B: Analysis and Application

Answer the following scenario-based questions in 2-3 paragraphs. Consider both Christian and Islamic perspectives and acknowledge diversity within traditions.

Scenario 1: A woman is diagnosed with a medical condition that makes pregnancy life-threatening. She is 8 weeks pregnant. How might Christian (Catholic, Protestant) and Islamic traditions respond to the question of abortion?

Answer: Catholic: Life at conception; direct abortion forbidden (but indirect abortion—treating mother's condition without intending fetal death—may be permitted). Protestant: Many would permit abortion to save mother's life; pregnancy is still early (before viability). Islamic: Before 120 days, preservation of mother's life and health is paramount; abortion would be permitted. All traditions prioritise mother's life, but disagree on personhood from conception.

Scenario 2: An elderly man is in a permanent vegetative state with no hope of recovery. His family wants to withdraw life support. How would Christian and Islamic traditions address this?

Answer: Christian: Palliative care is mandatory; withdrawal of futile life support is increasingly accepted (allowing natural death rather than prolonging suffering). Islamic: Prohibited from intentionally killing; however, withdrawing artificial support when there is no hope of recovery is increasingly accepted in Islamic bioethics. Both traditions distinguish between allowing death and causing death.

Unit 27 Summary: Themes A & B

Overarching Themes

1. Human Dignity and Sanctity: Both Christianity and Islam affirm that human life, sexuality, and relationships are sacred and reflect divine purpose.

2. Creation and Purpose: Humans are created in God's image (Christian Imago Dei) or as vicegerents (Islamic Khalifah); we have a divine calling to love, procreate, and maintain justice.

3. Diversity Within Traditions: Neither Christianity nor Islam is monolithic. Complementarians and egalitarians, traditionalists and progressives, conservatives and liberals grapple with these topics differently.

4. Scriptural Interpretation: How we read Genesis, the letters of Paul, and Qur'anic Surahs shapes our ethics. Context, history, and theological tradition all influence interpretation.

5. Science and Faith: Modern science (evolutionary biology, neuroscience, medicine) challenges and reshapes religious teaching without necessarily contradicting core theological principles.
