Your Phrase Toolkit

Every phrase you meet in the Modern Life role plays, gathered in one place and sorted not by situation but by what it does — so when you need to say no, be honest, or stay close, you know exactly where to reach.

How to use it. Don't read it like a list — raid it. Before your next real message, call or awkward conversation, find the job you need doing below, borrow the phrase, and say it out loud until it feels like yours. The same few moves — saying no kindly, telling the plain truth, heading off guilt, staying warm — work almost anywhere.

🫸 Getting ahead of an awkward conversation

Opening gently, before a plan or a problem hardens.

"Can I be totally honest with you for a sec?"
Asks permission to be honest and keeps it warm and personal — the other person leans in instead of bracing themselves.
The Group Chat
"…before this snowballs any further."
Flags that you're speaking up early, while things can still change — not derailing something already decided.
The Group Chat

🙅 Saying no — warmly and clearly

Declining this one thing without disappearing from the friendship.

"I'm going to have to sit this one out."
"Sit one out" = skip a single round, not quit for good. Warm, reluctant, and clearly just about this time.
The Group Chat
"The truth is, it's a bit out of my budget right now."
Owns the real reason plainly and lightly. A true reason beats an elaborate excuse almost every time.
The Group Chat

🛟 Heading off the guilt

Stopping people from "fixing" your no — and stopping it becoming a drama.

"Please don't let me hold anyone back."
Warm but firm — it actively protects everyone else's plan so your no doesn't reshape their day.
The Group Chat
"I'd hate for this to become a thing."
"A thing" = a drama everyone has to tiptoe around. Lightly asks the group to let it go, without a lecture.
The Group Chat

🤝 Turning a no into a smaller yes

Showing you're still in — just moving the plan, not skipping it.

"Here's what I can absolutely do…"
Bright and definite. Following a no with a confident yes proves you're still fully in the friendship.
The Group Chat

💛 Staying close — and a warm close

Making sure you're still one of the group, and ending on their joy, not your absence.

"Keep me in the loop for everything else, though."
"In the loop" = kept informed and included. Says "I'm out of the trip, not out of the friendship."
The Group Chat
"I'm genuinely made up for you — go and have the best time."
"Made up for you" is warm British English for thrilled for someone. Leaves them hearing your happiness for them.
The Group Chat

🫱 Raising something sensitive — gently

Opening a delicate subject so it doesn't sound like an attack.

"Can I raise something — and it's honestly not a criticism?"
Names what you're NOT doing (attacking), so they relax and listen instead of bracing to defend themselves.
The Screen-Time Talk
"Tiny favour — genuinely no big deal."
Shrinks a request down before you make it, so a boundary lands light rather than heavy.
A Photo of You

🪞 Owning your share & saying how you feel

Disarming defensiveness by taking some of it on yourself — and using "I feel", not "you always".

"I'm just as bad, I know."
Takes the blame off them and makes it a shared problem, not an accusation — nobody has to get defensive.
The Screen-Time Talk
"It's not about the phone — it's that I miss you."
Goes past the habit to the feeling underneath. "I miss you" can't be argued with the way "you're always on your phone" can.
The Screen-Time Talk

🎯 Kind, useful criticism

Honest feedback that helps rather than wounds — specific, balanced, and warm.

"Most of it was lovely — one thing let it down."
Balances the good with the off, so the criticism sounds fair, not like an attack. Then be specific about the one thing.
The Honest Review
"I only mention it because I want this place to do brilliantly."
Frames the criticism as support — you're on their side. Turns feedback from a complaint into a gift.
The Honest Review

🌉 Reconnecting & making it real

Crossing a long silence, and turning "we should catch up" into an actual plan.

"I know it's been way too long — that's on me as much as anyone."
Names the gap and owns your share without grovelling — no guilt-trip, no long apology.
Back in Touch
"Let's not do the 'we must catch up' thing and then not — are you free on the 20th?"
Kills the vague promise by naming a real date on the spot. The person who makes the plan keeps the friendship.
Back in Touch

🚧 Holding a boundary, warmly

Asking for what you need — and standing by it without a fight or a lecture.

"Would you mind taking that one down?"
A clear, direct request wrapped in "would you mind" — polite, but it actually asks for the thing.
A Photo of You
"It's nothing you did — I just keep my [face / the kids / my nights out] off there."
Explains your why simply, without a privacy lecture, and reassures them they haven't done anything wrong.
A Photo of You
"Honestly, no drama at all — I just prefer it that way."
Holds the boundary lightly if they push back. You don't owe a big justification for a simple preference.
A Photo of You
