🏃 Let's Learn: Action Verbs

These are the verbs you use to describe what you do every day. Here's a realistic daily routine:

🛏️
Wake up - stop sleeping, open your eyes in the morning
🚿
Shower - wash your body with water
🍽️
Eat breakfast - have your first meal of the day
💼
Go to work - travel to your workplace
💻
Work - do your job
🍽️
Have lunch - eat your midday meal
🏠
Come home - return to your house
🍳
Cook - prepare food
📺
Watch TV - look at television
😴
Go to bed - lie down to sleep
💡 Good to know: With "I", we use the simple form: "I wake up", "I eat", "I work" (NOT "I wakes" or "I eats")
💡 Good news: In English the verb barely changes: "I wake up", "you wake up", "we wake up", "they wake up" are all the same word. Only he / she / it adds an -s — "he wakes up".

🕐 Let's Learn: Time Expressions

To describe routines, you need words for WHEN you do things:

Morning / Afternoon / Evening / Night

  • "I wake up at 7 o'clock in the morning."
  • "I have lunch at noon / 12 o'clock."
  • "I work in the afternoon."
  • "I watch TV in the evening."

🗣️ How to Say It

Wake up = wayk UP (two words, stress the second)

Every = EV-ree or EV-uh-ree (two or three syllables)

Morning = MOR-ning (two syllables, short O)

Usually = YOO-zhuh-lee (three syllables, stress first one)

Breakfast = BREK-fust (not "break-fast"—sounds like one word)

Days of the Week

  • Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday
  • "I always work on Monday."
  • "I never work on Sunday."

Frequency Adverbs (How often?)

  • Always = every time, 100%
  • Usually = most times, ~80%
  • Often = many times, ~60%
  • Sometimes = occasionally, ~40%
  • Never = 0%, not at all
💡 Pattern: "I [frequency word] [verb]" → "I always wake up at 7" / "I never eat breakfast"
