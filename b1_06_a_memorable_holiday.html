A Memorable Holiday

B1 English Lesson | Describing & Asking Questions

Getting Started

Think About a Holiday

Think about a memorable holiday you have had. Consider these questions while you think:

  • Where did you go?
  • Who did you go with?
  • How long did you stay?
  • What was the weather like?
  • What activities did you do?
  • What made it memorable?

Holiday Topics

Let's explore different aspects of a holiday:

The Journey: Was it smooth, tiring, or long?
The Destination: Was it crowded, quiet, or beautiful?
The Food: Was it delicious, spicy, or disappointing?
The Weather: Was it boiling, wet, or perfect?
Accommodation: Was it luxurious, comfortable, or basic?
Overall Experience: Was it relaxing, wonderful, or educational?

Package holiday: Everything is organized and included (flight, hotel, activities)

Backpacking: Independent travel with minimal planning, staying in budget accommodation

Skiing holiday: Winter vacation focused on skiing in mountain regions

Beach holiday: Relaxation at a seaside destination

Exotic destination: Unusual, distant, or unfamiliar places

Asking Questions About Holidays

Key Questions to Ask

These questions help you learn more about someone's holiday:

How was the journey?
Use this to ask about the travel experience (flight, train, car, etc.)
Where did you go?
The most basic question about the destination
What did you visit?
Ask about specific places, attractions, or activities
Did you enjoy it?
A general question about their overall impression
How about the food?
Ask specifically about local cuisine and dining experiences
Did you go alone?
Ask if they travelled solo or with others
Did you like their culture?
Ask about their experience with the local customs and way of life

Practice Questions

Match the questions to appropriate follow-up responses:

Q: Where did you go? A: To Thailand.

Q: How was the journey? A: It was quite long, about 14 hours total.

Q: Did you enjoy it? A: Absolutely! It was really memorable.

Q: How about the food? A: Very authentic but sometimes too spicy for me.

Q: Did you go alone? A: No, I went with my family.

Q: What did you visit? A: The famous temples and some beautiful beaches.

Using Adverbs with Adjectives

Understanding Adverbs

Adverbs modify adjectives to show the degree or intensity of a quality.

Extreme adjectives (freezing, exhausted, amazing, terrible):

Use absolutely, really, incredibly, extremely

Example: "The weather was absolutely freezing."

Gradable adjectives (cold, good, tired, hot, crowded):

Use very, quite, pretty, extremely

Example: "The weather was very cold."

Choose the Right Adverb

Which adverbs would you use with these adjectives?

Hot

Very hot or extremely hot (gradable adjective)

Happy

Incredibly happy or really happy (can be both, but "really" works well)

Freezing

Absolutely freezing (extreme adjective)

Tired

Very tired or quite tired (gradable adjective)

Exhausted

Pretty exhausted or absolutely exhausted (extreme adjective - "absolutely" is best)

Complete the Sentences

We had a _________ smooth journey. We had no problems at all.

very smooth journey

The weather was _________ glorious. There were blue skies and sunshine all week.

absolutely glorious

The beach was _________ crowded. There was almost nowhere to sit.

very crowded

The hotel was _________ comfortable, but not luxurious.

quite comfortable

The service was _________ bad. The staff were _________ rude.

pretty bad. The staff were really rude.

The food was _________ delicious!

absolutely delicious

Speaking Practice: Describe Your Holiday

Preparation

Prepare to tell about a memorable holiday you have had. Think about:

The Journey: Was it smooth, tiring, or long?
The Food: Was it delicious, nothing special, or spicy?
Accommodation: Was it luxurious, impersonal, or run-down?
The Weather: Was it wet, awful, or boiling?
The Destination: Was it modern, quiet, or crowded?
Overall: Was it relaxing, wonderful, or enjoyable?

Useful Adverbs for Your Story

Absolutely / incredibly (for extreme adjectives)
Really (for most adjectives)
Very / quite / pretty / extremely (for gradable adjectives)

Timer for Your Story

Take 3 minutes to tell your holiday story. Use the questions to help structure your description.

3:00

Example Holiday Story

"Last summer, I went to Spain with my family. The journey was quite long, about 4 hours on a plane, but it was very smooth. We stayed in a beautiful coastal town for two weeks.

The weather was absolutely glorious—perfect beach weather. The accommodation was quite luxurious, which was wonderful. The food was incredibly delicious! We tried authentic Spanish dishes and paella every night.

The beach was very crowded in August, but we didn't mind. We visited some amazing historical sites and really enjoyed the local culture. Overall, it was an absolutely memorable holiday. I would definitely recommend it!"

Speaking Tips

Use complete sentences: "The weather was..." instead of just "Weather—hot"
Use adverbs: "It was very beautiful" is better than "It was beautiful"
Use past tense: "I went", "I stayed", "It was"
Give reasons: Explain WHY it was memorable
