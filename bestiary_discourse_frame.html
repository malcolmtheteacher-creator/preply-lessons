The Secret

The Problem

Abstract phrases like "I'd want to caveat that by saying..." are almost impossible to recall under pressure. In a meeting, a presentation, or an exam — your mind goes blank.

But your brain is exceptional at remembering vivid images and stories. That's the Bestiary system.

The Trick

Each expression has a key word. The first 2 letters of that word map to a creature. You build a vivid scene for each creature. Under pressure, you recall the creature — and the phrase comes with it.

The Sequence

These aren't in a strict order like the essay skeleton — they're five tools you can reach for independently. But learning them as a chain helps lock them in.

When to Use Each One
Numbat — "That's a nuanced question..." When someone asks something complex and you need a moment to think. Shows you're taking the question seriously.
Bat — "On balance, I'd say..." When you've weighed both sides and want to signal a considered position. Professional hedge that sounds decisive.
Cow — "It's conceivable that..." When you want to introduce a possibility without committing to it. Opens a door without walking through it.
Frogmouth — "Let me frame this another way..." When your first explanation didn't land, or you want to shift perspective. Recovery phrase.
Camel — "I'd want to caveat that by saying..." When you need to add a condition or exception. Protects your position while showing sophistication.
