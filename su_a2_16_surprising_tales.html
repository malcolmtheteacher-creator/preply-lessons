Surprising Tales

A2 English Lesson | Storytelling & Adverbs

Do You Like Stories?

Let's think about stories:

Do you prefer telling stories or listening to stories? Why?

Some people like being the storyteller because they can make people laugh or keep them excited. Others prefer listening because they love discovering what happens next and feeling the emotions of the story.

What makes a good story?

Try to think of 5 things that make a story interesting and worth telling or listening to.

  • Surprise: Something unexpected happens
  • Emotion: You feel something (scared, happy, embarrassed)
  • Action: Things happen quickly and keep you interested
  • Interesting characters: People or animals that are different or funny
  • A good ending: The story finishes in a way that satisfies you

Adverbs for Storytelling

Identify the Adverbs

Read these sentences from a story about a lost bike. Find the adverbs (words that describe HOW something happened):

1. "I rode happily for about an hour."
2. "I carefully locked up my bike and went into the café, but unfortunately I'd dropped my keys on the way in."
3. "Luckily, my friends saw me drop my keys."
4. "Suddenly I realised my bike was missing. It was gone!"
5. "...when strangely I heard someone laughing."

What Do These Adverbs Mean?

🔄 Something Unusual: strangely — something is weird or unexpected
⚡ Happens Quickly/Surprise: suddenly — something happens fast, without warning
😊 Something Good: luckily, happily — positive things
😞 Something Bad: unfortunately — something sad or unlucky
🛡️ Careful Action: carefully — doing something with attention and precision

Where Do Adverbs Go in a Sentence?

Position 1 - Beginning of sentence:

Suddenly I realised my bike was missing.

Position 2 - Before the verb:

I carefully locked up my bike.

Position 3 - After the verb:

I rode happily for about an hour.

Practice: Complete the Story Fragments

Fragment 1:

"I spent an hour looking for my missing phone. Suddenly, ... Unfortunately, ..."
"I spent an hour looking for my missing phone. Suddenly, I found it in my jacket pocket. Unfortunately, the battery was dead."

Fragment 2:

"I called my friend and she picked up the phone. Strangely, ... She shouted happily, ..."
"I called my friend and she picked up the phone. Strangely, she already knew something was wrong. She shouted happily, 'I found your bike!'"

How to React to Stories

When someone tells you a story, it's important to react and show interest. Click on each phrase to see when to use it:

"Oh no!"
When to use: Something bad or sad happened in the story. The person is telling you about a problem or loss.
Example: "My bike was stolen!" / "I lost my phone..."
"You're joking!"
When to use: You find the story hard to believe or you're surprised by what they're telling you. You think it's funny or unbelievable.
Example: "I found $100 on the street!" / "My teacher said I was her best student!"
"And what did you do?"
When to use: You want to keep the story going. You're interested and want to know what happens next.
Example: (They tell you about a problem) / (They describe something unusual)
"That's lucky!"
When to use: Something good happened to them. They avoided a problem or got something they wanted.
Example: "My friends saw me drop my keys." / "The train was late, so I missed a car accident."
"Yes, it's nice there."
When to use: They mention a place you know or a place you've been to. You're agreeing about something positive.
Example: "I visited Paris last summer." / "I went to the beach yesterday."

Your Story

📝 Make Notes About Your Story

  • When? What day and time did it happen?
  • What? What did you do?
  • How? How did you feel? What was it like?
  • Result? What happened in the end?
  • Adverbs? Which adverbs can you add to make it better?
