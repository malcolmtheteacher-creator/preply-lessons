Name five different answers to 'What happens when we die?' from the unit.

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Christian: resurrection, heaven/hell, judgment. Islamic: Akhirah, Barzakh, Jannah/Jahannam. Buddhist: rebirth, nirvana. Hindu: reincarnation, moksha. Secular: death is the end, but life has meaning.

What is the body-soul problem, and what are the two main positions?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Can the mind/soul exist without the body? Dualism: yes, they're separate. Physicalism: no, mind is produced by brain.

How does belief in an afterlife typically affect someone's ethics?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: It can create accountability (judgment), incentives for good behaviour (karma, reward), or awareness of cosmic meaning. Without it, people ground ethics in empathy and consequences.

What is nirvana, and how is it different from both Christian heaven and Hindu moksha?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Nirvana is cessation of suffering and escape from the self (Buddhist). Heaven is a place of reward (Christian). Moksha is union with Brahman (Hindu—more like heaven but non-dualist).

What do Buddhism and Hinduism agree on, despite their differences?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Both teach rebirth driven by karma. Both see the world as containing suffering. Both emphasise practice and discipline over belief alone. Both reject the idea of divine judgment.

How do humanists find meaning in life without belief in an afterlife?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Through legacy, relationships, contribution to causes larger than themselves, flourishing in this life, and the intrinsic value of experience and beauty.

Consolidate key terms and contrasts from the entire unit.

What is dualism, and which religions rely on it?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Belief that mind/soul is separate from body. Christianity and Islam are often dualist (though not always). Eastern and secular views vary.

What is the difference between resurrection and reincarnation?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Resurrection: one life, one death, one judgment, then eternal existence. Reincarnation: many lives, each determined by karma, cycling until liberation.

What is karma, and what assumption does it make about justice?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Karma is the automatic moral consequence of actions. It assumes justice is built into the universe, not administered by a judge.

Why might belief in an afterlife be comforting? And why might lack of belief be liberating?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Comfort: reunion, justice, meaning, escape from fear. Liberating: no judgment, no dogma, freedom to define meaning, focus on this life.

How do funeral practices reflect beliefs about the afterlife?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Burial (body matters), cremation (body temporary), mummification (body needed), sky burial (body irrelevant), secular memorial (focus on legacy, not afterlife).

Quick-Check Questions

1. Which tradition teaches that there is no permanent self?

Answer: Buddhism (anatta/no-self)

2. What is purgatory, and which church teaches it?

Answer: A state of purification after death before entering heaven (Catholic and Orthodox churches)

3. In Islamic teaching, what is Barzakh?

Answer: The waiting period after death; the soul remains aware while the body is in the grave

4. What is atman in Hinduism?

Answer: The eternal soul that is part of Brahman (ultimate reality)

5. How do humanists find meaning without an afterlife?

Answer: Through legacy, relationships, contribution to causes, and the intrinsic value of experience

Unit 17 Summary: Death, Afterlife & Meaning

Big Question: What happens when we die, and does it matter?

Five answers you've studied:

  • Christianity: Resurrection, heaven, hell, judgment
  • Islam: Akhirah, Barzakh, paradise, hellfire
  • Buddhism: Rebirth, karma, nirvana (no permanent self)
  • Hinduism: Reincarnation, karma, moksha (union with Brahman)
  • Humanism/Secularism: Death is the end, but life has meaning through legacy, relationships, and contribution

The insight across all traditions: Beliefs about death are inseparable from ethics. How you answer the afterlife question shapes how you live now.
