Hot Takes: The Iceberg

A take is the tip. Below the surface — the real belief.

The five-whys move

Most hot takes are just the visible 10% of what someone actually believes. The other 90% sits below the surface — values, fears, assumptions, lived experience. To find it, ask "why?" five times in a row. By the fifth, you usually find what the take is really about — and it's almost never what the take itself said.

For each iceberg below, click each layer to reveal the next "why?", then speak your answer. Don't explain to the teacher — explain to yourself. Each layer goes deeper than the last.

↓ Reveal

↓ Reveal

Which iceberg surprised you?

Did the "real concern" turn out to be different from the take you started with? On which one was the gap biggest?

Where do your icebergs share values?

Look at Layer 4 across all four icebergs. Do the same one or two values keep appearing — fairness, freedom, dignity, safety? That's probably what you actually care about. The takes are just how it shows up.

Use it on someone else

Next time someone says something that annoys you — apply five "whys" silently. By the fifth, you usually meet a value you also care about. That's where the conversation can actually start.
