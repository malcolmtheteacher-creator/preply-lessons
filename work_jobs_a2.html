What Makes a Job Good or Bad?

💼 The average person will spend 90,000 hours at work in their lifetime!

Meet David, a 35-year-old accountant from Toronto, Canada. He works for a large company. His salary is $85,000 per year (very good!). He has excellent benefits: health insurance, 4 weeks of vacation, and a pension plan. The office is modern and comfortable.

But David is miserable. He wakes up every Monday morning and feels sick to his stomach. He says: "I hate my job. The work is boring. I sit at a computer all day doing the same thing over and over. My boss micromanages everything I do. I have no freedom. The salary is good, but I feel like I'm wasting my life. I'm only 35, but I feel like I'm just waiting to retire."

Now meet Maria, also 35 years old, from Barcelona, Spain. She's a primary school teacher. Her salary is only $32,000 per year (much less than David!). She works long hours—she arrives at school at 7:30 AM and often doesn't finish planning lessons until 6:00 PM. She spends her own money buying supplies for her classroom.

But Maria loves her job. She says: "Yes, I'm tired at the end of the day. Yes, I wish I earned more money. But every day is different. I see children learning and growing. When a student finally understands something difficult, I feel amazing. My job has meaning. I can't imagine doing anything else."

😔 Research shows that 67% of workers are "not engaged" at work — they show up but don't care about their job

Dr. James Park, a work psychologist at Harvard University, studied 50,000 workers in 20 countries over 10 years. He wanted to understand: What makes people happy or unhappy at work?

His surprising discovery: Salary is NOT the most important factor for job satisfaction!

Here's what really matters for job happiness (in order of importance):

  1. Feeling like your work matters (having purpose and meaning)
  2. Good relationships with coworkers (having friends at work)
  3. Having control over your work (not being micromanaged)
  4. Learning and growing (developing new skills)
  5. Fair salary (earning enough to live comfortably)

Dr. Park explains: "Money matters, but only up to a point. Once you earn enough to cover your basic needs comfortably, more money doesn't make you much happier. A person earning $40,000 doing meaningful work they love is usually happier than someone earning $100,000 doing meaningless work they hate."

🔄 Here's something shocking: The average person will change careers 5-7 times in their life!

Not just changing jobs—changing entire careers! This is very different from 50 years ago, when people usually stayed in one career their whole life.

Another interesting statistic: 85% of people say they would quit their job today if they could afford to do so. This includes people with good salaries, benefits, and comfortable offices.

Dr. Park's research found three types of workers:

Type 1: "The Trapped" (50%)

  • They dislike or hate their job
  • They stay because they need the money
  • They dream about quitting but feel they can't
  • They experience high stress and low life satisfaction

Type 2: "The Content" (35%)

  • They don't love their job, but they don't hate it either
  • Work is just work—it pays the bills
  • They find meaning and happiness outside of work
  • They have good work-life balance

Type 3: "The Passionate" (15%)

  • They love what they do
  • Work gives them energy, not just stress
  • They often work long hours but don't mind
  • They would do their job even if the salary was lower

Think About Your Work Experience:

  • Are you more like David (good salary but unhappy) or Maria (lower salary but passionate)?
  • Which type are you: Trapped, Content, or Passionate?
  • What's more important to you: high salary or meaningful work?
  • If you could do any job in the world, what would you choose?

5 Phrases for Talking About Work

Click each phrase to see examples and practice using it!

1. "I'm stuck in..."
Formality: Informal
Meaning: You feel trapped in a situation you don't like and can't easily leave. This is often used for jobs, routines, or life situations that feel difficult to change.
Example from David's story:
"I'm stuck in a job I hate, but I need the money."
"I feel stuck in this career—I want to change but I don't know what else to do."
"I'm stuck in the same routine every day—wake up, work, sleep, repeat."
NOW YOU TRY:
Have you ever felt stuck in a job or situation? Tell your teacher: "I'm stuck in _______" OR "I've never felt stuck because _______"
(Example: a boring job, the same routine, a career I don't like, my hometown)
2. "It pays the bills"
Formality: Informal (very common)
Meaning: The job gives you enough money to live, but you don't have strong feelings about it (positive or negative). It's practical, not passionate. This is what "Content" type workers often say.
Example:
"I'm not in love with my job, but it pays the bills."
"Why do I work here? It pays the bills—that's enough."
"My job isn't exciting, but hey, it pays the bills."
NOW YOU TRY:
Do you have (or have you had) a job that's just "okay"—not great, not terrible? Say: "It pays the bills" about your current or past job. Or explain what you need besides just paying the bills.
(Example: I need meaning, I need creativity, I need to help people)
3. "I can't see myself doing... forever"
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: You can't imagine continuing this job or career for the rest of your working life. You think you'll eventually change to something else.
Example:
"I can't see myself doing this office job forever—I want to start my own business someday."
"I'm a waitress now, but I can't see myself doing this forever. I want to become a chef."
"I can't see myself living in this city forever—I want to move somewhere quieter."
NOW YOU TRY:
What can't you see yourself doing forever? Say: "I can't see myself doing _______ forever" OR "I can see myself doing _______ forever because _______"
(Example: working in an office, teaching, living abroad, staying in my current job)

More Phrases for Work & Jobs

4. "What I love most about my job is..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: You identify the best part of your job—the thing that makes you happy or keeps you motivated. This is what "Passionate" type workers often say.
Example from Maria's story:
"What I love most about my job is seeing children learn and grow."
"What I love most about my job is that every day is different—I never get bored."
"What I love most about my job is working with a great team."
NOW YOU TRY:
Think about your current or past job. Say: "What I love most about my job is _______" OR "What I loved most about my previous job was _______"
(Example: the people, the variety, helping customers, being creative, working independently)
5. "If I could start over, I would..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: If you could go back in time and make different career or life choices, this is what you would do differently. This phrase shows reflection and sometimes regret about past decisions.
Example:
"If I could start over, I would study medicine instead of business."
"If I could start over, I would take more risks and travel more when I was younger."
"If I could start over, I wouldn't change anything—I'm happy with my choices."
NOW YOU TRY:
What would you do differently if you could start your career or life over? Say: "If I could start over, I would _______" OR "If I could start over, I wouldn't change anything because _______"
(Example: study a different subject, start my own business, learn more languages, save more money)
