Today's Question

In your English class you have been discussing the effects of mass tourism on local communities. Now your teacher has asked you to write an essay on this topic.

Write an essay discussing TWO of the impacts below. Explain which one you think is most damaging to local communities, giving reasons in support of your answer.

  • damage to the local environment
  • the rising cost of housing for residents
  • the erosion of authentic local culture

Some opinions expressed in the discussion:

"Tourists trash beaches and overload water supplies — the environment pays first."
"Locals can't afford to live in their own neighbourhoods any more."
"Old shops are gone — every street looks the same now."

Write your essay in 220-260 words in an appropriate style.

Key rule: Discuss exactly TWO of the three bullets — never all three. Develop both in depth. Your opinion in CAE is which of the two bullets matters more, woven through the evaluation and stated in the conclusion. Adding a third "own point" paragraph is a stretch technique for Band 5+ candidates only — it is not what Cambridge asks for, and it carries word-count risk.

Essay Structure — the orthodox shape

This is the structure Cambridge expects. Four paragraphs, two bullets developed in depth, opinion delivered through evaluation and verdict — not through a fourth invented idea.

Paraphrase the topic + name the two bullets you'll discuss + hint at your verdict.

Body 1: First bullet

75-85 words
Body 2: Second bullet

Clear verdict — which of the two bullets matters more, and why. No new arguments.

Model Essay: Band C1+

Read carefully. Notice how the writer (1) discusses two of the three bullets, (2) adds a fresh impact of their own, and (3) delivers a clear verdict. Highlighted phrases are the advanced structures examiners reward.

⭐ Note — this model demonstrates the STRETCH version (with an optional fourth body paragraph adding the writer's own point). For most candidates, the safer orthodox structure is intro + 2 bullets developed in depth + conclusion. Read this model for the language and evaluation moves, but you do not need to copy the four-body structure. See Tab 6 for guidance on when the stretch own-point is worth adding.
INTROAs global travel rebounds to record levels, popular destinations are once again grappling with the costs of their own success. This essay will examine the rising cost of housing for residents and the erosion of authentic local culture before turning to a further impact often overlooked in these debates.
BODY 1 — HOUSINGIt is hard to overstate the pressure that mass tourism places on local housing. In Lisbon and Barcelona, the spread of short-term holiday rentals has driven rents to levels at which ordinary workers — teachers, nurses, bus drivers — can no longer afford to live in the centres they serve. The economic gains, in short, mask a quieter form of displacement.
BODY 2 — CULTUREEqually damaging, by contrast, is the slow hollowing-out of the culture that drew visitors in the first place. As family-run shops and traditional cafés are replaced by souvenir stalls and identical chain restaurants, neighbourhoods lose the texture that made them distinctive. Cities such as Venice now describe themselves, with painful accuracy, as theme parks rather than living communities.
BODY 3 — OWN POINTA further impact that deserves attention is the strain on everyday public services — water supply, waste collection, public transport — when populations triple in summer. Beach towns across Greece and Croatia regularly face shortages that residents, not visitors, are left to endure long after the tourists have flown home.
CONCLUSIONIn conclusion, although cultural erosion is more visible, it is the housing crisis — worsened by overstretched services — that hits residents hardest. Were authorities to regulate short-term rentals strictly, local communities would survive tourism rather than be hollowed out by it.

Total: 254 words ✓ (within 220-260)

Participle clause opener (intro): "As global travel rebounds to record levels..." — sets the scene economically

Litotes / understatement (Body 1): "It is hard to overstate the pressure..." — a Band 5 register marker that signals serious analysis

Tricolon with em-dashes (Body 1): "teachers, nurses, bus drivers" — concrete people make abstract displacement vivid

Adverbial fronting (Body 2): "Equally damaging, by contrast, is the slow hollowing-out of..." — inverts the natural order for emphasis

Compound noun phrasing (Body 2): "the texture that made them distinctive" — precise, evaluative, uniquely C1

Specific case study (Body 2): "Cities such as Venice... theme parks rather than living communities" — turns analysis into vivid image

Own-point signal (Body 3): "A further impact that deserves attention is..." — examiner IMMEDIATELY recognises independent thinking

Embedded tricolon (Body 3): "water supply, waste collection, public transport" — packs three services into one phrase

Adversative emphasis (Body 3): "shortages that residents, not visitors, are left to endure" — contrast pinned exactly where it matters

Conditional inversion (Conclusion): "Were authorities to regulate..." — instead of "If authorities regulated..."

Cleft-pattern (Conclusion): "it is the housing crisis — worsened by overstretched services — that hits residents hardest" — emphasis lands on the winner.

⭐ Stretch: Your Own Point (Band 5+ only)

This is the paragraph that lifts the essay to top-band territory. You add an impact NOT in the three bullets — showing independent thinking. Keep it slightly shorter (45-55 words).

What counts as "your own point"?

Any genuine impact that isn't "environmental damage", "housing pressure" or "cultural erosion". Strong options include: strain on everyday public services, overcrowding and noise, seasonal/insecure work for residents, loss of traditional skills, dependence on a single fragile industry, or damaged social trust between residents and visitors.
