The Company That Changed Everything

A Danish company made energy from oil, gas and coal. Then it decided to change — and it changed almost everything.

Ten words you need

Tap a card to see the meaning.

energy
noun
Power for lights, machines and heat. Electricity is one kind of energy.
"This company makes energy."
coal, oil and gas
nouns
Three things we take out of the ground and burn to make energy. Together we call them fossil fuels.
"They burned coal to make electricity."
wind farm
noun
A big group of tall machines that make electricity from the wind. Some wind farms are in the sea.
"They built a wind farm in the sea."
expensive
adjective
It costs a lot of money. The opposite is cheap.
"Wind farms were very expensive."
to build
verb — built, built
To make something big, like a house or a machine.
"They built many wind farms."
to borrow
verb
To take money from a bank now and give it back later.
"The company borrowed a lot of money."
to sell
verb — sold, sold
To give something to another person and take their money. The opposite is to buy.
"In 2017 they sold the oil and gas business."
a risk
noun
A chance that something bad will happen. To take a risk = to do something when you can lose.
"It was a very big risk."
to succeed
verb
To do the thing you wanted to do. The noun is success.
"For ten years, the plan succeeded."
to fail
verb
To not do the thing you wanted to do. The opposite is to succeed.
"Two projects in America failed."

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then answer the questions before you read the next part.

Part 1 — A company nobody liked

Denmark is a small country in the north of Europe. In 2008 it had a big energy company. The name was DONG. The company made electricity for people's homes.

It made this electricity from coal, oil and gas. Eighty-five per cent of its energy came from these things. They are cheap, but they are dirty. They make the air bad and they change the weather of the planet.

Then the company made a decision. It wanted to stop using coal, oil and gas. It wanted to make energy from the wind instead. It wanted to build big wind farms in the sea.

This was a very big risk. Wind farms in the sea were new. They were very expensive. The company had to borrow a lot of money from banks. Many people said, "This is a stupid plan. It will not work."

In 2012 there was a big problem. The company lost a lot of money. It nearly died. But the new boss did not stop the plan. He kept it.

True or false? Click your answer.

1. In 2008, most of the company's energy came from the wind.

2. Wind farms in the sea were cheap and easy.

3. In 2012 the company had serious money problems.

Talk about it
  • Why did people say the plan was stupid? Give two reasons from the text.
  • The company had problems in 2012, but it did not stop. Was that brave or stupid? What do you think?

Part 2 — The good years

Then the plan started to work.

The wind machines got bigger. Companies learned how to build them faster. So the price went down, down, down. Wind energy became much cheaper.

The Danish company was the first big company to try this. So now it was the best at it. It won contracts all over the world.

In 2017 the company sold all its oil and gas business. It did not want it any more. It also changed its name. The new name was Ørsted, after a famous Danish scientist.

Ørsted became the biggest wind farm company in the world. Its shares became very expensive. Business schools taught the story to students. Everybody said, "Look! An old dirty company can change. This company succeeded."

True or false? Click your answer.

1. Wind energy became more expensive in these years.

2. The company sold its oil and gas business in 2017.

3. Ørsted is the name of a Danish scientist.

Talk about it
  • Why did wind energy become cheaper? Find two reasons in the text.
  • The company changed its name. Why do you think it did that?

Part 3 — Then it got hard again

After 2022, two things happened at the same time.

First, banks started to ask for much more money for their loans. This was a big problem, because the company borrowed a lot of money.

Second, steel, cables and workers became much more expensive. But the company promised its prices years before. It could not change them now.

In 2023 Ørsted stopped two big projects in America. It lost billions. Its shares fell by about seventy-five per cent. The boss left the company.

But here is the interesting part. The idea was not wrong. People still need electricity. Countries still want their own energy. Companies are still building wind farms today.

The company was right about the future. It was wrong about the price.

True or false? Click your answer.

1. After 2022, borrowing money became more expensive.

2. The company stopped two projects in Denmark.

3. Today, nobody builds wind farms any more.

Talk about it
  • "It was right about the future. It was wrong about the price." Explain this in your own words.
  • Was this a good decision or a bad decision? Say why.
  • Tell the whole story again in six sentences. Use: decided · built · sold · became · lost · but.

Past simple — talking about finished time

This story happened in the past. It is finished. So we use the past simple.

1. Two kinds of verb

Regular verbs: add -ed.

work → worked  ·  want → wanted  ·  start → started  ·  decide → decided  ·  borrow → borrowed

Irregular verbs: you must learn them. There is no rule.

make → made  ·  sell → sold  ·  build → built  ·  become → became  ·  lose → lost

2. Negatives and questions — the important rule

In negatives and questions we use did, and the main verb goes back to its normal form.

TypeExampleCareful!
PositiveThey sold the oil business.—
NegativeThey did not sell the wind business.NOT did not sold
QuestionDid they sell the oil business?NOT Did they sold?
