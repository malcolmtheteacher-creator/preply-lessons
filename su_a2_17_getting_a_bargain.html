The First Conditional

Form: If + present simple, subject + can/should/will/might + infinitive

We use the first conditional to talk about future possibilities and their results.

Examples:

  • "If it rains later, I might stay indoors." (possible future situation)
  • "If my sister passes her exams, I might buy her a present." (condition and possible result)
  • "If the lockdown continues, many people will lose their jobs." (condition and certain result)

Notice the modal verbs:

  • will — certain result
  • might/could — possible result
  • should — advisable result
  • can — ability result

Practice the first conditional:

1.
If winter comes early, ...
I'll buy a warm coat. / We might not be prepared. / People will need more heating fuel.
2.
If I wake up late, ...
I might miss the bus. / I'll skip breakfast. / I could be late for work.
3.
If I go to this party, ...
I'll need a new outfit. / I might meet new friends. / I should bring a gift.
4.
If he doesn't phone me, ...
I'll send him a message. / I might be upset. / I could call him instead.
5.
If you buy that car, ...
You should get insurance. / You'll need to maintain it. / You might spend a lot on petrol.
6.
If I get a new job, ...
I'll celebrate. / I might earn more money. / I could move house.
7.
If my parcel doesn't arrive, ...
I'll contact the company. / I might ask for a refund. / I should track my order.

Shopping Advice Phrases

When giving advice about buying important items, we often use the first conditional with these sentence starters:

  • "If you buy a smartphone, you might want to..." (consider getting a case, checking storage, etc.)
  • "If you want a house, you will need to..." (save money, get a loan, check the area, etc.)
  • "If you get a tablet, you could..." (use it for reading, watching films, working, etc.)
  • "If you want a good computer, you should..." (compare prices, check reviews, consider your needs, etc.)

Common shopping advice topics:

  • Comparing prices before buying
  • Reading customer reviews and ratings
  • Checking warranty and return policies
  • Considering your actual needs vs. wants
  • Looking for seasonal sales or discounts
  • Getting advice from friends or experts
  • Taking time before making a decision (not impulse buying)
  • Thinking about long-term costs (repairs, maintenance, accessories)

Phrases to structure your advice:

  • "First of all..." or "To start with..."
  • "Another important thing is..."
  • "Don't forget to..."
  • "It's essential that you..."
  • "Finally, you should consider..."

Give Shopping Advice

Choose one of these items and imagine you're giving buying advice to a friend:

  • A computer or laptop
  • A tablet
  • A smartphone
  • A house

Think about:

  • What should someone consider before buying?
  • What research should they do?
  • What problems might they have?
  • How can they find a good bargain?

Use the first conditional to give your advice.

Discussion Questions
