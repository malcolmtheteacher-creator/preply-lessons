Recall: Creation and Humanity's Role

Warm-up: 5 minutes
5:00

Christians believe God created the world and gave humans a special role. Let's review these foundations before exploring creation in depth.

What is the opening line of the Bible?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth." (Genesis 1:1) — God is presented as the creator of everything.

What does "dominion" mean in Genesis?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Dominion means authority and responsibility. God gave humans dominion over creation — the power to use it, but also the duty to care for it.

What is stewardship?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Stewardship is the responsibility to care for and manage something (like creation) on behalf of someone else (God). Christians are stewards of the Earth.

What is the Logos?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Logos is Greek for "Word" — a theological term for the divine principle of reason and order. In John 1, Jesus is called the Logos.

Genesis 1-2: Two Creation Accounts

Genesis 1: God creates in six days, culminating in humans (male and female) made in God's image. Creation is "very good." Humans are given "dominion" over the animals.

Genesis 2: God creates the man, then the woman from his rib. They are placed in a garden to "tend and keep it" (stewardship language). They are free to eat from any tree except the "tree of the knowledge of good and evil."

Key theme: Humans have a unique place in creation — made in God's image, given authority, and given responsibility to care for creation.

Scripture: Creation Through the Word (Logos)

Genesis 1:1 — "In the beginning God created..."

This opening establishes God as creator. God creates through speaking: "And God said, 'Let there be light'" (Genesis 1:3). God's word has creative power.

John 1:1-3 — "In the beginning was the Word..."

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was with God in the beginning. Through him all things were made; without him nothing has been made that has been made."

John presents the Logos (Word) as an eternal principle existing with God, and identifies this Logos as Jesus. This means Jesus was active in creation. So creation involved all three persons of the Trinity: God the Father creates, the Word (Son) is the means of creation, and the Spirit (Genesis 1:2) hovers over creation.

Dominion and Stewardship: Humanity's Responsibility

Genesis 1:28: God blesses humans and says, "Fill the earth and subdue it. Rule over the fish in the sea and the birds in the sky and over every living creature that moves on the ground."

Dominion interpretation: This could mean:

  • Exploitation: Humans have absolute power to use creation as they wish.
  • Stewardship: Humans have authority AND responsibility — to manage creation wisely on God's behalf.

Genesis 2:15: God places the man in the garden "to tend and keep it." This language (tend, keep) suggests careful management and responsibility, not domination.

Modern Christian environmental ethics: Many Christians argue that stewardship — caring for creation, using resources sustainably, protecting endangered species — is a core Christian value. Humans are caretakers, not owners, of the Earth.

Literal vs Metaphorical: How Christians Interpret Genesis

Literal interpretation: Genesis describes actual historical events. God created in six literal 24-hour days, approximately 6,000-10,000 years ago. Adam and Eve were the first humans. This view is held by young-earth creationists and some evangelical Protestants.

Liberal/Metaphorical interpretation: Genesis is theological poetry and myth, not literal history. The message (God created; creation is good; humans have divine image; stewardship matters) is true, but the timeline and literal details may not be. This view is held by many Catholic, Orthodox, and progressive Protestant scholars.

Theistic evolution: God works through the process of evolution. Evolution is God's method of creation. Genesis teaches theological truth (God created; creation reflects divine order) but not scientific mechanism.

Imago Dei: The Image of God in Humans

Genesis 1:27: "So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them."

Christians understand "image of God" (Latin: imago Dei) to mean:

  • Rationality: Humans possess reason and the ability to understand truth (reflecting God's wisdom).
  • Moral agency: Humans can make genuine choices and are accountable for them (reflecting God's justice).
  • Capacity for relationship: Humans can love and be loved; we can relate to God and to each other (reflecting God's love).
  • Creativity: Humans can create art, music, ideas (reflecting God's creative nature).

This is why human life has special dignity in Christian ethics — every person bears God's image and is worthy of respect.

The Role of God's Word (Logos) in Creation

John's presentation of the Logos has profound implications:

  • The Logos is the eternal, divine principle through which all things exist (John 1:3).
  • Everything is held together through the Logos — meaning reality has rational order and intelligibility.
  • When the Word became flesh in Jesus (John 1:14), God entered into creation personally.
  • This means Jesus is not a foreign god imposed on creation; He is the divine principle that always animated creation, now appearing in human form.

Review: Creation & God's Word

Quote the opening of Genesis and explain its significance.

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth" (Genesis 1:1). It establishes God as creator of all reality.

What does "imago Dei" mean?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Imago Dei means "image of God" — the belief that humans are made in God's image, giving them special dignity and moral status.

What is the difference between dominion and stewardship?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Dominion = authority to rule. Stewardship = responsibility to care for on God's behalf. Genesis suggests both: authority with accountability.

How are the Trinity involved in creation according to John 1?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The Father creates, the Word/Logos (Son) is the means through which all things are made, and the Spirit hovers over creation. All three persons are active.

What are three major interpretations of Genesis creation?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Literal creation (6 days ~6,000 years ago), metaphorical/liberal (theological poetry, not literal history), and theistic evolution (evolution as God's method).
