💨 The /h/ Sound

A2 • Italian/French/Spanish/Portuguese • Pronounce H and don't add it where it doesn't exist

🔍 Let's notice the H problem

Listen to these common mistakes:

❌ "'ave a nice day"

Should be: "Have a nice day" (dropping the H)

❌ "An hexample"

Should be: "An example" (adding H where it doesn't exist)

What's the /h/ sound? Why do some speakers drop it and others add it?

🎯 The Problem:

Italian, French, Spanish, and Portuguese speakers often DROP the /h/ because their languages don't use this sound (or it's completely silent/written differently).

But English /h/ MUST be pronounced: have, house, help, happy, history.

Sometimes they also ADD /h/ where it doesn't exist: "hexample" instead of "example," "hegg" instead of "egg."

🛠️ The /h/ sound explained

💡 What is /h/?

The /h/ sound is a gentle BREATH, like a sigh or like the sound you make when you breathe on a mirror. It's voiceless (no vibration in your throat) and made at the back of your mouth.

✅ PRONOUNCE the H — these words START with H:

✅ PRONOUNCE /h/

have (HAV)
house (HOWS)
help (HELP)
happy (HAP-ee)
history (HIS-tuh-ree)
hand (HAND)
head (HED)

❌ DON'T ADD H — these words DON'T start with H:

❌ NO /h/ sound

example (igZAM-puhl) NOT "hexample"
egg (EG) NOT "hegg"
apple (AP-uhl) NOT "happle"
eight (ATE) NOT "haite"
orange (OR-inj) NOT "horange"
answer (AN-ser) NOT "hanswer"

✏️ Guided practice — Say these words

Pronounce (with /h/ at the start):

🔵 have → HAV (breathe out the H)

🔵 house → HOWS (H like a breath, then OWS)

🔵 help → HELP (clear H at the start)

🔵 happy → HAP-ee (H with energy at the start)

Pronounce (WITHOUT /h/ — no breath sound):

🔴 example → igZAM-puhl (starts with vowel sound, not H)

🔴 egg → EG (just the E sound, no H before)

🔴 apple → AP-uhl (starts with A, no H)

🔴 orange → OR-inj (starts with O, no H)

🚫 Common mistakes to AVOID

❌ 'ave instead of have (dropping the H)

❌ appy instead of happy (dropping the H)

❌ hexample instead of example (adding H where it doesn't exist)

❌ hegg instead of egg (adding H before a vowel)
