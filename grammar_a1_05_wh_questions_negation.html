Question Words: "Where do you...?"

Asking real, everyday questions with what, where, when, who, why and how.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday questions:

  • "Where does your brother live?"
  • "What does she do at the weekend?"
  • "Why do they close the shop on Mondays?"

Before you check: where does "do" or "does" sit, and what happens to the main verb after it?

Check the pattern CLICK
Wh-word + do/does + subject + base verb ...? The main verb never changes — no "-s", even after "does" (not "does she does").
wh-word + do / does + subject + base verb ...?

When there's no action verb — just "be"

  • "Where are you from?"
  • "How is your sister?"
  • "Who is that man?"

Same question: what's different here?

Check the pattern CLICK
With be, there's no "do" at all — wh-word + am/is/are + subject ...? "Do" only appears with action verbs like live, work, close.
wh-word + am / is / are + subject ...?

Saying No: "I don't..." / "It isn't..."

The same helper words, now joined with "not" to say no.

Spot the pattern

  • "I don't eat meat."
  • "She doesn't drink coffee."
  • "We aren't ready yet."

Before you check: which subjects go with "don't", which go with "doesn't", and what's different about the third sentence?

Check the pattern CLICK
I / you / we / they + don't + base verb. He / she / it + doesn't + base verb. But "aren't" is a form of be — with be, "not" joins straight on (isn't / aren't / am not) and there's no "do" at all.
I / you / we / they + don't + base verb
he / she / it + doesn't + base verb
am not / isn't / aren't (with be)

"He doesn't work on Sundays."

Same Engine: Questions and "No" Together

Do/does never disappears — it just moves. Here's the connection in full.

Watch it move

"You live here."
→
"Do you live here?"
→
"You don't live here."
"She works on Saturdays."
→
"Does she work on Saturdays?"
→
"She doesn't work on Saturdays."

In the statement there's no "do" at all. In the question, do/does jumps to the front. In the negative, do/does stays next to the subject and joins with "not". Exactly the same helper word — three jobs. It works with "be" too: "is" moves to the front for a question ("Is she ready?") and joins "not" for a negative ("She isn't ready.").

statement: subject + verb (+ -s for he/she/it)
question: do/does + subject + base verb ...?
negative: subject + don't/doesn't + base verb
