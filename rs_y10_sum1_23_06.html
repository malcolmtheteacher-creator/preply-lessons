Recall Questions: Unit 23 Review

Click each card to reveal answers. Use this to identify areas needing revision before the exam questions.

What are the six articles of faith in Sunni Islam?

Answer: Tawhid (God), angels, holy books, prophets, Day of Judgement, predestination.

What are the five roots of Shi'a Islam?

Answer: Tawhid, Adalat (justice), Nubuwwah (prophethood), Imamah (leadership), Mi'ad (Day of Return).

What is Tawhid?

Answer: Belief in the absolute oneness and uniqueness of God.

What is Shirk?

Answer: Associating partners with God; the gravest sin in Islam.

Name the four archangels and one role of each.

Answer: Jibril (revelation), Mika'il (sustenance), Israfil (end-times trumpet), Azra'il (death).

What are the 99 Names of Allah?

Answer: Divine attributes revealing different facets of God's nature (e.g., Al-Rahman = Most Merciful).

What does Al-Qadr mean?

Answer: Divine predestination; the belief that God knows and has predetermined all events.

How do Muslims reconcile predestination with free will?

Answer: God's knowledge exists outside time; God knows choices but doesn't cause them. Humans are free and accountable.

What is the main theological difference between Sunni and Shi'a Islam?

Answer: Sunni: community leadership (Caliphate); Shi'a: divinely guided Imams (Imamah).

Why do recording angels matter to Muslims?

Answer: They record every action and present records on the Day of Judgement, encouraging ethical behaviour.

  • A) Jibril
  • B) Mika'il

  • D) Azra'il

Answer: A

  • A) Breaking Islamic law
  • B) Associating partners with God

Short Answer (4 points each)

Q6: Explain the main difference between Sunni and Shi'a belief about leadership.

Model answer: Sunni Muslims believe in community-chosen Caliphs; Shi'a believe in divinely guided Imams who have special authority and knowledge. This reflects different views on how God guides the Muslim community after the Prophet.

Q7: What is Tawakkul, and how does it relate to Al-Qadr?

Model answer: Tawakkul is trust in God. It relates to Al-Qadr because Muslims accept God's predestination while actively striving--they work hard and make good choices, then trust God with the outcome. This balances effort with acceptance of divine will.

Q8: How do recording angels (Kiraman Katibin) influence Muslim moral behaviour?

Model answer: Knowing that angels record every action encourages Muslims to act ethically, knowing their deeds are witnessed and will be presented on the Day of Judgement. This promotes accountability and righteous conduct.

Guidance

Essay 1: The Foundations of Islamic Belief (8 marks)

"The six articles of faith form the foundation of Islamic belief." Explain and evaluate this statement.

  • Explain: Name all six articles
  • Show how each is foundational (interconnected, shape daily life)
  • Consider: Are all equally important, or is one more foundational?
  • Use examples

Guidance

Essay 2: Unity and Justice in Islamic Theology (12 marks)

"In Islam, God's mercy is shown through justice, and God's justice is shown through mercy." Discuss.

  • Define both concepts (Rahmah, Adl)
  • Explain how they work together in God's nature
  • Use Qur'anic evidence
  • Apply to real-life situations (suffering, accountability)
  • Reach a conclusion

Guidance

Essay 3: Faith and Effort in Islam (12 marks)

"If God has predestined all things, Muslims should not strive to achieve goals or improve themselves." Evaluate.

  • Explain Al-Qadr and the apparent paradox
  • Show how Muslims reconcile predestination with effort (Kasb, Tawakkul)
  • Discuss Qur'anic encouragement to strive (Jihad)
  • Explain moral responsibility
  • Conclude with evaluation

GCSE 12-Mark Practice Essays

Choose one essay to write. Allow 20 minutes. Structure: Introduction + 2-3 developed points + counter-argument + Conclusion. Aim for 400-500 words.

Guidance

Essay A: "The differences between Sunni and Shi'a beliefs are more important than the similarities."

Plan your response:

  • Intro: Identify key differences (Imamah, Adalat) and similarities (Tawhid, Qur'an, prophets)
  • Point 1: Differences matter: theologically distinct views on leadership and justice
  • Point 2: Similarities matter: both affirm core Islamic beliefs; differences are interpretive
  • Counter: Consider whether differences have caused harmful divisions (historical conflicts)
  • Conclusion: Weigh: are differences fundamental or complementary?

Guidance

Essay B: "Tawhid is the most important belief in Islam."

Plan your response:

  • Intro: Define Tawhid and its centrality (first article, Shahada, daily prayer)
  • Point 1: Tawhid is foundational (other beliefs depend on it)
  • Point 2: Other beliefs are equally vital (prophethood, Day of Judgement, predestination)
  • Counter: Without Tawhid, other beliefs lose meaning
  • Conclusion: Is Tawhid "most important" or "foundational-to-all"?

Guidance

Essay C: "Belief in angels is not important for Muslims today."

Plan your response:

  • Intro: Define angels (Malaikah) and their roles
  • Point 1: Belief matters: guardian protection, moral accountability, spiritual connection
  • Point 2: Counter-argument: modern Muslims may view angels symbolically, not literally
  • Point 3: Even symbolic understanding shapes Muslim ethics and worldview
  • Conclusion: Is angelic belief peripheral or integral to Muslim identity?

Unit 23 Summary: What You've Learned

Lesson 1: Six Articles & Five Roots

Key Knowledge: Sunni Islam teaches six foundational beliefs (Tawhid, angels, holy books, prophets, Day of Judgement, predestination). Shi'a Islam emphasises five roots (Tawhid, Adalat, Nubuwwah, Imamah, Mi'ad), with major differences in theology of leadership and justice.

GCSE Focus: Compare Sunni-Shi'a beliefs, explain why they matter, and evaluate their importance.

Lesson 2: Tawhid - God's Oneness

Key Knowledge: Tawhid is the foundation of Islam--belief in the absolute oneness of God. Shirk (associating partners with God) is the gravest sin. The Shahada and Surah 112 encapsulate this belief.

GCSE Focus: Explain why Tawhid is central, contrast with Shirk, and evaluate its importance to Islamic ethics.

Lesson 3: God's Nature - Attributes & Names

Key Knowledge: God possesses key attributes (Al-Rahman, Al-Rahim, Al-Adl, Al-Qadir) embodied in the 99 Names of Allah. Islamic theology reconciles God's mercy and justice, addressing the problem of suffering and theodicy.

GCSE Focus: Identify divine attributes, use Qur'anic evidence, and evaluate how God's nature is known.

Lesson 4: Angels (Malaikah)

Key Knowledge: Angels are created beings of light who serve God. The four archangels (Jibril, Mika'il, Israfil, Azra'il) have specific roles. Recording angels monitor human behaviour. Satan (Iblis) represents rebellion against God.

GCSE Focus: Name angels and their functions, explain their significance to Muslim belief, and evaluate their importance today.

Lesson 5: Predestination (Al-Qadr)

Key Knowledge: Al-Qadr teaches that God knows and has predetermined all events. Yet humans remain morally responsible for their choices. Islamic theology holds this paradox, with Sunni and Shi'a emphasising different aspects (Sunni: trust God's wisdom; Shi'a: emphasise Adalat/justice requires free will).

GCSE Focus: Explain the predestination-free will paradox, use Qur'anic evidence, and evaluate effects on Muslim life and ethics.
