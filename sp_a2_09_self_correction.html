Self-Correction

Fix mistakes smoothly without losing flow

🎯 Fluency & Flow

The Spark: Everyone Makes Mistakes

Here's a secret: Native speakers make mistakes ALL the time.

The difference? They correct themselves so smoothly that you barely notice.

Watch This:

❌ Without correction

"So yesterday I go to... I went... um... the shopping... sorry, my English is bad..."

✅ With smooth correction

"So yesterday I go— I mean, I went to the shopping center and..."

The second speaker made the SAME mistake. But they fixed it naturally and kept talking.

Common Mistakes to Correct

  • Wrong word: "I saw a nice... sorry, I mean beautiful sunset."
  • Wrong tense: "Yesterday I eat— I mean, I ate pizza."
  • Wrong idea: "It was expensive... actually, no, it was quite cheap."
  • Unclear: "The thing is... what I'm trying to say is..."

Today you'll learn: 5 phrases to correct yourself naturally without breaking the flow.

Micro-Skill: 5 Self-Correction Phrases

Click each phrase to learn when to use it:

I mean, ... Click to expand

The most common and natural self-correction phrase:

"I've been learning English for three— I mean, four years now."

"She lives in Paris— I mean, London."

"It was really boring— I mean, not boring, but not very exciting."

When to use: Quick corrections, wrong words, small mistakes.

🎤 NOW YOU TRY

Say: "I have two brothers— I mean, [correct number]..."

What I meant was... Click to expand

For clarifying when you said something unclear or confusing:

"The project was a disaster. What I meant was, it didn't go as planned."

"She's difficult to work with. What I meant was, she has high standards."

When to use: When someone might misunderstand you, or when your first attempt wasn't clear.

🎤 NOW YOU TRY

Say something about your job/studies, then clarify with "What I meant was..."

Actually, ... Click to expand

For correcting facts or changing your mind:

"I think I'll have coffee... Actually, I'll have tea instead."

"The meeting is at 2pm. Actually, no, it's at 3pm."

"I don't like spicy food. Actually, that's not true— I just don't like it too hot."

When to use: Changing your answer, correcting facts.

🎤 NOW YOU TRY

Say your favorite food, then change your mind with "Actually,..."

Sorry, let me rephrase that... Click to expand

For completely restating something more clearly:

"The situation is... it's complicated because... Sorry, let me rephrase that. Basically, we need more time."

Variations:

  • "Let me put it another way..."
  • "Let me start again..."
  • "In other words,..."

🎤 NOW YOU TRY

Explain something complicated, then use "Let me rephrase that..." to simplify.

Or rather, ... Click to expand

For making your language more precise:

"He's my friend— or rather, he's my colleague."

"I like the movie— or rather, I liked certain parts of it."

"It's cheap— or rather, it's affordable."

When to use: Finding a more accurate word, being more precise.

🎤 NOW YOU TRY

Describe someone you know, then be more precise with "or rather,..."
