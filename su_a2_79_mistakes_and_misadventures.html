Narrative Tenses - Telling Stories About the Past

When telling a story, we use two main tenses to show what happened.

Past Simple

Use the past simple for completed actions in a story:

The family made a big decision, and in May they all travelled to New York.

Past Continuous

Use the past continuous for:

  • An interrupted action: Paul was walking home from work when he saw the old man.
  • Two things happening at the same time: I was working hard while my cat was sleeping.

Choose the Correct Tense

Which tense is correct?

1. We played / were playing tennis when John hurt / was hurting his ankle.

Were playing... hurt — The first action (playing) was in progress when the second action (hurt) interrupted it.

2. Susie watched / was watching a film when she heard / was hearing the noise.

Was watching... heard — She was in the middle of watching when she heard the noise.

3. He worked / was working in a bank when he met / was meeting his wife.

Was working... met — He was working when he met her (a completed action that happened while something else was in progress).

4. What did you do / were you doing when I called / was calling you last night?

Were you doing... called — The caller is asking what was happening when they called.

5. When I left / was leaving the house, it snowed / was snowing.

Left... was snowing — Leaving the house is the completed action; snowing was already happening.
