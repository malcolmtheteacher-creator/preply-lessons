Recognising Signpost Language

Lecturers use signpost language to guide you through their talk. These words and phrases tell you what's coming next.

Common Signpost Phrases

Starting an explanation: "Let me explain...", "Let me start with...", "I'll begin by..."
Listing points: "Firstly", "Secondly", "Finally", "First of all", "In addition", "Moreover"
Moving to a new point: "Moving on...", "Now let's look at...", "Another point is...", "Let me turn to..."
Summarising: "To summarise", "In summary", "In short", "To recap", "What I've covered so far..."
Giving examples: "For example", "For instance", "Such as", "To illustrate", "Here's an example"
Concluding: "In conclusion", "Finally", "To sum up", "In the end", "Last but not least"

Why This Matters

When you hear these phrases, you know important information is coming. They help you:

  • Predict what's coming next
  • Know when to expect examples or explanations
  • Identify main points vs supporting details
  • Stay focused and take better notes
  • Understand the structure of the lecture

Predicting Content and Staying Focused

Good lecture listeners actively predict. Before the lecturer speaks, you can guess what they'll discuss.

Before the Lecture

  • Read the lecture title and syllabus
  • Review previous lecture notes
  • Note any questions you have
  • Predict 3-4 main topics

Staying Focused

  • Listen for signpost language
  • Write down main points, not everything
  • If you miss something, keep listening—don't get stuck
  • Ask questions during question time
  • Review notes within 24 hours

Identifying Signpost Language

Read the transcript excerpt and identify what the signpost phrase tells you.

Question 1

"Today I'll discuss three main causes of climate change. Firstly, we need to look at carbon dioxide emissions..."

What does "Firstly" tell you?

This tells you there are multiple points coming (at least 2 more). You should expect "Secondly" and "Thirdly". This is the first main point of the lecture.

Question 2

"...and that's the historical background. Now let me turn to the economic implications of this policy..."

What's happening at this moment?

The topic is changing. The first topic (historical background) has finished. A new topic (economic implications) is starting. This is a good time to create a new section in your notes.

Question 3

"...to illustrate this point, let me give you an example. In Japan, renewable energy adoption..."

What should you expect?

A specific example showing how the theory works in practice. This makes the abstract concept concrete. Important for understanding.

Question 4

"...and that covers the main theories. To summarise what we've discussed: four key frameworks exist..."

What's the speaker doing?

Summarising main points. This is a good time to check your notes and ensure you've captured the key points. The lecture is moving toward a conclusion.
