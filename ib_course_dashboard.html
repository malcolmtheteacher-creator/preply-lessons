The IB pathway — which programme is this for?

The IB has two relevant programmes. A typical diagnostic worksheet — hybrid of MCQ comprehension and essay analysis — fits both, but the essay register ("authorial choices", "effects on the reader") is firmly in the Diploma Programme (DP) register. Most students using this course are in DP1 (Year 12) preparing for Paper 1.

MYP English Language & Literature

Years 7–11 (Ages 11–16)

Four criteria, each marked out of 8:

  • A: Analysing
  • B: Organising
  • C: Producing text
  • D: Using language

Final assessment in Year 11 (eAssessment). Worksheets in this programme tend to use simpler register — "techniques and their effects" rather than "authorial choices".

DP English Language & Literature (more likely)

Years 12–13 (Ages 16–18)

Three external assessments:

  • Paper 1: Guided textual analysis of an unseen non-literary text (1.25 hrs SL / 2.25 hrs HL)
  • Paper 2: Comparative essay on two studied literary works (1.75 hrs)
  • HL Essay / IO: Coursework + Individual Oral (10 mins recorded analysis)

The typical diagnostic worksheet — "authorial choices" + reader effect — is straight DP Criterion A territory. This is the most likely match.

The 4 DP assessment criteria (out of 10 each, except Crit D which is out of 5)

CriterionWhat it measuresWhere this course targets it
A Analysis & Evaluation Understanding of the text + interpretation of authorial choices and their effects. Lessons 1, 2, 3, 6 — the spine of the course.
B Focus & Organisation Coherent structure, paragraph development, logical sequencing. Lessons 4 (PACER) + 5 (integration).
C Knowledge & Understanding (Paper 2) Grasp of the studied literary works and their context. Targeted in separate literature lessons (not in this course).
D Language Clarity, accuracy, register, style of the student's own prose. Light-touch coverage in Lesson 6 self-mark; a separate "register" lesson would deepen this if needed.

Where the typical entry student sits (best estimate from a diagnostic essay)

  • Criterion A: Band 4–5 of 10. Identifies techniques but rarely names them precisely; rarely articulates the effect on the reader.
  • Criterion B: Band 4–5 of 10. Paragraphs exist but lack a 5-slot shape; ideas are listed rather than argued.
  • Criterion D: Band 3 of 5. Clear prose with some imprecise vocabulary ("shows", "tells us").
Realistic target after the 6 lessons: Criterion A → Band 6/7 of 10; Criterion B → Band 6 of 10; Criterion D → Band 4 of 5. Translates to an overall grade jump of roughly one IB level (e.g. 4 → 5, or 5 → 6) by the next mock.

How to run the course

Per-lesson rhythm (50 minutes)

  1. 0–5 min — Recall. One question from last week's lesson (e.g. "Name three effect verbs from Lesson 3"). Use the Cheat Card from the previous lesson if needed.
  2. 5–15 min — Tab 1 of the lesson. The hook / weak-vs-strong comparison. Keep it conversational.
  3. 15–35 min — Tabs 2–3. The new vocabulary / framework + worked examples. Reveal answers after the student attempts.
  4. 35–47 min — Tab 4 ("Your Turn"). Student writes; you coach. Use the Answer Key (Lesson 1) or the embedded reveal buttons.
  5. 47–50 min — Cheat Card & assignment. Quick review + the homework note inside that lesson.

Between lessons

  • Open the IB Teacher Tracker and update the coverage grid + lesson notes.
  • Tick the band you would award the student's work this week. The trajectory chart will fill in over the 6 weeks.

Adapting for a single 50-minute slot

If you only get one slot rather than six, run a diagnostic-and-toolkit lesson:

  • 10 min — Lesson 1 Tab 2 ("The Ladder")
  • 10 min — Lesson 2 Cheat Card (technique vocabulary)
  • 15 min — Lesson 3 Tab 1 + Tab 4 (effect verbs in context)
  • 15 min — Lesson 4 Tab 2 (PACER slots) + one written paragraph

If the student gets stuck on a specific slot

  • Cannot write a P (thesis): ask "what is the writer doing that they did not have to do?" Force an arguable claim.
  • Cannot write a C (connection): insist on one connector phrase from Lesson 5 Tab 1 — e.g. "this is extended by…", "by contrast…".
  • Cannot write an E (evaluate): prompt with "Together, these two choices…" and have the student finish the sentence.
  • Writes "this shows that…": stop, point to Lesson 3 Cheat Card, replace with an effect verb on the spot.
Programme philosophy: the goal is not to teach every IB technique. It is to give the student repeatable moves they can run on any unseen text in any pressure situation. Six lessons. One paragraph shape. One verb toolkit. One self-mark protocol.
