🚢 Ship vs Sheep — /ɪ/ vs /iː/

A2 • Italian/Spanish/French/Portuguese • Master the vowel length difference

🔍 Let's notice the vowel problem

Listen to these common mistakes:

❌ "I sat in the sheep"

Should be: "I sat in the ship"

❌ "The ship is fleeing"

Should be: "The sheep is fleeing" OR "I am fleeing"

What's the difference between "ship" and "sheep"? Why do they sound similar but different?

🎯 The Problem:

Italian, Spanish, French, and Portuguese speakers have ONE "i" sound in their languages. English has TWO:

🔵 /ɪ/ SHORT — ship, sit, bit, live, fill, pin (short, relaxed)

🔴 /iː/ LONG — sheep, seat, beat, leave, feel, peen (long, tense)

The difference is DURATION (length) and TENSION (how much your mouth stretches).

🛠️ The two "i" sounds in English

🔵 /ɪ/ SHORT

ɪ
How long: SHORT (about 0.1 seconds)
How tense: RELAXED lips
Examples:
ship, sit, bit, live, fill, pin, big

🔴 /iː/ LONG

iː
How long: LONG (about 0.3 seconds)
How tense: SPREAD lips
Examples:
sheep, seat, beat, leave, feel, peen, bead
💡 Key difference:

DURATION is the most important. Say "ship" quickly, then stretch out "sheep" — you'll hear the difference!

✏️ Guided practice — Say these words

Pronounce (SHORT /ɪ/ — say quickly!):

🔵 ship — short, quick, relaxed lips

🔵 sit — your legs bent, sitting

🔵 bit — a small piece

🔵 fill — to make full

Pronounce (LONG /iː/ — stretch it out!):

🔴 sheep — long, tense, spread lips like a smile

🔴 seat — somewhere to sit

🔴 beat — a rhythm or to strike

🔴 feel — to touch or experience

🚫 Common mistakes to AVOID

❌ Saying "ship" and "sheep" the SAME way

❌ Saying "sit" instead of "seat" (or vice versa)

❌ Not stretching out the /iː/ sound long enough

❌ Not relaxing your lips enough for /ɪ/
