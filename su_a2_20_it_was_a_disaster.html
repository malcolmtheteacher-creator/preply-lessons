It Was a Disaster

Talking about bad experiences and showing sympathy

Let's Think About Holidays

We all have different views about what makes a good holiday and what makes a bad one. Let's explore this together.

What makes a good holiday?

Think about a holiday you've enjoyed, or imagine one you'd like. What factors made it special? Consider things like:

  • The weather and scenery
  • The people you travelled with
  • The accommodation and food
  • The activities you did
  • How smoothly everything went

What makes a bad holiday?

Now think about the opposite. What factors would ruin a holiday for you? Consider:

  • Travel problems (delays, lost luggage, etc.)
  • Bad weather
  • Poor accommodation
  • Difficult people in your group
  • Unexpected problems or emergencies

Let's discuss: Which of these factors do you think is most important for a good holiday? What's the worst thing that could happen during a holiday? Have you experienced any major problems when travelling?

Dialogue: A Disastrous Holiday

Read this conversation between Michael and Zara. Notice how Zara describes her bad experiences and how Michael shows sympathy. The phrases are highlighted for you.

Michael:
How was your holiday?
Zara:
Actually, it was a disaster!
Michael:
Oh no! What happened?
Zara:
Well, first of all, our flight was delayed. We had to wait around in the airport for eight hours!
Michael:
That's too bad. Delays are annoying, but at least you made it.
Zara:
Yes, but things only got worse from there. When we arrived, our bags weren't there. It was three days before the airline finally found them. Also, it was rainy and chilly when we arrived, and we weren't wearing jackets.
Michael:
What bad luck! I hope things got better when you arrived at your hotel.
Zara:
Unfortunately, they didn't. The hotel was overbooked. The only hotel that had rooms was very dirty and far from the downtown area.
Michael:
I'm sorry you had such a terrible experience. Did the weather get any better?
Zara:
It actually got worse. The rain turned into a small flood. We ended up spending most of the week in our hotel room.
Michael:
Wow, that really was a disaster!
Zara:
Yes. After that, coming back to work feels like a holiday!

Key Phrases from the Dialogue

Describing bad experiences:

  • "It was a disaster"
  • "Things only got worse"
  • "Unfortunately, they didn't"
  • "It actually got worse"

Showing sympathy:

  • "Oh no!"
  • "That's too bad."
  • "What bad luck!"
  • "I'm sorry you had such a terrible experience."
  • "Wow, that really was a disaster!"

Tips for Using These Adjectives

  • Use them to describe nouns: "The hotel was dirty and smelly."
  • Combine them: "It was a terrible, disastrous experience."
  • Use them in sentences: "The weather was awful." "The service was disappointing."
  • Choose the strongest adjective that fits: "horrible" is stronger than "annoying"

Discussion & Reflection

Let's think more deeply about travel experiences and what we've learned.

Discussion Questions

Have you ever gotten lost while travelling?

What happened? How did you feel? How did you find your way?

Who was the most difficult person you've travelled with?

Why did they make travel harder? What did they do that was frustrating?

What was the worst weather/accommodation/food you've experienced?

Describe the experience. How did it affect your trip?

Have you ever had problems at the airport?

Delays, lost luggage, security issues? Tell us what happened and how you handled it.
