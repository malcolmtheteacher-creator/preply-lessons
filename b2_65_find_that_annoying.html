I Find That Annoying

Expressing Dissatisfaction & Starting Conversations

Opening Lines for Strangers

Starting conversation with people you don't know is a useful skill. Here are common opening lines:

Natural Conversation Starters

Do you come here often? Classic opener in cafés, bars, or venues—asks about their familiarity with the place
Terrible weather, isn't it? Safe opener using weather as common ground—works anywhere
How much longer are we going to have to wait? Shared frustration opener—good in queues, waiting rooms, or delayed events
So, how do you know Mark? Connection finder—works after someone introduces you
I love your shoes! Compliment opener—genuine praise as conversation starter
Have you been waiting long? Empathy opener—acknowledges shared experience
What department do you work in? Professional opener—works at business events or conventions

Tips for Success

Be authentic: Use openers that feel natural to you.

Match the situation: Weather talk works in waiting areas. Compliments work in social settings.

Ask follow-up questions: Once they respond, ask more about their experience or opinion.

Listen actively: Show genuine interest in their answer.

Phrases for Expressing Dissatisfaction

Different ways to express unhappiness or annoyance with varying degrees of intensity:

Mild to Strong

It's not good. Mild, understated dissatisfaction
It's really bad. Moderate dissatisfaction
It's very disappointing. Expresses let-down expectations
It's so annoying! Strong annoyance, with emphasis
It's terrible, isn't it? Seeking agreement on serious dissatisfaction
This is the worst... Extreme dissatisfaction
Just awful. Emphatic, brief condemnation

Real-World Examples

Problem: The restaurant is too cold.

"It's really quite cold in here, isn't it? This is quite annoying because I can't relax and enjoy my meal."

Problem: The service is very slow.

"The waiters always take a long time to bring your drink. It's very disappointing. I've been waiting 20 minutes already."

Problem: The band only plays heavy metal.

"The band only plays heavy metal. That's terrible for a party like this. It's so annoying when you can't dance to what you like."

Intonation in Expressing Dissatisfaction

How you say something matters as much as what you say. Intonation (the rise and fall of your voice) carries emotion.

Stress Patterns

Mark the PRIMARY stress (the loudest, most important word) in these dissatisfaction phrases:

Listen and mark the stress:

It's REALLY bad. (Stress on "really" = emphasis on how bad)

It's TER-rible, isn't it? (Stress on "terrible" = emphasis on the problem)

This is the WORST train I've ever taken. (Stress on "worst" = emphasis on extremeness)

Just AW-ful. (Stress on "awful" = strong emotion)

It's so AN-noying! (Stress on "annoying" + rising tone = more emotional)

It's NOT good. (Stress on "not" = emphasis on negation)

It's VERY dis-AP-point-ing. (Stress on "very" and "pointing" = multiple stresses for emphasis)

How Tone Affects Meaning

  • Rising intonation: Shows frustration or seeks agreement ("Isn't it?")
  • Falling intonation: Shows certainty and finality ("It's terrible.")
  • Loud volume + stress: Shows strong emotion
  • Slow speech: Emphasizes how serious you are

Role Play: The Cruise Ship Scenario

You're on a New Year's Eve cruise with fellow guests. The party is very disappointing.

Your Situation

You are seated at a table with other guests you don't know. The ship is called Titan Nick. You've decided to spend New Year's Eve on this cruise, and the staff have organized a party in the Grand Ballroom. However, the party is very, very disappointing.

Problems to Address

Choose one or more problems to start conversations:

  • The food doesn't taste good.
  • The room is very cold.
  • The waiters always take a long time to bring drinks.
  • The band only plays heavy metal.
  • The captain of the ship appears to be drunk.
  • The tablecloths are covered in stains.

How to Approach the Role Play

Step 1: Choose a problem from the list above

Step 2: Start a conversation with one of your table-mates using an opening line

Step 3: Express your dissatisfaction using appropriate phrases

Step 4: Use correct intonation and stress to show your emotions

Step 5: Continue the conversation by asking questions or sharing more concerns

Example Conversation

You: "Have you tried the food? It's really bad, isn't it?"

Guest: "I know, I've hardly eaten anything."

You: "And this room is so cold. It's very disappointing for a New Year's Eve party. I was expecting something special."

Guest: "I completely agree. The whole evening has been a disaster."

Reflection

After your role play, reflect:

  • Were you able to start a natural conversation?
  • Did your dissatisfaction phrases sound authentic?
  • How did the other guest respond? Did they share your feelings?
  • What would you do differently next time?

Remember: Expressing dissatisfaction is about starting conversations, not being rude. Tone and sincerity matter.
