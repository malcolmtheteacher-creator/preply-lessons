The People We Used to Be

How much have you changed? | B1 English

Are you the same person you were 10 years ago?

Most people say "yes, but different." Scientists say something more surprising...

63 years

In a famous Scottish study, researchers tested children's personalities in 1947, then found the same people in 2012. After 63 years, there was almost no connection between their childhood personality and their adult personality. They had become completely different people.

Source: Harris et al., 2016 - University of Edinburgh

63%

If you are an extroverted teenager, you have a 63% chance of still being extroverted in your 60s. That sounds high, but it means 37% of people completely change their core personality.

Source: Roberts, University of Illinois

Marta, 52, Poland (lives in Spain)

"I used to be so shy. At university, I would sit at the back of every class and never speak. I would always eat lunch alone because talking to people made me nervous. Now? I run a hotel. I talk to strangers every day. My university friends cannot believe it. They say, 'Who is this person?'"

Kenji, 41, Japan (lives in Germany)

"When I was young, I used to be very serious. I would work until midnight every night. I never took holidays. My wife says I was 'a robot.' After my father died, something changed. Now I'm not so focused on work anymore. I've become more relaxed. I cook, I garden, I laugh more."

Think about it: Would your younger self recognise you today? What would surprise them most?

The Science of Change

Psychologists have found that people generally become:

  • More emotionally stable (less worried, calmer)
  • More agreeable (kinder, more patient)
  • More conscientious (more organised, reliable)

This is called the "maturity principle" - we naturally grow and change as we get older.

Famous Quote: "I can't go back to yesterday - because I was a different person then." - Lewis Carroll

Talking About Your Past Self

These structures help you describe who you were and how you've changed.

1. I used to be + adjective

Click to open
Meaning

Describes a past state or characteristic that is no longer true.

Form

Subject + used to be + adjective

Negative: I didn't use to be... | Question: Did you use to be...?

Examples
"I used to be really shy when I was younger."
"She used to be so impatient, but now she's very calm."
"I didn't use to be confident at all."
Pronunciation

/juːst tə bi/ - "used to" sounds like "yoost-tuh" (the 'd' is not pronounced strongly)

Now You Try!

Complete this sentence about yourself: "When I was younger, I used to be more/less..."

2. I used to + verb

Click to open
Meaning

Describes a past habit or regular action that you don't do anymore.

Form

Subject + used to + base verb

Negative: I didn't use to... | Question: Did you use to...?

Examples
"I used to smoke, but I quit five years ago."
"We used to live in the city centre."
"Did you use to play any sports?"
Note

Use this for habits that have completely stopped. If you still do something, don't use "used to."

Now You Try!

What is something you used to do when you were younger, but don't do anymore?

3. I would always...

Click to open
Meaning

Describes repeated past actions, often with nostalgia or emotion. Similar to "used to" but only for actions, not states.

Form

Subject + would + (always/often/never) + base verb

Examples
"When I was a child, I would always wake up early on Saturdays."
"My grandmother would often tell us stories at bedtime."
"I would never ask for help - I was too proud."
Important!

You CANNOT say: "I would be shy" (state) - only "I used to be shy"

You CAN say: "I would hide in my room" (action)

Now You Try!

Think of a childhood memory. Start with: "When I was young, I would always/often/never..."

Describing the Change

Now let's look at how to talk about the change itself.

4. I'm not... anymore / I no longer...

Click to open
Meaning

Emphasises that something has stopped being true. Often used with "used to" for contrast.

Form

Subject + am/is/are + not + adjective + anymore

Subject + no longer + verb/adjective (more formal)

Examples
"I'm not as stressed about work anymore."
"She's not afraid of public speaking anymore."
"I no longer worry about what people think."
Register

"Anymore" = everyday conversation | "No longer" = slightly more formal/written

Now You Try!

Complete: "I used to be ________, but I'm not ________ anymore."

5. I've become more/less...

Click to open
Meaning

Describes a gradual change from the past until now. The change is still relevant today.

Form

Subject + have/has + become + more/less + adjective

Also: "have grown" / "have gotten" (informal)

Examples
"I've become more patient over the years."
"She's become less interested in material things."
"We've grown closer as a family."
Tip

This structure is great for reflecting on personal growth. It sounds thoughtful and mature.

Now You Try!

What have you become more or less of over the years? "I've become more..."

Quick Summary

Structure Use for...
I used to be... Past states/characteristics
I used to + verb Past habits (stopped)
I would always... Repeated past actions (nostalgic)
I'm not... anymore Emphasising change
I've become more/less... Gradual change until now

Your Turn

Choose 2-3 topics that interest you. Use the language from Tabs 2-3!

Your Younger Self

If your 18-year-old self could see you now, what would surprise them most? What would disappoint them? What would make them proud?

Personality Shifts

Are you more or less patient than you used to be? More or less worried? More or less ambitious? What caused these changes?

Habits Lost

What did you use to do that you don't do anymore? Is this a good change or a bad one? Do you miss any old habits?

The Turning Point

Was there a moment, event, or person that changed you significantly? What happened? How are you different now?

What Stayed the Same

Despite all the changes, what is the same about you? What is the "core" that has never changed since childhood?

Future Change

How do you want to change in the next 10 years? What do you hope you'll no longer care about? What do you hope you'll become?
