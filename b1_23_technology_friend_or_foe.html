Debate Language

These phrases help you structure arguments and respond to different points of view.

Introducing Points

What about social media? It can be addictive.
For instance, teenagers spend hours on their phones.
Take me for example—I"ve noticed how technology changes my sleep.
Just look at how people interact in cafés nowadays.
You need to consider the environmental cost of manufacturing devices.

Agreeing and Disagreeing

I understand but not everyone has the same experience.
I can"t agree with that argument because the facts don"t support it.
Exactly! You"ve hit on the real issue here.
I agree to a point, although there"s more to consider.
Yes, that"s very true but what about the benefits for accessibility?

Interrupting Politely

I"d just like to add something important here.
Can I just say something? I think you"ve missed a key point.
I"m sorry to interrupt but I need to challenge that.
Let"s get back to what we were discussing about privacy.

Other Useful Expressions

Yes, you"re absolutely right but don"t forget about innovation.

Mini-Debate: Your Turn

Choose a statement and prepare arguments for and against it using the debate language you"ve learned.

3:00
You have 3 minutes to prepare
1. Social media brings more risks than benefits.

For the argument: Privacy invasion, mental health issues, misinformation spreads rapidly, cyberbullying, addiction.

Against the argument: Connects people globally, supports social movements, creative communities, small business opportunities.

2. Tablets will completely replace laptops within 10 years.

For the argument: Tablets are lighter, more portable, touchscreen is intuitive, apps are becoming more powerful.

Against the argument: Professionals need keyboards and trackpads, file management is different, gaming/design needs more power, habits are hard to break.

3. Governments should heavily control internet access.

For the argument: Protects children from harmful content, prevents cybercrime, stops spread of illegal materials, national security.

Against the argument: Freedom of speech and expression, privacy rights, innovation suffers, creates surveillance state, people find workarounds.

4. E-books will completely replace printed books.

For the argument: Digital books are cheaper, searchable, don"t require physical space, instant access, adjustable text size.

Against the argument: Readers prefer the tactile experience, no eye strain from paper, beautiful design in physical books, libraries strengthen communities.

5. Technology makes humans lazy and dependent.

For the argument: GPS replaces navigation skills, calculators replace mental math, delivery apps replace cooking, reduced physical activity.

Against the argument: Frees time for higher-level thinking, enables people with disabilities, automates tedious tasks, allows focus on creativity.

6. Video games are harmful for teenagers.

For the argument: Addiction potential, violent content, poor sleep habits, reduced social interaction, eye strain.

Against the argument: Develops problem-solving skills, improves hand-eye coordination, builds teamwork, stress relief, vibrant gaming communities.

7. Phone addiction is a serious public health problem.

For the argument: People can"t focus, anxiety from notifications, FOMO, neck pain from usage, sleep disruption, depression.

Against the argument: Tools for communication and work, reduces isolation, people can choose limits, technology supports mental health apps.

8. Tech companies force upgrades just to make more money.

For the argument: Planned obsolescence, slowing down old devices, minor improvements between models, expensive repairs, unfixable batteries.

Against the argument: Security updates are necessary, performance genuinely improves, new features are useful, competition keeps prices reasonable.

Discussion Questions

Think about these questions and share your thoughts. Use the debate language you"ve learned.

What"s your favourite piece of technology?

Why do you love it? How would your daily life be different without it? Would you survive without it?

What"s the most important technological creation in human history?

Is it the printing press, electricity, the internet, or something else? What would the world look like without it?

What technological invention do you think has made the world worse?

Does it have benefits too? Can we fix the problems it created?

What major technology changes have happened in your lifetime?

How have these changes affected how you work, learn, play, and connect with people? What would you tell someone from 30 years ago about life today?
