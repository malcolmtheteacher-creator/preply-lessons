Environmental Problems Around Us
The world faces many environmental challenges. Some affect our air, water, and land. Others threaten plants and animals. Think about these problems:
Air Pollution Cars, factories, and industry release harmful gases into the air.
Endangered Species Many animals and plants are dying out. Hunting, habitat loss, and climate change threaten them.
Household Waste Plastic, paper, and other waste fills landfills and pollutes oceans.
Water Pollution Chemicals and waste contaminate rivers, lakes, and oceans.
Destruction of Green Spaces Forests and natural habitats disappear when land is cleared for cities and farms.
Think about it: Which of these problems affects your country or community most? What examples have you seen or heard about?

Understanding Text Organisation: The American Bison
Read the text below. It shows how authors organise information about problems and recovery.
The American Bison Story
The American bison was once the largest land animal in North America. Before the 1800s, approximately 60 million bison lived across the continent. However, the population fell dramatically to just 500 animals in 1889. This decline was caused by hunting for meat and hides, as well as diseases. Fortunately, people recognised the problem and took action. Bison became protected by law. Conservation programmes helped numbers increase again. Today, there are approximately 350,000 bison in North America.

Conservation Project: Southern White Rhino
Task: Read paragraphs A-F about white rhino conservation. Then match each paragraph to the correct information statement below. Click each statement to reveal the matching paragraph letter.
Time remaining: 5:00
Reading Passage: The White Rhino
A.
The white rhino has five species of rhino living in Africa and Asia. Some of these species have very few members left. The Javan rhino and Sumatran rhino both have fewer than 100 individuals remaining in the wild.
B.
The southern white rhino is a large animal with thick, grey skin. It has two horns on its head: a long horn at the front and a smaller horn behind it. Adults can weigh up to 2,300 kilograms.
C.
In the early 1900s, the southern white rhino was hunted so heavily that only about 50 animals remained. The species was nearly extinct. Without intervention, it would have disappeared completely.
D.
Protection laws were introduced to stop hunting. National parks and reserves were created to provide safe habitats. These actions saved the species from extinction and allowed populations to grow.
E.
As a result of these conservation efforts, southern white rhino numbers increased dramatically. By the 2000s, the population had grown to over 20,000 individuals. This was seen as a major conservation success.
F.
However, poaching remains a serious threat today. Rhino horns are illegally hunted and sold for traditional medicine. Conservation programmes must continue to protect these animals from extinction.
Match the Information:
1. Describe what was done to save the rhino (what actions were taken)
Answer: D — Laws and parks protected the rhinos
2. Explain the danger the species faced (showing the problem)
Answer: C — Only 50 animals remained; species was nearly extinct
3. Give the positive result of conservation (showing what happened next)
Answer: E — Population grew to over 20,000; major success
4. Provide information about different rhino species
Answer: A — Five species; Javan and Sumatran have fewer than 100
5. Describe the physical appearance of the southern white rhino
Answer: B — Large, two horns, weighs up to 2,300 kg
6. Explain the current threat to rhino survival
Answer: F — Poaching for horns; sold for traditional medicine
Reflection and Discussion

1. Attitudes to Hunting

In some countries, hunting is allowed or traditional. In others, it is illegal. What is the situation in your country? Do you think hunting animals is acceptable? Why or why not?

2. Safari Tourism and Conservation

Safari holidays bring money to African countries. Some people argue this helps preserve wildlife. Others worry about animal stress and habitat damage. Do you think safari tourism helps protect endangered animals?

3. Environmental Projects Nearby

Are there any environmental protection projects in your area or country? What do they do? Have you seen changes in the environment or wildlife because of these projects?

4. Endangered Species You Know

What endangered animals do you know about? Why are they endangered? Do you think they can be saved? What would need to happen?

5. Zoos of the Future

Some people say zoos are important for conservation. Others believe wild animals should only live in nature. How do you feel about zoos? Should they keep endangered species to protect them, or is this wrong?
