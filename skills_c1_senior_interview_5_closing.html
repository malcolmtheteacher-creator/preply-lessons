Your Questions, Your Terms, Your Close

Ask senior-level questions, discuss compensation with confidence, and close the interview well.

Today: you will ask sharp questions, negotiate professionally and finish with a clear plan so you can leave a strong final impression.

The last ten minutes count

The end of an interview is not a formality. Your questions show how you think, your negotiation shows how you handle pressure, and your closing is what the interviewers remember.

Language you can reuse

Each card describes a moment in the interview. Decide what you would say, then check the phrases.

Situation: You ask about success and priorities in the role.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “How does the firm define success for this role in the first year?”
  • “What are the biggest challenges the team is facing?”
  • “What would distinguish an outstanding hire from a good one?”
  • “Where do you see the greatest opportunity over the next three years?”
Situation: You ask about culture, decision-making and leadership.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “How are major decisions made between the leadership team and the practices?”
  • “What would you say is the firm’s biggest strength, and where does it want to improve?”
  • “How does the leadership team support new partners or directors?”
Situation: You open the conversation about compensation.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “Could we talk about the overall package?”
  • “Based on my experience and the scope of the role, I’d expect a total package in the range of [range].”
  • “I’m open to discussing the structure: base, variable and any long-term incentives.”
  • “What does the variable component depend on?”

Talk about the total package and the scope of the role. Give a range, not a single figure.

Situation: You negotiate an offer that is below your expectation.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “Thank you, I’m very enthusiastic. May I have a few days to consider it?”
  • “That is slightly below what I was expecting. Is there flexibility?”
  • “I’d be more comfortable at [figure], given [reason].”
  • “If we could meet in the middle at [figure], I’d be ready to accept.”
  • “I’d like to understand the full picture before I respond.”
Situation: You describe your plan for the first three months.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “In my first thirty days, I’d focus on listening and learning.”
  • “By day sixty, I’d have mapped the key relationships and agreed priorities.”
  • “By ninety days, I’d aim to have delivered one visible early win.”
  • “I’d build relationships with [groups] before changing anything.”
Situation: You close the interview and follow up.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “Thank you for your time. I’m even more interested after our conversation.”
  • “Is there anything about my background you’d like me to clarify?”
  • “What are the next steps, and what is the timeline?”
  • “Thank you again for meeting me on [day]. Our discussion about [topic] confirmed my interest.”

Upgrade the sentence

Rewrite each sentence so it sounds like a confident senior candidate. Then compare.

Make it sound senior: “I have no questions.”
I do have a few. Could you tell me how the firm defines success for this role in the first year?
Make it sound senior: “How much money will I get?”
Could we talk about the overall package, including how the variable element is structured?
Make it sound senior: “I want more money than this.”
That is slightly below what I was expecting. Given the scope of the role, is there flexibility?
Make it sound senior: “I will change many things when I start.”

Which candidate sounds more senior?

Read both answers. Decide which one an interviewer would rate higher, and say why before you click.

“Do you have any questions for us?”
A“Yes, two. What does the leadership team see as the biggest opportunity for this practice in the next three years? And what would distinguish an outstanding hire from a good one in the first year?”
B“No, I think you explained everything. When do I get a decision, and how many holiday days do I have?”
A asks strategic questions that show interest in the organisation’s direction. B has no questions and focuses on personal benefits.
You receive an offer lower than you hoped.
A“Yes, that’s fine. I accept the offer. Thank you very much.”
B“Thank you, I’m very enthusiastic about the role. The base is slightly below what I was expecting, given the scope. Is there flexibility, or could we look at the variable component?”
B thanks them, shows commitment and negotiates politely. A accepts immediately and gives up the chance to discuss terms.
“What would you do in your first ninety days?”
A“I would change the structure and fix everything that is not working.”
B“In the first thirty days, I’d listen and meet the key people. By sixty, I’d agree priorities with my manager and the team. By ninety, I’d aim to deliver one visible early win.”
B is structured and respectful of what exists. A sounds arrogant and gives no plan.

Plan your first ninety days

A clear plan shows you think like a senior hire. Write your own plan for a role you would like.

Interview question

“What would you do in your first ninety days?”

Build your answer in three parts

Listen: “In the first thirty days, I’d focus on listening. I’d meet the leadership team, the key clients and my own team, and understand what is working and what is not.”

Align: “By day sixty, I’d have mapped the key relationships and agreed three priorities with my manager.”

Deliver: “By day ninety, I’d aim to deliver one visible early win, for example advancing a client opportunity, and have a plan for the following year.”

Notice: listen before acting, involve others, and commit to one visible result.

Speak

Close the notes and give your answer aloud in about ninety seconds. Then give it a second time with one improvement.

Write a short follow-up note (three or four sentences) to send the day after an interview.

“Dear Ms Laurent, thank you again for meeting me yesterday. Our discussion about the practice’s growth plans confirmed my interest in the role. I’m confident I could contribute to the growth plan we discussed for the practice. Please let me know if I can provide anything further. I look forward to the next steps.”

Refer to one specific topic from the conversation so the note does not sound generic.

Questions to ask

  4. Who are the most important people I would work with in the first months?

Compensation and expectations

  1. What are your salary expectations?
  2. How flexible are you on the structure of the package?
  3. What would make you accept this offer, and what would make you decline?
  4. How do you prefer to be measured and rewarded?

Your first months and closing

  1. What would you do in your first ninety days?
  2. What do you need from us to be successful?
  3. Is there anything about your application you would like to clarify?
  4. Why should we hire you rather than the other finalists?
