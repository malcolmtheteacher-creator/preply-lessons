Key words

improvise (verb) — to make up music, words or a plan as you go along, without preparing it. Example: "She forgot her notes, so she had to improvise the rest of the speech."
swing (noun) — a relaxed, bouncing rhythm in which the first of each pair of short notes is held a little longer than the second. Example: "The band played with so much swing that nobody could keep still."
riff (noun) — a short musical phrase that is repeated, often behind a solo. Example: "The whole song is built on one catchy guitar riff."
virtuoso (noun) — a performer with outstanding technical skill. Example: "By the age of twenty he was already a piano virtuoso."
groundbreaking (adjective) — completely new and changing the way things are done. Example: "It was a groundbreaking album that every later band copied."
fusion (noun) — a mix of two or more different styles that creates something new. Example: "The restaurant serves a fusion of Japanese and Peruvian cooking."
laid-back (adjective) — relaxed and calm, never in a hurry. Example: "I love the laid-back feel of Sunday-morning music."
mellow (adjective) — soft, warm and gentle, without anything harsh. Example: "The singer has a mellow voice that is perfect late at night."
frantic (adjective) — very fast and wild, almost out of control. Example: "The drummer kept up a frantic pace for the whole set."
pave the way (phrase) — to make it possible or easier for something to happen later. Example: "Early experiments with radio paved the way for television."
