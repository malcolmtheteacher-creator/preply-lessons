What does akhirah mean in Islam?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Akhirah means 'the afterlife' or 'the hereafter.' It is the belief that after death, the soul is judged by Allah and enters either Jannah (paradise) or Jahannam (hell). It is one of the core beliefs in Islam.

What happens on the Day of Judgement (Yawm al-Qiyamah)?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: All humans are resurrected and stand before Allah. Their deeds are weighed on the Mizan (scales of justice). They must cross the Sirat (a narrow bridge over hell). The righteous enter Jannah; the sinful face Jahannam.

What is Barzakh?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Barzakh is the state between death and the Day of Judgement. The soul remains conscious but is separated from the body. It is a waiting period before the final resurrection and judgement.

How does belief in akhirah affect Muslim behaviour?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: It motivates ethical living because Muslims know every action is recorded and will be judged. Fear of Jahannam discourages sin, while hope for Jannah encourages good deeds, charity, honesty, and patience in hardship.

Risalah: The Concept of Prophethood

Risalah means 'prophethood' or 'messengership' in Islam. It is the belief that Allah has communicated with humanity throughout history by sending prophets (nabis) and messengers (rasuls) to guide people to the straight path.

Belief in the prophets is one of the Six Articles of Faith in Sunni Islam and is also central to Shi'a belief. Muslims believe that Allah sent approximately 124,000 prophets to every nation and community, though only 25 are named in the Qur'an.

The role of prophets:

  • Receive revelation from Allah, usually through the angel Jibril (Gabriel)
  • Guide humanity to worship Allah alone (Tawhid) and reject idolatry (shirk)
  • Warn people against sin and remind them of the Day of Judgement
  • Model righteous living — prophets are the best examples of how to live
  • Establish justice and fairness in society

Key point: Prophets are human, not divine. They eat, sleep, and have families. However, they are protected from major sin (ismah) — this means Allah preserves them from committing serious wrongdoing so their message stays trustworthy.

The Key Prophets in Islam

While all prophets carry the same core message — worship Allah alone — certain prophets hold special significance:

Adam: The first human and the first prophet. Allah taught Adam the names of all things and commanded the angels to bow before him. Adam's story teaches that all humanity shares a common origin.

Ibrahim (Abraham): Known as the 'father of monotheism' and 'Khalilullah' (Friend of God). Ibrahim smashed the idols of his people, was willing to sacrifice his son Isma'il when Allah commanded it (commemorated in Eid ul-Adha), and built the Ka'bah in Makkah with Isma'il.

Musa (Moses): Received the Torah (Tawrat) from Allah. Led the Israelites out of slavery in Egypt. One of the most frequently mentioned prophets in the Qur'an — his story teaches courage, faith, and obedience to Allah against oppression.

Dawud (David): Received the Psalms (Zabur). Known for his wisdom and justice as a ruler. The Qur'an describes him as a righteous king who judged fairly between people.

Isa (Jesus): A major prophet in Islam — but not the Son of God. Muslims believe Isa was born of the Virgin Maryam through a miracle, received the Injil (Gospel), performed miracles by Allah's permission, and was not crucified but was raised to heaven. He will return before the Day of Judgement.

Muhammad: The Seal of the Prophets

Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) holds a unique position in Islam as the final prophet — the 'Seal of the Prophets' (Khatam an-Nabiyyin).

"Muhammad is not the father of any of your men, but he is the Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets." (Qur'an, Surah 33:40)

Why is Muhammad so important?

  • Final revelation: The Qur'an, revealed to Muhammad through Jibril over 23 years, is the complete and final message from Allah to humanity
  • Perfect example: Muhammad's life (the Sunnah) is the model for how Muslims should live — from prayer and charity to family life and justice
  • Hadith: The recorded sayings and actions of Muhammad, collected by scholars like Bukhari and Muslim, form the second source of authority in Islam after the Qur'an
  • Universal message: Unlike earlier prophets sent to specific nations, Muhammad's message is for all humanity until the end of time

The Shahadah connection: The Islamic declaration of faith — "There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is His messenger" — shows how central Muhammad's prophethood is to Muslim identity. Every Muslim affirms this belief daily.

Sunni and Shi'a Views on Prophethood

Both Sunni and Shi'a Muslims agree on the importance of prophethood and accept all the prophets mentioned in the Qur'an. However, there are some differences in emphasis:

Sunni perspective: Sunnis emphasise that all prophets are equal in their role as messengers of Tawhid. After Muhammad's death, leadership passed to the four Rightly Guided Caliphs (Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali) chosen by the community. The prophetic line ended with Muhammad.

Shi'a perspective: Shi'a Muslims give special importance to the Ahl al-Bayt — the Prophet's family, especially his cousin and son-in-law Ali and his descendants. They believe that spiritual authority passed through the Imams (descendants of Ali), not the elected caliphs. While prophethood ended with Muhammad, divine guidance continues through the Imams.

What both agree on: Muhammad is the final prophet. The Qur'an is the word of Allah. All prophets taught Tawhid. The prophets are sinless in their prophetic mission (ismah).

Why Prophethood Matters for Muslim Life Today

Belief in the prophets isn't just a historical fact for Muslims — it shapes daily life and practice:

1. Following the Sunnah: Muslims look to Muhammad's example for guidance on everything from prayer rituals to business ethics, marriage, and conflict resolution. The Hadith collections provide detailed guidance.

2. Respect for all prophets: Muslims say "peace be upon him" (PBUH) after mentioning any prophet's name. This shows reverence and acknowledgement that all prophets came from the same God with the same core message.

3. Moral examples: Each prophet's story teaches specific virtues — Ibrahim's courage and obedience, Musa's perseverance against injustice, Isa's compassion, Muhammad's mercy and justice. Muslims draw on these stories for moral guidance.

4. Unity of faith: Believing in all prophets connects Islam to the Abrahamic tradition. Muslims see Judaism, Christianity, and Islam as part of one continuous revelation from Allah, with each prophet building on the message of the last.

5. The Qur'an as final authority: Because Muhammad is the Seal of the Prophets, the Qur'an is the final, uncorrupted word of God. This gives Muslims confidence that their scripture is complete and authoritative.

Review: Risalah — Prophethood

What does risalah mean?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Risalah means prophethood or messengership. It is the belief that Allah sent prophets throughout history to guide humanity to worship Him alone and follow the straight path.

Name five key prophets in Islam and one fact about each.

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Adam — first prophet and first human. Ibrahim — father of monotheism, built the Ka'bah. Musa — received the Torah, led the Israelites from Egypt. Isa — born of the Virgin Maryam, a prophet not the Son of God. Muhammad — Seal of the Prophets, received the Qur'an.

What does 'Seal of the Prophets' mean, and which Qur'anic verse supports this?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: It means Muhammad is the final prophet — no more prophets will come after him. Surah 33:40 states: 'Muhammad is not the father of any of your men, but he is the Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets.'

What is the Sunnah and why is it important?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The Sunnah is the example and practice of Prophet Muhammad — how he lived, what he said, and what he did. It is recorded in the Hadith collections and is the second source of authority in Islam after the Qur'an. Muslims follow the Sunnah to model their lives on the Prophet's example.

What does ismah mean in relation to prophets?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Ismah means 'protection from sin.' Muslims believe prophets are protected by Allah from committing major sins, so their message and example remain trustworthy and pure. Prophets are human but divinely guided.

How do Sunni and Shi'a views on prophethood differ?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Both agree Muhammad is the final prophet and all Qur'anic prophets are accepted. Sunnis emphasise equality of all prophets and elected caliphs after Muhammad. Shi'a give special status to the Ahl al-Bayt (Prophet's family) and believe divine guidance continues through the Imams descended from Ali.
