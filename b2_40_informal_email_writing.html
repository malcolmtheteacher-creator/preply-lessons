Why Write Informal Emails at Work?

Informal emails are used to build relationships with colleagues, share updates with teammates you know well, and create a friendly, approachable professional tone.

The BLADE Structure for Planning Informal Emails

Use this framework to structure and organize your informal email:

BLADE Components

B - Background
L - Link
A - Action
D - Details
E - Ending

What to Include

Start with context or a friendly greeting
Connect to previous conversation or shared interest
State what you want or need from the reader
Provide supporting information and examples
Close warmly and reinforce relationship

Example BLADE Email Structure

Background: "Hi Sarah! I hope you're doing well. I wanted to reach out about the project we discussed last week."

Link: "Remember how we talked about improving our team communication? Well, I've been thinking about that conversation."

Action: "I'd like to propose we try having weekly sync-ups on Tuesdays."

Details: "I think 30 minutes would be enough time, and we could cover project updates and any blockers the team is facing."

Ending: "Let me know what you think! Looking forward to hearing from you. Cheers!"

Planning Questions:

Before you write, ask yourself: What background does the reader need? What have we discussed before? What do I want them to do? What details support my request?

Features of Informal Language in Emails

Informal emails use different language than formal ones. Click to compare:

Key Language Features

Feature Formal Example Informal Example
Sentence Structure Long, complex sentences with multiple clauses Short, simple sentences that are easy to read
Voice Passive voice: "The report has been submitted" Active voice: "I've submitted the report"
Contractions Avoid contractions: "I will" not "I'll" Use contractions: "I'm", "we've", "it's"
Personal Pronouns Impersonal: "One might suggest" Personal: "I think" or "We've noticed"
Tone Distant and professional Friendly and warm; personality shows
Vocabulary Formal words: "commence", "endeavor" Everyday words: "start", "try"

Practice Identifying Informal Features

Which sentence is more informal?

A) "The results of last year's survey have been put in the report."

B) "I've put the survey results in the report."

Show Answer
Answer: B - It uses the active voice ("I've put"), contractions ("I've"), and shorter structure. Sentence A uses passive voice and longer, more formal construction.

How would you make this more informal?

"I suggest that this be added to the agenda for the meeting tomorrow."

Show Answer
More informal version: "Could we add this to tomorrow's meeting agenda?" or "I think we should add this to tomorrow's agenda." This uses simpler words ("add" not "suggest"), active voice, and shorter sentences.

Rewrite in informal style:

"My apologies for not being able to finish the tasks assigned."

Show Answer
More informal version: "Sorry I couldn't finish the tasks." or "I'm sorry I haven't finished the tasks yet." This uses simpler language, contractions, and active voice.
