FOR the motion
  • Railways, roads, hospitals and schools built in colonies
  • English became a global lingua franca — economic opportunity today
  • 56 Commonwealth nations voluntarily remain — ongoing partnership
  • Parliamentary democracy and rule of law spread globally
  • The Great Exhibition (1851) — Britain led global industrial progress
AGAINST the motion
  • Transatlantic slave trade — estimated 12 million people enslaved
  • Colonies kept deliberately underdeveloped to serve British extraction
  • Indigenous languages, religions and cultures systematically suppressed
  • Amritsar massacre, Bengal famine, Mau Mau — direct colonial violence
  • Oliver Twist's world: poverty and exploitation at home AND in the empire

My methodology choices

Presenting Lesson 2 to the panel — key phrases

In Lesson 1 I used PPP + CLIL to build content knowledge. In Lesson 2 I shifted to CLT because the objective had changed — from knowing about the empire to evaluating it. Methodology should serve the objective, not the reverse.
The debate creates a genuine information gap in the sense that neither side knows what the other will argue — students must listen, process, and respond in real time. This is authentic communicative pressure.
The two images — the Great Exhibition and the workhouse — embody the debate motion visually. Students feel the contradiction before they articulate it. This activates critical thinking before any language input.
The progression across the two lessons reflects Bloom's Taxonomy: Lesson 1 builds knowledge and comprehension; Lesson 2 requires analysis, evaluation and synthesis — the higher-order thinking skills.
I used delayed, selective error correction during the debate — noting errors silently and feeding back after the task. This maintains fluency and communicative confidence, consistent with CLT principles.
Assigning the disruptive student the opening speaker role applies a positive behaviour support approach — the student is given status and responsibility rather than restriction, which research shows is more effective for engagement.
