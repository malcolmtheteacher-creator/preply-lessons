The Bet

Anton Chekhov (1889) — a banker and a young lawyer make a strange bet, and one man waits fifteen years

Original version available: read this lesson at B2/C1 level →

Read the story in three parts

This is a famous short story, told here in simple English. Read each part, then stop and talk about the questions. The end is a big surprise — read slowly and do not jump ahead.

Part 1 — The bet

Many years ago, a rich banker had a party. At the party, the people talked about prison. Which is worse, they asked: many years in prison, or no life at all?

A young lawyer was there too. He and the banker did not agree. They started to argue.

The banker was angry. "You cannot stay alone in one room for fifteen years," he said. "I bet you two million! If you do it, the money is yours."

The young lawyer was proud. "Yes! I will do it," he said. "I will stay alone for fifteen years. Then you will give me the two million."

Stop and talk:
  • What did the banker and the lawyer argue about?
  • What is the bet? How long must the lawyer stay alone?
  • Do you think the lawyer is clever or foolish to say yes? Why?

Part 2 — Fifteen years alone

So the young lawyer went to live in a small house. He could not go out. He could not see his family or his friends. He was completely alone.

For the first year, he was very sad. The days were long and quiet. But then he started to read. He read one book, then another book, then many, many books.

The years passed. He learned new languages. He read about history, science, and God. He thought a lot. Slowly, year after year, the young lawyer changed. He was not the same man now.

Stop and talk:
  • How did the lawyer feel in the first year? Why?
  • What did he do for fifteen years in the small house?
  • Do you think you could live alone for so long? What would you do?

Part 3 — The last night

Fifteen years is a long time. The banker was not rich now. He lost most of his money, and he was afraid. "Tomorrow I must pay two million," he thought. "But I do not have it!"

On the last night, the banker went to the small house. The lawyer was asleep. On the table, there was a letter. The banker read it.

In the letter, the lawyer wrote something surprising. "Now, books and ideas are more important to me than money. I do not want your two million. So I will leave this room a few hours early, before the time ends."

And he did. A few hours before the fifteen years ended, the lawyer left the room. He lost the bet — but he chose to lose it.

The end — stop and talk:
  • Why was the banker afraid on the last night?
  • The lawyer did not want the money. Why not? What changed him?
  • He left the room early on purpose, so he lost the bet. Why do you think he did this?

Past simple and time words

This story happened in the past. We use the past simple to tell stories about the past, and we use small time words to say when things happened.

Past simple = a finished action. He made a bet. She read a book. They lived alone.
Many verbs add -ed (talk → talked, live → lived). But some are special: make → made, read → read, go → went, lose → lost.

for, since, and ago — the easy rule

for = how long (a period of time). for fifteen years, for two hours, for a long time.

since = the start point (when it began). since 1889, since Monday, since the party.

ago = how long before now. It comes after the time. fifteen years ago, two days ago, a long time ago.

How the writer surprises you

A good short story is more than just words. The writer uses tricks to make you feel something. Here are two simple ones from this story.

1. The surprise ending (the "twist")

For the whole story, we think the lawyer wants the two million. He waited fifteen long years for it! Then, at the end, he does not want it. He even leaves early, so he loses the bet. This big surprise at the end is called a twist. It makes us think about the whole story again.

2. The man who changed

At the start, the lawyer is young and proud. He wants money. But the books and the quiet years change him. At the end, he is a different man. Money is not important to him now. When a person changes inside like this, we feel the story is true and deep.
