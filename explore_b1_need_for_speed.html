Ancient Speed

Roman chariot races reached 60 km/h

That's the same as a modern city speed limit. 2,000 years ago, 250,000 Romans packed the Circus Maximus to watch. Drivers were celebrities — like Formula One drivers today. Many died. The crowd loved it.

Why do you think humans have always loved watching speed — even when it's dangerous?

✈️
Breaking the Impossible

Chuck Yeager and the sound barrier (1947)

Engineers believed planes would break apart if they flew faster than sound. Pilots called it "the demon in the sky." Chuck Yeager flew the X-1 rocket plane and broke through at 1,127 km/h. He had two broken ribs from a horse riding accident and told nobody.

People said it was impossible. What "impossible" things have we actually done? Can you think of examples?

📡
Speed of Information

From 3 months to 3 milliseconds

In 1800, a letter from London to New York took about 3 months by ship. The telegraph cut that to minutes. Today, data crosses the Atlantic in about 3 milliseconds through fibre-optic cables on the ocean floor. Speed isn't just about vehicles — it's about information too.

Has the speed of information made our lives better, or more stressful? Or both?

😶
The Slow Movement

Italy started the global backlash against speed

In 1986, Carlo Petrini protested against a McDonald's opening near the Spanish Steps in Rome. He started the Slow Food movement — the idea that faster isn't always better. It became Slow Living, Slow Cities, Slow Travel. Today, the "slow movement" is worth billions and growing.

The Slow Movement started in Italy! What do you think — is going slower sometimes better?
