At the Pharmacy

You don't feel well. You have a cough. Now you are at the pharmacy — time to say what is wrong, ask for medicine, and learn how to take it.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

You are not well. You have a cough. It started a few days ago, and now you can't sleep at night. This morning you say, "OK, I need some help." So you walk to the pharmacy near your house.

Nobody can help you if you say nothing. So you must do two things: say what is wrong, in simple words — and then listen. How much do you take? When? Is it safe with your other medicine? These small questions are important.

Behind the counter is the pharmacist. They are calm and kind, and they know a lot about medicine. They will help you — you only need to tell them the problem.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. When you are ill, what do you do? Do you go to the pharmacy, go to the doctor, or stay in bed?
  2. How do you say "I have a cough" or "my head hurts" in your language? Is it easy or hard to say?

Who's who

You
The Customer 🤧

Tired and not well. You want to say what is wrong, get some medicine, and learn how to take it.

The other person
The Pharmacist 💊

Calm and kind. They know a lot about medicine. They will help you — and they always check what is safe.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. It is OK to speak slowly. It is OK to say "Sorry, again please?" if you don't understand. Good customers ask questions!

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for how the customer says what is wrong, and how they check the medicine.

PharmacistHello. Can I help you?
YouHello. Yes, can you help me? I don't feel well.
PharmacistI'm sorry. What is the problem?
YouI have a bad cough. It started three days ago, and I can't sleep at night. But I don't have a fever.
PharmacistOK. This medicine is good for a cough. It is not strong.
YouThank you. How do I take it?
PharmacistTwo spoons, three times a day.
YouTwo spoons, three times a day. Before or after food?
PharmacistAfter food, please. Not on an empty stomach.
YouOK. And is it safe with paracetamol? I take it for my head.
PharmacistYes, it is safe. But look at the box — don't take too much.
YouThank you very much. That is very kind.
PharmacistYou're welcome. Drink lots of water and rest. Feel better soon!

From memory — don't look up!

1. What is wrong with the customer? Say two things.

2. How does the customer take the medicine? (How much? How many times? Before or after food?)

3. The customer asks one safety question. What is it?

1. The customer has a bad cough (for three days) and can't sleep at night. There is no fever.
2. Two spoons, three times a day, after food (not on an empty stomach).
3. "Is it safe with paracetamol?" — because the customer takes paracetamol for their head. (The answer: yes, but don't take too much — look at the box.)

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use in any pharmacy. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it slowly and be polite. You do not need long sentences. Short and clear is best. And it is always OK to ask: "Sorry, again please?" A good pharmacist is happy to help you.

🚪 Asking for help

Phrase 1

You arrive. You want to ask for help in a nice, soft way. Which sentence is polite?

A. "Give me medicine."
B. "Can you help me? I don't feel well."
C. "I am ill. Quick, please."
B — "Can you help me? I don't feel well."
"Can you help me?" is soft and polite. A and C sound like an order. People help you more when you are kind. Also good: "Excuse me, can you help me?"
Phrase 2

You want to say you have caught a small illness. Complete it: "I think I have a ___."

"I think I have a cold."
Use "I think I have a…" for a small illness: "I think I have a cold", "I think I have a bug". "I think" is soft — you are not 100% sure, and that is fine.

🤒 Saying what is wrong

Phrase 3

You want to say your cough is bad and it stops your sleep. Complete it: "I have a bad cough, and I can't ___ at night."

"I have a bad cough, and I can't sleep at night."
"I have a…" is the easy way to say a symptom: "I have a headache", "I have a cold". Add "I can't sleep" to say how bad it is.
Phrase 4

You want to say when the problem began. Put the words in order:

three days agostartedit
"It started three days ago."
"It started … ago" tells the pharmacist how long you have been ill: "It started two days ago", "It started last week". This helps them choose the right medicine.

💊 Asking for medicine and how to take it

Phrase 5

You want the pharmacist to give you something for the cough. Which sentence is best?

A. "What is the cure?"
B. "I want strong medicine."
C. "Can you give me something for it?"
C — "Can you give me something for it?"
"Can you give me something for…?" lets the pharmacist choose the best medicine for you. Also good: "What can I take for a cough?"
Phrase 6

The pharmacist gives you the medicine. You want to know when to take it. Complete it: "How do I take it — before or after ___?"

"How do I take it — before or after food?"
Always ask "How do I take it?" Then check the details: "Before or after food?", "How many times a day?" This is how you take medicine safely.

🛡️ Checking it is safe, and saying thank you

Phrase 7

You already take another medicine. You want to know the two are OK together. Complete it: "Is it safe ___ my other medicine?"

"Is it safe with my other medicine?"
"Is it safe with…?" is a very important question. It stops two medicines from clashing. You can also say: "Can I take it with paracetamol?"
Phrase 8

The pharmacist helped you a lot. You want to say a warm, big thank you. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you very much. That is very kind."
"That is very kind" tells the person you noticed their help. Warm words make people happy to help you. You can also say: "Thank you so much!"

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in any pharmacy, and some work in shops and cafés too. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"I have a ___." — Think of a problem you can have. What is it? (a cough, a headache, a cold…)

"Can you give me something for ___?" — What do you want medicine for?

"Is it safe with ___?" — Name a medicine or drink you want to check.

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — ask for help → say what is wrong → ask for medicine → check how to take it and that it is safe → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your pharmacy. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the customer 🤧

The pharmacist speaks — you answer. Say what is wrong, then check the medicine before you leave.

PharmacistHello. Can I help you?
PharmacistI'm sorry to hear that. What is the problem?
PharmacistOK. This medicine is good for that. It is not strong.
PharmacistTwo spoons, three times a day.
PharmacistAfter food, please. Anything else?
PharmacistYes, it is safe. Look at the box — don't take too much.

Round 2 — now YOU are the pharmacist 💊

Change places. You work at the pharmacy. Be calm and helpful. Choose the medicine, and tell them how to take it. Remember to check what is safe.

CustomerHello, can you help me? I have a headache. It started this morning.
CustomerIt is behind my eyes, and it is worse on the computer.
CustomerThank you. Is it safe with my other medicine?
CustomerThank you very much. That is very kind.
The Twist

🌩️ The customer wants a strong medicine

One more time — no help this round. You are the pharmacist. A customer says: "I have a big day tomorrow. Give me the strongest medicine you have!" But then they tell you something important: they already take another medicine. Now you are not sure it is safe. What do you say? Stay calm and kind. You must NOT give a medicine when you are not sure. A good pharmacist says: "I'm sorry, I can't give you that. Please see a doctor." Start the timer and try!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I asked for help in a soft, polite way.
  • I said what was wrong with "I have a…".
  • I asked how to take the medicine (how much, when).
  • I checked it was safe with "Is it safe with…?".
  • I said "thank you" warmly at the end.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

In 1348, a terrible illness came to Europe. People call it the Black Death. Nobody knew what it was, and nobody knew how to stop it.

People went to the "medicine man" for help. But they got flowers to smell and strange drinks — things that did not work. There was no real medicine, and no good advice.

Today, you went to the pharmacy and got real medicine that works — and a clear answer about what is safe. That is a big, wonderful change. You only had to ask.

Two questions to talk about

  1. Long ago, people took medicine that did not work. Today, how do we know a medicine is good? Do you trust it because of the science, or because a person tells you?
  2. Sometimes a good pharmacist says "No, see a doctor." Is it good to say "I'm not sure, so no"? When do you say this in your life?
