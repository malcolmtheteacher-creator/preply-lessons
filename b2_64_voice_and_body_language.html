Voice and Body Language

Public Speaking Delivery Techniques

Effective Body Language for Presentations

Non-verbal communication is crucial for confident public speaking.

What Creates a Calm, Credible Image?

  • Posture: Stand upright with shoulders relaxed. Avoid slouching or leaning.
  • Eye Contact: Look at your audience. This builds trust and engagement.
  • Hand Gestures: Use natural, purposeful movements to emphasize points. Avoid fidgeting.
  • Facial Expression: Smile and show genuine interest in your topic.
  • Movement: Move purposefully across the space. Avoid pacing nervously.

Common Bad Habits to Avoid

Which body language habits distract your audience?

  • Playing with keys, pens, or jewelry
  • Crossing arms (appears defensive)
  • Constant pacing back and forth
  • Avoiding eye contact
  • Standing behind furniture or podium too much
  • Swaying or shifting weight constantly
  • Touching your face or hair repeatedly

Key Point: Your body language should support your message, not distract from it. Authenticity matters more than perfection.

Techniques to Maximize Vocal Delivery

Key Vocal Elements

  • Pace: Vary your speed. Slow down for important information, speed up for excitement.
  • Volume: Speak loudly enough to be heard. Vary volume for emphasis.
  • Tone: Show emotion and conviction through your voice.
  • Stress: Emphasize key words and phrases.
  • Pauses: Use silence strategically to let information sink in.

Why Variety Matters

A monotone voice loses audience attention. By varying pace, volume, and tone, you:

  • Keep your audience engaged
  • Emphasize important points
  • Show confidence and credibility
  • Make your message more memorable

Chunking & Pausing Strategy

Chunking: Breaking speech into meaningful units, each ending with a stressed word followed by a pause.

Example Speech: Water and Coke

Read this first without chunking—notice how it runs together:

"The world's most popular drink is water. You probably knew that already, after all, it's a basic requirement of life on earth. But did you know that the world's second most popular drink is coke? And that the human race drinks six million cokes a day? Now, let's put that into some kind of perspective. It means that every week of every year people drink enough coke to fill a stadium."

Now With Chunking and Pausing

Notice the slashes (/) indicate where you pause, and [notes] explain how to deliver each chunk:

/ The world's most popular drink /
/ is water / [slower]
/ You probably knew that already. / [faster]
/ After all, /
/ it's a basic requirement of life on Earth /
/ But /
/ did you know / [slower—build interest]
/ that the world's second most popular drink /
/ is Coke? / [slower—new information]

And that the human race drinks six million cokes a day? Now, let's put that into some kind of perspective. It means that every week of every year people drink enough coke to fill a stadium.

Compare: Does the chunked version sound more engaging? More natural? More memorable?

How to Mark Your Speech

  1. Read your speech aloud slowly
  2. Mark natural pauses with slashes (/)
  3. Underline words you want to stress
  4. Add notes: [slower], [faster], [pause for effect]
  5. Practice reading it multiple times

Putting It All Together

Deliver a short speech using effective body language and vocal delivery techniques.

Preparation Steps

Step 1: Prepare Your Speech

  • Choose a speech excerpt (2-3 minutes)
  • Mark chunks and pauses in the text
  • Plan to vary speed: fast for excitement, slow for new information
  • Use good body language and limit distracting gestures
  • Practice at least 3 times

Step 2: Deliver Your Speech

  • Stand with good posture
  • Make eye contact with your audience
  • Use your marked pauses and chunking
  • Vary your pace intentionally
  • Use natural hand gestures
  • Speak with confidence and conviction

Feedback Template

If working with a partner, provide feedback on these areas:

Body Language: Did they maintain good posture? Was eye contact natural? Were gestures purposeful?

Vocal Delivery: Did they vary pace and volume? Were pauses used effectively? Did stress fall on important words?

Overall Impact: How confident did they sound? Was the message clear and engaging?
