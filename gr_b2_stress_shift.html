The idea

In English, the SAME sentence can mean completely different things depending on which word you stress. This game is about hearing and feeling that difference.

Example: "I didn't say she stole the money."

Stress "I" → Someone else said it, not me.
Stress "say" → I didn't say it — I implied it.
Stress "she" → She didn't steal it — someone else did.
Stress "stole" → She didn't steal it — she borrowed it.
Stress "money" → She stole something, but not money.

How each round works

A sentence appears on screen. Tap a word to stress it. Then:

Step 1: Say the sentence out loud, stressing that word.
Step 2: Explain what that stress IMPLIES — what hidden meaning does it create?
Step 3: The other person picks a DIFFERENT word. Say it again with the new stress. New meaning.

Take turns picking which word to stress. Push each other to find the strangest or most surprising meaning shift.

Quick warm-up

Say this sentence 3 different ways, stressing a different word each time: "You want ME to do THAT right NOW?" How does the feeling change each time?

The Stress Challenge

Now flip it around. Instead of seeing the sentence and choosing the stress, you'll see the MEANING — and you have to figure out which word to stress.

Reflection

Which sentence had the most surprising stress shift? Was there a meaning you didn't expect?
In your language, does stress work the same way? Can you change the meaning of a sentence just by emphasising a different word? Give an example.
Think about misunderstandings. Have you ever said something and someone understood it differently because of HOW you said it — not WHAT you said?

Real life

Sentence stress is one of the biggest reasons for misunderstandings in English — especially on the phone or in meetings where you can't see body language. Next time you hear a native speaker say something that feels "loaded," listen for which word they stress.
