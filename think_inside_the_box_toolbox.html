63 Tools for Thinking

The grids, pyramids, loops, curves and diagrams that people use to think clearly — in business, psychology, medicine, schools, science, the army and everyday life.

← Back to the lessons and the map

What are these tools called? There is no single official name. People call them frameworks or models. A grid is a matrix (plural: matrices), and a four-box grid is a 2×2 (say “two-by-two”). Researchers who sort people or things into types call the result a typology.

How many are there? Nobody knows — hundreds, and new ones appear every year. Most collections choose the best 50 to 80. This toolbox holds the ones you are most likely to meet, in six families, grouped by their shape. The shape tells you how the tool thinks: a grid sorts, a pyramid ranks, a loop repeats, a curve shows a pattern, a diagram connects, and a checklist makes sure nothing is forgotten.

Are they only for business? No. Use the buttons below to see the same kind of thinking in psychology, medicine, education, the military and politics. Tools marked lesson have a full lesson.
