At the Vet

Your dog is not eating. He is quiet and tired. He can't tell the vet what is wrong — so you must do it, in simple, clear words.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

Your dog is called Biscuit. He loves his food — he eats everything, every day. But this morning, he did not eat. At lunch, he did not eat again. Something is wrong.

Biscuit is very quiet. He is tired, and he does not want to play. You are worried. But Biscuit can't talk, so he can't say what hurts. You must tell the vet. This is not easy — but simple, clear words help a lot.

You call the vet, and they say, "Come now." Soon, you are in the waiting room with Biscuit on your lap. The vet is kind and calm. Now it is your turn to speak.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you have a pet, or did you have one? What is its name? What is it like?
  2. When you feel ill, is it easy to tell the doctor what is wrong? Why, or why not?

Who's who

You
The Worried Owner 🐾

You love Biscuit. You are worried. You must say what is wrong, answer the vet's questions, ask about the price, and say thank you.

The other person
The Vet 🩺

Kind and calm. The vet asks simple questions, looks at Biscuit, and tells you what to do. The vet wants to help.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. It is OK to speak slowly. It is OK to say "I'm not sure." You can also ask the vet to say it again.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the simple, clear words.

VetHello, come in. So this is Biscuit! What is the problem today?
YouHello. Thank you for seeing us. My dog is not well. He is not eating, and he is very quiet.
VetOh, poor Biscuit. When did it start?
YouIt started yesterday. In the morning he was fine, but in the evening he was tired and quiet. He was sick one time in the night.
VetOK. Did he eat something bad? Maybe something on a walk?
YouI'm not sure. Maybe. On the walk yesterday, he put his nose in a bush for a long time.
VetGood to know. Let me look at him. … His tummy is a little sore here, and he needs more water. But it is not very bad.
YouThat's good to hear. So it is not serious?
VetI think he has a small stomach problem. But I want to do a blood test, to be sure.
YouOK. How much does it cost?
VetThe blood test is ninety pounds. An X-ray costs more. Let's do the blood test first, and then we can decide.
YouYes, good idea. Let's do the blood test first.
VetGood. At home, give him small meals and lots of water. If he is sick again, please call me.
YouThank you very much for your help. Come on, Biscuit, let's go home.

From memory — don't look up!

1. What is wrong with Biscuit? Say two things.

2. What does the vet want to do first — the blood test or the X-ray?

3. The vet gives the owner two things to do at home. What are they?

1. Biscuit is not eating, and he is very quiet and tired. (He was also sick one time in the night.)
2. The vet wants to do the blood test first. The X-ray costs more, so they wait and decide later.
3. Give Biscuit small meals and lots of water — and call the vet if he is sick again.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use at the vet — and at the doctor's too. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

It's OK to be slow. You are worried, and that is normal. Speak slowly and use short sentences. If you don't know a word, use easy words. And it is fine to say "I'm not sure" — that is honest and helpful.

🚪 Coming in

Phrase 1

You come in with your dog. Say the big problem in a simple way. Complete it: "My dog is not ___."

"My dog is not well."
"Not well" means ill or sick. It is a simple, clear way to start. You can say it about people too: "My son is not well today."
Phrase 2

The vet asks, "What is the problem?" Which answer is clear and easy to understand?

A. "Something is not right, I think, maybe."
B. "He is not eating. He is very quiet."
C. "Just look at him, please."
B — "He is not eating. He is very quiet."
Two short, clear sentences. You name the problem, so the vet knows where to start. A is not clear, and C gives the vet no help.

🔎 Saying what is wrong

Phrase 3

The vet asks, "When did it start?" It began yesterday. Put the words in order:

itstartedyesterday
"It started yesterday."
"It started…" tells the vet when the problem began. You can add more: "It started yesterday, in the evening." Time words help a lot.
Phrase 4

In the night, Biscuit vomited one time. Complete it: "He was ___ one time in the night."

"He was sick one time in the night."
"To be sick" means the food comes back up (to vomit). It is a very common word. "He was sick two times" tells the vet how many times.

❓ When you are not sure

Phrase 5

The vet asks, "Did he eat something bad?" You did not see. You don't really know. Which answer is honest?

A. "No, never."
B. "Yes, for sure."
C. "I'm not sure. Maybe."
C — "I'm not sure. Maybe."
You did not see, so a strong "No" or "Yes" can be wrong. "I'm not sure" is honest and helps the vet. It is always OK to say it.
Phrase 6

Then you remember: on the walk, Biscuit put his nose in a bush. Complete it: "Maybe he ate something on his ___."

"Maybe he ate something on his walk."
"Maybe…" is perfect when you are not sure. This small memory can help the vet a lot. Little details are useful!

💷 Money and thank you

Phrase 7

You want to know the price of the test. Put the words in order:

how muchdoes itcost?
"How much does it cost?"
This is the normal, polite way to ask about a price. It is not rude to ask — it is good to know. You can use it in shops and cafés too.
Phrase 8

At the end, the vet helped you a lot. Say a warm thank you. (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you very much for your help."
Add "very much" and "for your help" to make your thank you bigger and warmer. A smile helps too! You can also say "Thank you so much."

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work at the vet, the doctor's, and more. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"It started ___." — Think of a small problem (a cold, a sore leg). When did it start?

"I'm not sure. Maybe ___." — Answer a question you don't really know the answer to.

"How much does ___ cost?" — What do you want to know the price of?

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — come in → say what is wrong → answer honestly → ask the price → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your pet, and your visit. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the owner 🐾

The vet speaks — you answer. Use simple, clear words. It is OK to say "I'm not sure."

VetHello, come in. So who is this? What is the problem today?
VetOh dear. When did it start?
VetDid he eat something bad? Maybe on a walk?
VetHis tummy is a little sore. I think it is a small problem, but I want to do a blood test.
VetThe blood test is ninety pounds. We can do an X-ray later if we need it.
VetGood. Give him small meals and lots of water. Call me if he is sick again.

Round 2 — now YOU are the vet 🩺

Change places. You are the vet now. Be kind and calm. Say "When did it start?", "Let me look", "Don't worry".

OwnerHello. My cat is not well. She is not eating, and she sleeps all day.
OwnerIt started two days ago. She is very quiet now.
OwnerIs it serious? How much does it cost?
OwnerOK, thank you. What must I do at home?
The Twist

🌙 The test costs a lot

One more time — no help this round. The vet says the blood test and the X-ray together cost a lot of money. It is too much for you this month. What do you say? Stay calm and polite. Ask a question: "Can we do only the blood test first?" or "Can I pay next week?" Show that you love your pet, but money is a problem. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said the big problem in a clear, simple way.
  • I said when it started.
  • I said "I'm not sure" when I did not know.
  • I asked "How much does it cost?"
  • I said thank you warmly at the end.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

In an old story by Rudyard Kipling, a big flood carries a small, wet animal — a mongoose — into a family's garden in India. The family take him in. They dry him, feed him, and give him a name: Rikki-Tikki-Tavi.

Soon, Rikki is not just an animal — he is family. And when two big snakes come, Rikki fights them to keep the family safe. He gives them everything.

It is the same with Biscuit. We take a little animal into our home, we feed it and love it, and it becomes family. So when Biscuit will not eat, you stop everything and take him to the vet. That is what family does.
