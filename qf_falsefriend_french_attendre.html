⏳ Wait vs Attend

B1 • French speakers • False friends: attendre ≠ attend

🔍 Let's notice what we see

Look at this sentence. What's the problem?

"I will attend for you at the café."

(But they're not at a meeting or event...)

Is this about going to an event? Or is it about waiting? What's the confusion?

⚠️ The False Friend:

The French word "attendre" LOOKS like English "attend", but they mean DIFFERENT things!

In French: attendre = to wait, to wait for someone (attends-moi = wait for me)

In English: attend = to be present at an event (attend a meeting, attend school)

In English: wait = to stay and expect someone (wait for me)

🛠️ Understand the difference

📊 Different contexts:

🇫🇷 French "attendre"

⏳ WAIT / WAIT FOR

Expect someone, stay somewhere

"Attends-moi" = Wait for me!

🇬🇧 English "attend"

🎭 BE PRESENT / GO TO

Go to an event, meeting, school

"I attended the meeting" = I was there

🎯 Key Rule:

French attendre → English WAIT (stay and expect)

English attend → French assister à (go to an event)

When someone stays and expects: WAIT. When going to an event: ATTEND.

🚀 Now you try — no help!

Choose the correct word. Check your answers when you're done.

1. "I'm going to _____ the wedding next Saturday." (wait for / attend)
Answer: "I'm going to attend the wedding."

→ Go to an event = attend.

2. "Can you _____ for me in the lobby?"
Answer: "Can you wait for me?"

→ Stay and expect = wait.

3. "He _____ the university for four years."
Answer: "He attended the university."

→ Go to an institution = attend.

4. "I _____ 30 minutes for her, but she never showed up."
Answer: "I waited 30 minutes."

→ Stay and expect = wait.

✅ I can say 'wait' instead of 'attend' when meaning to stay and expect someone
