Winning and Growing Client Relationships

Talk about portfolios, revenue and relationships, and advise senior stakeholders with confidence.

Today: you will talk about winning, growing and advising clients with numbers and credibility so you can show business impact.

Relationships that last

At senior level, you are hired for the relationships you can open and the revenue you can grow. Interviewers listen for numbers, for a method, and for how you speak to people at the top.

Language you can reuse

Each card describes a moment in the interview. Decide what you would say, then check the phrases.

Situation: You talk about the size of a portfolio or a target.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “I managed a portfolio worth around €8 million.”
  • “It was a seven-figure account.”
  • “We grew revenue by 18% year on year.”
  • “We went from €2 million to €3.5 million in two years.”
  • “Our conversion rate was three out of five proposals.”

Say numbers clearly: “eighteen per cent”, “three point five million”.

Situation: You explain how you open the door with a new client.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “I opened the door through a warm introduction.”
  • “I drew on my network to…”
  • “I identified an unmet need and brought them a point of view.”
  • “I positioned us as a trusted adviser, not a supplier.”
Situation: You describe growing a small engagement into a long-term relationship.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “We started with a small pilot and expanded into a multi-year programme.”
  • “I became their go-to adviser on [topic].”
  • “Our renewal rate with key accounts was above 90%.”
  • “I identified further needs across the organisation.”
Situation: You describe advising a board or C-level executive.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “I framed the discussion around their strategic priorities.”
  • “I translated the technical detail into business impact.”
  • “I challenged their assumption, respectfully but clearly.”
  • “I was candid about the risks.”
Situation: You explain how you handled a relationship that was under strain.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “I raised the issue early rather than letting it grow.”
  • “I proposed a recovery plan with clear milestones.”
  • “I managed expectations by being transparent about trade-offs.”
  • “We rebuilt trust by delivering on small commitments first.”
Situation: You contribute to the growth of the wider practice, beyond your own accounts.

Say how you would handle it first.

  • “I helped build the pipeline for the whole team.”
  • “I mentored colleagues in how to approach senior clients.”
  • “I developed a new offering based on what clients were asking for.”

Upgrade the sentence

Rewrite each sentence so it sounds like a confident senior candidate. Then compare.

Make it sound senior: “I have many clients and good relationships.”
I managed a portfolio of twelve clients worth around €9 million, and eight of them have worked with us for over five years.
Make it sound senior: “I found new customers for the company.”
I opened four new accounts in eighteen months, two of them through introductions from existing clients, worth €3 million in total.
Make it sound senior: “The client was unhappy so I tried to make them happy.”
The client was concerned about delays, so I raised it with their director before they did, presented a recovery plan, and they renewed the contract.
Make it sound senior: “I told the CEO the technology was very good.”
I showed the CEO how the technology would reduce costs by 15% and what the investment would return in two years.

How would a client-facing leader answer?

Read both answers. Decide which one an interviewer would rate higher, and say why before you click.

“How do you generate new business?”
A“I call many companies, I send lots of emails, and I tell them how good our services are. I never give up.”
B“I start with my existing network, because warm introductions convert much better. I research each target’s strategic priorities, bring a point of view on one of them, and offer a small first piece of work to prove value.”
B shows a repeatable method focused on the client’s priorities. A is activity without strategy, and it sounds like selling features.
“Tell me about your largest account.”
A“It was a seven-figure account that I grew from €1.2 million to €2.8 million over three years by becoming their adviser on two strategic programmes.”
B“It was a very big client and a very important one for the company. They were happy with us and we did a lot of work together.”
A has the size, the growth and the reason. B is vague and gives the interviewer nothing to remember.
“A C-level executive disagrees with your recommendation. What do you do?”
A“I accept their decision, because they are the client and the executive is always right.”
B“I ask what is driving their view, then show the data behind mine and the risk of the alternative. If they still disagree, I respect the decision but make sure the risks are recorded and we agree how to monitor them.”
B shows candour, evidence and respect, which is what senior clients value in an adviser. A shows deference without value.

Interview question

“Tell me about a significant client relationship you built or grew.”

Build your answer in three parts

Context: “A national healthcare provider was spending heavily with three competitors and had no single adviser for its digital strategy. I saw an opportunity to become that adviser.”

Approach: “I secured an introduction through a former colleague, prepared a short point of view on their data challenges, and proposed a six-week pilot. After the pilot, I met their chief executive every quarter and used those conversations to identify two further programmes.”

Impact: “The account grew from €400,000 to €2.5 million in three years. They are now one of our top three clients, and they introduced us to two other organisations.”

Notice: a clear opportunity, a repeatable method, specific numbers and evidence that the relationship lasted.

Speak

Close the notes and give your answer aloud in about two minutes. Then give it a second time with one improvement.

Answer in two or three sentences: “How do you decide which clients to invest time in?”

“I look at three things: strategic fit with our strengths, growth potential over three years, and the quality of the relationship at the top. I would rather invest deeply in five accounts where I can become a trusted adviser than spread myself across twenty.”
