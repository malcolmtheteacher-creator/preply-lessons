The Keyword Builder

Build your word map. Then speak from it.

🎯 Fluency + Vocabulary

What Happens When You Write a Word?

Think about the word "travel." What other words come to mind?

Maybe: adventure, passport, culture, explore, flight, holiday...

Your brain already does this — every word you know is connected to hundreds of others. The problem is that when you're speaking, you only reach for the same few. The connections are there, but they stay hidden.

🤔 Why does this matter for speaking? (click to discuss)

When you prepare for a speaking topic, you usually write keywords and speak from them. That works — it builds fluency.

But what if you could see the connections before you speak? What if writing one word opened up a whole map of related words — synonyms, opposites, collocations — that gave you choices you wouldn't normally reach for?

That's what we're doing today. You write the keywords. The page shows you what's connected. You pick what's useful. Then you speak.

Today's Goal: Build your own keyword maps and speak from them — using words you chose, not words someone gave you.

How the Keyword Builder Works

There are three steps. They're simple, but the combination is powerful.

Step 1: You write a keyword

You get a question. You type the first keyword that comes to mind — your anchor word for that idea. Don't think too hard. Just write it.

Step 2: The page ignites

Related words appear around your keyword. You'll see:

Similar words Opposites Related ideas Common combinations

These aren't instructions. They're invitations. You scan them. Some will spark ideas. Some won't. That's fine.

Step 3: You pick and speak

Click any word that gives you an idea — it joins your keyword map. Then when you've got enough anchors, you speak from them.

💡 The key idea:

You're not just speaking from keywords anymore. You're building a word map and then speaking from it. The building is where your brain makes connections. The speaking is where it uses them.

🤔 Did the ignited words change what you said? (click after all 3 rounds)

Most people find that seeing related words — even ones they didn't pick — changes the direction of their response. A synonym makes them choose a more precise word. An opposite makes them add a counterargument they wouldn't have thought of.

That's the point. The ignited words aren't there to give you answers — they're there to give your brain more material to work with.

Use this method when you prepare for any lesson:

1. Read the prep pack — absorb the ideas.

2. For each discussion question, type your keywords into the builder.

3. Explore the connections. Pick words that spark ideas.

4. Speak from your map — out loud, not in your head.

The word map is yours. Nobody else's keywords will activate the same connections in your brain.

Recall & Reflect

Quick Recall

🎙 What are the three steps of the keyword builder? Explain them without looking back.

1. Write your keywords — the anchor words for your ideas.

2. Explore the ignited words — synonyms, opposites, related ideas, common combinations.

3. Pick and speak — choose what's useful, build your word map, then speak from it.

🎙 What's the difference between speaking from keywords and speaking from a word map?

Keywords give you anchors — starting points for your brain to build from.

A word map gives you choices — your anchors plus related words that open up new directions, more precise vocabulary, and ideas you wouldn't have reached for on your own.
