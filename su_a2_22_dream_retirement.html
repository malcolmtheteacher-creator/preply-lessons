Warm-Up: Let's Talk About Retirement

Before we explore different retirement destinations, let's think about what matters to you.

Discussion Questions

1. What is the retirement age in your country?

In many countries, it varies between 60-67 years old. What is it where you live?

2. Where are popular retirement spots in your country or region?

Think about places known for good weather, lower costs, or beautiful scenery.

3. What three things matter most to you for retirement?

For example: good food, good weather, affordable prices, friendly people, excellent healthcare, interesting culture, beautiful nature...

Take your time thinking about these questions. Your answers will help us explore different retirement destinations today.

What Matters Most?

When choosing a retirement destination, many factors are important. Here are seven key features to consider. Click on each one to learn more.

Features to Consider

1
Living Costs
How much it costs to pay for housing, food, transport, and daily life. Lower costs mean your money goes further in retirement.
2
Local Restaurants & Cuisine
The quality and variety of food culture. Good local cuisine means delicious meals and authentic experiences.
3
House Prices
The cost of buying or renting a home. Lower house prices mean you can afford a bigger or nicer home on your retirement budget.
4
Cultural Diversity
Different cultures, traditions, and communities living together. This creates interesting experiences and tolerance for all kinds of people.
5
Sandy Beaches
Beautiful coastal areas for relaxation and recreation. Great for swimming, walking, or just enjoying the sun.
6
Medical Care
Quality of hospitals, doctors, and healthcare. Important for staying healthy and getting good treatment if you need it.
7
Public Transport
Buses, trains, taxis, and other ways to travel around. Good public transport means you don't need a car and can move around easily.

Reflection

Which of these is most important to you?

Everyone has different priorities. Someone who loves beaches might rank them high. Someone who enjoys art and culture might prioritize cultural diversity. There are no wrong answers!

Second Conditional: Imagined Situations

The second conditional talks about imagined situations — things that are unlikely or not true now.

Structure

If + past simple, would + infinitive

If I had more money, I would travel more.
If I lived by the sea, I would swim every day.
If I spoke Portuguese, I would go to Portugal.

Note: We use "were" for all subjects with "be" — "If I were rich...", "If you were there...", etc.

Real vs. Imagined

Look at these examples. The sentences in the green box describe real situations (present facts). The sentences in the purple box describe imagined situations (things that might happen in the future or are unlikely now).

Real Situations

I have money.
I speak English.
I live in a cold place.

Imagined Situations

If I had more money, I would travel.
If I spoke Spanish, I would live in Mexico.
If I lived somewhere warm, I would be happy.

Dream Destinations

Let's explore four popular retirement destinations. Each place offers different advantages. Read about each one and think about which might suit different people.

🇲🇾 Malaysia
  • Lush forests and beautiful nature
  • Warm, tropical weather all year
  • Culturally very diverse (Malay, Chinese, Indian communities)
  • Reasonable and affordable living costs
  • Cheap and healthy local cuisine
  • Good public transport network
  • Kuala Lumpur has a major international airport
Who might like Malaysia? Someone who loves nature, enjoys tropical weather, and wants affordable living costs.
🇪🇸 Spain
  • Famous for delicious local cuisine
  • Very low house prices compared to other European countries
  • Excellent medical care and hospitals
  • Culturally and artistically rich (Picasso, Gaudí, architecture)
  • Beautiful sandy beaches along the coast
  • Sunny, warm weather for much of the year
  • Strong culture of relaxing lifestyle
Who might like Spain? Someone who enjoys art and culture, loves good food and beaches, and wants affordable European living.
🇵🇹 Portugal
  • Fantastic beaches along the Algarve and Atlantic coast
  • Warm, sunny weather year-round
  • Very friendly and welcoming people
  • Lisbon: one of Europe's oldest and most interesting capitals
  • Excellent restaurants serving fresh, local food
  • Strong football tradition (Sporting, Benfica, Porto — and players like Ronaldo)
  • Excellent public transport, especially in cities
Who might like Portugal? Someone who enjoys beaches, friendly communities, good food, and is interested in sports and culture.
🇹🇭 Thailand
  • Special retirement visa available for older residents
  • Chiang Mai is especially popular with retirees
  • High-quality medical and dental care at great prices
  • Fantastic, authentic cuisine and street food
  • Amazing beaches and tropical islands
  • Reasonable and very affordable living costs
  • Warm weather and interesting Buddhist culture
Who might like Thailand? Someone seeking adventure, exceptional value for money, good healthcare, and a warm tropical climate with cultural experiences.

Your Turn: Speaking Task

Language Support

Compound Nouns (Key Vocabulary)

  • Living costs — the expense of day-to-day life
  • Local restaurants — authentic eating places in the area
  • Local cuisine — traditional food of a region or country
  • House prices — the cost of buying property
  • Cultural diversity — mix of different cultures
  • Sandy beaches — coastal areas with sand
  • Medical care — healthcare and hospital services
  • Public transport — buses, trains, taxis

Second Conditional Structure

If + past simple, would + infinitive

  • If I had more money, I would travel.
  • If I lived by the sea, I would swim every day.
  • If I spoke Portuguese, I would go to Portugal.
  • If I were younger, I would move to a big city.

Useful Phrases

  • I would choose… because of…
  • The best thing about… is…
  • I like the idea of…
  • This place has…
  • I enjoy…
  • I prefer…
