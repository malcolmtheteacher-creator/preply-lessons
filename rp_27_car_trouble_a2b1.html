Car Trouble

Your car has a problem. There is a strange noise, and a little light is on. Time to take it to the garage and talk to the mechanic.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

Your car is not happy. For a week now, there is a strange noise when you stop the car. It is a low noise. It is not loud, but it is there — every time you brake.

There is also a small orange light on the dashboard. It came on a week ago, and it does not go off. You are not a mechanic. You do not know what is wrong. But you know it is time to ask for help.

So this morning you drive to the garage. A mechanic is there. You need to say what is wrong, and you want to know one thing: how much is it? You have money for a small job — but maybe not for a big one.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you have a car? When something breaks — a car, a phone, a washing machine — do you try to fix it, or do you ask an expert?
  2. When a job costs a lot of money, do you ask "is it cheaper any other way?" Or do you just pay? Why?

Who's who

You
The Driver 🚗

You are not a mechanic, but you are not silly. You know the noise, and you know how much money you have. You want a fair price.

The other person
The Mechanic 🔧

They know cars very well. They are friendly and honest. If you say the problem clearly, they can help you — and find a cheaper way.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. It is OK to speak slowly. It is OK to say "sorry, again please?" You do not need big words — short and clear is best.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for how the driver says the problem, and how they ask about the price.

MechanicGood morning. How can I help you?
YouGood morning. Can you look at my car today? There is a problem.
MechanicOf course. What is the problem?
YouThere is a noise when I brake. It is a low noise, and it is louder now.
MechanicOK. Is there anything else?
YouYes. The engine light is on. It doesn't go off.
MechanicI see. I think it is the brakes. But I need to look, to be sure.
YouOK, thank you. How much is it?
MechanicThe brakes are about two hundred pounds. I can tell you more later.
YouThat is more than I thought. Is there a cheaper way?
MechanicYes. I can use different parts. They are good, and they cost less.
YouThat is very kind. Thank you. How long will it take?
MechanicYou can have the car this afternoon. I will call you first, before I start.
YouPerfect. Thank you very much! Here are the keys.

From memory — don't look up!

1. The driver tells the mechanic two things that are wrong. What are they?

2. The price is more than the driver thought. What does the driver say and ask?

3. How does the mechanic make the price lower?

1. There is a noise when the driver brakes (a low noise, and louder now), and the engine light is on and doesn't go off.
2. The driver says "That is more than I thought" — and then asks, "Is there a cheaper way?"
3. The mechanic uses different parts. They are good, but they cost less. And the mechanic will call first, before starting the work.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use at any garage. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it calmly. You are not angry — you just need help. Speak slowly and be friendly. The words please, thank you and sorry make everything easier. Short sentences are perfect.

🔧 At the garage

Phrase 1

You want the mechanic to check your car today. Complete it: "Can you ___ at my car today?"

"Can you look at my car today?"
"Can you look at…?" is an easy, polite way to ask someone to check something. You can also say "Can you have a look at it?" — it means the same.
Phrase 2

You want to say your car is not working well. Which sentence is best?

A. "My car is bad."
B. "There is a problem with my car."
C. "Car no good."
B — "There is a problem with my car."
"There is a problem with…" is the clear, calm way to say something is wrong: "There is a problem with my phone", "There is a problem with the heating". A and C are not clear English.

🔊 What is wrong

Phrase 3

You want to say when the noise comes: when you brake. Put the words in order:

there isa noisewhen I brake
"There is a noise when I brake."
Say when the noise comes — it helps the mechanic a lot. Other examples: "There is a noise when I turn", "There is a noise when I start the car".
Phrase 4

A little orange light is on, and it will not turn off. Complete it: "The engine light is ___."

"The engine light is on."
Simple and clear. If it will not turn off, you can add: "It doesn't go off." Use "is on" for any light: "The red light is on."

💷 The price

Phrase 5

You want to ask the price, in a polite way. Which one is best?

A. "Give me the price."
B. "How much money you want?"
C. "How much is it?"
C — "How much is it?"
"How much is it?" is short, easy and polite. You can also say "How much will it cost?" A is like an order, and B is not correct English.
Phrase 6

The price is high. You want to ask if there is a cheaper way — without a fight. Complete it: "Is there a ___ way?"

"Is there a cheaper way?"
This is calm and friendly. You are not saying "too expensive!" — you are asking for help. You can also say "That is more than I thought. Is there a cheaper way?"

💛 Saying thank you

Phrase 7

The mechanic helps you and finds a cheaper way. Which is a warm, friendly thank you?

A. "OK."
B. "That is very kind. Thank you."
C. "Fine."
B — "That is very kind. Thank you."
"That is very kind" tells the mechanic you see their help. A and C are cold — not wrong, but not warm. Warm words make people happy to help you.
Phrase 8

At the end, you want to say a big thank you and give the keys. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you very much! Here are the keys."
Add "very much" to make your thank you bigger and warmer. "Here are the keys" is what you say when you give someone your car keys. A smile helps too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work at a garage, a shop, a repair shop — everywhere. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"Can you look at ___?" — What do you need help with? (my car, my phone, my computer…)

"There is a problem with ___." — Think of a thing that can go wrong. What is it?

"How much is ___?" — What is something you want to buy or fix? Ask the price.

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — arrive → say what is wrong → ask the price → ask for a cheaper way → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your car and your money. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Role-play timer: 3:00

Round 1 — you are the driver 🚗

The mechanic speaks — you answer. Be clear and polite: say what is wrong, then ask about the price calmly.

MechanicGood morning. How can I help you?
MechanicOf course. What is the problem?
MechanicI think it is the brakes. But I need to look, to be sure.
MechanicIt could be about two hundred pounds, all together.
MechanicYes, I can use cheaper parts. You can have the car this afternoon.

Round 2 — now YOU are the mechanic 🔧

Change places. Now you work at the garage. Be friendly and honest. Say "Of course", "I see", "I think it is…", "I can help".

DriverGood morning. Can you look at my car today? There is a problem.
DriverThere is a noise when I brake, and the engine light is on.
DriverHow much is it?
DriverThat is more than I thought. Is there a cheaper way?
The Twist

🌩️ It is a bigger job

One more time — no help this round. The mechanic calls you at lunchtime. It is not only the brakes. There is a bigger problem, and now the price is much higher. But you need the car this weekend! What do you say? Stay calm and friendly. Ask: "Is there a cheaper way?" Ask: "Do I need all of it now, or can some of it wait?" Ask about a small car to borrow. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I asked the mechanic to look at my car, politely.
  • I said what was wrong — the noise AND the light.
  • I asked the price with "How much is it?"
  • I stayed calm and asked "Is there a cheaper way?"
  • I said "thank you" warmly at the end.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

Before they made the first plane, two brothers, Wilbur and Orville Wright, fixed bicycles for a living. Every day they did the same thing you did today: they listened to a machine, found the noise, and worked out which part was broken.

They did not just believe what other people said. They tested everything. They built their own machine to check the numbers. They believed only what they could see for themselves.

So the next time your car makes a strange noise, don't worry. The two men who gave the world flight started in a small workshop, just like your garage — trying to work out what was wrong.

Two questions to talk about

  1. Fewer people can fix their own machines now. When you cannot check something yourself, how do you know to trust the expert and the price?
  2. The Wright brothers tested everything and believed only what they could see. Is that a good way to live? Or do we sometimes need to trust other people?
