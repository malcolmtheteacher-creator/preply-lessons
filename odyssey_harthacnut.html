The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. He planned to invade England to take the throne.
to invade (v) — to enter with an army.
2. He raised a heavy tax to pay his large fleet.
a tax (n) — money that people must pay to the government.
3. Two royal tax-collectors were killed in Worcester.
to collect (v) — (collect) to gather money owed.
4. The people of Worcester chose to riot.
to riot (v) — to behave violently in a public protest.
5. He sent an army to punish the city.
to punish (v) — to make someone suffer for a crime.
6. He decided to invite his half-brother Edward back from Normandy.
to invite (v) — to ask someone to come.
7. He was willing to share the household with Edward.
to share (v) — to have or use together.
8. He fell in a convulsion during a wedding feast.
a convulsion (n) — a sudden, violent shaking of the body.
9. His heavy taxes made him unpopular.
unpopular (adj) — not liked by many people.
10. He was about to toast the bride and groom.
to toast (v) — to raise a drink in honour of someone.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The delayed heir

Harthacnut, son of Cnut and Emma, was king of Denmark. He had been prevented from coming to England by war in Scandinavia. In 1040, as he was preparing to invade, his half-brother Harold died, and Harthacnut landed peacefully with a large fleet.

He had Harold's body dug up and thrown away — an angry start.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why had Harthacnut been delayed?

2. What did he do to Harold's body?

1. War in Scandinavia kept him in Denmark.

2. Had it dug up and thrown away.

Discuss:
  • What does this first act tell us about him?

Part 2 — The tax and the riot

To pay his fleet, Harthacnut raised a heavy tax. In 1041 two of his tax collectors were killed in Worcester by angry citizens. In response, Harthacnut sent an army that burned the city and ravaged the area.

He used to be remembered as a cruel king because of this.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened in Worcester in 1041?

2. How did the king respond?

1. Two royal tax-collectors were killed.

2. He sent an army to burn and ravage Worcester.

Discuss:
  • Was the king's response justified?

Part 3 — Brother and death

In 1041 Harthacnut invited his half-brother Edward — Emma's son by Æthelred — back from Normandy and seems to have made him part of his household, perhaps as heir. On 8 June 1042, at a wedding feast in Lambeth, the Chronicle says he died "as he stood at his drink", with a terrible convulsion. He was about twenty-four.

Edward became king, and the old English line returned.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Whom did he invite back, and why is that important?

2. How did Harthacnut die?

1. Edward, his half-brother — who became the next king, restoring the old English line.

2. He collapsed with a convulsion at a wedding feast in Lambeth.

Discuss:
  • Why would a king invite a possible rival into his household?

Grammar — Used to & Would — Past Habits

To describe how life used to be — the habits and customs of a reign — English uses used to and would.

How it works:
• used to + verb for past habits and states: "People used to believe the king could heal."
• would + verb for repeated actions only (not states): "Every Christmas he would hold court."
• Negative: didn't use to.

1. used to or would?

Complete each sentence about Harthacnut. Use the word in brackets.

1. Danish kings (keep) large fleets paid by English taxes.

2. The tax-collectors (go) from town to town.

3. Worcester (be) a busy city before the burning.

1. used to keep

2. would go / used to go

3. used to be

2. Complete with the correct form

1. People (not / like) the heavy tax.

2. At feasts, the king (drink) with his nobles.

3. Edward (live) in Normandy.

1. didn't use to like / did not like

2. would drink

3. used to live

Going deeper

Harthacnut's two years were marked by harshness, but his decision to invite Edward made a peaceful succession possible. Whether he was ill, poisoned or simply had a stroke, we cannot know.

Worth noticing: his death ended Danish rule — but the Godwin family, and Edward's Norman connections, would shape the next 24 years.
Learning from the past — for today: Unpopular decisions need explanation. Heavy demands without a clear reason create resistance. When you ask people to "pay", explain why.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Was the Worcester response a crime?
  • Why did Harthacnut invite Edward?
  • How do you explain hard decisions to your team?
