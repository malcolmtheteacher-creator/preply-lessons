What is the Crucifixion, and why do Christians believe it was important?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Jesus's execution by Roman crucifixion. Christians believe it was an act of love and sacrifice for sin, fulfilling Old Testament prophecy (Isaiah 53: Suffering Servant).

What is the Resurrection, and why is it central to Christianity?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Jesus rising from the dead 3 days after crucifixion. It proves Jesus is God's son, defeats sin and death, and gives believers hope for eternal life.

Explain the difference between heaven and hell in Christian belief.

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Heaven = eternal life in God's presence. Hell = eternal separation from God. Different traditions interpret these differently (literal, metaphorical, or universalist).

What is original sin, and how is it different from personal sin?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Original sin = inherited from Adam & Eve, affects all humans. Personal sin = sins you commit yourself. Original sin is our condition; personal sin is our choice.

What does atonement mean, and how do different Christians explain it?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Atonement = Jesus's death reconciles humans to God. Satisfaction (debt paid), Moral Influence (inspired by love), Penal Substitution (Jesus bore our penalty).

Review: Core Beliefs Summary

The Crucifixion & Resurrection: Bookends of Salvation

Jesus's crucifixion and resurrection are the two pivotal events of Christianity. The crucifixion shows God's sacrificial love. The resurrection proves Christ's victory and offers eternal life to believers.

The Afterlife: Heaven, Hell, Judgement

Christians believe in heaven (eternal with God), hell (separation from God), and the Last Judgement (when all people are judged). The Catholic Church also teaches purgatory. Views vary between literal and metaphorical interpretations.

Sin & Salvation: The Human Problem & God's Solution

Sin (separation from God) is universal. Salvation (rescue through Christ) is God's solution. Protestants emphasise faith alone; Catholics add works. The Holy Spirit enables salvation in all traditions.

Christ's Roles: Saviour, Redeemer, Mediator

Jesus saves (rescues from sin), redeems (buys back through his death), and mediates (bridges gap between God and humanity). Atonement theories explain HOW his death accomplishes this.

Doctrinal Connections

Incarnation → Crucifixion → Resurrection → Ascension → Intercession: God became human to die for sin. Death conquered sin. Resurrection proved victory. Jesus ascended to heaven where he intercedes for believers. This is the grand narrative of salvation.

Test Your Knowledge: Quick-Fire Questions

What does "penal substitution" mean?

Answer: Jesus bore the penalty of sin in our place. God punished Jesus instead of us to satisfy divine justice.

Which Christian church teaches purgatory?

Answer: The Roman Catholic Church. Purgatory is a temporary state of purification after death before entering heaven.

What is theosis (or deification)?

Answer: An Orthodox and early Christian concept: union with God. God became human so humans could become united with God.

What is the difference between a mortal sin and a venial sin?

Answer: Mortal sin = serious, breaks relationship with God. Venial sin = minor, damages but doesn't break the relationship.

Who taught the "Moral Influence Theory" of atonement?

Answer: Abelard (1079–1142). Jesus's death demonstrates God's love and inspires us to respond with love and transformation.

What does "redemption" mean in Christian theology?

Answer: Being bought back or ransomed. Jesus redeems humanity by dying—his death is the price that frees us from sin.

What is the connection between the Incarnation and the Crucifixion?

Answer: God had to become human (Incarnation) to die for sin (Crucifixion). Only a divine-human figure can save humanity.

How do Protestants and Catholics differ on salvation?

Answer: Protestants: Faith alone (sola fide). Catholics: Faith + works (sacraments, good deeds). Protestants emphasise God's grace; Catholics emphasise human cooperation.
