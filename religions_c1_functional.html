Real Stories

Fatima, 31, Dubai to London: "In Dubai, nobody asked about my faith - it was just assumed. Moving to London, suddenly I had to explain Islam to colleagues who'd never met a Muslim. I became more religious abroad than I ever was at home."

Dr. Bart Ehrman, religious scholar: "I went from being a born-again Christian to an agnostic. It wasn't a crisis - it was a 20-year intellectual journey. Most people assume something dramatic happened, but it was just... reading and thinking."

Kenji, 28, Tokyo: "I'm Buddhist at funerals, Shinto at New Year, and I had a Christian wedding because my wife liked the dress. In Japan, this isn't contradictory - it's normal. Religion isn't about exclusive truth here."

Think about it...

Religion was supposed to disappear as the world modernized. Instead, it's evolving - going online, mixing traditions, becoming personal. Is this the death of organized religion, or its rebirth in a new form?

Functional Language for Sensitive Topics

Religion, politics, values - these conversations require diplomatic language. Today's phrases help you share your perspective while respecting others' beliefs. They're useful far beyond religious discussions.
A note on today's topic: We're learning language for discussing beliefs respectfully - not debating which beliefs are "correct." The goal is to express yourself thoughtfully while remaining open to different perspectives.

1. "From what I understand..."

▼
Function
To share information about someone else's beliefs/culture while acknowledging you might not have complete knowledge. Shows intellectual humility.
Form
From what I understand, + statement about others' beliefs/practices

Variations: "From what I've read...", "From what I've been told...", "As far as I know..."
Explaining another religion "From what I understand, Ramadan isn't just about fasting - it's also about charity and self-reflection."
Discussing unfamiliar practices "From what I've read, meditation in Buddhism isn't prayer exactly - it's more about training the mind."
Why It Works
You share knowledge without claiming to be an expert on someone else's beliefs. It invites correction rather than creating conflict. Much better than stating things as absolute facts.

Now You Try

Use "From what I understand..." to explain something about a religion or belief system different from your own background. (It's okay to not be 100% sure - that's the point of this phrase!)

2. "I was raised to believe... but now I..."

▼
Function
To share your personal journey of belief - acknowledging your background while showing how your thinking has evolved. Creates space for nuance rather than binary positions.
Form
I was raised to believe + past belief, but now I + current position

Variations: "Growing up, I was taught that... These days, I...", "My family always said... but I've come to think..."
Faith evolution "I was raised to believe there was only one path to God, but now I think spirituality is more personal than that."
Changing views "I was raised to believe religion was unnecessary, but now I can see the community and meaning it gives people."
Why It Works
You honor your background (and your family) while showing you've thought independently. It models that changing your mind is healthy, not shameful. Avoids the trap of "my parents were wrong."

Now You Try

Complete this sentence honestly: "I was raised to believe... but now I..." (This could be about religion, values, politics, or any belief that has evolved.)

3. "I can see why someone might think that, but..."

▼
Function
To disagree respectfully by first validating the other person's perspective before presenting your own. Shows you've genuinely considered their view.
Form
I can see why someone might think that, + but + your perspective

Variations: "I understand that perspective, however...", "That's a fair point, though I'd argue...", "I get where you're coming from, but..."
Respectful disagreement "I can see why someone might think religion causes conflict, but I'd argue it's more about how people use religion than religion itself."
Offering another view "I can see why someone might think children should choose their own beliefs, but I think giving them a foundation first isn't the same as forcing them."
Why It Works
The other person feels heard before you disagree. This dramatically reduces defensiveness and keeps conversations productive. The word "someone" also depersonalizes - you're not attacking them directly.

Now You Try

Think of a belief you disagree with. Use "I can see why someone might think that, but..." to express your disagreement diplomatically.

4. "It's not for me to say whether..."

▼
Function
To acknowledge that some questions are beyond your authority to answer definitively - especially regarding others' personal beliefs or life choices. Shows respect for individual autonomy.
Form
It's not for me to say whether + statement/question you're stepping back from

Variations: "It's not my place to judge...", "Who am I to say if...", "I don't think it's for anyone else to decide..."
Avoiding judgment "It's not for me to say whether her decision to convert was right - that's between her and her conscience."
Respecting autonomy "It's not for me to say whether they should raise their kids religious or not. Every family has to decide that for themselves."
Why It Works
You can participate in a discussion without pronouncing judgment on deeply personal matters. It signals maturity and shows you understand the limits of your own perspective. Very useful when you have private opinions but recognize they shouldn't override others' choices.

Now You Try

Think of a choice someone made that you might privately disagree with, but recognize is their right to make. Use "It's not for me to say whether..."

5. "Speaking purely from my own experience..."

▼
Function
To share personal perspective on sensitive topics while making clear you're not claiming universal truth. Grounds your opinion in lived experience rather than abstract argument.
Form
Speaking purely from my own experience, + personal perspective

Variations: "In my own experience...", "Personally speaking...", "For me personally...", "What I've found in my own life is..."
Sharing personal faith "Speaking purely from my own experience, prayer has been helpful during difficult times - but I know that's not everyone's experience."
Non-religious perspective "Speaking purely from my own experience, I've found meaning and community outside of religion, but I respect that others find it within."
Why It Works
Personal experience is inarguable - nobody can tell you that you didn't feel what you felt. But by framing it as YOUR experience, you avoid generalizing. It invites others to share their (possibly different) experiences rather than defending against your "truth."

Now You Try

Use "Speaking purely from my own experience..." to share something personal about faith, meaning, or values in your life.

Quick Review: 5 Phrases

  1. "From what I understand..." - sharing others' beliefs with humility
  2. "I was raised to believe... but now I..." - showing your journey
  3. "I can see why someone might think that, but..." - respectful disagreement
  4. "It's not for me to say whether..." - avoiding judgment
  5. "Speaking purely from my own experience..." - grounding in personal truth

2
"Spiritual but not religious" - meaningful personal path, or just avoiding commitment?
Think about: What's lost and gained by leaving organized religion?

Think about: What fills the gap when religion leaves?
4

Think about: Western vs. Eastern approaches to religious identity
5

6
Should religion be completely private, or does it have a legitimate role in public life and politics?
Think about: Where do you draw the line?
