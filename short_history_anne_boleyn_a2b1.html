Anne Boleyn

The queen who changed England — and the mother of a future great queen.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The king who wanted to marry her

Anne Boleyn was born around 1501 in England. Her family was rich and important. When she was young, she went to live in France for some years. There she learned about fashion, music and beautiful manners. When she came back to England, she was clever, stylish and very interesting. Many people looked at her.

King Henry VIII saw Anne, and he wanted her. But Anne said no. She did not want to be just a girlfriend, like her sister. She wanted to be his wife. The more she said no, the more the king wanted her. He wrote her many letters and gave her many gifts. There was a big problem: Henry was already married, to a woman called Catherine of Aragon. Catherine had a daughter but no son, and Henry wanted a son very much.

Henry decided to marry Anne. But only the Pope in Rome could end his first marriage — and the Pope said no. So Henry did something huge. He said no to the Church in Rome. He made himself the leader of a new church in England. Now he could end his marriage. In 1533, he married Anne, and she became queen. One woman's "no" helped to change the religion of a whole country.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why was Anne special when she came back from France?

2. What big thing did Henry do so he could marry Anne?

1. She learned fashion, music and beautiful manners in France, and she was clever and stylish. She said no to the king, and this made him want her more.

2. The Pope said no, so Henry said no to the Church in Rome. He made a new church in England and made himself its leader, so he could marry Anne.

Talk about it:
  • A whole country changed its religion partly because of one marriage. Do you think that is a good reason to change a country? Why, or why not?

Part 2 — Queen for a short time

Now Anne was queen. This was the top of the world. But there was one big problem: the king wanted a son. In 1533, Anne had a baby. It was not a son — it was a girl, called Elizabeth. Henry was not happy. Anne was clever and said what she thought, and this made some powerful people at court angry with her. She had enemies.

The next two years were hard. Anne lost a baby before it was born. This was very sad, and maybe the baby was a boy. At the same time, the king started to look at another woman: Jane Seymour, one of Anne's own ladies. Jane was quiet and calm — very different from Anne. Now Jane was Anne's rival. The king who changed everything to marry Anne did not love her anymore.

Around the king, there were powerful men. The most powerful was Thomas Cromwell. He was Anne's friend before, but now they wanted different things, and he became her enemy. We do not know exactly what the king and his men said in 1536. But we know the end: they decided to get rid of Anne. And in those days, that did not mean a simple divorce. It meant something much worse.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did the king want, and what baby did Anne have in 1533?

2. Who was Jane Seymour, and why was she a problem for Anne?

1. The king wanted a son. Anne had a daughter, Elizabeth. Henry was not happy.

2. Jane Seymour was one of Anne's ladies. The king started to like her, so she became Anne's rival, and Anne was in danger.

Talk about it:
  • Anne's place as queen was safe only if she had a son — but no one can choose this. Is it dangerous to build your life on one thing you cannot control?

Part 3 — The trial and the Tower

In May 1536, everything happened very fast. Soldiers arrested Anne and took her to the Tower of London, a famous prison. They said she loved other men, even her own brother, and that she wanted to kill the king. These were terrible things to say. Almost certainly, they were not true. But at her trial, the court said she was guilty. The truth is, people decided this before the trial started.

Anne was calm and brave, even at the end. On 19 May 1536, she was executed at the Tower. The king sent for a special swordsman from France — a small kindness from the man who once changed a kingdom for her. Anne was about thirty-five years old. She was queen for only about one thousand days. A few days later, Henry planned to marry Jane Seymour.

But this is not the end of the story. The little daughter, Elizabeth, grew up. She became Elizabeth I — one of the greatest queens in the history of England. People still talk about that time as a golden age. So the woman who died as a "traitor" became the mother of a great queen. Today, people still argue about Anne: was she clever and dangerous, or was she just a victim? Maybe she was more than one simple thing.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did people say Anne did? Were the things true?

2. What happened to Anne's daughter, Elizabeth, in the end?

1. They said she loved other men and wanted to kill the king. Almost certainly, the things were not true, but the court said she was guilty anyway.

2. Elizabeth grew up and became Elizabeth I, one of the greatest queens in English history.

Talk about it:
  • People remember Anne in very different ways — as a bad woman, a victim, or a great queen's mother. Why do we like to give people from the past one simple label?

Grammar: the past simple

This whole story happened long ago. So we tell it in the past simple. This is the most important tense for telling a story.

Regular verbs: add -ed. want → wanted, marry → married, want → wanted: "Henry wanted a son." "Anne married the king."
Irregular verbs: you must learn them. They do not add -ed:
be → was / were · have → had · go → went · become → became · say → said · take → took · give → gave · see → saw · make → made.
Negatives and questions use did + the base verb:
• "She did not want to be a girlfriend." (not "did not wanted")
• "Did Anne have a son?" — No, she had a daughter.

1. Regular verbs — write the past simple

Add -ed (careful with spelling). Then read the sentence out loud.

1. King Henry (want) a son very much.

2. Anne (marry) the king in 1533.

3. The king (start) a new church in England.

4. Soldiers (arrest) Anne and took her to the Tower.

1. wanted · 2. married · 3. started · 4. arrested

Regular verbs add -ed. "marry" → "married" (y becomes i).

2. Irregular verbs — write the past simple

These verbs do not add -ed. Use the list in the box above to help you.

1. When she was young, Anne (go) to live in France.

2. In 1533, Anne (have) a baby girl, Elizabeth.

3. Elizabeth (become) a great queen.

4. The king (be) not happy with a daughter.

1. went · 2. had · 3. became · 4. was

These are irregular — you learn them one by one.

3. Tell a short part of the story

Put each verb into the past simple (regular or irregular). Read it aloud when you finish.

Anne (go) to France as a girl. Later she (marry) King Henry and (become) queen. She (have) a daughter, but the king (want) a son.

went (go) · married (marry) · became (become) · had (have) · wanted (want)

Regular: married, wanted. Irregular: went, became, had.
