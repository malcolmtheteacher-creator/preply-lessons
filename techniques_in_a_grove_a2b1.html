In a Grove

A story by Ryūnosuke Akutagawa (1922) — a dead man in a forest, and three people who all say: 'I killed him.'

Key words — think first, then check

Before we read, let's learn the words you'll need. Think first — then click Reveal to check.

A. Guess from the sentence

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then reveal.

1. The body was in a small forest of bamboo and thin trees, far from the road, where nobody goes.
a forest (n) — a large area of trees. (Here, a small forest — a grove.)
2. The samurai carried a long sword at his side, and a bow and arrows on his back.
a sword (n) — a long, sharp metal weapon.
3. The thief tied the man to a tree with a rope, so he could not move.
a rope (n) — a thick, strong string for tying things.
4. The woman used a comb to make her long hair smooth and neat.
a comb (n) — a flat object with teeth, for your hair.

B. Which word is it?

Read the meaning. Say the word. The first letter helps you.

5. A person who steals things — often from travellers on the road. It begins with t.
a thief (n). Plural: thieves. Tajōmaru was a famous thief.
6. The person in a court who listens to everybody and decides who is guilty. It begins with j.
a judge (n) / to judge (v). The judge asked each person questions.
7. The spirit of a dead person, who can sometimes speak. It begins with g.
a ghost (n). At the end, the dead man's ghost tells his story.

C. Choose the meaning

Read the sentence and choose a, b or c. Then reveal.

8. The man was dead. He lay on his back, and the blood on his chest was dry. — dead means: (a) sleeping (b) not alive (c) hurt
(b) — dead (adj) — not alive. to die – died – dead
9. Three people say 'I killed him.' But only one story can be the truth. — the truth is: (a) a lie (b) what really happened (c) a story
(b) — the truth (n) — what is real; what really happened. Tell me the truth.
10. The dead man's wife, Masago, was nineteen years old. — a wife is: (a) a woman who is married to someone (b) a sister (c) a daughter
(a) — a wife (n) — a married woman; the woman a man is married to.

The story, in three parts

This is a famous Japanese story, told here in simple English. A samurai is dead in a forest. A judge asks seven people what happened. There is no narrator — only the people speaking. Read each part, then try the questions from memory.

Part 1 — Four witnesses

The woodcutter: "Yes, sir. I found the body this morning. I went up the hill to cut wood, and there he was, in a small forest of bamboo. He lay on his back in a blue robe. There was one wound in his chest, and dry blood on the leaves. A sword? No. I saw only a rope by a tree, and a comb. The grass was flat everywhere — I think he fought before he died."

The priest: "I saw the man yesterday at noon, on the road. He was walking next to a woman on a horse. I couldn't see her face — she wore a veil. The man had a sword, and a bow with about twenty arrows. Poor man. Life is so short."

The policeman: "I caught the thief last night — Tajōmaru, the famous thief. He fell off a horse near the bridge. He had a bow and seventeen arrows — the dead man's. And the horse was the woman's horse. So Tajōmaru killed him. But sir — Tajōmaru likes women. Where is the woman now? Please, look for her."

The old woman: "The dead man is my daughter's husband. His name was Takehiro, a samurai, twenty-six years old — a gentle man. My daughter is Masago. She is nineteen, and very brave, like a man. They left for Wakasa yesterday. Now he is dead — and where is my daughter? Please, sir, find her! And that thief — I hate him!" (She cried and could not speak.)

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did the woodcutter find next to the body?

2. Who is the dead man, and who is missing?

1. A rope by a tree, and a comb. No sword.

2. Takehiro, a samurai, twenty-six years old. His wife Masago, nineteen, is missing.

Discuss:
  • The priest says twenty arrows. The policeman says seventeen. Why are the numbers different?

Part 2 — The thief's story

Tajōmaru: "Yes, I killed the man. But I did not kill the woman. Where is she? I don't know."

"I met them on the road at noon. The wind lifted the woman's veil for a second, and I saw her face. It was beautiful like a goddess. I decided: I will take this woman, even if I must kill the man."

"You think killing a man is a big thing? You kill people too — with money, with power, with nice words. No blood, but you kill them. Who is worse, you or me?"

"I told the man a lie: there is treasure in the forest — old swords and mirrors — and I will sell it cheap. He was greedy. He came with me. The woman waited on her horse. In the forest, I jumped on him and tied him to a tree with my rope. Then I went back to the woman and said: 'Your husband is ill — come!' She came. When she saw her husband tied up, she pulled out a small knife and tried to kill me! What a fierce woman! But I knocked the knife from her hand. And I took her."

"I did not want to kill the man. But then the woman held my arm and cried: 'One of you must die — you or my husband! I cannot live with two men knowing my shame. I will go with the one who lives.' When I saw her eyes, I wanted her for my wife. So I untied the man and said: 'Fight me.' We fought with swords. On the twenty-third stroke, my sword went through his chest. Twenty-three! No other man ever fought me so long."

"Then I turned round — and the woman was gone. I took the man's sword and bow and left. That is all. Hang me if you like."

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did the thief get the man into the forest?

2. According to the thief, why did he kill the husband?

1. He told him a lie about treasure — old swords and mirrors — buried in the forest. The man was greedy and followed him.

2. The woman said one of the two men must die, and she would go with the one who lived. So he untied the husband and they fought with swords. He killed him on the twenty-third stroke.

Discuss:
  • The thief says: 'You kill people with money and power. I use a sword. Who is worse?' What do you think?

Part 3 — The wife, and the dead man

The wife, Masago, at a temple: "After the thief hurt me, he laughed at my husband, tied to the tree. I ran to my husband. And I saw his eyes. They were not angry. They were not sad. They were cold — full of contempt for me. That look hurt me more than anything. I fainted."

"When I woke up, the thief was gone. My husband's eyes were still cold. I said: 'I cannot live after this. I will die. But you must die too — you saw my shame.' He said nothing. His mouth was full of leaves. But his eyes said one word: 'Kill.' I took my little knife, and I put it into his chest. Then I tried to kill myself. I tried the knife. I tried the river. I could not do it. And so I am here. What should I do? What should I…" (She cried.)

The dead man, speaking through a ghost-woman: "After the thief hurt my wife, he sat down and talked to her softly. 'Come with me,' he said. 'Be my wife.' I could not speak. I looked at my wife with my eyes: don't believe him! But my wife lifted her face. She was more beautiful than ever. And she said: 'Yes. Take me with you.'"

"Then she did something worse. She pointed at me and screamed: 'Kill him! I cannot go with you while he lives!' Even the thief went pale. He looked at her — and he kicked her to the ground. Then he asked me: 'What shall I do with this woman? Kill her, or let her go?' For those words, I can forgive the thief everything."

"My wife ran away into the forest. The thief could not catch her. He took my sword and my bow, cut one of my ropes, and left. Everything was quiet. Someone was crying — it was me. I got free. My wife's little knife was on the ground. I picked it up, and I put it into my own chest. There was no pain. Everything went quiet and dark. Then… someone came softly. I could not see who. A hand took the knife out of my chest. And I went down into the dark for ever."

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. According to the wife, who killed the husband, and why?

2. According to the dead man, who killed him?

1. She did. His eyes were full of contempt for her, and they 'said' one word: 'Kill.' So she put her knife in his chest.

2. He killed himself with his wife's knife, after she asked the thief to kill him and then ran away. At the end, someone took the knife from his chest.

Discuss:
  • Three people say 'I killed him.' Who do you believe? And who took the knife at the end?

The grammar this story runs on: asking questions

The judge asks questions all the way through the story — we just don't hear them. To find the truth, you need good questions. Here is how to make them.

Question words: Who? (person) · What? (thing) · Where? (place) · When? (time) · Why? (reason) · How? (way) · How many? (number) · Whose? (owner).
Past simple questions: question word + did + person + verb.
• "Where did you find the body?" • "Why did she run away?" • "How many arrows did he have?"
The verb stays in the base form: ✗ Where did you found? ✓ Where did you find?
With be: question word + was / were.
• "Who was the dead man?" • "Where were you at noon?"
Careful with Who as the subject: "Who killed him?" — no did, because who is the person doing it.

Why people still read this story

Ryūnosuke Akutagawa (1892–1927) was one of the most important Japanese writers of the twentieth century. He wrote this story in 1922, using an old tale from the twelfth century. In 1950 the director Akira Kurosawa made it into a famous film, Rashōmon. Today, when people tell different stories about the same event, we call it 'the Rashomon effect'.

Everyone in this story says 'I did it'. The thief says he won a fair fight. The wife says her husband asked her to kill him. The husband says he killed himself like a samurai. Every story makes the speaker look brave, or sad, or honourable. And at the very end, a hand takes the knife. Who? The story never says.

Look at the woodcutter again. He found a rope and a comb — but no knife. And somebody took the knife from the dead man's chest. Who was in the forest that evening?
