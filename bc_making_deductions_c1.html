Making Deductions and Recommendations

Learn to write reports with confidence and clarity

Modals for Making Deductions

When you make deductions about the past, you express how confident you are about what happened. Use the right modal to show your level of certainty.

Certain (Positive)

must have + past participle — You feel sure this is what happened.

Example: The company must have employed too many staff.

This suggests you are confident about why the company failed.

Certain (Negative)

can't have / couldn't have + past participle — You feel sure this did NOT happen.

Example: The company can't have sold enough products to be profitable.

You are confident about what did not occur.

Possible

might have / could have / may have + past participle — You think it is possible that something happened, but you are not certain.

Example: The company might have overextended their debt levels.

This is one possible explanation, but there could be others.

Practice: Identify the Modal

Which sentence shows CERTAINTY?

"The company must have faced serious cash flow problems." — The word "must have" indicates certainty. The speaker is confident this is what happened.

Which sentence shows POSSIBILITY?

"The pension fund managers could have made poor investment choices." — The word "could have" indicates possibility. This is one possible explanation among others.

Language for Recommendations

When you make recommendations in a report, choose language that matches your level of confidence and urgency.

Strong Recommendations

Use these phrases when something MUST change:

  • "It is recommended that the company..."
  • "We would strongly advise..."
  • "It is essential that..."
  • "The company must urgently..."

Tentative Recommendations

Use these phrases when you suggest a possible improvement:

  • "It might be worth..."
  • "One possibility would be to..."
  • "It could be beneficial to..."
  • "The company may wish to consider..."

Example Recommendations

Strong: Labour costs need to decrease

"It is essential that the company reviews its labour costs and negotiates new wage agreements with the workforce."

Tentative: Explore new markets

"One possibility would be to explore new markets for the company's existing products."

Company Report: Glaxanon

Read these findings from a management consultant's report about Glaxanon, a company in financial difficulty.

Finding 1: Rising Costs
There has been a significant rise in costs at Glaxanon for many years. Labour costs have risen dramatically following industrial action. The price of raw materials and fuel have also increased substantially.

Finding 2: Pension Problem
Generous pension provisions and poor investment choices have created a serious pension fund shortfall. This shortfall is currently being paid through short-term borrowing and is expected to increase further.

Finding 3: Share Price Collapse
The share price has been falling for years. Since filing for bankruptcy, the share price has dropped to less than a tenth of its value from five years ago.

Finding 4: Sales Turnaround
Sales plummeted for five years due to competition. However, a new product line released in January has reversed this trend slightly. Experts are skeptical about company projections.

Finding 5: Tax Problem
Corporate tax rates have increased following government changes. The shift from profit taxation to turnover taxation has particularly affected Glaxanon. Further increases are projected.

Make Deductions

For each finding, use modals of deduction to explain what probably happened:

About rising labour costs: What must have caused industrial action?

Example: "Workers must have felt their wages were not keeping pace with inflation. The management could have refused earlier wage negotiation requests, leading to industrial action."

About the pension fund shortfall: What might have gone wrong?

Example: "The pension fund managers might have made poor decisions during market downturns. Interest rates might have dropped, reducing pension fund returns."

About the share price collapse: What can't have worked?

Example: "The company can't have managed its public relations effectively during the bankruptcy announcement. Investors can't have had confidence in management's ability to recover."
