Talking About Your ACCA Exams

B1 Level · Study Vocabulary, Present Perfect & Speaking Practice

Getting Started

Today we are going to talk about your ACCA journey. There is no specific accounting content here — the focus is on the language you need to describe your progress, explain how you study, and talk about exam pressure in clear, natural English.

Warm-up Questions

1. When did you start your ACCA studies?

Try to give a longer answer — not just the date. Why did you start? What stage are you at now?

2. How many papers have you sat so far? How many are left?

Use "I have sat…" or "I have passed…" with numbers.

3. What does a typical study week look like for you?

When do you study? Where? With whom? How many hours?

4. How are you feeling about your next paper?

Use adjectives like confident, nervous, prepared, behind, on track, overwhelmed.

Exam Language

Click each card to see the meaning. Try to use the word in a sentence about your own studies.

to sit an exam To take an exam. "I'm sitting my next paper in June."
a paper A single exam in a course. ACCA has several papers across different levels.
the pass mark The lowest score you need to pass. For ACCA exams it is usually 50%.
to resit / a resit To take an exam again after failing the first time. "I had to resit one paper."
a mock exam A practice exam under real exam conditions, usually before the real one.
the syllabus The full list of topics you need to study for an exam.
revision Reviewing material you have already studied, usually close to the exam.
to cram To study a lot of material in a short time, often the night before. Not recommended!
to fall behind To not finish work on time. "I've fallen behind with my revision."
to be on track To be making progress according to your plan. "I'm on track to finish by July."

Talking About Progress

To talk about your studies from when you started up until now, we use the present perfect.

Two Useful Patterns

Present Perfect Simple: "I have passed three papers."
(Use this for finished actions — results, totals, achievements.)
Present Perfect Continuous: "I have been studying ACCA for two years."
(Use this for things that started in the past and are still happening now.)

Spot the Difference

Read each pair. Which form is correct? Why?

A. "I have studied for three hours today."

B. "I have been studying for three hours today."

Both are correct, but they feel different. A emphasises the result (you did it). B emphasises the activity (you are tired now, or you might continue).

A. "I have passed four papers so far."

B. "I have been passing four papers so far."

A is correct. "Pass" is a finished result, not an ongoing activity. The continuous form does not work here.

A. "I have prepared for this exam since January."

B. "I have been preparing for this exam since January."

B sounds more natural. "Prepare" is an ongoing process, and "since January" tells us it is still happening.

Stress & Time

Exam preparation can be hard. Here is some natural language for talking about pressure, time management, and how you cope.

Useful Phrases

"I've been struggling with…" (used for difficulties, ongoing problems)
"I tend to…" (used for habits and tendencies)
"What helps me most is…" (used for strategies that work)
"I find it hard to…" (used for personal challenges)
"I make a point of…" (used for things you do on purpose, deliberately)
"Whenever I can, I try to…" (used for flexible habits)

Match the Sentence Endings

How might each sentence finish? There is no single right answer — suggest your own ending first, then check.

"I've been struggling with…"

…keeping up with the syllabus.
…finding time to revise after work.
…remembering all the standards.

"I tend to…"

…study better in the early mornings.
…leave revision to the last minute.
…take short breaks every hour.

"What helps me most is…"

…doing past papers.
…explaining things out loud.
…having a clear study plan.

Talking About the Future

For future plans — especially when you are not 100% sure — we often use hedging language. It sounds more careful and realistic.

"I'm hoping to sit the next paper in September."
"If all goes well, I should finish by next year."
"I'm aiming to pass two more papers this year."
"Fingers crossed, I'll be qualified by then."

Discussion

These are bigger conversation questions. Take your time. Try to give longer answers and explain your reasons.

Explore These Questions

1. Why did you choose ACCA over other accounting qualifications?

What attracted you to it? Did anyone recommend it?

2. What is the hardest part of studying for these exams?

The volume of material? The exam format? Balancing work and study? Something else?

3. How do you stay motivated when you feel tired or behind?

Do you have any rituals, rewards, or people who support you?

4. What advice would you give to someone who is just starting ACCA?

Think about what you wish you had known at the beginning.

5. Once you finish, how do you think your career will change?

What is the dream job? Where would you like to work? In what country?

6. Some people say professional exams are out of date and should be replaced. What do you think?

Push yourself to give two reasons for your opinion.

To give deeper answers:
  • Don't stop at one sentence — add an example or a personal story.
  • Use "because", "for example", and "the main reason is…" to explain yourself.
  • It is fine to pause and think. Try "That's a good question, let me think…"
