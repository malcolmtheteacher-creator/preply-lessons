The Alamo

A small group of men held an old fort against a huge army — and lost, but were never forgotten.

Want more detail? Read this lesson at B2/C1 level →

Today: tell the story of the Alamo — a small group of men in an old fort, a huge army, and thirteen hard days in 1836. Grammar: the past simple, for telling a story that is finished.
Warm up — talk before we read.
  • Have you heard the words "Remember the Alamo"? What do you think happened?
  • A few men are in an old fort. A big army is outside. How do you think the men feel?
  • Why do people still tell stories about brave people, even when they lost?

B. Work it out from the sentence

The sentence helps you guess the bold word. Say what you think it means, then reveal.

7. to surround
The big army stood on every side of the fort. There was no way out. They began to surround it.
to surround — to stand on all sides of a place so nobody can get out.
8. to attack
Early one morning, thousands of soldiers ran at the walls with their guns. They were ready to attack.
to attack — to start to fight someone, often first and with force.

C. Which word is close in meaning?

9. to give up
The men would not give up the fort. Which is closest — to stop and let the enemy win, or to keep fighting?
Closest is to stop and let the enemy win. to give up — to stop fighting and let the other side take control.
10. to remember
For many years, people said "Remember the Alamo!" Which is closest — to forget, or to keep something in your mind?
Closest is to keep something in your mind. to remember — to keep a person or an event in your mind and not forget it. The opposite is to forget.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A small fort and a big army

In 1836, Texas was part of Mexico. But many people in Texas were not happy. The president of Mexico, General Santa Anna, took a lot of power for himself. The people of Texas wanted to rule their own land. So they started a fight for freedom.

In the town of San Antonio there was an old building called the Alamo. Long ago it was a church, built by Spanish priests. It had thick stone walls, so the Texans used it as a fort. A small group of soldiers lived inside. Two men were the leaders: a young officer called William Travis, and a famous frontiersman called Jim Bowie.

On 23 February 1836, Santa Anna came with a very big army. There were only a few hundred men in the fort, but there were thousands of soldiers outside. The army began to surround the Alamo. Santa Anna put up a red flag. The red flag meant one thing: no mercy. The men in the fort knew this was very bad news.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did the people of Texas start to fight?

2. What was the Alamo, and who were its two leaders?

1. Santa Anna took a lot of power. The people of Texas wanted to rule their own land, so they fought for freedom.

2. It was an old church with thick walls, used as a fort. The leaders were William Travis and Jim Bowie.

Talk about it:
  • Two men were the leaders of the same fort. Do you think it is easy or hard when two strong people must work together? Why?

Part 2 — Thirteen days

For almost two weeks, Santa Anna's big guns hit the walls again and again. The men inside could not sleep. There were only about two hundred of them. Jim Bowie became very ill and had to stay in bed, so Travis was now the only leader.

From the fort, Travis wrote a famous letter. He said he would never give up. He would fight to the end. He asked other Texans, again and again, to send more men to help. He signed the letter with three strong words: "Victory or Death."

Only a small group of men came to help. They passed the enemy in the night and joined the fort, even though they knew the fight was almost lost. One of them was very famous: Davy Crockett, a frontiersman and a great shot with a gun, who came to Texas to start a new life. There is a well-loved story that says Travis drew a line on the ground and asked the men to step across it if they wanted to stay and fight. Maybe it is true, maybe not — but the men did stay.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did Travis say in his famous letter?

2. Who was Davy Crockett?

1. He said he would never give up and would fight to the end. He asked for more men and signed it "Victory or Death."

2. A very famous frontiersman and a great shot with a gun. He came to Texas for a new life and joined the men in the fort.

Talk about it:
  • The men stayed, even when the fight was almost lost. Why do you think they did not run away?

Part 3 — The end, and what came after

Early in the morning on 6 March 1836, Santa Anna gave the order to attack. In the dark, thousands of soldiers ran at the walls. The men in the fort pushed them back at first, but there were too few of them. In the end, the soldiers came over the walls. The fight was short. By morning it was over, and almost all of the men in the fort — Travis, Bowie and Crockett too — were dead.

Santa Anna let a few people live. One was Susanna Dickinson, the wife of a soldier, and her baby girl. He sent her away to tell everyone what happened. He wanted the story to make people afraid and stop the fight. But it did the opposite. When people heard the sad news, they did not stop. They became angry and strong.

Six weeks later, on 21 April 1836, the Texan leader Sam Houston found Santa Anna's army near a river. His men attacked and shouted "Remember the Alamo!" In less than twenty minutes, they won. They caught Santa Anna, and Texas became free. The men of the Alamo lost their battle, but their story helped win the war. People still remember them today.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened at the fort on 6 March 1836?

2. The Alamo was a defeat. How did it help the Texans in the end?

1. Santa Anna's army attacked. The soldiers came over the walls, and almost all the men in the fort were killed, including Travis, Bowie and Crockett.

2. The sad news made the Texans angry and strong. Six weeks later, shouting "Remember the Alamo!", they beat Santa Anna and Texas became free.

Talk about it:
  • Santa Anna wanted the story to make people afraid, but it made them brave instead. Why do you think this happened?

Grammar: Past simple

This story happened in 1836. It is finished. To tell a finished story in English, we use the past simple.

Regular verbs: add -ed.
want → wanted · start → started · ask → asked · attack → attacked · shout → shouted
Many common verbs are irregular. You must learn them.
come → came · write → wrote · fight → fought · win → won · take → took · give → gave · say → said · become → became
Negatives and questions use "did".
He did not give up. (not "did not gave")  ·  Did the men win?  ·  They did not run away.

Write the past simple of the verb in brackets.

1. In 1836, the people of Texas (want) to be free.

2. Santa Anna's army (attack) early in the morning.

3. The Texan soldiers (shout) "Remember the Alamo!"

3. Tell a short part of the story

Put each verb in the past simple. Some are regular, some are not. Read it aloud when you finish.

Santa Anna (arrive) with a big army. The men in the fort (not / give) up. They (fight) for thirteen days. In the end, the Texans (win) and Texas (become) free.

arrived · did not give · fought · won · became

Regular verbs add -ed (arrived). Irregular verbs change (fought, won, became). Negatives use did not + base verb (did not give).

Task B · Stay or go? (3 minutes)
