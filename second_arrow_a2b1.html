Two arrows

Read one part. Then answer the question without looking. Then check.

I

The prince

About 2,500 years ago, a prince lived in a big palace in what is now Nepal. His name was Siddhartha. His father kept all sad things away from him.

One day, when he was 29, Siddhartha went outside. He saw an old man, a sick man and a dead man. He was shocked. He thought: "Everybody gets old. Everybody gets ill. Everybody dies. How can people live with this?"

He left the palace to find the answer. After many years, he sat under a tree and found it. People called him the Buddha. "Buddha" means "the one who woke up".

What three people did Siddhartha see?

An old man, a sick man and a dead man.
II

The arrow story

The Buddha told a story. A man is hit by an arrow. It hurts. Then a second arrow hits him. Now he has two hurts, not one.

The first arrow is the bad thing that happens. Everybody gets this arrow.
The second arrow is the angry or worried thoughts after. We give this arrow to ourselves.

The Buddha said: we can't stop the first arrow, but we can learn to stop the second one.

Which arrow can we stop?

The second arrow: the angry or worried thoughts we add after the bad thing.

First arrow or second arrow?

Example"I failed my test." → First arrow. It happened.
"I'm useless. I will never pass." → Second arrow. It's a thought I add. It makes the hurt bigger.

Key words

Read the sentence aloud. What does the bold word mean? Guess, then click.

1. The fall was small, but the hurt in my knee lasted all week.
hurt (n) — pain in your body or in your feelings. Example: "The hurt from his words lasted for days."
2. She worries about her exam every night and can't sleep.
worry (v) — to keep thinking about problems or bad things that might happen. Example: "Don't worry, the doctor says you're fine."
Talk: Think of a small problem from this week. What was the first arrow? Was there a second arrow? What did you think?

"I should have…"

The second arrow is often made of words. One of the most common is "I should have…".

"I should have left at 8:00" means: I made the wrong choice in the past, and I feel bad about it now.

I should have + 3rd form of the verb
"I should have left earlier." "I should have bought the cheaper one."
The 3rd form: leave – left – left · buy – bought – bought · take – took – taken · go – went – gone · tell – told – told · listen – listened – listened

Check the grammar

Say the sentence with the right word. Then check.

1. It's raining! I should have ______ (take) an umbrella.
I should have taken an umbrella.
2. I'm so tired. I should have ______ (go) to bed early.
I should have gone to bed early.
3. My friend was right. I should have ______ (listen) to her.
I should have listened to her.
4. You knew about the party? You should have ______ (tell) me!
You should have told me!

3. I regret eating that last piece of cake. Now I feel sick!
regret (v) — to feel sad about something you did or didn't do. Example: "I regret not saying goodbye to her."
4. My friend stayed calm when we missed the plane. I was shouting!
calm (adj) — quiet and relaxed, not angry or worried. Example: "The sea was calm this morning."

The mustard seed

This is a very famous Buddhist story. It is about the biggest first arrow of all: losing someone you love.

I

Kisa Gotami

Kisa Gotami was a young mother. Her little son, her only child, got ill and died. She could not believe it. She carried him from house to house. She asked everyone: "Please, give me medicine for my son!"

Someone sent her to the Buddha. He said: "I can help you. Bring me some mustard seeds." Mustard seeds are very small seeds for cooking. Every kitchen had them. "But," he said, "they must come from a house where nobody has ever died."

Why did Kisa Gotami think this was easy?

Because every kitchen had mustard seeds. She only needed one house.

Knock on the doors with her

Click each door. Read what the family says.

II

Evening

In the evening, Kisa Gotami had no seeds. And she understood. Death comes to every family. She was not alone. She buried her son. Then she went back to the Buddha and learned from him. Later, she became a nun.

Why did the Buddha send her to the houses? Why didn't he just say "Everybody dies"?

She didn't want to…At the houses, she saw…
She was too sad to listen to words. At the houses, she saw for herself that every family loses someone. She learned it from real people, so she could believe it.

The big idea: everything changes

The same candle, a few hours later.

Everything changes: phones, towns, our bodies, our friends. The Buddha said: we are unhappy when we want things to stay the same for ever, because they never do.

5. My hair changed from black to grey in twenty years.
change (v) — to become different. Example: "The town has changed a lot since I was a child."
6. In the end, Kisa Gotami accepted that her son was dead.
accept (v) — to agree that something is true, even when you don't like it. Example: "He accepted that his team lost the game."

Hold on, let go

Sea water: the more you drink, the more thirsty you get.

Why do we shoot the second arrow? The Buddha said: because we hold on too tight. We want things. Then we want more. And we don't want anything to change.

Buddhist teachers say it is like drinking sea water. You drink because you are thirsty, but the salt makes you more thirsty.

Examples from everyday life"Just one more video…" It's 2 a.m. and you're still watching.
You put a photo online. You check the "likes" every five minutes.
A new phone is exciting for one week. Then you want the next one.

The Buddha did not say "Don't love anything." He said: don't hold on too tight. Hold life in an open hand, not in a closed fist.

7. He holds on to old clothes he never wears.
hold on (phr v) — to keep something tightly in your hand or in your life. Example: "Hold on to the rail on the stairs."
8. It was a small argument, years ago. It's time to let go.
let go (phr v) — to stop holding something; to stop worrying about it. Example: "Let go of my arm, please!"

The doctor

When you are ill, a good doctor does four things. People often say the Buddha was like a doctor. His most famous teaching has the same four steps.

PrescriptionFour steps
1"What is wrong?"
"You have a bad back."Life has pain, and we add more: the two arrows.
2"Why?"
"Because you sit all day."Because we hold on too tight.
3"Can it get better?"
"Yes, it can."Yes. We can let go, and the second arrow can stop.
4"What is the medicine?"
"Walk every day."Think wisely, speak and act kindly, and train your mind.

Before you click: what do you think the Buddha says for each step?

Train your mind: one quiet minute

How to do itSit still. Breathe normally. Thoughts will come. That's OK. Imagine each thought is a car on a road. Just watch the cars go past. Don't get into a car! If you get in, just get out and watch again.

The fire goes out

When a fire has no more wood, it goes out.

The Buddha said: when we stop holding on, the "fire" of wanting goes out, and we are at peace. His word for this is nirvana. It means "blowing out", like a candle.
