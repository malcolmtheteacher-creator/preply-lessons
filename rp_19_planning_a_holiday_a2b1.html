Planning a Holiday

It is grey and cold outside. You want some sun. You have a little money, not a lot. Time to walk into a travel shop and find a good holiday.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is winter. It is cold and grey, and it rains every day. You are tired of it. You want to go somewhere warm and sunny for one week.

You walk into a small travel shop. You do not know where you want to go yet. You want some sun and a little history — and it must not be too expensive. You have about a thousand pounds for two people.

Behind the desk is the travel agent. They are kind and they know a lot of places. They will help you find a good holiday. But first, you must say what you want, ask some questions, and think about the money.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like the beach, or do you like cities? What is your best kind of holiday?
  2. When you buy something big, do you ask a lot of questions first, or do you decide fast? Why?

Who's who

You
The Traveller 🧳

You want sun and a little history, and a good price. You are friendly. You ask questions before you say yes.

The other person
The Travel Agent 🌍

Kind and helpful. They know many places. They can give you two or three good holidays — and honest advice, if you ask.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the easy questions: How much is it? What is the difference? Is breakfast included? These small questions help you a lot.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the easy questions.

AgentHello! Come in. It is cold today! Do you want a holiday in the sun?
YouYes, please! We would like some sun and a little history. But not too expensive.
AgentNo problem. I can help you. How much can you spend?
YouWe can spend about a thousand pounds, for two people.
AgentGood. For that money, I have two nice holidays. Portugal, or the Greek islands. Both have sun and old buildings.
YouThey both sound nice. What is the difference?
AgentPortugal is cheaper, and the flight is short. Greece is very beautiful, but the food and the boats cost more.
YouI see. Can I ask — is breakfast included in the price?
AgentYes. Breakfast, the hotel, and the flights are all included. But lunch and dinner are not.
YouGood to know. And the room with a sea view — is it worth the extra money?
AgentBetween you and me — no. Save your money. Use it for a boat trip. That is a better memory.
YouThank you, that is very helpful. Can I think about it? I can come back this afternoon.
AgentOf course. No problem. I will keep this price for you until five o'clock.
YouThank you very much. Yes — I would like to book Portugal. You are very kind.

From memory — don't look up!

1. What two things does the traveller want on holiday? And what is the money problem?

2. What is the difference between Portugal and Greece?

3. Does the agent say "buy the sea view room"? What does the agent say to do with the money instead?

2. Portugal is cheaper and the flight is short. Greece is very beautiful, but the food and the boats cost more.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use in any travel shop. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Ask, don't worry. It is OK to ask a lot of questions before you say yes. That is normal and polite. Use "please" and "thank you", speak slowly, and it is fine to ask them to say it again.

☀️ Saying what you want

Phrase 1

You want to say what kind of holiday you want. Complete it: "We would ___ some sun and a little history, please."

"We would like some sun and a little history, please."
"We would like…" is the polite way to say "we want". You do not need to say a country — just say the feeling, and let the agent help you.
Phrase 2

You are not sure yet, and you want the agent to help. Which is best?

A. "Give me the cheapest holiday."
B. "We are not sure yet. Can you help us?"
C. "Book Portugal now."
B — "We are not sure yet. Can you help us?"
This is open and polite. It tells the agent you want ideas, not one fast answer. A and C decide too soon — you have not asked any questions yet!

💰 Talking about money

Phrase 3

You want to ask the price. Put the words in order:

how muchisit?
"How much is it?"
This is the easy, normal way to ask a price. You can also ask: "How much is the holiday?" or "How much is the flight?"
Phrase 4

You want to say how much money you have, in a soft way. Complete it: "We can ___ about a thousand pounds."

"We can spend about a thousand pounds."
"We can spend…" tells the agent your budget in a friendly way. The word "about" means "more or less" — it is not the exact number, so it feels softer.

⚖️ Comparing and asking

Phrase 5

You have two holidays, and you want to know how they are different. Complete it: "What is the ___ between them?"

"What is the difference between them?"
Use this when you have two choices and you want to compare them. It asks the agent to tell you the good and the bad of each one.
Phrase 6

You want to know if breakfast is in the price. Put the words in order:

breakfastisincluded?
"Is breakfast included?"
"Is … included?" asks if something is already in the price, with no extra money. Very useful: "Is the flight included?", "Are the transfers included?"

✅ Deciding

Phrase 7

You like it, but you want a little time before you say yes. Which is polite?

A. "No." (and you leave)
B. "Can I think about it, please?"
C. "Maybe. Bye."
B — "Can I think about it, please?"
This is polite and clear. It says you are interested, but you want a little time. A and C sound like "no", and the agent may not keep the price for you.
Phrase 8

You decided! You want to book the holiday. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Yes, I would like to book it, please."
"I would like to book it" is the clear, polite way to say yes. You can add the place: "I would like to book Portugal, please." A "thank you" at the end is always nice.

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in a travel shop, but also in a shop, a café — anywhere you buy something. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"We would like ___, please." — What holiday do you dream of? (sun, snow, a big city…)

"We can spend about ___." — How much money for your next trip or day out?

"Is ___ included?" — Think of a thing you want to check is in the price.

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — say what you want → talk about money → ask the difference → ask what is included → think → book. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your holiday. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Role-play timer: 3:00

Round 1 — you are the traveller 🧳

The agent speaks — you answer. Be polite: use please, thank you, and we would like.

AgentHello! Come in out of the rain. Do you want a holiday in the sun?
AgentI can help with that. How much can you spend?
AgentFor that, I have Portugal or Greece. Both have sun.
AgentPortugal is cheaper. Greece is prettier but costs more for food.
AgentBreakfast, hotel and flights are included. There is a sea-view room for extra money.
AgentBetween us — no. Save it for a boat trip. So, shall I book it?
AgentOf course. I will keep the price until five. But the good rooms go fast!

Round 2 — now YOU are the travel agent 🌍

Change places. You work in the travel shop. Be friendly and helpful. Make up the places, the prices, and your honest advice.

TravellerHello. We would like some sun, but not too expensive. Can you help us?
TravellerWe can spend about a thousand pounds, for two people.
TravellerThey both sound nice. Is breakfast included in the price?
TravellerIf it were you, which one would you book?
The Twist

🌴 A little too much money

One more time — no help this round. You found the perfect holiday… but it is a little too expensive. It is one hundred pounds more than your money. There is a cheaper holiday too, but it is not as nice. Start the timer and talk to the agent. Can you make it cheaper — fewer days, or a different airport? Or do you pay the extra money? You must decide before the timer stops. Say why!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said what holiday I want, in a clear, easy way.
  • I talked about my money politely ("We can spend about…").
  • I asked at least two questions before I said yes.
  • I said "thank you" warmly.
  • I stayed friendly the whole time.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

A very long time ago, Greek writers made a list of the seven most amazing things to see in the world. We call them the Seven Wonders. There was a huge pyramid, gardens that seemed to hang in the air, and a giant statue of a god.

In those days, there were no travel shops and no cheap flights. To see just one wonder, you travelled for months. It cost a lot of money, and it was not safe. But people still went, because some things were worth the whole trip.

Today, you just chose sun and a little history, and thought about the money. It is a small choice. But it is the same old feeling: people love to travel far, and pay a little more, just to see something wonderful.
