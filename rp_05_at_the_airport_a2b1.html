At the Airport

You are at the airport. You need to check in, drop your bag, and get a good seat. But then the board says your flight is late. Stay calm, be polite, and talk it through.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is Friday evening. The airport is very busy. There are lots of people, and the big board shows many flights. You are flying to Lisbon tonight, and you need to be there by morning.

You go to the check-in desk with your bag. You want three easy things: to check in, a good seat, and your bag to go all the way to Lisbon. This part is simple. You are calm and polite.

Then you see a problem. The board says your flight is late — delayed. You are not happy, but you do not shout. You ask calm questions, and the person at the desk helps you. When you are polite, people help you more.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like flying? What is good about it, and what is not so good?
  2. When your bus or train is late, how do you feel? Do you get angry, or do you stay calm?

Who's who

You
The Traveller ✈️

Tired, but calm and polite. You want to check in, get a good seat, and — when the flight is late — some help and a clear plan.

The other person
The Check-in Agent 🛄

Friendly and busy. They can give you a good seat, take your bag, and help with the delay — if you stay calm and kind.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the calm, polite words: please, thank you, can I, is that a problem? Small words like these help a lot.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the calm, polite words.

AgentGood evening! Where are you flying today?
YouGood evening. I am flying to Lisbon, on the eight-forty. But I got a text about a delay.
AgentLet me look for you. Can I have your passport and your booking, please?
YouHere you are. I have one bag to check in. And can I have an aisle seat, please?
AgentOf course. You have seat 22C — that is on the aisle. Now, please put your bag on the scale.
YouHere it is. I think my bag is a little heavy. Is that a problem?
AgentLet me see… twenty-three kilos. That is fine! It is not too heavy.
YouGood, thank you. Can you send my bag to Lisbon, please? I don't want to take it in Madrid.
AgentYes, no problem. Now, about the delay — your flight is now at half past ten.
YouOh. That is a shame, but these things happen. How long is the delay — about two hours?
AgentYes, about two hours. I am sorry. Here is a voucher — you can get some food while you wait.
YouThank you very much! That is very kind.
AgentYou're welcome. Your gate is number 14. Please watch the board. Have a good flight!

From memory — don't look up!

1. At the desk, the traveller asks for two things. What are they?

2. The traveller thinks the bag is too heavy. Is there a problem? What does the agent say?

3. The flight is late. What does the agent give the traveller for the wait?

1. An aisle seat, and to send the bag all the way to Lisbon (so they don't have to take it in Madrid).
2. No, there is no problem. The bag is twenty-three kilos — the agent says that is fine, not too heavy.
3. A voucher for some food while they wait. The agent also tells them the gate (number 14) and says to watch the board.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use at any airport. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Stay calm and polite. At the airport, things can go wrong — a late flight, a long line, a heavy bag. It is OK to ask for help. A calm voice and the words please and thank you do a lot. Short sentences are perfect.

🛫 Checking in

Phrase 1

You are at the desk. You want to start your flight — give your bag and get your seat. Complete it: "Good evening. I would like to check ___, please."

"I would like to check in, please."
To check in = to tell the airport you are here, and start your flight. "I would like…" is the polite way to say "I want". Use it everywhere: "I would like a coffee", "I would like a window seat".
Phrase 2

You want a seat by the aisle (so you can get up easily). Which sentence is polite?

A. "Give me an aisle seat."
B. "Can I have an aisle seat, please?"
C. "I sit on the aisle now."
B — "Can I have an aisle seat, please?"
"Can I have…, please?" is a soft, polite way to ask. A sounds like an order, and C is not correct English. An aisle seat is by the walkway; a window seat is by the window.

🧳 Your bag

Phrase 3

Your bag feels heavy. You want to ask if that is OK, before they say something. Complete it: "I think my bag is a little ___. Is that a problem?"

"I think my bag is a little heavy. Is that a problem?"
Saying it first, in a calm way, is friendly — you and the agent solve it together. "Is that a problem?" is a very useful, polite way to check: "I am five minutes late — is that a problem?"
Phrase 4

You have a connection in Madrid, but you want your bag to go all the way to Lisbon. Put the words in order:

can yousend my bagto Lisbon?
"Can you send my bag to Lisbon?"
This means: my bag goes all the way to the last city, and I do not pick it up on the way. "Can you…?" is a simple, polite way to ask for help: "Can you help me?", "Can you show me the gate?"

⏳ When the flight is late

Phrase 5

The board says your flight is late. You are not happy — but you know the calm traveller gets more help. Which reply keeps the agent on your side?

A. "This is terrible! What is wrong with you people?"
B. "That is a shame, but these things happen."
C. "Someone must fix this right now."
B — "That is a shame, but these things happen."
This says you are not happy, but you are calm and you do not blame the agent. ("These things happen" = it is nobody's fault; it just happens sometimes.) A and C are angry — and angry words make people help you less.
Phrase 6

Now you want to know how late the flight is. Complete it: "How long is the ___?"

"How long is the delay?"
A delay is when something is late. "How long is…?" asks about time: "How long is the flight?", "How long is the wait?" You can also ask: "What time is the new flight?"

✈️ A new plan & thank you

Phrase 7

The wait is long. You want to ask for an earlier flight, in a polite way. Put the words in order:

isan earlier flightpossible?
"Is an earlier flight possible?"
"Is … possible?" is a lovely, easy way to ask for something without pushing: "Is a window seat possible?", "Is a later time possible?" Earlier = sooner, before; later = after.
Phrase 8

The agent helps you a lot — a good seat, your bag, and a food voucher. You want to say a big, warm thank you. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you very much! You are very kind."
Add "very much" to make your thank you bigger. "You are very kind" tells the person you saw their help. A smile helps too — warm words make people happy to help you.

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work at airports, stations, shops — everywhere. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"Can I have ___, please?" — What do you want to ask for next time you go out? (a coffee, a map, a seat…)

"Is ___ possible?" — Think of something you would like to ask for.

"That is a shame, but these things happen." — When did something go wrong for you? What was it?

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — check in → ask for a seat → ask about your bag → hear the delay, stay calm → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your flight. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the traveller ✈️

The agent speaks — you answer. Be polite: use please, thank you, and I would like. And when the news is bad, stay calm.

AgentGood evening! Where are you flying today?
AgentLet me look. Can I have your passport, please?
AgentOf course. Now put your bag on the scale, please.
AgentThat is fine — not too heavy. But I am sorry, your flight is late tonight.
AgentAbout two hours, I'm afraid. Here is a food voucher for the wait.
AgentLet me check for you. Your gate is number 14 — please watch the board.

Round 2 — now YOU are the agent 🛄

Change places. You work at the airport. Be friendly and helpful. Say "Of course", "No problem", "I'm sorry", "Here you are".

TravellerGood evening. I would like to check in, please. I am flying to Rome.
TravellerHere you are. Can I have a window seat, please?
TravellerThank you. Oh — I think my bag is a little heavy. Is that a problem?
TravellerGood. And is my flight on time?
The Twist

🌩️ The last flight is full

One more time — no help this round. You want an earlier flight, but the agent says: "I'm sorry, the earlier flight is full." What do you say? Stay calm and polite. Ask for something else — maybe a good seat on your late flight, help to find some food, or a plan to get to Lisbon by morning. Try to leave happy. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said where I was going, clearly.
  • I used "please" and "Can I have…?" to ask for things.
  • I stayed calm when I heard about the delay.
  • I asked a clear question about the wait.
  • I said "thank you" warmly at the end.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

In 1937, a pilot called Amelia Earhart tried to fly all the way around the world. She was two-thirds of the way there when her plane went missing over the sea. There was no board, no gate, no kind agent to help her find a new flight.

She flew with an old radio and small maps. She flew into big storms. Today, you wait safely inside, warm and dry, while other people work to keep you safe.

So next time the board says your flight is late, try to think of it a different way. A late flight is not so bad. Amelia would love the small problem you have tonight.
