🔄 Reported Questions — Word Order

Why the verb goes to the end | B1 Level

What do you notice?

A student wrote this sentence. Something is wrong with the word order. What is it?

She asked me where was I going.

What needs to change — and why?

✓ Correction

She asked me where I was going.

📌 Why does the verb go to the end?

In a real question, we invert: "Where WAS I going?" — the auxiliary comes first.

But in reported speech, the embedded question behaves like a normal statement, not a question. The subject comes first, and the verb follows — which pushes it towards the end of the clause.

❌ She asked where was I going. — question word order

✓ She asked where I was going. — statement word order

More examples:

✓ He asked what I wanted. (not: what did I want)

✓ They wondered when the train arrived. (not: when did the train arrive)

📚 The Rule

🗣️ DIRECT question

❓

"Where ARE you going?"

"What DID she say?"

"Why HAS he left?"

Auxiliary + subject → inverted

📝 REPORTED question

💬

She asked where I was going.

He asked what she had said.

I wondered why he had left.

Subject + verb → statement order

Why the verb ends up at the end:

She asked where I was going . ↑ subject first, then verb — verb lands at the end
He wanted to know what time the meeting started .
I asked whether she was coming .
💡 Yes/No questions: When the direct question has no question word, use whether or if.
"Are you coming?" → She asked whether / if I was coming.
