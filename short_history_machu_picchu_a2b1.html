Machu Picchu

A city in the clouds — built, left empty, and lost for hundreds of years.

Want more detail? Read this lesson at B2/C1 level →

Today: tell the story of Machu Picchu, a famous old city in Peru. Grammar: the second conditional (If I visited Peru, I would climb the mountain).

Key words — match them first

Words

  1. an empire
  2. a mountain
  3. stone
  4. to build
  5. to leave (a place)
  6. lost
  7. an explorer
  8. a ruin

Meanings

  • A. a person who travels to find new places
  • B. a very high hill, often with rock at the top
  • C. a hard, grey material that you find in the ground
  • D. many lands and people ruled by one king or country
  • E. an old building that is broken and falling down
  • F. to make a building from stone, wood, or other things
  • G. to go away from a place and not come back
  • H. when nobody can find something for a long time

5 = G (to leave) · 6 = H (lost) · 7 = A (an explorer) · 8 = E (a ruin)

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A city for a king

About 600 years ago, the Inca people had a big empire in South America. It was in the mountains, in a country we now call Peru. Their king was called the emperor. The biggest city was Cusco, high in the mountains.

Around the year 1450, the emperor wanted a special place in the mountains. So his workers built Machu Picchu, high above a river. It was not a normal city. It was a quiet, private home for the king and his family.

The building was amazing. The Inca had no metal tools and no machines. But they cut big stones so well that the stones fit together perfectly — you cannot put a knife between them! They also cut flat steps into the mountain to grow food. And they made water move through the city in small stone channels.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who was Machu Picchu built for, and what was it?

2. Why was the building so amazing?

1. It was built for the Inca emperor (the king). It was a quiet, private home in the mountains for him and his family — not a normal city.

2. The Inca had no metal tools and no machines, but they cut big stones so well that they fit together perfectly. They also made steps for food and moved water through the city.

Talk about it:
  • The Inca built this with very simple tools. Is that more amazing than building with machines today? Why?

Part 2 — Empty and forgotten

People only lived in Machu Picchu for about 80 years. Then, in the 1530s, soldiers from Spain came to South America. Their leader was Francisco Pizarro. The Spanish soldiers had horses, guns, and new illnesses. Very quickly, they destroyed the Inca empire. They killed the emperor, and Cusco fell.

At this time, the Inca left Machu Picchu. We do not know exactly why. But here is the important thing: the Spanish never found it! The city was very high in the mountains, far from the roads. So the Spanish never went there, and they never broke it.

So Machu Picchu stayed empty. The trees and plants grew over the houses. For almost 400 years, the world outside forgot the city. But the local farmers near the mountain still knew it was there.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened to the Inca empire in the 1530s?

2. Why is it important that the Spanish never found Machu Picchu?

1. Spanish soldiers, led by Francisco Pizarro, came with horses, guns, and new illnesses. They quickly destroyed the empire, killed the emperor, and Cusco fell.

2. Because they never found it, they never broke it. So the city stayed safe and whole for hundreds of years.

Talk about it:
  • Machu Picchu was safe because nobody could find it. Can you think of other things that are safer when they are hidden?

Part 3 — Found again

In 1911, an American explorer called Hiram Bingham came to Peru. He was looking for old Inca cities. A local farmer showed him the way up the steep mountain. Near the top, Bingham met farmers who lived there. Then a small local boy walked with him to the old stones.

People often say that Bingham "discovered" Machu Picchu. But that is not really true. The local people always knew about it! Bingham took photos and wrote about the city. Because of this, the whole world heard about Machu Picchu for the first time.

After that, Machu Picchu became very famous. Today, about 1.5 million people visit every year — so many that Peru must control the numbers. The quiet, private home of a king is now one of the most famous places in the world.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did Hiram Bingham do in 1911 — and why is "discovered" the wrong word?

2. How famous is Machu Picchu today?

1. A local farmer and a local boy showed him the way. He took photos and wrote about it, so the world heard about it. "Discovered" is wrong because the local people always knew it was there.

2. Very famous. About 1.5 million people visit every year — so many that Peru must control the numbers.

Talk about it:
  • The local people knew the city, but Bingham got famous. Is that fair? Who should we remember?

Grammar: the second conditional

We use the second conditional to talk about things that are not real now — things we only imagine or dream about.

How to make it: If + past simple, + would + verb.
For example: "If I visited Peru, I would climb to Machu Picchu."
This is not real — I am not in Peru. I am only imagining it. We use the past simple here, but we are talking about now or the future, not the past.

1. Complete the sentences

1. If I (have) more money, I (travel) to Peru.

2. If the Inca (be) here today, they (be) very surprised.

3. If fewer tourists (come), the old stones (last) longer.

1. had / would travel

2. were (or: was) / would be

3. came / would last

2. Finish the sentence about you

Put the verb in the right form. Then read it out loud.

If I (go) to Machu Picchu, I (take) lots of photos and I (feel) very happy.

went · would take · would feel

Remember: If + past simple, then would + verb. This is something you imagine, not something real.

Task C · A special place (3 minutes)

Tell me about a famous old place in your country. What is it? Who built it? Why is it special? Would you like to take a friend there one day?

Not sure what to talk about? Choose one:

  • An old castle or fort near your home. Who built it? Why is it still there?
  • A big, old church, temple or mosque. It is very old and people built it by hand.
  • An old part of your city — small streets and old houses, hundreds of years old.
  • A place you saw on a school trip when you were a child. What do you remember?
  • A place that almost disappeared, but people saved it. If they had not saved it, it would be gone now.
