Raising Safety Concerns With a Client Under Pressure

B2 Level · Engineering · Client Communication

Engineering · Dewey 620

First Thoughts

The client wants to hit the deadline. You have seen something on site that is not safe. The client needs to hear it — and they need to hear it in a way that does not sound like you are inventing problems.

Raising safety is not about being negative. It is about being the engineer who does the job properly, and says so clearly.

Take a moment with each question below. Share as much detail as you like — specific examples, what you would actually say, what tripped you up last time.

1. When was the last time you had to raise a safety concern? How was it received?

2. Why can ‘safety first’ be a difficult message for a client under pressure to accept?

3. Is there a difference in how you raise a concern in writing and on site? Which is more effective?

4. Have you ever not raised a concern because of the relationship? What happened?

Key Language

Useful chunks of language for this conversation. Read each phrase aloud and think about when you would actually reach for it.

Raising the concern clearly

  • “I need to raise something with you before we move on.”
  • “I am going to flag a safety concern now — I would rather raise it early than be explaining it later.”
  • “I have just come off site and there is something I want to walk you through.”

Being firm without being aggressive

  • “I understand the pressure on the timeline, and I want to help you hit it. On this specific point, I cannot compromise.”
  • “This is not a preference — it is a non-negotiable.”
  • “If you ask me to sign off on this in its current state, my answer is no. I want to tell you that calmly.”

Offering a path forward

  • “Here is what I think we can do to protect both the timeline and the safety of the team.”
  • “If we bring the corrections forward to tomorrow, we can still hit Friday’s milestone.”
  • “Let me send you a short note with three options by end of day.”

Protecting the relationship

  • “I want you to know this comes from the same place as wanting the project to succeed.”
  • “Please do not hear this as me being difficult — hear it as me doing my job properly.”
  • “I am raising it now so that we are on the same page, not on different ones later.”

The Scenario

The situation: You are the project engineer on a mid-sized construction site. The client’s project manager is under pressure to hit Friday’s milestone. During your site walk this morning, you identified a scaffolding issue on the east side that in your professional view is not compliant and must be corrected before work continues on that zone.

Your task: Have a ten-minute conversation with the client’s PM. By the end of it, she should understand (a) exactly what the issue is, (b) that you are not signing off on that zone until it is corrected, and (c) a realistic plan that still protects Friday’s milestone.

Step 2 · Deliver

When you are ready, role-play the conversation out loud. Be ready to handle the follow-up questions below.

Follow-up questions to handle:

  • Is this you being cautious, or is this an actual regulation?
  • If I escalate to your boss, will they take a different view?
  • What does ‘not compliant’ mean in writing — do you need me to stop that whole zone?

Reflection
