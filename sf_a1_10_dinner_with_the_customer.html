Part 1 — A table for two

Seven o'clock. The meeting was good. Very good. Now Mr Hill takes Leo to dinner: a small Italian restaurant near the river.

Waiter: Good evening. Do you have a reservation?

Mr Hill: Yes. Hill, a table for two.

Waiter: This way, please. Here's the menu. Would you like something to drink?

Mr Hill: A glass of red wine, please. Leo?

Leo: Just water for me, please. Sparkling.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How many people are at the table?

2. What does Leo drink?

1. Two: Leo and Mr Hill.

2. Sparkling water. Mr Hill has a glass of red wine.

Talk about it:
  • And you? Wine or water at a business dinner? Why?

Part 2 — Are you ready to order?

Waiter: Are you ready to order?

Mr Hill: Yes. For the starter, the soup, please. And then the fish.

Waiter: And for you, sir?

Leo: I'll have the salad to start. And the pasta, please.

Waiter: The pasta with tomato or with mushrooms?

Leo: With tomato, please.

Waiter: Thank you.

The food comes. It's delicious. Mr Hill talks about London, about football, about his children. Leo understands almost everything. He asks questions. He smiles.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What does Leo order?

2. What do they talk about?

1. The salad to start, then the pasta with tomato.

2. London, football, and Mr Hill's children.

Talk about it:
  • And you? What do you usually order: meat, fish or pasta? A starter, or just the main course?

Part 3 — The bill

Mr Hill: Would you like a dessert?

Leo: No, thank you. Just a coffee.

Mr Hill: Two coffees, please. And the bill.

Leo: Please, let me pay.

Mr Hill: No, no. You are in London. Next time, in your city.

Leo: OK. Next time, my city. Thank you.

Outside, the rain has stopped. Leo walks back to the hotel and calls Carla. "How was it?" "Good," he says. "Very good." And then, in English, because he can: "It was delicious."

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who pays? Why?

2. What does Leo say at the end, in English?

1. Mr Hill. Leo is in London; next time Leo pays, in his city.

2. "It was delicious."

Talk about it:
  • And you? Who pays at a business dinner in your country? What do you say at the end of a good dinner?

Grammar: ordering food

In a restaurant you need one shape for ordering and one shape for asking. Everything else is the menu.

I'll have + the food. "I'll have the fish." "I'll have the soup to start." (I'll = I will — but here it just means: this is my order.)
For the starter / for the main course: "For the starter, the salad. For the main course, the chicken." · No starter? "Just the main course, please."
Could I / Could we have…? = asking, very politely: "Could we have the bill, please?" "Could I have some water?" "Could we have a table by the window?"
with / without · a glass of / a bottle of: "pasta with tomato" · "coffee without sugar" · "a glass of wine" · "a bottle of water" · "a cup of tea"
