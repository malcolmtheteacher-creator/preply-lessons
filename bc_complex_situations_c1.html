Getting Started: Large-Scale Projects

Discussion Questions

Think about large infrastructure projects you know of — bridges, dams, transport hubs, new buildings. What makes them controversial?

What examples of large-scale building projects exist in your country or region?

Consider infrastructure like high-speed railways, new airports, dam projects, metro systems, or urban development zones. These often generate public debate.

Why might such projects become controversial in the community?

Common concerns include environmental impact, ecosystem damage, disruption during construction, aesthetic changes, cost concerns, noise pollution, and traffic congestion.

What strategies can developers use to address public concerns?

Public consultation, transparent communication, environmental impact assessments, community meetings, phased construction, traffic management plans, and clear timelines build trust.

Language Focus: Causative Verbs

In project management, you often need to talk about making others do things. Study these causative structures: make, get, and have.

Get + Infinitive (Most Common in Project Context)

We need to get the PR department to consult with the public about environmental concerns.

Use when persuading someone to do something.

Make + Infinitive (Require or Force)

The PR department must make people aware of the benefits of the project.

Use when someone has an obligation or must ensure something happens.

Have + Infinitive (Less Formal)

We should have the city council introduce traffic-calming measures to minimise disruption.

Use when someone arranges or authorises an action.

Language Focus: Future Time Expressions

Project updates require precise language about timing and progress stages. Click each expression to see how to use it.

NEAR FUTURE — In Progress or About to Start
We're going to be... / We're about to...
We're going to be doing a cost-benefit analysis next week. We're about to finalise the budget.
UPCOMING — Before Long
Before too long... / By the time...
Before too long we'll begin the consultative process. By the time we've finished the design, we'll need a strategy for public presentation.
UNCERTAIN OR UNDECIDED
... is still up in the air
Finalising the timeframe for construction is still up in the air. We haven't decided on the start date yet.
SCHEDULED OR PLANNED
We're meant to have... by...
We're meant to have finalised the timeframe for construction by the end of the year.
IMMINENT — On the Verge Of
We're on the verge of...
We're on the verge of breaking ground. The construction phase is about to begin.
DISTANT FUTURE
... is quite a long way off / still a long way off
Opening the bridge is still a long way off. The project won't be complete for several years.
