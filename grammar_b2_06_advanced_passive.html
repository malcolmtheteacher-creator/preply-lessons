Check your answer CLICK
1. "had already been ripped out" — this finished before another point in the past (the electrician arriving).
2. "the bathroom's being retiled" — happening right now, in progress.
3. "I've just had the front door repainted" — Alex didn't paint it himself; he arranged for someone else to.

Three ways to talk about things being done — one about sequencing the past, one about now, one about who actually does the work.
Today: had been done (before another past moment), is being done (happening right now), and have/get something done (arranged for you, or bad luck) — so you can tell a fuller, more natural story about your life, your home, and the things that happen to you.

Before That Happened

For showing that one thing was already finished before another moment in the past.

Spot the pattern

  • "By the time the fire brigade arrived, the small fire had already been put out."
  • "We got to the wedding late — the speeches had already been given."
  • "I was gutted — my favourite jacket had been sold before I could get back to the shop."

What's the shared structure, and what job is it doing? Say it, then check.

Check the pattern CLICK
had + been + past participle. One action finished before another point in the past — it's the backstory to the main event. Compare: "the jacket was sold" (just a fact) vs "the jacket had been sold" (specifically before I got back — that's exactly why I missed out).
had + been + past participle  (one thing finished before another past moment)
Don't confuse this with the simple past passive. "The bridge was closed" just states a fact. "The bridge had been closed" specifically tells you it happened before another past moment you're describing.

It's Being Sorted

For something happening right now, in progress, not yet finished.

Spot the pattern

  • "The road outside is being resurfaced — the noise is unbelievable."
  • "Our photocopier's being looked at, so print everything downstairs for now."
  • "Two new flats are being built where the old garage used to be."

What's the structure, and how is it different from the news form you already know ("has been done")? Say it, then check.

Check the pattern CLICK
is / are + being + past participle. The action is happening right now, still going on. Compare with "has been done" (finished, and the result matters now) — "is being done" means it's still in progress as you speak.
is / are + being + past participle  (happening now, in progress)
A common slip: "is been" doesn't exist anywhere in English. For something ongoing, it's always is being done, never "is been done".

Getting It Done

For work someone else does for you — whether you arranged it, or it just happened to you.

Spot the pattern — arranged

  • "I'm having the boiler serviced next Tuesday."
  • "She got her nails done before the wedding."
  • "We're getting the windows cleaned this weekend."

None of these people are doing the work themselves. What's the structure? Say it, then check.

Check the pattern CLICK
have / get + something + past participle — you arrange for someone else to do it. Compare: "I cut my hair" (you did it yourself, maybe badly!) vs "I had my hair cut" (a hairdresser did it for you).
have / get + object + past participle  (arranged — someone else does it for you)

Spot the pattern — bad luck

  • "I had my bike stolen outside the station last week."
  • "He got his wallet pickpocketed on holiday."
  • "They had their flight cancelled at the last minute."

Nobody arranged any of this! Same structure, different job. Say the difference, then check.

Check the idea CLICK
Exactly the same structure — have / get + something + past participle — but now nobody arranged it. It's bad luck, something that happened to you. Only the situation tells you which meaning is intended: a haircut you booked, or a phone you lost to a thief.
Watch the word order carefully. Never say "I had stolen my phone" — that means you stole a phone from someone else! The object always goes straight after have/get: had MY PHONE stolen, had THE BOILER serviced.
