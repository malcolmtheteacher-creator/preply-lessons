Common Workplace Ethical Issues

Do you consider these behaviours ethical or unethical?

Scenario A: Dishonest Reference

Show Details

The situation: "He was so awful, we just wanted to see the back of him. I didn"t mention his compulsive lying in that reference he needed!"

What"s unethical: Deliberately omitting important information in a reference to harm someone"s chances of getting a job.

Scenario B: Personal Use of Company Resources

Show Details

The situation: "Of course I use it to phone my family in Australia. There must be some perks for working here!"

What"s unethical: Using company resources (phone line) for personal benefit without permission.

Scenario C: Unfair Use of Break Time

Show Details

The situation: "Smokers get breaks every half an hour, so why shouldn"t I take a couple of hours for lunch instead of one? We haven"t had a pay rise in ages, so they have it coming!"

What"s unethical: Retaliating against unfair company policies by breaking rules, rather than raising the issue through proper channels.

Scenario D: Sick Leave Abuse

Show Details

The situation: "I really fancied a long weekend, so I just took a day off sick. There"s no way I was losing any annual leave."

What"s unethical: Lying about being ill to get extra time off, which may affect colleague workload.

Scenario E: Nepotism (Hiring Family)

Show Details

The situation: "In the current market, it would have been impossible for Sara to get a job anywhere else. It was the least I could do for my niece."

What"s unethical: Hiring a relative over other candidates based on family loyalty, not merit. This unfairly disadvantages other applicants.

Modal Verbs for Ethical Discussions

Use these modals to talk about what people should or shouldn"t do.

Obligation & Permission

must Strong obligation (legally required). "You must follow company policy."
mustn"t Prohibition (forbidden). "You mustn"t take company property home."
should Advice (recommended). "You should report unethical behaviour."
shouldn"t Advice against (not recommended). "You shouldn"t lie on your resume."
don"t have to No obligation (optional). "You don"t have to attend the optional meeting."
can"t Impossibility or prohibition. "You can"t just ignore ethical violations."

Examples Using Modals

Show Examples

About the scenarios:

"People shouldn"t take long lunch breaks, even when they haven"t received a pay rise."

"Managers must provide honest references."

"You don"t have to worry about reporting something that"s not your responsibility, BUT you should if you witness serious wrongdoing."

"Companies mustn"t hire based on family connections instead of merit."

"I can"t just turn a blind eye to dishonest behaviour."

Key point: When discussing ethical issues, use strong modals (must/mustn"t) for laws and rules, and softer modals (should/shouldn"t) for advice and recommendations.

GloboSport Ethical Dilemmas

You work for GloboSport, a multinational sportswear manufacturer. Analyse these situations and decide:

  • How serious is the ethical issue?
  • What can be done to correct it?
  • What are the wider implications?
  • Should someone be warned or disciplined?

Situation 1: Whistleblowing (Ito & Laura)

Show Details

The issue: Ito works in Marketing promoting GloboSport"s social responsibility. But a newspaper recently published articles about human rights abuses in GloboSport factories—low pay, poor conditions, even child labour. At an office party, Ito overhears Laura saying she supplied this information to the newspaper.

Questions to consider:

  • Is Laura a hero for exposing wrongdoing, or did she betray the company?
  • Was going to the press the right way to report the problem?
  • What should happen to Laura? (Warning, dismissal, promotion?)
  • Should GloboSport address the human rights issues?

Situation 2: Nepotism (Gan & Lee)

Show Details

The issue: Gan works in Human Resources and has agreed to give a reference for his cousin Lee, who"s applying for a job at GloboSport. Lee also asks Gan for a copy of the interview questions to prepare.

Questions to consider:

  • Is giving a reference to a family member unethical?
  • Sharing interview questions is clearly unfair to other candidates—should Gan do it?
  • What"s the difference between helping family and workplace dishonesty?
  • What action should the company take?

Situation 3: Accepting Gifts (Eola & Gavin)

Show Details

The issue: Eola is a buyer for GloboSport. On her birthday, she receives an expensive hamper from Gavin, one of her suppliers, with a card saying "For you and your family to enjoy!" Her son Daniel eagerly opens it.

Questions to consider:

  • Is this gift innocent generosity or a bribe to influence purchasing decisions?
  • Should suppliers be allowed to give gifts?
  • Has Eola already accepted the gift (since Daniel opened it)?
  • Is there a conflict of interest?
  • What should Eola do now?

Situation 4: Misusing Company Resources (Claudia & Leo)

Show Details

The issue: Claudia is a PA to the Finance Director. The company photocopier doesn"t track usage by department. Her son Leo is directing a play at a youth club and needs to photocopy 15 scripts (600 pages) for tonight"s rehearsal. He asks Claudia to do it at work.

Questions to consider:

  • Is this a minor perk that all employees do, or theft of company resources?
  • Does the fact that the photocopier doesn"t track usage make it OK?
  • Is helping your son different from doing personal business?
  • What"s the financial impact on the company?
  • Should Claudia be disciplined?

Your Analysis & Discussion

Discussion Questions

1. Do you consider yourself an ethical person? Give an example of when you acted ethically even when it was difficult.

2. How would you feel about reporting the unethical behaviour of a colleague? Would you do it? Why or why not?

3. What would you do if you found a wallet with $100 in it but no ID? Would you try to return it or keep it?

4. Do companies ever act unethically towards their employees? Give examples.

5. Is stealing always wrong? Are there situations where taking something without permission might be justified?

Written Reflection (optional): Choose one GloboSport scenario and write a brief analysis of what you think should happen. Use modal verbs: "Gan should...", "The company mustn"t...", etc.

Discussion time: 5:00

Key Takeaway: Ethical decisions at work involve balancing personal loyalty, company rules, fairness to others, and your own values. There"s rarely a perfect answer, but thinking through the implications helps you make better choices.
