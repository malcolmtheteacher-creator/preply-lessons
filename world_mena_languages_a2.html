“Everyone Speaks Arabic” — Or Do They?

Arabic has many voices. And people speak Persian, Turkish, Kurdish and other languages too.

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. She can speak three languages.
speak (v) — to say words with your voice. Example: “She can speak three languages.”
2. I can't understand that accent.
understand (v) — to know what someone means. Example: “I can't understand that accent.”
3. Arabic is written from right to left.
write (v) — to put words on paper or a screen. Example: “Arabic is written from right to left.”
4. The Arabic alphabet has 28 letters.
letter (n) — one sign in an alphabet, like A or B. Example: “The Arabic alphabet has 28 letters.”
5. “Sugar” is an Arabic word in English.
word (n) — a group of letters with a meaning. Example: ““Sugar” is an Arabic word in English.”
6. Egyptian films are popular in many countries.
film (n) — a story shown on a screen. Example: “Egyptian films are popular in many countries.”
Talk it through:
  • Which word is new for you? Say a sentence with it.
The stereotype: “Everyone in the region speaks Arabic.”

The story

Part 1 — Written and spoken Arabic

In newspapers and on the news, people use one standard form of Arabic. At home, people speak their own dialect. A Moroccan and an Iraqi cannot always understand each other. They often change to standard Arabic or ask the other person to speak slowly.

Quick check:

1. Which form of Arabic is on the news?

2. Can a Moroccan always understand an Iraqi?

1. Standard Arabic.

2. No, not always.

Discuss:
  • Do you speak differently at home and at work?

Part 2 — Other languages

People in Iran speak Persian. People in Turkey speak Turkish. Kurdish people speak Kurdish in several countries. In North Africa, millions of people speak Amazigh languages. Many people can speak French or English too, and some can speak three or four languages.

Quick check:

1. Which language do people speak in Iran?

2. Who speaks Amazigh languages?

1. Persian.

2. Millions of people in North Africa.

Discuss:
  • Which language would you like to speak?

Part 3 — Films and words

Egyptian films are famous, so many people can understand Egyptian Arabic. English has many words from Arabic, like “coffee”, “sugar” and “zero”. The Arabic script goes from right to left. Persian and Urdu use a similar script but are different languages.

Quick check:

1. Which films are famous in the region?

2. Which way is Arabic written?

1. Egyptian films.

2. From right to left.

Discuss:
  • Which words in your language come from other languages?

Grammar — can and can't

Can + verb for ability: “She can speak three languages.”
Can't + verb for no ability: “I can't read Arabic.”
Questions: Can you speak Persian? — Yes, I can. / No, I can't.
