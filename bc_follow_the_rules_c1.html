Smoking in the office

Signposting in Presentations

Signposting language helps your audience follow your ideas. Click each category to see examples of how to structure a professional presentation.

Opening Your Presentation

INTRODUCTION
I'm going to talk about our email policy.
Direct statement of your topic
INTRODUCTION
My topic today is workplace confidentiality.
Formal way to announce your subject
INTRODUCTION
I'd like to begin by explaining our dress code policy.

Structuring Your Ideas

OVERVIEW
My talk is divided into three parts.
Tell your audience how many points you'll cover
SEQUENCING
First of all, I'll explain the background. Then I'll outline the current rules.
Show the order of your ideas clearly
TRANSITION
Now we'll move on to the exceptions.
Signal a shift to the next point
TRANSITION
I'd like to turn to the question of enforcement.
Introduce a new topic with formality
DEEPENING
The next issue I'd like to discuss is what happens if someone breaks the rules.
Move to a related but distinct point

Concluding Your Presentation

CLOSING A SECTION
That's all I'd like to say about the dress code.
Signal you're finishing one topic
CLOSING A SECTION
So much for that. Let me now explain the consequences.
Move away from one point and introduce another
SUMMARY
To sum up, these three rules are essential for a healthy workplace.
Recap your main ideas before finishing
SUMMARY
To conclude, everyone has a responsibility to follow these guidelines.
Strong, formal ending to your presentation
SUMMARY

Listening for Rules and Information

When you listen to a presentation about workplace rules, focus on these questions. This helps you understand the key information.

Key Listening Questions

What is the rule exactly?

Write down the specific policy being explained.

Why does this rule exist?

Listen for the reason or purpose behind it.

Who does this apply to?

Does it affect all employees, or specific groups?

Are there exceptions?

Can the rule be broken in certain situations?

What happens if someone breaks the rule?

What are the consequences?

Example: A Listening Exercise

Read this example policy explanation. Can you identify the rule, reason, scope, exceptions, and consequences?

Email Usage Policy:

"Our company policy requires all employees to use company email for business purposes only. We monitor this because we need to protect client information and company data. The rule applies to everyone in the organisation. However, brief personal use is acceptable during lunch breaks. Serious breaches can lead to disciplinary action."

The rule: Company email for business only

The reason: Protect information and data

Who: All employees

Exceptions: Brief personal use during lunch

Consequences: Disciplinary action for serious breaches

Giving Your Presentation

You will give a 3-5 minute presentation about a workplace rule or policy from your field. Use signposting language to structure your ideas clearly.

Your goal:
  • Choose one workplace rule or policy
  • Explain the rule and why it exists
  • Describe who it applies to and any exceptions
  • Explain the consequences of breaking it
  • Use signposting language throughout

Structure Your Presentation

1. Opening (use signposting):

"My topic today is... I'd like to begin by explaining..."

2. Main points (3-4 ideas, each with signposting):

"First of all... Now we'll move on to... The next issue I'd like to discuss..."

3. Closing (use signposting):

"To sum up... To conclude... In short..."
