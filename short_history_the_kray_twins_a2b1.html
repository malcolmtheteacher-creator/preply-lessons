The Kray Twins

Two brothers who controlled crime in London — and became famous.

Today: tell the story of the Kray twins, and talk about crime and fame. Grammar: past simple and past continuous.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The East End boys

Ronnie and Reggie Kray were twin brothers. They looked exactly the same. They were born in 1933 in a poor part of London called the East End.

As young men, they were good boxers. But they liked fighting too much. One day, Ronnie hit a policeman in the street. Then Reggie hit a second policeman. The brothers went to prison for a short time.

Later, the army wanted them to be soldiers. But the twins did not want to. They ran away again and again, so the army sent them away. After that, they chose a life of crime. They made a gang and took protection money from local shops. People in the East End were afraid of them.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What sport were the twins good at? Why did they stop?

2. How did their gang make money?

1. They were good boxers, but they got into trouble (they hit two policemen) and the army sent them away — so they stopped.

2. They took "protection money" from shops in the East End.

Talk about it:
  • The twins were good at boxing, but they chose crime. Why do you think some people choose crime?

Part 2 — Famous and dangerous

In the 1960s, the twins became famous. They owned a popular nightclub. Famous photographers took their pictures, and they met film stars and singers. Many people liked them — they looked exciting and glamorous!

But they were also dangerous and violent. There is one famous story. Ronnie was once in a hospital, because doctors said he was mentally ill. One day, Reggie came to visit him. The brothers were wearing the same clothes. Because they were identical twins, they secretly swapped places! Ronnie walked out of the hospital, and Reggie stayed inside. The police could not say which twin was which — so Ronnie escaped.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did the twins become famous?

2. How did Ronnie escape from the hospital?

1. They had a popular nightclub, famous photographers took their pictures, and they met film stars and singers.

2. The identical twins wore the same clothes and swapped places during a visit; the police could not tell them apart.

Talk about it:
  • Many people liked the Krays, even though they were dangerous. Why do you think people like dangerous men in films and stories?

Part 3 — The end

But the twins became more and more violent. In 1966, Ronnie killed a man in a pub. The next year, Reggie killed another man. This time, there were witnesses — people who saw it happen.

A clever detective worked for many months. Slowly, he found witnesses who were brave enough to talk. In 1968, the police arrested the twins. In 1969, a court sent them to prison for life — at least thirty years.

Ronnie died in hospital in 1995. Reggie died in 2000. But people still remember them today. There are many films and books about the famous Kray twins.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What finally stopped the twins?

2. What happened to them in the end?

1. They killed two men in front of witnesses, and a detective found witnesses brave enough to talk. The twins were arrested and sent to prison.

2. They went to prison for life. Ronnie died in 1995; Reggie died in 2000.

Talk about it:
  • The twins wanted to be famous, and they got their wish. Was it worth it?

Grammar: past simple & past continuous

To tell a story, we use two past tenses together.

Past simple = a finished action: "they made a gang," "Ronnie killed a man."
Past continuous (was/were + -ing) = a longer action, happening at that time: "while Reggie was visiting the hospital…"
We often use them together with while and when: a long action (continuous) and a short action that happens inside it (simple).

1. Choose the right tense

1. While the twins (run) the nightclub, their crimes (get) worse.

2. When the police (arrive), Ronnie (already / leave).

3. Reggie (visit) the hospital when the brothers (swap) places.

1. were running / were getting (both long actions happening at the same time)

2. arrived / had already left (or: "left") (a short, finished action)

3. was visiting / swapped (long action + short action inside it)

2. Tell a short part of the story

Put the verbs in the past simple or past continuous. Then read it out loud.

One evening, a man (drink) in a pub. He (not see) Ronnie come in. Suddenly, everything (change). A few minutes later, Ronnie (leave) and (disappear) into the streets.

was drinking · did not see · changed · left · disappeared

The long "background" action is in the past continuous; the sudden, finished actions are in the past simple.
