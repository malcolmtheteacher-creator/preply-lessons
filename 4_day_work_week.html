The 4-Day Work Week

Would you work less and produce more?

The Iceland Experiment

Between 2015 and 2019, Iceland ran the world's biggest test. Over 2,500 workers - about 1% of the entire country - worked 4 days instead of 5, with the same salary.

The result? Researchers called it an "overwhelming success." Productivity stayed the same or improved. Workers reported less stress and better work-life balance. Today, 86% of Icelandic workers have moved to shorter hours.

Thomas, 34, software developer in Lyon: "My company tested it for 6 months. At first, I didn't believe it would work. But I started finishing tasks faster because I knew I had less time. And Fridays with my kids? That changed everything."

Marie, 41, HR manager in Paris: "We lost some employees because they wanted the 4-day week. Our competitors offer it. Now we're discussing it too - not because we want to, but because we have to."

The French Connection

France already has the 35-hour work week. Some French companies are now testing the 4-day model. The debate is growing: is this the future of work, or just a trend for tech companies?

Language Focus A

Click each card to reveal the meaning and examples. Then practice with "Now You Try"!

work-life balance
Meaning:

The amount of time you spend working compared to time for family, hobbies, and rest

Form:

Noun phrase (uncountable) - "good/bad/better work-life balance"

"The 4-day week gave me a much better work-life balance."
NOW YOU TRY: How would you describe YOUR work-life balance right now? What would make it better?
to be skeptical (about something)
Meaning:

To have doubts; to not fully believe something will work

Form:

Adjective - "I'm skeptical about..." / "to be skeptical that..."

Pronunciation:

/ˈskeptɪkəl/ - SKEP-ti-kul

"My boss is skeptical about the 4-day week. He thinks we'll just work less."
NOW YOU TRY: What is something new at work that you were skeptical about at first?
to boost productivity
Meaning:

To increase how much work gets done; to make people/systems more efficient

Form:

Verb + Noun - "boost productivity/sales/morale"

"Surprisingly, working fewer days can actually boost productivity."
NOW YOU TRY: What boosts YOUR productivity? What kills it?

Language Focus B

to cut down on (something)
Meaning:

To reduce the amount of something; to do less of something

Form:

Phrasal verb - "cut down on" + noun/gerund

"We cut down on unnecessary meetings. That saved 5 hours per week."
NOW YOU TRY: What could your company cut down on to be more efficient?
to afford to (do something)
Meaning:

To have enough money/time/resources to do something (often used in negative: "can't afford to")

Form:

Verb + to infinitive - "can/can't afford to..."

"Small companies say they can't afford to lose a whole day of work."
NOW YOU TRY: Do you think YOUR company can afford to try a 4-day week? Why or why not?

Quick Review

You now have 5 useful phrases:

  1. work-life balance - the time split between work and personal life
  2. to be skeptical - to have doubts about something
  3. to boost productivity - to increase efficiency/output
  4. to cut down on - to reduce something
  5. to afford to - to have the resources to do something
