When a Superpower Arrests a President

🚨 Breaking: The US Just Arrested Venezuela's President

What happens when the world's most powerful nation decides the rules of international law don't apply to them? We're about to find out.
January 3, 2026 US military forces captured Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro in Caracas and flew him to New York to face criminal charges

⏱️ Timeline of Events (Today)

Early Morning: US forces launched "Operation Absolute Resolve" – a large-scale military strike on Caracas, Venezuela's capital
Mid-Morning: Elite Delta Force soldiers captured President Maduro and his wife Cilia Flores at their compound
Afternoon: Maduro transported to Guantanamo Bay, then flown to New York
Evening: Maduro processed at DEA headquarters in Manhattan, fingerprinted, photographed, and transferred to MDC Brooklyn federal detention center
Now: Maduro awaits arraignment on Monday. Trump announces the US will "run Venezuela" until a "proper transition"
$50 Million The bounty placed on Maduro's head – the largest reward ever offered for a sitting head of state

📋 The Charges Against Maduro

The US Department of Justice has charged Maduro with:

  • Narco-terrorism conspiracy – allegedly partnering with drug cartels
  • Cocaine importation conspiracy – accused of facilitating drug shipments to the US
  • Weapons charges – possession of machine guns and destructive devices
  • Selling diplomatic passports to known drug traffickers (2006-2008)

Attorney General Pam Bondi called Maduro "one of the world's most notorious narco-traffickers" and a "threat to national security."

⚖️ The Legal Controversy

Legal experts are divided. The operation raises unprecedented questions:

  • International law: "I see no legal basis for us to go into another country and take a leader without an extradition treaty" – Mark Nevitt, former Navy attorney
  • Head of state immunity: International law typically protects sitting leaders from arrest by foreign courts
  • The US position: Washington has not recognized Maduro as legitimate since 2019, arguing the 2018 and 2024 elections were fraudulent
  • Precedent: This is the first time the US has militarily captured a foreign head of state since Manuel Noriega in Panama (1989)
8 Million Venezuelans who have fled the country under Maduro's rule – one of the largest refugee crises in modern history

🌍 Global Reactions

Celebrations: Venezuelan Americans in New York gathered outside the detention center, celebrating Maduro's capture. María Corina Machado, opposition leader and 2025 Nobel Peace Prize winner, is being discussed as a potential new leader.

Criticism: Former VP Kamala Harris said: "Donald Trump's actions in Venezuela do not make America safer... That Maduro is a brutal, illegitimate dictator does not change the fact that this action was both unlawful and unwise."

Inside Venezuela: The Supreme Court has named Vice President Delcy Rodríguez as acting president. She must constitutionally hold elections within 30 days.

🤔 Think about it: Does the end justify the means? If Maduro truly is a dictator and drug trafficker, does that make this operation legitimate – or does violating international law set a dangerous precedent?

Key Expressions (Part 1)

👆 Click each card to reveal meaning, examples, and practice prompts

1. "To set a precedent"
Formality: Formal (legal/business)
Meaning: To establish a pattern or example that others will follow in the future. In legal and business contexts, precedents are previous decisions that influence future ones. Can be positive or negative.
Example: "Legal experts warn that Operation Absolute Resolve sets a dangerous precedent – if the US can arrest foreign leaders without extradition treaties, what stops other powers from doing the same?"
Business context: "When our company approved remote work permanently, it set a precedent that other departments quickly followed."
NOW YOU TRY: Think of a decision in your country, workplace, or personal life that set a precedent – either good or bad. Describe what happened and its consequences.
2. "To be held accountable"
Formality: Formal (corporate/legal)
Meaning: To be required to explain and accept responsibility for one's actions, decisions, or failures. Common in corporate governance, politics, and legal contexts.
Example: "Attorney General Bondi declared that 'under President Trump's leadership, Maduro will be held accountable for his despicable crimes.' But critics ask: who holds the US accountable for violating international law?"
Business context: "After the financial scandal, shareholders demanded that the entire board be held accountable for their oversight failures."
NOW YOU TRY: Think of a situation where someone should have been held accountable but wasn't – or was held accountable unfairly. What happened? What should have been different?
💼 Language Tip: All five expressions are commonly used in professional, academic, and media contexts. You'll hear them in news reports, business meetings, political debates, and formal writing – mastering them signals sophisticated English fluency.

Key Expressions (Part 2)

👆 Click each card to reveal meaning, examples, and practice prompts

3. "To overstep (one's boundaries/authority)"
Formality: Neutral to Formal
Meaning: To go beyond what is acceptable, appropriate, or legally permitted. To exceed the limits of one's power or role.
Example: "Critics argue the Trump administration has overstepped its authority by conducting military operations on foreign soil without congressional approval or international legal justification."
Business context: "The regional manager overstepped his authority when he approved a $2 million contract without consulting headquarters."
NOW YOU TRY: Describe a situation where someone overstepped their authority – at work, in government, or in a relationship. How did others react? What were the consequences?
4. "To tip the balance"
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: To be the deciding factor that changes a situation from one state to another. To shift the balance of power or influence.
Example: "Maduro's capture could tip the balance of power in Latin America. China and Russia have lost a key ally, while the US has reasserted dominance in the Western Hemisphere."
Business context: "The new sustainability regulations could tip the balance in favor of companies that invested early in green technology."
NOW YOU TRY: What factors typically tip the balance when you're making an important decision? Think of a specific example where one thing changed everything.
5. "To face the consequences"
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: To experience the results (usually negative) of one's actions or decisions. To deal with the outcomes, whether intended or not.
Example: "While Maduro faces the consequences of alleged drug trafficking, the US may also face consequences – diplomatically, economically, and in terms of international reputation."
Business context: "Companies that ignored ESG requirements are now facing the consequences as investors withdraw funding and regulators impose fines."
NOW YOU TRY: Describe a situation in business or geopolitics where someone had to face the consequences of a controversial decision. Was it fair?
💡 Quick Review – All 5 Expressions:
  1. Set a precedent – Establish a pattern for the future
  2. Be held accountable – Be required to take responsibility
  3. Overstep (boundaries) – Go beyond acceptable limits
  4. Tip the balance – Be the deciding factor that changes things
  5. Face the consequences – Deal with the results of actions
