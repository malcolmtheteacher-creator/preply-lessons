👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Parents vs Relatives

A2 • Italian speakers • False friends: parenti ≠ parents

🔍 Let's notice what we see

Look at this sentence. What's the problem?

"I visited my parents last weekend."

(But they mean their aunts, uncles, cousins, and grandparents — not just mom and dad)

Does it mean they visited ONLY their mom and dad? Or their whole family? What's the issue?

⚠️ The False Friend:

The Italian word "parenti" looks like English "parents", but they mean DIFFERENT things!

In Italian: parenti = all family members (parenti = relatives)

In English: parents = ONLY your mother and father

In English: relatives = everyone in your family (aunts, uncles, cousins, grandparents, etc.)

🛠️ Understand the difference

🚀 Now you try — no help!

Choose the correct word: parents or relatives. Check your answers when you're done.

1. "My _____ are very old now. They're in their 80s." (parents / relatives)
Answer: "My parents are very old now."

→ Because this is about aging people they live with (their mum and dad).

2. "Last month, I had a party and invited all my _____ from across the country."
Answer: "Last month, I had a party and invited all my relatives from across the country."

→ "All my relatives" = extended family from different places.

3. "My _____ taught me how to cook. My mother learned from her mother."
Answer: "My parents taught me how to cook."

→ It's about parents passing knowledge to their children.

4. "At Christmas, we always gather with _____ — about 30 people in total!"
Answer: "At Christmas, we always gather with relatives — about 30 people in total!"

→ 30 people = the whole extended family, not just parents.
