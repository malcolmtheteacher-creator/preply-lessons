Conversation Starters for Different Situations

At a Wedding

"Do you know the bride or groom?"
"That was a fantastic ceremony, wasn't it?"
"How do you know the couple?"

At a Work Dinner

"I don't think we've met. My name is..."
"What department do you work in?"
"How's your day going?"

At a Conference

"Do you usually attend this conference?"
"Which sessions have you been to so far?"
"That was an interesting presentation, wasn't it?"

Waiting for a Meeting

"Have you been waiting long?"
"Do you work in this building?"
"I hope this meeting starts soon. How's your day going?"

On a Train or Flight

"Are you travelling for work or pleasure?"
"Do you often take this route?"
"It's a lovely day, isn't it?"

How to Say Goodbye Professionally

"Let me give you my business card" — Offer contact information

"It's been lovely chatting to you" — Show appreciation for the conversation

"Can I take your number?" — Ask for contact information

"Let's catch up again later" — Suggest staying in touch

"I hope you enjoy the rest of the evening" — End with a kind wish

Practice Starting Conversations

Scenario 1: You're at a work conference coffee break

You see someone standing alone by the coffee table. You don't know them. Start a conversation. Use a question about the conference or the day.

Example response: "Hi, I'm Alex. Do you usually attend this conference? I've really enjoyed the morning sessions so far."

Scenario 2: At a wedding dinner

You're sitting next to someone at dinner. You haven't met before. Break the ice and find out how they know the couple.

Example response: "That was a beautiful ceremony, wasn't it? I'm Sarah, by the way. How do you know the bride?"

Scenario 3: Waiting for a meeting with a new colleague

You're both waiting outside the boss's office. Make friendly conversation about your day.

Example response: "Hi, I don't think we've met. I'm from the marketing team. How's your day going so far?"

Scenario 4: Saying goodbye professionally

You've had a great conversation at a networking event. Now you need to say goodbye and exchange contact information before moving to another conversation.

Example response: "It's been lovely chatting to you. Let me give you my business card so we can catch up again later. I hope you enjoy the rest of the evening!"

Reflect on Socialising Customs

1. Networking customs: What are the most important things to remember when networking in your country or culture? What topics are safe to discuss with strangers?

2. Small talk styles: Is small talk common where you live? What topics do people usually talk about with colleagues or acquaintances?

3. Business cards: How common is it to exchange business cards or contact information in your country? When is the right time to do this?

4. Personal questions: Are there questions that are considered too personal when meeting someone for the first time in your culture?

5. Difficult conversations: Describe a time when you found it difficult to start or continue a conversation in English. What could you say next time?
