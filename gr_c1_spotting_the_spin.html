"I completely understand" + acknowledging the feeling + then pivoting to action. Classic empathy-first structure.

Redirect

The question was about tax. The answer is about jobs and infrastructure. Total topic change, wrapped in "what we should focus on."

Tech CEO · Asked if layoffs are coming
"We are looking at every option to ensure the long-term health of the company. We are committed to making the right decisions for all stakeholders."
Which move?

Vague Commitment

"Looking at every option" and "committed to" — sounds active and responsible, but says absolutely nothing specific. The question was yes or no. The answer was neither.

Bridge

"That is a fair question" = small agreement. "And what I would add" = the bridge to their preferred message. Classic acknowledge-then-pivot.

Airline CEO · After flight cancellations
"What has not been widely reported is that 94% of our flights operated on time last month. We handled 3 million passengers safely. The cancellations, while regrettable, affected less than 2% of our total schedule."
Which move?

Context Bomb

"What has not been widely reported" is the classic trigger. Then flooding with positive statistics to make the problem look small in context.

Company spokesperson · Asked about merger rumours
"I am not in a position to comment on market speculation. What I can say is that the company remains focused on delivering value to our shareholders."
Which move?

Non-Answer

"Not in a position to comment" = textbook non-answer. The add-on about shareholders is just filler to not end on silence.

Energy company CEO · Asked about pollution
"I hear the concerns of communities living near our facilities, and those concerns are valid. Nobody should worry about the air their children breathe. That is why we have invested 500 million in clean technology this year alone — more than any other company in the sector."
This one uses more than one move. Can you spot them both?
↓ Tap to check

Empathy Shield + Context Bomb

Opens with empathy ("I hear the concerns... those concerns are valid"). Then drops the context bomb with the 500 million figure. Double move — very effective.

Politician · Asked why they broke an election promise
"You are absolutely right that we made that commitment, and I stand by the intention behind it. The reality is that when we took office, we inherited a deficit that was far larger than anyone expected. We have had to make difficult choices, and I believe those choices have put us in a stronger position for the future."
How many moves can you find? There are at least three.
↓ Tap to check

Bridge + Context Bomb + Redirect

"You are absolutely right" = bridge. "We inherited a deficit" = context bomb (it is not our fault). "Stronger position for the future" = redirect away from the broken promise to future success. A masterclass in spin.

Scenario: Pharmaceutical Company

A pharmaceutical company has been accused of pricing a life-saving drug at 50 times its production cost. The CEO is holding a press conference.

Journalist
"Your drug costs $2 to make and you sell it for $100. How do you justify that?"
CEO
"What people do not always see is the 15 years and 2 billion dollars of research that went into developing this drug. The $2 is the manufacturing cost — it does not reflect the decades of science behind it."
Which move? Was it effective? Would it convince you?
↓ Tap to check

Context Bomb

The R&D cost reframes the story entirely. Whether you believe it depends on whether you think $100 per dose over millions of patients is justified by $2 billion once.

Journalist
"But people are dying because they cannot afford it. Does that not matter to you?"
CEO
"Of course it matters. Every patient matters to us. That is exactly why we have a programme that provides the drug free of charge to anyone who cannot afford it. We have given away over 5 million doses."
Which move? How does the emotional appeal change things?
↓ Tap to check

Empathy Shield + Context Bomb

"Every patient matters" = empathy. "5 million free doses" = context bomb. Notice how the CEO never actually addresses the pricing itself — just keeps adding positive context.

Journalist
"Will you lower the price?"
CEO
"We are constantly reviewing our pricing to make sure it is fair and sustainable. We are committed to working with governments and insurers to improve access."
Did they actually answer? What did they commit to?
↓ Tap to check

Vague Commitment

"Constantly reviewing" and "committed to working with" = absolutely zero commitment to lowering the price. The answer is no, but it sounds like maybe.

Everyday Spin

These techniques are not just for press conferences. People use them on you every day. Can you spot them?

Your boss · When you ask about promotion
"I really value your contribution. You have been doing great work. Let me have a conversation with HR and we will revisit this soon."
What is the actual answer? What move is being used?
↓ Tap to check

Empathy Shield + Vague Commitment

The answer is effectively "not now" — but it is wrapped in praise and a promise to "revisit." The word "soon" does the heavy lifting. How would you pin them down?

A friend · Who always cancels plans
"I know, I know — I am the worst! But you know how crazy work has been. Next weekend, I promise. Let me check my schedule and I will text you."
Genuine or spin? Which moves?
↓ Tap to check

Empathy Shield + Context Bomb + Vague Commitment

Self-deprecation is a form of empathy shield. "Crazy work" is the context bomb (it is not my fault). "Let me check" is the vague commitment. Three moves in three sentences. Whether it is genuine depends on whether next weekend actually happens.

Customer service · When you complain
"I completely understand your frustration, and I apologise for the inconvenience. Your feedback is really important to us. I am going to escalate this to our specialist team and someone will be in touch within 48 hours."
How many moves? Is this good service or good spin?
↓ Tap to check

Empathy Shield + Bridge + Vague Commitment

This is interesting because it might be completely genuine OR completely scripted. The moves are identical either way. The test: does someone actually call in 48 hours?

Spin vs Counter-Spin

Now the real test. The teacher will play a politician, CEO, or everyday spin-user. Your job is to spot the move in real time and call it out.

Round 1: The Interview

The teacher will give a spun answer to a question. You have 15 seconds to:

  1. Name the move they just used
  2. Ask a follow-up question that the spin cannot dodge

A good follow-up is specific, direct, and forces a real answer. "Can you give me a specific date?" "That is not what I asked — my question was..." "You mentioned X, but what about Y?"

Tell the teacher you are ready. They will begin.

Round 2: Write the Spin

Now YOU write the spin. Here is the question:

"Your company has been dumping waste in the river for 10 years. What do you have to say?"

Write the most convincing spun answer you can, using at least 2 different moves.

SPEAK: Now read it out loud. Does it sound convincing? Which moves did you use?

Round 3: The Journalist

Swap roles. The teacher now gives you a scenario and you ask the tough questions. Your goal: do not let them spin you. Keep asking until you get a real answer.

Tips for good journalism:

  • "With respect, you have not answered my question."
  • "Let me ask that again more simply..."
  • "You said X, but the evidence shows Y."
  • "Can you give me a yes or no?"
