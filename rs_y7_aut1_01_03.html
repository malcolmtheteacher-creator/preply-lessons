Smart's Dimensions Applied

The Big Idea

If religion really is seven dimensions in different combinations, then all worldviews should have all seven. The difference is not whether they have them, but how they express them.

The Story and the Belief (Narrative + Doctrinal)

Every worldview has a story about who we are and core beliefs about meaning and truth.

Christianity

Narrative: God created the world, humans fell into sin, Jesus came to save us, and we'll face judgment.
Doctrinal: Jesus is God's Son. The Trinity. Salvation through faith in Christ.

Islam

Narrative: God created all; prophets were sent to guide humanity; the Qur'an is God's final revelation; the Day of Judgment will come.
Doctrinal: Tawhid (absolute oneness of God). Muhammad is the final prophet. The Five Pillars define right living.

Buddhism

Narrative: Siddhartha discovered that life contains suffering caused by craving; he found the path to end it.
Doctrinal: The Four Noble Truths. The Eightfold Path. No creator God, but a path to enlightenment.

Humanism

Narrative: Humans evolved; we have no cosmic destiny; meaning comes from the here-and-now.
Doctrinal: Reason and evidence matter. The supernatural doesn't. Human flourishing is the goal.

How to Live and How to Practice (Ethical + Ritual)

Judaism

Ethical: The Torah commands justice, mercy, and love of neighbour. The Talmud teaches how to apply these daily.
Ritual: Shabbat (weekly rest), kashrut (dietary laws), festivals, Bar/Bat Mitzvah, prayer.

Hinduism

Ethical: Dharma — your duty based on your stage of life and role. Right action leads to good karma.
Ritual: Puja (worship), yoga, meditation, pilgrimage, festivals like Diwali and Holi.

Sikhism

Ethical: Honesty, hard work, sharing with others. All people are equal before God.
Ritual: Prayer, langar (communal meal open to all), wearing the Five Ks, Gurdwara worship.

Experience, Community, and the Physical (Experiential + Institutional + Material)

All Seven in Action: Christianity at Christmas

Narrative + Doctrinal: The birth story of Jesus · Ethical: Messages of peace and goodwill · Ritual: Church services, carol singing, advent candles · Experiential: Awe when you hear a choir; joy with family · Institutional: Churches organize services; clergy lead · Material: Christmas trees, nativity scenes, church buildings

All Seven in Action: Buddhism's Bodhi Day

Narrative + Doctrinal: The Buddha's awakening · Ethical: Commitment to compassion · Ritual: Temple meditation, lighting lanterns · Experiential: Peace during meditation · Institutional: Monasteries organize events · Material: Temples, statues, prayer beads, incense

Different stories, different rituals, different buildings — but the same seven dimensions.

Match Examples to Dimensions

Model: The Islamic Hajj

Narrative: Following in the footsteps of Muhammad and Abraham · Doctrinal: One of the Five Pillars · Ethical: Teaches humility and equality (all wear the same white clothes) · Ritual: Circling the Kaaba, prayers · Experiential: Profound spiritual experience; connection to 2 billion Muslims · Institutional: Organised by Islamic authorities · Material: The Kaaba, sacred stones, pilgrim's dress

One practice can express all seven dimensions. They're not separate boxes but interlocking parts of a whole system.

Key Terms

Narrative Dimension
The stories and myths that give a worldview its origin, identity, and direction.
Doctrinal Dimension
The core beliefs and truth claims — what a worldview says is real and true.
Ethical Dimension
Rules for living — how to treat others and live a good life.
Ritual Dimension
Practices, worship, and ceremonies that bind communities and focus the mind.
Experiential Dimension
Feelings, emotions, and transformative experiences within a worldview.
Institutional Dimension
Organisations, leaders, and community structures that keep a worldview alive.
Material Dimension
Buildings, objects, art, and physical things connected to a worldview.
Interlocking
Smart's dimensions don't work alone — they connect and reinforce each other within any practice.
