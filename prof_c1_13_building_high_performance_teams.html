Building High-Performance Teams

C1 Level · Team Leadership

Key Vocabulary

Click each card to reveal the definition.

Cohesion The degree to which team members bond together and work toward a common goal; team unity and togetherness.
Diversity The inclusion of people with different backgrounds, perspectives, skills, and experiences on a team.
Psychological Safety The belief that you can take interpersonal risks in a team without fear of negative consequences or embarrassment.
Accountability The responsibility each team member takes for their actions and contributions to the team's success.
Trust Confidence that team members will follow through on commitments and act in the team's best interest.
Synergy The interaction of team members that produces a combined effect greater than the sum of individual contributions.
Role Clarity Clear understanding of each team member's responsibilities, expectations, and areas of authority.
Conflict Resolution The process of addressing disagreements and tensions within the team in a constructive and respectful way.
High-Performer A team member who consistently exceeds expectations, demonstrates excellence, and contributes significantly to goals.
Underperformer A team member whose output or behavior falls below acceptable standards and impacts team results.

Tuckman's Stages of Team Development

Bruce Tuckman identified four stages that most teams go through. Understanding these helps leaders support their teams effectively.

Stage 1: Forming

What's happening: The team is new. Members are getting to know each other, defining roles, and establishing ground rules. Energy is high but anxiety may be present.

Leader's role: Provide clarity on goals, expectations, and roles. Be welcoming and set a positive tone. Facilitate introductions and relationship-building.

Characteristics: Politeness, uncertainty, enthusiasm, dependence on the leader.

Stage 2: Storming

What's happening: Differences emerge. Team members compete for influence, challenge authority, or disagree on direction. This can feel chaotic or uncomfortable.

Leader's role: Don't avoid conflict—manage it. Acknowledge different perspectives. Establish clear rules and decision-making processes. Stay calm and fair.

Characteristics: Conflict, frustration, competition, resistance to authority.

Stage 3: Norming

What's happening: The team develops shared norms and values. Cohesion increases. Members support each other and focus on collective goals rather than individual agendas.

Leader's role: Reinforce positive behaviors. Celebrate milestones. Encourage collaboration and peer recognition. Step back gradually to empower the team.

Characteristics: Agreement, harmony, cooperation, shared responsibility.

Stage 4: Performing

What's happening: The team is fully functional and productive. Members work independently but in coordination. They solve problems together without constant direction.

Leader's role: Delegate and trust. Focus on strategy and removing obstacles. Allow the team autonomy. Provide growth opportunities and stretch assignments.

Characteristics: Autonomy, high productivity, mutual support, shared leadership.

Key Insight

Teams don't move linearly through these stages. They may regress when new members join, roles change, or external pressures increase. Good leaders recognize these dynamics and adjust their approach accordingly.

Language for Setting Expectations

Clear Goal-Setting:

"Our goal this quarter is to [specific outcome]. Here's why it matters: [business impact]. Your role is to [specific responsibility]."

"I expect you to [behavior/outcome] because [reasoning]. How can I support you in achieving this?"

Language for Running Effective Meetings

Opening with Purpose:

"We're here to [specific purpose]. We have [time] and we need to [what success looks like]."

Soliciting Input:

"I'd like to hear from everyone. Who has thoughts on this?"

"What are we missing? What concerns do you have?"

"[Name], you've been quiet. What's your perspective?"

Making Decisions:

"We've heard all the perspectives. Here's my decision and why: [reasoning]."

"I'm open to this decision being revisited if circumstances change, but for now we're moving forward with [decision]."

Closing with Clarity:

"To summarize: [decisions], [action items], [ownership]. [Name] is responsible for X by [date]. Does anyone have questions?"

Language for Addressing Poor Performance

Initial Conversation (Private):

"I've noticed [specific behavior/outcome]. This is impacting [team/project]. Can you help me understand what's going on?"

Listen first. There may be barriers you're unaware of.

Setting Clear Standards:

"Moving forward, I need you to [specific, measurable expectation]. I'm here to support you, but this change is non-negotiable."

"Let's check in weekly to discuss progress. Here's what I'm looking for: [specifics]."

Recognizing Improvement:

"I've seen real improvement in [specific area]. Thank you for taking this seriously. This is what I expect going forward."

If Performance Doesn't Improve:

"We've given this [timeframe] and the improvement hasn't met the standard we discussed. I need to be honest that [consequence: role may not be the right fit, we may need to explore other options, etc.]."

Language for Building Trust & Psychological Safety

Inviting Dissent:

"I value perspectives that challenge my thinking. If you disagree, I want to hear it."

"This is a complex issue. There's no obvious answer. Let's debate this openly."

Admitting Mistakes:

"I handled that badly. Here's what I should have done instead. I appreciate your patience."

"I don't have all the answers. Let's figure this out together."

Celebrating Effort Over Perfection:

"You took a risk here and it didn't work out. That's how we learn. What did we take away from this?"

"I appreciate you being honest about the challenge rather than hiding it."

Reflection: Your Ideal Team

Think about the best team you've been part of. What made it work?

Consider these questions:

  • What were the defining characteristics of the team?
  • How did the leader contribute to its success?
  • How did team members treat each other?
  • How were conflicts handled?
  • What role did diversity play?
  • How was accountability managed?
