A Leaving Party

Your friend from work is leaving. Today is their last day. Time to say goodbye, say thank you, and wish them good luck.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is Friday evening. There is a small party in the office. There is cake, there are drinks in plastic cups, and everyone is talking. It is a happy party, but a little bit sad too.

It is sad because your friend Sam is leaving. Sam worked here for eight years. Now Sam has a new job in another city. Today is Sam's last day, and after today you will not see Sam every day.

So you want to say some nice words. Not a big speech — just a few kind words. You want to say goodbye, say thank you for the good years, and wish Sam good luck. Short and warm is perfect.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Has a friend from your work, school, or class ever left? How did you feel — happy for them, sad, or both?
  2. When someone leaves, do you say goodbye, or is it hard for you? Why?

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each, then change. Find the phrases from your toolkit. Listen for the warm, kind words.

SamWell, today is my last day. I feel a little sad.
YouMe too, Sam. We will miss you. Eight years — that is a long time!
SamIt is a long time. This is a good place, and you are good people.
YouThank you for everything, Sam. You helped me a lot in my first year.
SamOh, that is kind. I liked working with you very much.
YouCan everyone listen for one minute? I want to say a few words about Sam.
SamOh no, please! You know I don't like a fuss.
YouJust a small one! Sam is leaving today for a new job in another city. We are all a little sad.
SamI am happy and sad at the same time. I will miss you all.
YouWe are happy for you too. Good luck in your new job, Sam!
SamThank you so much. Everyone, please keep in touch!
YouOf course! Now — everyone, please raise your glass. To Sam!
SamHa! Thank you, everyone. This is not goodbye — it is "see you soon"!
YouSee you soon, Sam. Take care, and be happy!

From memory — don't look up!

1. Why is Sam leaving? Where is Sam going?

2. How does Sam feel about leaving — only happy, or something else?

3. At the end, Sam does not say "goodbye". What does Sam say instead? Why is that nicer?

1. Sam has a new job. It is in another city, so Sam is leaving this office.
2. Sam feels happy and sad at the same time — happy about the new job, but sad to leave good friends.
3. Sam says "see you soon" instead of "goodbye". It is nicer because it sounds like the friends will meet again — it is not the end.

The Echo 🕰️

A long time ago, in 1916, a man named Ernest Shackleton had to say goodbye to his men. They were on a cold, empty island. Shackleton got in a small boat to go and find help. Before he left, he made a big promise: "I will come back for you."

Many people say "keep in touch" or "I will visit you". Then they forget. But Shackleton did not forget. After many hard weeks, he came back — and he saved every man. Nobody died.

So when you say goodbye to a friend and say "keep in touch", try to keep your promise. A promise you keep turns a goodbye into a "see you soon".

Two questions to talk about

  1. When you say goodbye, do you say "keep in touch"? Do you really do it, or do you forget?
  2. What is harder, do you think — to be the person who leaves, or the person who stays and waves goodbye?
