A Leaving Party

Your friend from work is leaving. Today is their last day. Time to say goodbye, say thank you, and wish them good luck.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is Friday evening. There is a small party in the office. There is cake, there are drinks in plastic cups, and everyone is talking. It is a happy party, but a little bit sad too.

It is sad because your friend Sam is leaving. Sam worked here for eight years. Now Sam has a new job in another city. Today is Sam's last day, and after today you will not see Sam every day.

So you want to say some nice words. Not a big speech — just a few kind words. You want to say goodbye, say thank you for the good years, and wish Sam good luck. Short and warm is perfect.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Has a friend from your work, school, or class ever left? How did you feel — happy for them, sad, or both?
  2. When someone leaves, do you say goodbye, or is it hard for you? Why?

Who's who

You
The Friend 🥂

You are happy for Sam, but a little sad too. You want to say goodbye in a warm way, and say thank you for all the good years.

The other person
Sam · The Leaver 📦

Kind, and a little shy today. Sam is happy about the new job, but sad to leave good friends. Sam does not like a big fuss.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the warm, simple words: we will miss you, thank you, good luck. Short words say a lot. It is OK to speak slowly.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the warm, kind words.

SamWell, today is my last day. I feel a little sad.
YouMe too, Sam. We will miss you. Eight years — that is a long time!
SamIt is a long time. This is a good place, and you are good people.
YouThank you for everything, Sam. You helped me a lot in my first year.
SamOh, that is kind. I liked working with you very much.
YouCan everyone listen for one minute? I want to say a few words about Sam.
SamOh no, please! You know I don't like a fuss.
YouJust a small one! Sam is leaving today for a new job in another city. We are all a little sad.
SamI am happy and sad at the same time. I will miss you all.
YouWe are happy for you too. Good luck in your new job, Sam!
SamThank you so much. Everyone, please keep in touch!
YouOf course! Now — everyone, please raise your glass. To Sam!
SamHa! Thank you, everyone. This is not goodbye — it is "see you soon"!
YouSee you soon, Sam. Take care, and be happy!

From memory — don't look up!

1. Why is Sam leaving? Where is Sam going?

2. How does Sam feel about leaving — only happy, or something else?

3. At the end, Sam does not say "goodbye". What does Sam say instead? Why is that nicer?

1. Sam has a new job. It is in another city, so Sam is leaving this office.
2. Sam feels happy and sad at the same time — happy about the new job, but sad to leave good friends.
3. Sam says "see you soon" instead of "goodbye". It is nicer because it sounds like the friends will meet again — it is not the end.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use at any goodbye. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it warmly. A goodbye does not need long, hard words. A kind voice, a smile, and simple words like thank you and good luck are perfect. It is OK if you feel a little sad — that is normal.

👋 Saying goodbye

Phrase 1

Your friend is leaving. You want to say that things will feel empty without them. Complete it: "We will ___ you."

"We will miss you."
"To miss someone" means you feel sad because they are not here. It is the simplest, warmest goodbye. You can also say "I will miss you" for one person.
Phrase 2

Your friend is going out the door. Which is a warm, kind goodbye?

A. "Bye."
B. "OK, see you."
C. "Take care of yourself!"
C — "Take care of yourself!"
"Take care" tells the person you care about them. A and B are not wrong, but they are cold. "Take care" is warm and easy to remember.

🙏 Saying thank you

Phrase 3

Your friend helped you many times over the years. You want to say a big thank you for all of it. Complete it: "Thank you for ___."

"Thank you for everything."
"For everything" means "for all the help, all the good times, all of it". It is short but it says a lot. A warm way to thank a friend when they leave.
Phrase 4

You want to say the years together were good. Say it your own way, out loud first. Think of one sentence. Then look.

"It was great to work with you."
Simple and true. You can also say: "I loved working with you" or "You were a great friend here". Any of these is warm and correct.

🍀 Wishing good luck

Phrase 5

Your friend has a new job. You want to wish them well in it. Complete it: "Good ___ in your new job!"

"Good luck in your new job!"
"Good luck" is what we say before something new and big. Use it for anything: "Good luck in your exam!", "Good luck with the move!"
Phrase 6

You want to say you hope they will be happy in the new place. Which one is warm and kind?

A. "I hope you are very happy there."
B. "I hope it is not too bad."
C. "Well, we will see."
A — "I hope you are very happy there."
"I hope you are happy" is a kind, warm wish. B and C sound a little cold or worried. When someone leaves, give them your best wishes.

🤝 Keeping in touch

Phrase 7

You want your friend to write to you and stay your friend after they leave. Put the words in order:

touchkeepin
"Keep in touch!"
"Keep in touch" means "please write to me / call me sometimes". A warm thing to say to a friend who is leaving. You can add: "Keep in touch, OK?"
Phrase 8

It is time for the little toast. You lift your glass and everyone looks at you. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Please raise your glass. To Sam!"
"Raise your glass" means "lift your drink up". Then say the person's name: "To Sam!" Everyone says it back. It is a happy, warm way to finish.

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work for any goodbye — at work, at school, with a friend. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"Good luck with ___!" — Think of a friend. What big thing is coming for them? (a new job, an exam, a move…)

"Thank you for ___." — Think of a person who helped you. What do you want to thank them for?

"I will miss ___." — Who or what will you miss one day?

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole goodbye out loud — say goodbye → say thank you → wish good luck → make the toast. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your goodbye party. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the friend 🥂

Sam speaks — you answer. Be warm: say we will miss you, thank you, and good luck.

SamWell, today is my last day. I feel a little sad.
SamThis is a good place, and you are good people.
SamOh, that is kind. I liked working with you very much.
SamThank you so much. I hope we can stay friends.
SamHa! This is not goodbye — it is "see you soon"!

Round 2 — now YOU are leaving 📦

Change places. Now it is your last day. Say thank you, take the kind words, and leave with a warm goodbye. Think up your new job and how long you were here.

FriendSo, today is your last day. We will really miss you here.
FriendThank you for everything. You helped all of us so much.
FriendGood luck in your new job! Please keep in touch.
FriendEveryone, please raise your glass — to a great friend!
The Twist

🌙 Sam is very shy

One more time. Today Sam is very shy and does not want a party. Sam says: "Please, no speech! I just want to go home quietly." What do you say? Stay kind and warm. Make it small and easy — maybe just a few words and one glass, not a big speech. Help Sam feel happy, not shy. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said goodbye in a warm way ("we will miss you").
  • I said "thank you" for the good years.
  • I wished them good luck.
  • I asked them to keep in touch.
  • I stayed kind and warm the whole time.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

A long time ago, in 1916, a man named Ernest Shackleton had to say goodbye to his men. They were on a cold, empty island. Shackleton got in a small boat to go and find help. Before he left, he made a big promise: "I will come back for you."

Many people say "keep in touch" or "I will visit you". Then they forget. But Shackleton did not forget. After many hard weeks, he came back — and he saved every man. Nobody died.

So when you say goodbye to a friend and say "keep in touch", try to keep your promise. A promise you keep turns a goodbye into a "see you soon".

Two questions to talk about

  1. When you say goodbye, do you say "keep in touch"? Do you really do it, or do you forget?
  2. What is harder, do you think — to be the person who leaves, or the person who stays and waves goodbye?
