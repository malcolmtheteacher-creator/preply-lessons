CAE Part 1 Essay Format — the Rules That Apply to Every Practice

220-260 words — under = penalty; over = time risk
TWO of three bullets — never all three
ONE own point — a fresh idea, not in the bullets
Reasoned verdict — which is most important, and why
Semi-formal register — no contractions, no slang
5 paragraphs — intro / body 1 / body 2 / body 3 / conclusion

Cambridge C1 Band 5 Criteria — What the Examiner Looks For

Content

  • Both chosen bullets discussed in depth
  • Independent own point included
  • Clear, reasoned verdict reached
  • Examples are specific, not vague

Communicative Achievement

  • Semi-formal register maintained
  • Reader understands the argument easily
  • Tone matches a thoughtful student essay
  • No contractions or colloquialisms

Organisation

  • Five clear paragraphs
  • Every body paragraph has a topic sentence
  • Own-point signalled explicitly in Body 3
  • Linkers used purposefully, not decoratively

Language — Range

  • Conditional inversion ("Were X to...")
  • Cleft patterns ("it is X that...")
  • Nominalisation & participle clauses
  • Topic-specific precise vocabulary

Language — Accuracy

  • Errors rare, not patterned
  • Ambitious structures attempted successfully
  • Correct use of linking devices
  • Appropriate article & preposition use

The Six Habits That Push You to Band 5

Paraphrase the prompt. Copying task wording is the fastest way to lose Content marks.
Signal the own point. "A further factor worth adding is..." — the examiner instantly sees the independent thinking.
Name names. Copenhagen, the UK soft-drinks levy, Chile's ad ban — real evidence beats abstract claims.
Show the downside. "However, as budgets are squeezed..." — balance proves analytical depth, not weakness.
Save the verdict. The conclusion is where your position should land cleanly — not in a vague hint.
Word count discipline. 220-260 words. Under is penalised; over wastes time. Aim for 240-255.
