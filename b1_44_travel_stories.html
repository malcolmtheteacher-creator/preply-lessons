Memorable Journeys

Every travel story starts with a moment that changed something. Maybe you discovered a place that felt like home, or got lost in a city and found a hidden gem. Maybe you met someone who became a lifelong friend, or had an adventure that scared you but made you braver.

Let's share:

• Describe your most memorable trip. Where did you go? When?

• What made that journey special? Who did you travel with?

• What was the most unexpected moment?

• Would you go back? What would you do differently?

Essential Travel Vocabulary

Itinerary A detailed plan of your travel route and activities
Accommodation A place where you stay (hotel, hostel, Airbnb, etc.)
Backpacking Budget travel, usually with a backpack; exploring independently
Sightseeing Visiting famous landmarks and tourist attractions
Local Cuisine Traditional food from a specific region or country
Culture Shock Discomfort when experiencing an unfamiliar culture
Off the Beaten Track Places not often visited by tourists; remote locations
Souvenir A small object kept as a memory of a place visited

Past Tense Story Phrases

Narrative phrases for telling travel stories:

• "I had been planning this trip for months when..."

• "We arrived in Paris on a rainy afternoon."

• "The hotel was smaller than I expected, but charming."

• "While we were exploring the old town, we stumbled upon..."

• "I had never experienced anything like it before."

• "By the time we reached the beach, the sun had already set."

• "Looking back, it was one of the best decisions I've made."

A Traveller's Unexpected Adventure

From Elena's Travel Blog

I had planned a straightforward two-week trip through Southeast Asia. I had booked my accommodation, created a detailed itinerary, and researched every temple and beach. But travel never goes exactly as planned, and thank goodness for that.

In Thailand, I missed my bus to Chiang Mai. Instead of panicking, I decided to stay in the small town I was stuck in. That night, I met a local woman who invited me to her family's dinner. I spent the evening learning to cook pad thai with her grandmother, listening to stories about Thailand before tourism exploded. I ate better food and learned more about the country in four hours than I would have in a week of sightseeing.

The next day, I still didn't take that planned bus. Instead, the woman's daughter offered to show me hidden waterfalls in the nearby countryside—"off the beaten track," as they say. We hiked through jungle, swam in crystal-clear pools, and talked for hours. She taught me Thai phrases, and I told her about my home. By the time I left, we had become genuine friends, not just tourist and guide.

I eventually made it to all the famous destinations. They were beautiful. But the memories I treasure most are from the hours I didn't plan for, the connections I didn't anticipate, and the moment I realized that flexibility is the greatest souvenir travel can give you.

Comprehension Questions

1. Why did Elena stay in a small town?

She missed her bus to Chiang Mai.

2. What did she do with the local woman's family?

She cooked pad thai with them and learned about Thai culture.

3. What does Elena say is the greatest souvenir of travel?

Flexibility—the willingness to deviate from your plans.
