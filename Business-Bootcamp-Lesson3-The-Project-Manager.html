⚡ Decision-Making Under Pressure

On The Apprentice, PMs face a constant stream of decisions. The winners don't always make the "right" call — but they make A call, and they commit to it.

The Decision Framework: AID

A — Assess: What's the situation? What are the options? What's at stake?

I — Input: Who has relevant information? Get it fast, but set a time limit.

D — Decide: Make the call. Communicate it clearly. Commit fully.

The 70% Rule

If you have 70% of the information you need, decide. Waiting for 100% means deciding too late. Most business decisions can be corrected — but indecision cannot.

🧪 Scenario 1: The Product Pivot

Situation: Your team is developing a new health drink. Halfway through the day, you discover your main competitor has just launched an almost identical product. Your sub-team has already bought ingredients. The pitch to retailers is in 4 hours.

What do you do?

A Stay the course — your product might be better. Focus on execution.
B Pivot completely — start over with a different product concept.
C Differentiate — keep the product but find a unique angle (organic, energy, kids' version).
D Call a team meeting to discuss and vote on the best approach.

👆 Click to see analysis

Analysis:

A (Stay the course) — Risky but valid if execution is strong. Shows confidence.

B (Complete pivot) — High risk. Wastes bought ingredients, creates time pressure. Rarely works.

C (Differentiate) — Often the smartest choice. Keeps momentum while adding uniqueness.

D (Team meeting) — Dangerous. Time is the enemy. Get input fast, but YOU must decide.

Lord Sugar would say: "Don't panic, don't waste money, but don't ignore the competition either. What's YOUR angle?"

🧪 Scenario 2: The Team Conflict

Situation: Two strong team members are disagreeing loudly about the marketing approach. One wants edgy and bold, the other wants safe and professional. They're both refusing to back down, and the rest of the team is uncomfortable. You're the PM.

What do you do?

A Let them argue it out — the best idea will win.
B Pick a side — make the decision yourself and end the debate.
C Compromise — find a middle ground that combines both approaches.
D Separate them — give each a different task and make the marketing call yourself.

👆 Click to see analysis

Analysis:

A (Let them argue) — Abdicates your role. PMs must manage conflict, not ignore it.

B (Pick a side) — Strong leadership, but may create resentment. Explain your reasoning.

C (Compromise) — Often produces weak, muddled results. "A camel is a horse designed by committee."

D (Separate & decide) — Often effective. Uses both people, avoids wasted energy, you retain control.

Lord Sugar would say: "Don't let two people derail the whole team. Show them who's boss."

💬 The Language of Leadership

How you say things matters as much as what you decide. Here's how PMs communicate with impact.

Setting Direction

✅ Strong:

"Right, here's the plan. Sarah, you're on research. Tom, production. I need research done by 2pm so we can start building."

❌ Weak:

"So, um, maybe Sarah could do research? And Tom, if you don't mind, could you possibly look at production? If that's okay with everyone?"

Delegating Tasks

✅ Strong:

"I need you to negotiate the venue hire. Aim for under £200. Call me if you hit any problems. Check back by 3pm."

❌ Weak:

"Can you sort out the venue stuff? Just... do your best, I suppose."

Giving Feedback

✅ Strong:

"That's good work on the design. One change: make the text bigger — it needs to be readable from 3 metres away."

❌ Weak:

"Yeah, it's... fine, I guess. Maybe change some stuff? I don't know."

Handling Pushback

✅ Strong:

"I hear you, and I understand your concern. But we need to move forward. Here's why we're going this direction..."

❌ Weak:

"Well, if you really think so... maybe you're right. Should we change it?"

Key Phrases for PMs

  • "Here's the plan..." (setting direction)
  • "I need you to..." (clear delegation)
  • "Report back to me by..." (accountability)
  • "I've decided that..." (owning decisions)
  • "I take responsibility for..." (accountability)
  • "Let's focus on solutions..." (forward-looking)

🏆 The Boardroom Defence

Every PM faces the boardroom. Whether you won or lost, you need to account for your leadership. Can you defend your decisions?

📺 Your Scenario

The task is over. Your team came second — you lost by a small margin. Lord Sugar wants answers. Your teacher will grill you as Lord Sugar, and you must defend your PM performance.

Prepare Your Defence

Before facing the boardroom, think through these questions:

  • What was your strategy and why?
  • What decisions did you make? Were they right?
  • How did you delegate? Did you use people well?
  • What went wrong? Who was responsible?
  • What would you do differently?
  • Why should YOU stay in the process?

Key Boardroom Phrases

  • "I take full responsibility for..." (own your mistakes)
  • "The decision I made was... because..." (justify logically)
  • "In hindsight, I would have..." (show learning)
  • "What I did well was..." (don't be too humble)
  • "I deserve to stay because..." (sell yourself)
