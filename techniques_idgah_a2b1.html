Idgah

Premchand's story of a festival day, a poor little boy with three coins, and a very unusual present.

Today: you'll read about a five-year-old boy who buys a strange present at a fair and wins an argument with his friends — and you'll practise also, too and as well, the words you need to add more reasons.
Warm up — before we read. Talk about these questions out loud. There are no wrong answers.
Discuss:
  • What is your favourite festival? What do children do on that day?
  • When you were a child, what did you buy with your pocket money?
  • What is the best present you ever gave to someone? Why was it good?

Now — the words for today's story

Ten words open the story. Do the four short activities. Say your answer out loud first, then click Reveal.

A · What does the word mean here?

1. Hamid's mother and father died. He lives with his grandmother. He is an orphan.
an orphan (n) — a child whose parents are dead.
2. There were rides, toys, sweets and music. Thousands of people walked between the stalls. It was a big fair.
a fair (n) — an event outside with rides, games and things to buy. Careful: the adjective fair means "right and equal".
3. The other boys ate sweets in front of Hamid and laughed. They said, "Poor Hamid has no sweets!" They teased him.
to tease (v) — to laugh at someone or say things to make them feel a little bad, sometimes as a joke.

B · Which word means…?

Three words: tongs · clay · coin. Say the right word, then reveal.

1. Which word means a small round piece of metal money?
a coin (n) — "He had three coins in his pocket." Paper money = notes (UK) or bills (US).
2. Which word means a kitchen tool with two long metal arms for picking up hot things?
tongs (n, plural) — like scissors, we say a pair of tongs. "Use the tongs — it's hot!"
3. Which word means soft brown earth that people use to make pots and toys?
clay (n) — "a clay pot", "clay toys". Clay toys are pretty but they break easily.

C · Choose the meaning — a / b / c

1. The toys were beautiful, but they were fragile. Fragile means…
  (a) expensive   (b) easy to break   (c) very old
(b) — fragile (adj) = easily broken. You often see it on boxes: FRAGILE. Opposite: strong.
2. Hamid and the shopkeeper argued about the price. To argue about the price is to…
  (a) talk until you agree on how much to pay   (b) steal something   (c) pay too much
(a) — another word for this is to haggle. In many markets around the world, people haggle every day.

D · Word families & word partners

1. Complete: Hamid didn't ___ money on sweets. He ___ his three coins on a present. (Same verb in both gaps.)
spend — spend, spent, spent. We spend money on something: "He spent his money on tongs."
2. Complete with burn or burnt: "Be careful — you'll ___ your fingers!" · "Ouch! I've ___ my hand."
you'll burn your fingers · I've burnt (or burned) my hand. Remember this word — it's the key to the story!

Now use them — out loud

Talk before we read:
  • Use orphan, coin, fair and tongs: what do you think happens in this story?
  • Which words are about money? Which are about things you can buy? Say them in two groups.

The story, in three parts

This is a famous story from India, retold here in simple English. Read each part. Then answer the questions from memory, and talk about one question.

Before you read: Eid is a big Muslim festival at the end of Ramadan, the month of fasting. An Idgah is a big open place where people pray on Eid. A paisa was a very small Indian coin.

Part 1 — Three coins

It was the morning of Eid, and the whole village was happy. The children were the happiest of all. They counted their money again and again. Mahmood had twelve coins. Mohsin had fifteen.

Hamid had three coins. He was only five years old. His father and mother were dead, and he lived with his old grandmother, Amina. But Hamid did not know the truth. His grandmother told him, "Your father is working far away, and your mother is visiting God. They will come back with lots of presents." So Hamid was happy.

Amina was very poor. She had almost no food in the house, and Hamid had no shoes. She cried a little. But Hamid smiled and said, "Don't worry, Granny! I'll come back soon!" Then he ran down the road with his friends to the Idgah, three miles away.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where are Hamid's parents really? What does he think?

2. Why is his grandmother sad?

1. They are dead. Hamid thinks his father is working and his mother is visiting God.

2. She is very poor: there is almost no food, and Hamid has no shoes for the long walk.

Discuss:
  • Is it OK that the grandmother doesn't tell Hamid the truth? Why or why not?

Part 2 — The fair

The walk was long and hot, and Hamid had no shoes. But the children saw big houses, a police station and a shop full of sweets, and they talked and laughed all the way. At the Idgah, thousands of people prayed together in long lines.

After the prayers, the children ran to the fair. There were rides, and the boys paid one coin each and went round and round. Hamid only watched. Then the boys bought clay toys: a soldier, a water-carrier and a lawyer. Hamid wanted a toy too, but he said, "They're only clay. If they fall, they will break." Then the boys bought sweets and ate them in front of Hamid. They teased him and laughed.

Then Hamid saw a shop with metal things, and he stopped. There was a pair of iron tongs. He thought, "Every day Granny makes bread on the hot pan, and she burns her fingers. With tongs, her fingers will be safe!" The tongs cost six coins. Hamid had only three. "Three?" he asked. The shopkeeper said no, and Hamid walked away. Then the shopkeeper called him back. "OK. Three." Hamid put the tongs on his shoulder like a soldier with a gun and walked back proudly.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What do the other boys buy at the fair?

2. Why does Hamid buy the tongs?

1. Rides, clay toys (a soldier, a water-carrier, a lawyer) and sweets.

2. Because his grandmother burns her fingers every day when she makes bread.

Discuss:
  • Would you buy a toy, sweets or the tongs? Why?

Part 3 — The winner

"Tongs? That's a stupid present!" laughed Mohsin. But Hamid said, "My tongs are a gun on my shoulder. They are also a musical instrument. And they are strong too. Your toys are clay. If they get wet, they will break. My tongs can go in the fire as well!" The boys stopped laughing. Soon they all wanted to hold the tongs. And by the evening, all the clay toys were broken.

When Hamid got home, his grandmother was angry. "Tongs? You didn't eat anything all day, and you bought tongs?" Hamid said quietly, "You burn your fingers on the hot pan. So I bought them for you." Amina's anger went away. She held Hamid and cried, and she thanked God for him again and again. The little boy was like a wise old man, and the old woman cried like a little girl.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Give two reasons Hamid says his tongs are better than the toys.

2. How does Amina feel first — and then?

1. Any two: they are a gun, a musical instrument, strong, and they can go in the fire; the clay toys break.

2. First angry, then very happy and full of love — she cries.

Discuss:
  • Why does Amina cry at the end? Is it a happy ending or a sad ending?

Grammar — adding ideas: also, too, as well

Hamid wins the argument because he gives his friends more and more reasons. To add an idea, we use also, too and as well. They mean the same thing, but they go in different places in the sentence.

Where do they go?
• also — before the main verb, but after be: "My tongs also make music." "They are also a gun."
• too — at the end: "They are strong too."
• as well — at the end: "They can go in the fire as well."
Careful with negatives: don't use too or also with not — use either:
• Hamid didn't buy a toy. He didn't buy sweets either. (NOT He didn't buy sweets too.)
• Short answers: "I'm hungry." — "Me too!"  ·  "I'm not hungry." — "Me neither."

1. also, too or as well?

Write one word or two words. Look at the place in the sentence!

1. Mahmood bought a toy. He bought sweets.

2. The tongs are useful. They are strong .

3. Hamid had no shoes. He had no money for sweets .

1. also   2. too (or as well)   3. as well (or too)

2. too or either?

Write too or either.

1. Hamid didn't ride on the wheel. He didn't eat sweets .

2. Mohsin laughed at Hamid. Mahmood laughed .

3. The soldier broke. The lawyer broke .

1. either (negative)   2. too   3. too

3. Hamid's argument — complete the paragraph

Write also, too, as well or either in each gap. Then read it aloud like Hamid!

"My tongs are a gun. They are a musical instrument. They are very strong . They can go in the fire . Your soldier can't fight. He can't hold a gun . Your toys are pretty, but they are fragile. And my grandmother will be happy !"

also · too / as well · as well / too · either · also · too / as well

Six reasons, one after another — that's how Hamid wins!

Going deeper

Premchand (1880–1936) is one of the most famous writers in India. He wrote in Hindi and Urdu, and he wrote about poor people in villages. He wrote "Idgah" in 1933. Today, children in India and Pakistan still read it at school.

The heart of it. Look at what we know and what Hamid knows. We know his parents are dead; he doesn't. So his happiness is beautiful and sad at the same time. Look at the fair: colours, rides, sweets, toys — and then the heavy grey tongs. And look at the ending. We know why Hamid bought the tongs, but his grandmother doesn't. We watch her angry face change to tears. At the end, the child acts like an old man, and the old woman becomes like a child.
Talk it through:
  • Why does the writer tell us about Hamid's parents at the start?
  • The other boys are not very kind. Are they bad children, or normal children?
  • All the clay toys break. Why do you think the writer does this?
  • Hamid is only five. Should a five-year-old think about his grandmother's fingers, or about toys?
