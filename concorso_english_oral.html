What You're Facing

During your 45-minute oral exam, the commission will spend 5-10 minutes testing your English. This is NOT a grammar test - they want to see if you can communicate naturally at B2 level.

The ABC Framework

A simple structure for ANY question they ask you

A

Answer Directly

Start with a clear, direct response to the question. Don't avoid it or give a vague introduction. Give them your answer in the first sentence.

B

Build with Examples

Add specific details, examples, or a brief story. This shows depth and gives you more speaking time. Use real experiences when possible.

C

Connect to Teaching

Link your answer back to education, teaching, or your professional experience. This shows you're thinking like a teacher.

Why ABC Works

  • A prevents you from rambling or going off-topic
  • B gives you content to speak about (no awkward silences)
  • C reminds the commission you're a professional teacher
Processing time tip: While they ask the question, mentally think "A-B-C" and plan which example you'll use for B.

The 5 Question Types

Based on reports from PNRR1 and PNRR2 candidates, these are the most common question types. Prepare for all five.

1. Personal Introduction

▼

What they ask:

  • "Tell me about yourself"
  • "Can you introduce yourself?"
  • "What's your background?"

What they want: A confident, organized introduction - not your life story. 60-90 seconds maximum.

ABC approach:
A = State your name and current role
B = Give 2-3 key facts about your background
C = Connect to why you want to teach

2. Teaching Experience

▼

What they ask:

  • "Tell me about your teaching experience"
  • "Where have you worked as a teacher?"
  • "Describe a memorable teaching moment"

What they want: Specific examples showing you understand teaching, not just a list of jobs.

ABC approach:
A = State your experience level clearly
B = Give ONE specific example or story
C = What you learned from that experience

3. Didactic/Methodology Questions

▼

What they ask:

  • "How would you support a student with learning difficulties?"
  • "What teaching methods do you prefer?"
  • "How do you use technology in teaching?"

What they want: Evidence that you think pedagogically, not memorized definitions.

ABC approach:
A = State your approach/answer directly
B = Give a concrete example of how you'd do it
C = Explain WHY this approach works

4. Image Description

▼

What they ask:

  • "Describe what you see in this image"
  • "What's happening in this picture?"
  • "What does this image make you think about?"

What they want: Organized description + personal response. Don't just list objects.

ABC approach:
A = Describe the main subject immediately
B = Add details (where, who, what's happening)
C = Connect to a teaching context or your opinion

5. Opinion Questions

▼

What they ask:

  • "What do you think about inclusive education?"
  • "What makes a good teacher?"
  • "How has education changed in recent years?"

What they want: A clear opinion with reasoning - not a neutral non-answer.

ABC approach:
A = State your opinion clearly
B = Give evidence or an example
C = Connect to your teaching philosophy

Time-Buying Phrases

If you need a moment to think, use these phrases naturally:

To start thinking
"That's an interesting question..."
To organize thoughts
"Let me think about that for a moment..."
To clarify
"If I understand correctly, you're asking about..."
To rephrase
"In other words, you'd like to know..."
Don't overuse these! One time-buying phrase per question maximum. Then start your answer.

Strong vs Weak Answers

See the ABC framework in action. Notice how strong answers are specific and connected.

Question: "Tell me about yourself"

WEAK ANSWER

"My name is Maria. I am Italian. I studied at university. I like teaching. I want to be a teacher because I like children. Teaching is important for society."

Problems: Too vague, no specific details, sounds memorized, doesn't show personality

STRONG ANSWER (ABC)

[A] My name is Maria Rossi, and I'm currently working as a support teacher at a secondary school in Milan. [B] I have a degree in Educational Sciences from the University of Bologna, and I've been working with students with special needs for the past three years. What I find most rewarding is seeing students gain confidence - I had a student last year who was terrified of participating in class, and by June he was volunteering to present his projects. [C] This experience is exactly why I'm pursuing the sostegno qualification - I believe every student deserves a teacher who understands their individual needs.

Question: "How do you support students with learning difficulties?"

WEAK ANSWER

"I use different methods. I try to be patient. I help them. It's important to include all students. Every student is different."

Problems: Generic statements anyone could say, no concrete examples, doesn't demonstrate real knowledge

STRONG ANSWER (ABC)

[A] I believe in creating individualized learning plans that address each student's specific challenges while building on their strengths. [B] For example, I worked with a student with dyslexia who struggled with written tests. I collaborated with his teachers to allow oral examinations for some subjects, and I used visual aids and mind maps to help him organize information. We also practiced test-taking strategies together, like breaking questions into smaller parts. [C] This approach taught me that accommodation isn't about making things easier - it's about removing barriers so students can show what they actually know.

Question: "Describe this image" (classroom scene)

WEAK ANSWER

"I see a classroom. There are students. There is a teacher. The students are sitting at desks. There are windows. The walls are white."

Problems: Just listing objects, no interpretation, no engagement with the image

STRONG ANSWER (ABC)

[A] This image shows a modern inclusive classroom where students appear to be working in small groups. [B] I can see approximately twenty students, and what strikes me is the diversity of the group - there seem to be students of different abilities working together at the same tables. The teacher is not at the front lecturing but moving between groups, which suggests a student-centered approach. I also notice that some students have laptops while others are using paper, indicating flexibility in how they can work. [C] This kind of learning environment is exactly what I aim to create - where collaboration and individual differences are both valued.

Key Differences

Weak Answers Strong Answers
Vague and generic Specific examples
Short, choppy sentences Connected, flowing ideas
Sounds memorized Sounds natural and personal
No teaching connection Always links back to education

Final Reminders

  • Don't panic if you make grammar mistakes - they expect B2, not perfection
  • Speak clearly and at a natural pace - don't rush
  • Use the ABC structure - it keeps you organized
  • Give specific examples - they're more memorable than general statements
  • Make eye contact with the commission
  • It's okay to pause briefly to think - use a time-buying phrase
