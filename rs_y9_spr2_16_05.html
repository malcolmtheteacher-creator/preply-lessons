Contraception and Family Planning

Catholic Teaching on Contraception

Humanae Vitae (1968): artificial contraception is sinful.

Sex serves two purposes: procreative and unitive. Contraception separates these.

Natural family planning approved (works with nature).

Reality: 80%+ Catholic women use contraception; younger Catholics disagree.

Protestant Perspectives on Contraception

Most approve. Distinguish responsible family planning from recklessness.

Contraception is tool for exercising responsibility God gave us.

Islamic Perspectives on Contraception

Views vary: Conservative forbid it; Progressive permit it; Most Islamic scholars today approve as responsible parenthood.

"There is no monasticism in Islam" - engage fully with family life, including wise planning.

Jewish Perspectives on Contraception

Procreation is mitzvah (commandment). But if pregnancy endangers mother's health, contraception obligatory.

Modern Judaism generally accepts for family planning and women's wellbeing.

Health, Autonomy & Poverty

Why family planning matters:

  • Women's health: Pregnancy/childbirth demanding.
  • Autonomy: Choice over if/when to have children.
  • Poverty: Large families struggle; planning breaks cycles.
  • Education: Fewer children = more resources per child.

Religious Values at Stake

What religions value:

  • Family: Large families blessed.
  • Nature: Accept natural fertility.
  • Responsibility: Care for children you have.
  • Women: Health, choice, dignity matter.

Deeper Investigation: Balancing Values

Traditional Religious View: Accept God's Will

"Children are blessing. Contraception rejects blessing, prioritises desires. Trust God's providence."

Progressive Religious View: Stewardship & Love

"God gave us reason and responsibility. Caring well means deciding how many we can nurture. Women's health matters."

Secular View: Personal Choice & Public Health

"Family planning is health right. Contraception access empowers people, especially women. Countries with access have better outcomes."
