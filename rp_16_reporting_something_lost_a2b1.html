Reporting Something Lost

You lost your bag on the train. Somebody kind might have it. Time to say what happened, describe your bag, and keep hoping.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

You are at the train station. You get off the train — and then you stop. Your bag is not on your shoulder. You had it on the train, and now it is gone. Oh no.

It is a small green bag. Inside there are normal things: a book, a jumper. But there is one important thing too — a small camera with photos on it. You cannot get those photos again. You really want that bag back.

At the end of the station, there is a small office. It is the "lost property" office — the place where lost things go. A worker sits there. They are kind and they help people every day. Now you go to the window to ask for help.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Have you ever lost something? What was it? Did you get it back?
  2. You find a bag on a train. There is a phone and some money inside. What do you do?

Who's who

You
The Owner 🎒

You lost your green bag. You feel worried, but you are calm and polite. You must say what happened, describe the bag, and ask for help.

The other person
The Lost Property Worker 🔦

Calm and kind. They help lost people every day. They cannot promise your bag is here — but they will look, and they want a happy end.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the polite words: excuse me, please, thank you. And it's OK to speak slowly, or to say "Sorry, can you say that again?"

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the polite words.

WorkerHello. You look worried. Can I help you?
YouYes, please. I think I left my bag on the train. It was the 4:15 train from Edinburgh.
WorkerOK, don't worry. Many bags come back. Can you describe your bag for me?
YouYes. It is a small green bag. It is quite old. There is a picture of a mountain on the front, so it is easy to see.
WorkerA mountain picture — good, that helps. And how big is the bag?
YouIt is about the size of a big box. Not very big.
WorkerAnd what is inside the bag? This helps me find it.
YouA book, a jumper, and a small camera. The camera is the important thing. It has my photos. It is not expensive, but it is special to me.
WorkerOK. I will write "camera" here. Wait one moment, please. I will look in the back.
WorkerI am sorry. I have two bags here, but they are not green. Your bag is not here yet.
YouOh. Do more bags come later? From that train?
WorkerYes, they do. Sometimes a bag comes one or two days later. So please don't give up.
YouThank you. Can you call me if you find it? Here is my name and number.
WorkerOf course. I will call you when a green bag with a mountain comes in.
YouYou are very kind. I will come back tomorrow too. Thank you so much for looking.

From memory — don't look up!

1. The worker asks about the bag. What two things do they want to know? (Think: the outside, and the inside.)

2. Which one thing in the bag is the most important, and why?

3. The bag is not there today. So why does the owner not give up?

1. They want to know how the bag looks (colour, size, the mountain picture) and what is inside it.
2. The camera. It is not expensive, but it has the owner's photos, and you cannot get those photos again.
3. The worker says bags sometimes come one or two days later. So there is still hope, and the owner will come back tomorrow.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases for when you lose something. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Stay calm and polite. You feel worried, and that is normal. But a calm, friendly voice helps a lot. Words like excuse me, please and thank you make the worker want to help you.

🎒 Telling them

Phrase 1

You are not sure, but you think you left your bag on the train. Say it in a soft way. Complete it: "I think I ___ my bag on the train."

"I think I left my bag on the train."
"I think I…" is soft — you are not sure, and you do not blame anyone. "I left" means you forgot it. This is calm and kind, and the worker will help you.
Phrase 2

You want to ask if anybody brought your bag in. Which one is polite?

A. "Where is my bag? Give it to me."
B. "Excuse me, did someone bring in a green bag?"
C. "You have my bag. I want it now."
B — "Excuse me, did someone bring in a green bag?"
"Excuse me" and a question are polite and friendly. A and C sound angry, like an order. The worker did not lose your bag, so be kind — they want to help you.

🔍 Describing it

Phrase 3

You want to say the colour and one special thing on the bag. Complete it: "It is a green bag, and there is a ___ of a mountain on it."

"It is a green bag, and there is a picture of a mountain on it."
Say the colour first, then one special thing (a picture, a name, a mark). One clear special thing helps the worker find your bag fast.
Phrase 4

The worker asks how big your bag is. You have no ruler, so you use a normal thing to help them see it. Put the words in order:

the size ofit is abouta big box
"It is about the size of a big box."
"It is about the size of…" + a normal thing is an easy way to say how big something is. For example: "the size of a book", "the size of a shoe".

❓ Answering questions

Phrase 5

The worker asks what is inside. The camera is the most important thing. You want to say why it matters — not that it is expensive. Say it out loud, then check.

"The camera is important to me. It has my photos."
Say why it is special, not the price. "It is special to me" or "It has my photos" makes it a real, human thing — and the worker wants to help a person, not a price.
Phrase 6

The worker asks: "Where did you last have the bag?" You are not 100% sure. Which answer is honest AND helps?

A. "I don't know. Anywhere."
B. "I'm not sure, but I think I had it on the train."
C. "I had it. One hundred percent. On the train."
B — "I'm not sure, but I think I had it on the train."
"I'm not sure, but I think…" is honest — you are not sure — and it still gives a good clue (the train). A gives no help; C is too strong when you are not really sure.

🤞 Keeping hope

Phrase 7

The worker goes to the back to look. You want to say "no problem, I can wait". Complete it: "Take your ___, no problem."

"Take your time, no problem."
"Take your time" means "you can be slow, I don't mind". It is kind and easy. It shows you are patient — and people help patient people more.
Phrase 8

The bag is not here yet. You want the worker to call you if it comes. You also want to thank them. Say it out loud, then check.

"Can you call me if you find it? Thank you so much for looking."
Ask with "Can you…?", then thank them for looking — not only for finding it. Add "so much" to make your thank you warmer. A smile helps too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work for any lost thing — a bag, a phone, keys, a coat. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"I think I left my ___ on the ___." — What could you lose, and where?

"It is a ___ , and there is a ___ on it." — Describe one of your things: colour + one special thing.

"The ___ is important to me, because ___." — What thing is special to you, and why?

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — say what happened → describe the bag → say what is inside → keep hope and say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your lost bag. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the owner 🎒

The worker speaks — you answer. Be calm and polite: use excuse me, please, and thank you.

WorkerHello. You look worried. Can I help you?
WorkerDon't worry, many bags come back. Can you describe your bag?
WorkerAnd what is inside the bag? This helps me find it.
WorkerWait one moment… I am sorry, your bag is not here yet.
WorkerYes, sometimes a bag comes one or two days later.
WorkerOf course. I will call you if it comes in.

Round 2 — now YOU are the worker 🔦

Change places. You work at the lost property office. Be calm and kind. Say "Don't worry", "Can you describe it?", "One moment, please".

OwnerHello. I think I left my suitcase on the platform, about an hour ago.
OwnerIt is a red suitcase. There is a little parrot tag on it. You can't miss it.
OwnerSome clothes, and a small wooden box from my grandmother. That is the important one.
OwnerOf course, take your time. Any luck?
OwnerThank you for looking. What do I do now?
The Twist

🌙 The last train

One more time — no help this round. It is late, and the office closes in ten minutes. The worker says: "Good news — a green bag came in this morning! But it is at the big office across town, and that is closed now until Monday." But you fly away tomorrow morning! What do you say? Stay calm and friendly. Can they call the big office now? Can a colleague keep the bag for you? Can they at least put your camera in a safe place? Try every kind idea — but stay polite. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said what happened in a calm, soft way.
  • I described my bag: the colour, the size, and one special thing.
  • I said which thing was important, and why.
  • I did not give up — I asked if bags come later.
  • I said "thank you" warmly for their help.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

In 1872, people found a ship in the sea. Its name was the Mary Celeste. The sails were up, the food was on the table — but there were no people on board. All the crew were gone. Nobody ever found them.

Today, you lose a green bag, and you go to a small window to ask for help. Some lost things come back one or two days later. Some things, like the people from that ship, never come back.

A lost bag is a small thing. A good description, a little patience, and a kind person — and often it comes home again.

Two questions to talk about

  1. You lose something you love. Is it better to keep looking and hoping, or to say goodbye and move on?
  2. When we lose something and never know what happened, we often make a story to explain it. Is that a good thing or a bad thing?
