Is the middle way always best, or are some things worth doing to the extreme?

One person works fourteen hours a day and almost never sees their family. Another does as little as possible and is always bored. Most of us feel the answer is somewhere in between. But where exactly is that middle, and is it the same for everyone? More than 2,300 years ago, Aristotle tried to answer that question.

Today: You'll meet Aristotle and his idea of the golden mean, so you can talk about balance in everyday life and explain why a good quality sits between two extremes.

Meet Aristotle

Read one part at a time. After each part, close your eyes and answer the question before you check.

I

The doctor's son

Aristotle was born in 384 BC in Stagira, in northern Greece. His father was a doctor. At seventeen, he went to Athens to study at Plato's Academy, and he stayed for twenty years. He admired Plato but often disagreed with him. Plato looked for truth in perfect ideas beyond this world; Aristotle wanted to study the real world around him: animals, plants, cities and people.

How was Aristotle's approach different from Plato's?

Plato looked for truth in perfect ideas beyond this world; Aristotle wanted to study the real world around him.
II

Tutor to a future king

In 343 BC, Aristotle began to teach a young prince who would become Alexander the Great. Later he returned to Athens and opened his own school, the Lyceum. He liked to teach while walking, so his followers were called the Peripatetics, which means 'the walkers'. He wrote about almost everything: logic, politics, poetry, animals and the stars. For centuries, many writers simply called him 'the Philosopher'.

Why were Aristotle's followers called 'the walkers'?

Because Aristotle liked to teach while walking around.
III

Happiness and the middle way

In his Nicomachean Ethics, Aristotle asks how to live a good, happy life. His answer is to build good character by finding the middle way. Courage, for example, sits between two extremes: too little is cowardice, and too much is recklessness. The right amount is not the same for everyone, and it depends on the situation. We learn it by practice, just as we become good musicians by playing music.

Which two extremes does courage sit between?

Cowardice, which is too little, and recklessness, which is too much.
Talk: Aristotle says we become good by practising, like learning an instrument. Do you agree that kindness or courage can be practised? How?

Key words

Each word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means. Then reveal and compare.

1. He enjoys chocolate in moderation: a small piece after dinner, never the whole bar.
moderation (n) — the quality of not doing too much or too little of something. Example: "Coffee is fine in moderation, but ten cups a day is too many."
2. Some diets go to extremes: no bread, no fruit, nothing but meat.
extreme (n) — the furthest possible point of something, very far from the middle. Example: "The weather here goes from one extreme to the other: boiling in summer, freezing in winter."
3. The party was a night of excess: too much food, too much wine and far too much noise.
excess (n) — an amount that is more than is needed, allowed or healthy. Example: "An excess of sugar can damage your teeth."
4. Honesty, kindness and courage are all virtues that most cultures admire.
virtue (n) — a good quality in someone's character, such as honesty or courage. Example: "Patience is a virtue, especially when your train is late."
5. Biting your nails is a bad habit that's hard to stop.
habit (n) — something you do so often that you do it without thinking. Example: "I'm trying to get into the habit of walking every morning."
6. It was reckless of him to climb the mountain alone without telling anyone where he was going.
reckless (adj) — doing dangerous things without thinking about what might happen. Example: "Driving fast in heavy rain is reckless."
7. Children flourish when they feel safe and loved.
flourish (v) — to grow or develop well and be healthy, happy or successful. Example: "The garden flourished after a month of sun and rain."

The idea: The golden mean

1
Too littleOne extreme: not enough, like cowardice.
2
Too muchThe other extreme: an excess, like recklessness.
3
The meanThe virtue lies between them: courage.
4
PractiseWe find the right amount by habit and good judgement, case by case.

See it in action

Here is the golden mean in action, with generosity. Your friend's birthday is coming. If you give nothing and forget the day, that is too little: meanness. If you spend a month's wages on a gift you can't afford, that is too much: wastefulness. The mean is a thoughtful gift that suits your friend and your budget. Notice that the mean is not a fixed number: a generous gift from a student looks very different from a generous gift from a millionaire.

Your turn

Now you: explain the golden mean to a friend who has never heard of it. Speak for about a minute. Use the starters if they help.

Aristotle believed that a good quality is...For example, courage sits between...The middle is not the same for everyone, because...We find it by...
Aristotle believed that a good quality is a middle point between two extremes. For example, courage sits between cowardice, which is too little, and recklessness, which is too much. The middle is not the same for everyone, because it depends on the person and the situation. We find it by practice and good judgement, just as we become good musicians by playing music. That's why he thought good character is built through habits.

Connect

Compare

Plato and Aristotle: Plato looked for truth in perfect ideas beyond this world, the Forms. His student Aristotle studied the real world of animals, plants and people. Which approach do you find more useful for learning how to live, and why?
Plato's approach gives us perfect ideals, like perfect justice, to aim for. Aristotle's approach starts from real life: what actually helps people flourish. Many people find Aristotle more practical, because the golden mean can guide everyday decisions.

From Plato

What does Plato's story of the cave show?

That we may be like prisoners who take shadows for reality: our senses show us appearances, and only careful thinking leads us to the truth.
From Democritus

What did Democritus say everything is made of?

Atoms: tiny, uncuttable pieces, far too small to see, moving in empty space, the void.

Final review

From memory, out loud. Then check.

1. What is Aristotle's golden mean?

The idea that a virtue, a good quality, is the middle point between two extremes, too much and too little. For example, courage lies between cowardice and recklessness.

2. Which famous young prince did Aristotle teach?

Alexander the Great.

3. How do we become good, according to Aristotle?

By practice and habit, just as we become good musicians by playing music.

4. What does 'moderation' mean? Give an example.

Not doing too much or too little of something. For example: drinking coffee in moderation.
