A Job Interview

B2 Level · Speaking & Interview Skills

Time for this lesson:

5:00

Getting Started

Think about this:

How was your last job interview? What did they ask you about? Did you feel nervous or confident?

Typical interview questions cover:
• Your previous work experience and what you learned
• Why you're applying for this job
• Your career goals and where you want to be in 5 years
• Your personality and how you work with others
• Details from your CV or resume
• Your strengths and areas you want to improve
• Your language abilities and skills
• Achievements you're proud of

Good interviews are conversations, not just questions and answers. Employers want to understand both your skills and who you are as a person.

Strengths & Weaknesses

How to Talk About Your Strengths

Key Phrases for Strengths:
  • "I consider myself to be very organised."
  • "I pride myself on my attention to detail."
  • "One of my strongest qualities is my reliability."
  • "I'm particularly good at problem-solving."
  • "My colleagues often praise my teamwork."
  • "I have a real talent for communication."
  • "I'm confident in my ability to meet deadlines."

How to Talk About Your Weaknesses

Key Phrases for Weaknesses (with improvement):
  • "I don't have much experience with event planning, but I'm eager to learn."
  • "I'm not particularly good at IT, but I'm taking courses to improve."
  • "I sometimes find delegation difficult, but I'm working on being more confident."
  • "Public speaking isn't my strongest area, though I've been practising."
  • "I can be too perfectionistic, but I'm learning to prioritise efficiency."
  • "I struggle with large group presentations, but I'm developing this skill."

Practice Patterns

Pattern 1: Strength with Example

"I consider myself to be [strength]. For example, at my last job, I [specific example that shows this strength]."

Pattern 2: Weakness with Action

"I don't have much experience with [weakness], but [what you're doing to improve or your approach]."

Practice: Fill in Your Own

Try these patterns with your own strengths and weaknesses:

1. "I consider myself to be very _______. For example, _______."
2. "I pride myself on my _______. At my last job, _______."
3. "I don't have much experience with _______, but _______."

The Interview

Your Task

Role-Play a Job Interview
Choose a job you're interested in. Prepare answers to possible interview questions. Then practise the interview with your teacher or conversation partner—they will play the interviewer and you will be the candidate.

Before You Start: Interview Checklist

During the Interview

Remember:

  • Listen carefully to each question
  • Give specific examples from your experience
  • Use the phrases you learned for strengths and weaknesses
  • Ask for clarification if you don't understand: "Could you tell me exactly what you mean?"
  • Or ask for more detail: "What in particular do you find important?"
  • Stay positive and confident

Follow-Up Phrases

When you need the interviewer to clarify or give more detail:
  • "Could you tell me exactly what you mean by that?"
  • "What in particular are you looking for in the ideal candidate?"
  • "Could you give me an example of what you mean?"
  • "What would success look like in this position?"
  • "Can you tell me more about the team I'd be working with?"

Your Thoughts

1. Which is harder to talk about—your strengths or your weaknesses?

Do you find it easier to describe what you're good at, or to admit areas where you need to improve? Why?

2. Does enthusiasm compensate for lack of experience?

If you lack specific experience but show genuine interest, can that make a positive impression? What other qualities matter in an interview?

3. Tell us about your first interview experience.

What made you nervous? What went well? What would you do differently now?

4. What's the hardest question an interviewer can ask?

Is it about weaknesses, salary expectations, difficult decisions you've made, or something else? How would you prepare for that question?

5. What advice would you give someone preparing for an important interview?

What helped you succeed? What should they definitely avoid doing?
