Encounter: One City, Three Faiths

Jerusalem is one of the most contested and sacred cities on Earth. It is holy to three major religions — Judaism, Christianity, and Islam — all of which trace their roots back to the prophet Abraham. This is why they are called the Abrahamic faiths.

Judaism

Western Wall (Kotel) — the last remaining wall of the Second Temple, destroyed by the Romans in 70 CE. Jews pray here and place written prayers in the cracks between the stones.

Temple Mount — the holiest site in Judaism. Jews believe this is where God's presence dwelt in the ancient Temple built by King Solomon.

Christianity

Church of the Holy Sepulchre — built on the site where Christians believe Jesus was crucified, buried, and rose from the dead. The holiest church in Christianity.

Via Dolorosa — the route Jesus walked while carrying the cross to his crucifixion. Pilgrims walk this path today.

Garden of Gethsemane — where Jesus prayed before his arrest.

Islam

Al-Aqsa Mosque — the third holiest mosque in Islam, after Mecca and Medina. Muslims believe Muhammad prayed here.

Dome of the Rock — marks the spot where Muslims believe Muhammad ascended to heaven during the Isra and Mi'raj (Night Journey).

Haram al-Sharif (Noble Sanctuary) — the compound containing both Al-Aqsa and the Dome of the Rock.

The tension: All three faiths claim the same small area of land as their holiest ground. The Temple Mount (Judaism) is the same location as the Haram al-Sharif (Islam). This overlap has fuelled centuries of conflict — but also moments of remarkable coexistence.

Evaluate: Can Jerusalem Be Shared?

Jerusalem is sacred to three faiths, but they often disagree about who has the strongest claim. Can this city ever be shared peacefully?

Optimistic: Shared roots show it's possible

Judaism, Christianity, and Islam all trace back to Abraham. They share prophets, stories, and values. If believers focused on what they have in common rather than what divides them, Jerusalem could be a symbol of unity rather than conflict. Interfaith dialogue has already made progress.

Pessimistic: Deep political conflict makes sharing impossible

The Israeli-Palestinian conflict has made Jerusalem a political battleground as well as a religious one. Both sides claim Jerusalem as their capital. When sacred sites become symbols of national identity, compromise becomes almost impossible.

Realistic: Compromise is hard but necessary

There are no easy answers. But history shows that Jerusalem has been shared before — imperfectly, but genuinely. Respect for all three traditions, protection of holy sites, and access for all pilgrims are minimum requirements for any lasting peace.

Question 1: Why is Jerusalem sacred to Jews?

🔒Try to answer from memory first!

Answer: Jerusalem contains the Western Wall (last remnant of the Second Temple) and Temple Mount (holiest site in Judaism, where God's presence dwelt). Jews pray at the Western Wall and place written prayers in the cracks.

Question 2: What is the Church of the Holy Sepulchre?

🔒Try to answer from memory first!

Answer: The holiest church in Christianity, built on the site where Christians believe Jesus was crucified, buried, and rose from the dead.

Question 3: Why is the Dome of the Rock important to Muslims?

🔒Try to answer from memory first!

Answer: Muslims believe the Dome of the Rock marks the spot where the Prophet Muhammad ascended to heaven during the Isra and Mi'raj (Night Journey). It sits within the Haram al-Sharif (Noble Sanctuary).

Question 4: What does "Abrahamic faiths" mean?

🔒Try to answer from memory first!

Answer: Judaism, Christianity, and Islam are called the Abrahamic faiths because they all trace their origins back to the prophet Abraham. They share common roots, prophets, and many beliefs.

Key Terms

Jerusalem
A city sacred to Judaism, Christianity, and Islam — one of the most contested holy places on Earth.
Western Wall
The last remaining wall of the Second Temple in Jerusalem. The holiest place where Jews can pray.
Church of the Holy Sepulchre
The holiest church in Christianity, built where Jesus is believed to have been crucified and resurrected.
Al-Aqsa Mosque
The third holiest mosque in Islam, located in Jerusalem within the Haram al-Sharif compound.
Dome of the Rock
Islamic shrine in Jerusalem marking where Muhammad is believed to have ascended to heaven.
Temple Mount
The holiest site in Judaism, believed to be where God's presence dwelt. Also the location of the Haram al-Sharif.
Via Dolorosa
The route in Jerusalem that Jesus walked while carrying the cross to his crucifixion. Pilgrims walk it today.
Abrahamic
Relating to the three faiths — Judaism, Christianity, and Islam — that trace their origins to the prophet Abraham.
