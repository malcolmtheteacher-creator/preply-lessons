The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A clever young man

Julius Caesar was born in Rome about 100 years before the birth of Christ. At that time, Rome did not have a king. Instead, important men shared the power, and they changed leaders often. They did not want one man to have all the power. That was very important to them.

Caesar was clever and he spoke very well. People liked to listen to him. He wanted to be powerful, so he spent a lot of money to make friends and win votes. He also made a deal with two strong men in Rome. Together, the three of them controlled the city.

Then Caesar became a general. He took an army to Gaul — the land we now call France — and he fought there for many years. He won, and he became rich and famous. His soldiers loved him. But the important men back in Rome were afraid of him now. They said, "Come back to Rome, but leave your army behind."

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Did Rome have a king when Caesar was young? What did the important men want?

2. How did Caesar become rich and famous?

1. No, Rome had no king. The important men shared the power. They did not want one man to have all the power.

2. He became a general, took an army to Gaul (France), and won many battles. He became rich and famous, and his soldiers loved him.

Talk about it:
  • Caesar spent a lot of money to make people like him. Is that a good way to become a leader? Why, or why not?

Part 2 — Crossing the river

There was a law in Rome. A general could not bring his army into Italy. A small river called the Rubicon showed where Italy began. If Caesar crossed it with his soldiers, that was a crime against Rome.

Caesar had to choose. If he came back with no army, his enemies could hurt him. If he came back with his army, there would be a war. He waited by the river. Then, one day in the year 49 before Christ, he made his choice. He crossed the river with his soldiers. People still say "to cross the Rubicon" today. It means to do something you cannot change or take back.

After that, there was a war between Caesar and the other leaders of Rome. Caesar won. He was a very good soldier, and his army was strong. Finally, he had no more rivals. The Senate gave him the highest power. He became "dictator" — the one leader of Rome. Later they said he could keep this power for all his life. No Roman had ever done this before.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was the law about the river Rubicon?

2. What happened after Caesar crossed the river? What did he become?

1. A general could not bring his army into Italy. The Rubicon showed where Italy began. To cross it with soldiers was a crime.

2. There was a war, and Caesar won it. He became "dictator" — the one leader of Rome — for all his life.

Talk about it:
  • "To cross the Rubicon" means to do something you cannot change. Have you ever made a big choice like this?

Part 3 — The 15th of March

Caesar used his power to change many things in Rome. He made new laws, he gave land to his soldiers, and he made a new calendar. One month, July, still has his name today. Many normal people loved him. But some important men in the Senate did not. They were afraid. They thought Caesar wanted to be a king, and they did not want a king.

So a group of these men made a secret plan. One of them was Brutus. Caesar trusted Brutus like a son. People say someone warned Caesar to be careful on the 15th of March. But Caesar did not listen, and he went to the Senate on that day.

There, the group of men attacked Caesar and killed him. It was the 15th of March, 44 years before Christ. But the plan did not save Rome. After Caesar's death, there were more wars. In the end, Caesar's young relative, Augustus, became the first emperor of Rome. Rome now had one ruler again, and the old way was gone. The name "Caesar" became a word for "ruler" for two thousand years.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why were some men in the Senate afraid of Caesar?

2. What happened after Caesar died? Did the plan save Rome?

1. They thought Caesar wanted to be a king, and they did not want one man to be king of Rome.

2. No. There were more wars. In the end, Caesar's relative Augustus became the first emperor, so Rome had one ruler again.

Talk about it:
  • The men killed Caesar to stop one man having all the power. But it did not work. Was their plan a good idea or a bad idea?

Grammar: past simple + time words

This is a true story from the past, so we use the past simple. We also use small time words to put the events in order: first, then, next, after that, finally.

The past simple tells us an action is finished and in the past.
• Regular verbs add -ed: work → worked, want → wanted, cross → crossed.
• Many common verbs are irregular: go → went, win → won, become → became, make → made, take → took.
Time words show the order of events:
First, Caesar fought in Gaul. Then he crossed the river. After that, there was a war. Finally, he became the leader of Rome.
