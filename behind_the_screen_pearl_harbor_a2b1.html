BEHIND THE SCREEN · A2/B1 · 50 min

Pearl Harbor

A big love story on top of a very real and very terrible morning. Two of the heroes never existed — but two other pilots did.

Want more detail? Read this lesson at B2/C1 level →

Today: tell the story of the film Pearl Harbor — two pilots, one nurse and a morning that changed the world — find out which parts are true and which parts the film invented, and learn to give reasons and results. Grammar: because and so.
Before we begin — talk about it: Do you like films about real events? Is it OK for a film to change a true story to make it more exciting? And do you remember a film that taught you something about history?

The film, in three parts

Michael Bay's Pearl Harbor (2001). Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one. (There are spoilers — that is fine, we want to talk about it.) This film is about a day when thousands of real people died, so we will talk about it seriously.

Part 1 — Two friends and a nurse

Rafe and Danny are best friends. When they were small boys, they played at flying planes. Now, in 1941, they are both young pilots in the American army. Rafe meets a nurse called Evelyn, and they fall in love.

America is not in the war yet. But Rafe wants to fight, so he goes to England to fly with the British air force. Evelyn and Danny go to Hawaii. Then, one day, bad news arrives: Rafe's plane was shot down over the sea. Everyone thinks he is dead.

Danny and Evelyn are very sad, and slowly they fall in love too. Then, on 6 December, Rafe comes back. He is alive! He sees Danny and Evelyn together, and the two friends have a big fight. They do not know it, but in a few hours something much bigger will happen.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why do Danny and Evelyn think they can fall in love?

2. What happens on 6 December?

1. Rafe goes to England to fight. His plane is shot down over the sea, so everybody thinks he is dead.

2. Rafe comes back alive. He finds Danny and Evelyn together, and the two friends fight.

Talk about it:
  • The film spends a long time on the love story before the history starts. Do you like this? Why, or why not?

Part 2 — Sunday morning

It is early on Sunday 7 December 1941. Suddenly, Japanese planes come over the island of Hawaii. They drop bombs and torpedoes on the American ships in the harbour. Big ships burn and sink. Many men cannot get out.

Rafe and Danny run to a small airfield, get into two planes and fly. They are brave, and they shoot down several enemy planes. In the film, they are almost the only Americans who fly that day.

In the hospital, Evelyn and the other nurses work all day. There are too many hurt people and not enough medicine. It is a very hard, very sad day. After the attack, the President speaks to the country, and America goes to war.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What do Rafe and Danny do during the attack?

2. What is happening in the hospital?

1. They run to a small airfield, fly two planes and shoot down some enemy planes.

2. Evelyn and the nurses work all day. There are too many hurt people and not enough medicine.

Talk about it:
  • Which is stronger for you — the exciting flying scenes, or the quiet hospital scenes? Why?

Part 3 — Answering back

America wants to answer the attack. A pilot called Doolittle plans something very dangerous: big bombers will fly from a ship, drop bombs on Japan, and then continue to China, because they cannot land on the ship again. Rafe and Danny both say yes.

The planes reach Japan and drop their bombs. Then they fly on until the fuel finishes, and they crash in China. Rafe and Danny are alive after the crash, but Japanese soldiers find them. Danny is shot while he is protecting Rafe, and he dies. Before he dies, he learns that Evelyn is going to have his baby.

Rafe goes home and marries Evelyn. Years later, they visit Danny's grave with a little boy. The boy has Danny's name. At the end, Rafe takes him up in a small old plane, over the same fields where two little boys once played at flying.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why is Doolittle's plan so dangerous?

2. How does the film end?

1. The bombers fly from a ship, but they cannot land on the ship again, so they must continue to China and hope the fuel is enough.

2. Danny dies in China. Rafe marries Evelyn and helps to bring up Danny's son.

Talk about it:
  • The film ends with a happy family. The real war continued for nearly four more years. Is this a good ending? Why, or why not?

Grammar: because & so

This story is full of reasons and results. Something happens, and then something else happens because of it. English has two small words for this, and they work in opposite directions.

because = the reason. Put the result first, then because + the reason:
• "She was tired because she worked all night."
• "The train stopped because there was snow on the line."
so = the result. Put the reason first, then so + the result:
• "She worked all night, so she was tired."
• "There was snow on the line, so the train stopped."
Feel the difference:
• Same two facts, different order! result + because + reason  /  reason + so + result.
• After because and after so you need a full sentence: a person or thing + a verb. ✗ because the snow → ✓ because there was snow.
• We often put a comma before so, but not before because.

The Real Story — what is true here?

The attack really happened. But many people say this film is one of the least true war films ever made. Here is the difference between the film and the record.

🎬 Rafe and Danny never existed

Rafe, Danny and Evelyn are not real people. The writers invented them. But two real pilots did something very similar on that morning: Kenneth Taylor and George Welch. They drove through the attack to a small airfield called Haleiwa, took off in P-40 planes and fought. Welch shot down four Japanese planes and Taylor shot down two.

About fourteen American pilots got into the air that day, and four of them were killed. Many years later, Kenneth Taylor watched the film. He did not like it. He called it "a piece of trash".

The film changes the dates and puts everything together

Doolittle's raid on Japan was real, but it was a different operation. It happened on 18 April 1942 — four months after Pearl Harbor, not a few weeks. Sixteen bombers flew from a ship called the USS Hornet, with eighty men. They bombed six different cities, not only Tokyo. The film puts all the planes together over one city, because that looks better on screen.

Rafe's story in England is also impossible. Americans really did fly with the British air force in special "Eagle" squadrons, but those squadrons stayed in Britain until the autumn of 1942. And the Battle of Britain finished in October 1940 — the film shows Rafe still fighting in it in 1941.

What the attack really caused

This part is not a story. On 7 December 1941, 2,403 Americans were killed and more than a thousand were hurt. 1,177 of them died on one ship, the USS Arizona, and 429 on the USS Oklahoma. The planes came in two waves. The three big American aircraft carriers were away at sea that morning, so they were not hit — and this was very important later in the war.

The next day, 8 December, the President asked the government for war, and America entered the Second World War. On 11 December, Germany and Italy also declared war on America. That is what the attack really brought: not a love story, but a world war with America in it.

  • Two real pilots were very brave, but most people do not know their names. Is that a problem? Why?
  • Is it OK for a film to invent people inside a true story? Where is your line?
