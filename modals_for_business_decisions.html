Modals for Business Decisions

Should, Could, Might - Making suggestions and giving opinions

A2 | Business English

Why Do We Need These Words?

Imagine this situation...

You are in a meeting. Your team needs to decide: expand to Germany or France first?

Two ways to give your opinion:

Direct (sounds strong/aggressive)

"We must go to Germany first."

Softer (sounds professional)

"We should go to Germany first."

"We could go to Germany first."

"We might want to go to Germany first."

In business English, softer = more professional.

These modal verbs help you sound confident but not aggressive. Your colleagues will listen to you more.

The Three Modals for Today

Modal When to use Strength
SHOULD Strong recommendation - "This is my advice"
COULD Suggestion / possibility - "This is one option"
MIGHT Gentle suggestion - "Just an idea..."

How to Build Sentences

The Structure (same for all three!)

Subject + MODAL + Base Verb

Good news: No conjugation! No "shoulds" or "coulds". Always the same form.

Base verb = infinitive without "to" (go, not "to go")

SHOULD - Strong Recommendation

Examples

We should hire more staff.

You should talk to the supplier.

The company should invest in marketing.

Negative: SHOULDN'T

We shouldn't wait too long.

You shouldn't ignore this problem.

COULD - Suggestion / Possibility

Examples

We could try a new approach.

You could contact the German distributor.

The team could meet on Friday.

COULD is softer than SHOULD. Use it when you want to offer options, not give strong advice.

MIGHT - Gentle Suggestion

Examples

We might want to reconsider.

It might be a good idea to wait.

You might want to check the numbers again.

MIGHT is the softest. Very polite. Good for sensitive topics or when talking to senior colleagues.

Quick Comparison

Situation: We need to reduce costs Feeling
"We should reduce the budget." Strong advice (I believe this)
"We could reduce the budget." One option (there are others)
"We might want to reduce the budget." Gentle idea (just thinking aloud)

Speaking: Business Decisions

Choose 2 scenarios. Give your opinion using should, could, and might.

1
New Market

Your pet food company wants to expand. You can choose: UK, Spain, or Poland. What do you recommend?

Sentence starters

"I think we should..." / "We could also..." / "We might want to consider..."

2
Difficult Supplier

Your main supplier is late with deliveries. What are the options? What do you recommend?

Sentence starters

"We should talk to them about..." / "We could find..." / "We might need to..."

3
New Product

Your team has an idea for organic cat food. Is it a good idea? What should the company do?

Sentence starters

"We should research..." / "We could start with..." / "We might want to test..."

4
Team Problem

Two employees in your team don't work well together. What do you recommend?

Sentence starters

"I think we should..." / "We could try..." / "It might be a good idea to..."

5
Marketing Budget

You have extra budget this quarter. How should the company spend it?

Sentence starters

"We should invest in..." / "We could spend it on..." / "We might want to save..."

6
Online vs Retail

Should your company focus on online sales or physical stores? What's your recommendation?

Sentence starters

"We should focus on..." / "We could do both..." / "We might want to start with..."

Lesson Review

SHOULD

Strong recommendation

"We should hire more staff."

COULD

Suggestion / possibility

"We could try a new approach."

MIGHT

Gentle suggestion

"We might want to reconsider."

Remember:

  • Structure: Subject + MODAL + Base Verb (no conjugation!)
  • Softer modals = more professional in business
  • Use SHOULD for advice, COULD for options, MIGHT for gentle ideas
