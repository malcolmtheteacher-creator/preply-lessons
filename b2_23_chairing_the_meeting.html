Handling Situations

A chairperson must handle challenges diplomatically. Read each scenario and consider what you would say.

Scenario 1: Someone Goes Off-Topic
Sarah starts discussing office parking instead of the project deadline you planned to cover. The meeting is already running late.
Model Response
"Sarah, that's a valid concern, but parking isn't on today's agenda. Could we add it to next week's agenda? Right now, we really need to focus on finalising the project deadline."
Scenario 2: Two People Disagree Strongly
James wants to cut costs by freezing salaries. Maria argues this will damage morale and retention. They're talking over each other.
Model Response
"Thank you both for those perspectives. James, you're concerned about costs; Maria, you're concerned about morale. These are both important. Let's hear each argument without interruption, then we'll explore if there's a middle ground."
Scenario 3: Someone Hasn't Spoken Yet
Half the team has been silent throughout the discussion on restructuring. You need their input.
Model Response
"I'd like to hear from everyone. Alex, you've been quiet—what's your take on this restructuring plan? We value your perspective."
Scenario 4: You Need to Skip an Agenda Item
The meeting is running 15 minutes late, but two important items remain. One is less urgent.
Model Response
"We're running a little over. Item 4 on the budget is critical, but Item 5 on office supplies can be handled by email. Shall we skip Item 5 and make sure we give Item 4 the time it deserves?"
Scenario 5: It's Time to Close
You've covered all agenda items. Three decisions were made, four actions assigned. Close the meeting clearly.
Model Response
"Excellent. To summarise: we've agreed to launch the campaign in April, we'll freeze admin salaries but not technical roles, and we're extending working-from-home to Fridays. The action items are clear—you'll each get a summary email. Thank you all for your engaged discussion. Meeting closed."

Chair the Meeting

Scenario: You are the chair of a board meeting for a design company called "Crew". The business is in trouble and costs must be cut by 20% within three months. Three proposals are on the table:

  • Proposal A: Shift to remote-first working (save 40% on office costs)
  • Proposal B: Reduce salaries across the board by 15%
  • Proposal C: Halt the internship programme (save £80k annually)

Your role: guide the team through a structured discussion, weigh pros and cons, and reach a decision everyone can support.

Role-Play Timer (3 minutes)

3:00

Your Role as Chair

  1. Open the meeting: explain the urgency and the three options
  2. Invite discussion on each proposal (pros and cons)
  3. Summarise agreements and disagreements
  4. Guide the team toward a decision or compromise
  5. Close by confirming what was agreed
Reflection & Feedback
  • Did you manage time effectively during the role-play?
  • How did you keep the discussion focused and balanced?
  • What techniques did you use to encourage quiet participants?
  • How did you handle disagreement or conflicting views?
  • Was the final decision clear and acceptable to the group?
