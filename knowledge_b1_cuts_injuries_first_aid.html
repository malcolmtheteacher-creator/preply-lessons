Guess first

Someone slips with a kitchen knife and cuts a finger. The cut is bleeding. What is the best first action?

💨A. Blow on it
🚿B. Hold it under water for a long time
🧻C. Press on it with a clean cloth

Check your answer
C. Pressing firmly with a clean cloth helps to slow the bleeding. You clean the cut after the bleeding slows down. If the bleeding doesn't stop, you need urgent help.

What Happened?

Spot the pattern

1. I was chopping vegetables when the knife slipped.
2. She was cycling home when a car stopped suddenly.
3. We were playing football when he fell over.
What do you notice? Which action was longer? Which action interrupted it? What tenses are used?
Check the pattern
was / were + -ing = the longer action (the background).
Past simple = the short action that interrupted it.
We join them with when.

Find the mistakes

One of these four sentences is correct. Find it, then fix the other three.

  1. I cutted my finger yesterday.
  2. I was cooking when I burnt me.
  3. I was washing up when the glass broke.
  4. It hurts me a lot.
Check
1. I cut my finger yesterday.
2. I was cooking when I burnt myself (or: burnt my hand).
3. Correct!
4. It hurts a lot. (We say "My finger hurts" or "It hurts" with no object.)
Say my finger, my hand, my head, not "the finger". We use myself only when we do the action to ourselves: "I burnt myself."

First Aid Steps

Put the steps in order

These five steps for a small cut are mixed up. Say the correct order using First, Then, Next, After that, Finally.

  • A. Rinse the cut with clean water.
  • B. Cover it with a clean plaster or bandage.
  • C. Wash your hands.
  • D. Raise the hand above your heart.
  • E. Press firmly on the cut with a clean cloth.
Check the order
  1. 1C. First, wash your hands.
  2. 2E. Then press firmly on the cut with a clean cloth.
  3. 3D. Next, raise the hand above your heart.
  4. 4A. After that, when the bleeding slows, rinse the cut with clean water.
  5. 5B. Finally, cover it with a clean plaster or bandage.

Getting Help

Where would you go?

For each situation, decide: stay at home / pharmacy, see a doctor soon, or go to emergency. Explain your choice.

  1. A paper cut on a finger. It stopped bleeding straight away.
  2. A deep cut. You pressed on it for ten minutes and it is still bleeding heavily.
  3. A cut from two days ago is now red, hot and swollen.
  4. A mild headache and a cold.
  5. A cut from broken glass. You can see something small inside it.
Suggested answers (details vary from country to country)
1. Home or pharmacy: clean it and use a plaster.
2. Emergency: heavy bleeding that doesn't stop needs urgent help.
3. Doctor soon: red, hot and swollen can mean an infection.
4. Pharmacy: a pharmacist can advise on medicine.
5. Emergency or urgent care: something inside a wound needs professional treatment.

Useful phrases

Patient

• I've cut my finger and it won't stop bleeding.
• It happened about an hour ago.
• It's quite deep, I think.
• Do I need stitches?
• Is it going to leave a scar?
• Should I come back to have it checked?

Pharmacist / Doctor

• What happened?
• When did it happen?
• Can you move your finger?
• Are you allergic to anything?
• When did you last have a tetanus injection?
• Keep it clean and dry, and come back if it gets worse.

Recall Zone

Without looking anything up, answer out loud:

What is the difference between "I was walking" and "I walked"?
"I was walking" is a longer action in progress. "I walked" is a finished action. We often use them together: "I was walking home when it started to rain."
