Key Fact: The IELTS Academic Reading test uses authentic texts from books, journals, magazines, and newspapers. These are written for a non-specialist audience, so you don't need expert knowledge of the topics.

What You Need to Know

  • 1
    No Extra Transfer Time: Unlike Listening, there is NO extra time to transfer answers. Write directly on your answer sheet as you go!
  • 2
    Passages Increase in Difficulty: Passage 1 is easiest, Passage 3 is hardest. Plan your time accordingly.
  • 3
    Each Question = 1 Mark: There's no weighting - every correct answer counts equally.
  • 4
    No Penalty for Wrong Answers: Never leave a blank! Always guess if you don't know.
  • 5
    Word Limits Matter: If a question says "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS," writing three words means zero marks.
WARNING: Time is your biggest enemy in IELTS Reading. Many students don't finish all 40 questions. Strategic time management is essential - we'll cover this in Tab 5.

Understanding the Three Passages

The test consists of three passages of increasing difficulty. Each passage has approximately 13-14 questions, totaling 40 questions across the entire test.

Passage Characteristics

Total Word Count: The three passages combined contain approximately 2,500-3,000 words. This means you need to read efficiently - not every word carefully, but strategically.

Types of Text You'll Encounter

  • 1
    Descriptive Texts: Explain how something works, historical accounts, descriptions of processes
  • 2
    Factual Texts: Present information, data, and research findings objectively
  • 3
    Discursive Texts: Present arguments, discuss viewpoints, analyze issues
  • 4
    Analytical Texts: Examine causes and effects, compare and contrast, evaluate evidence
Strategy Tip: Don't read passages in detail before looking at questions. Skim the passage quickly (60-90 seconds) to get the general idea, then let the questions guide your detailed reading.

The 14 Question Types

Click on each card to learn more about the question type. You'll practice each type in detail in future lessons.

Group 1: True/False Questions

True/False/Not Given Type 1

Determine if statements about facts agree with, contradict, or are not mentioned in the text.
Key Strategy: TRUE = matches the text, FALSE = contradicts the text, NOT GIVEN = information not present. Focus on MEANING, not exact words. Watch out for qualifiers like "all," "some," "always," "never."

Covered in: Lesson 4

Yes/No/Not Given Type 2

Similar to T/F/NG but tests the writer's opinions and claims rather than facts.
Key Strategy: Look for opinion language: "I believe," "it seems," "arguably." YES = writer agrees, NO = writer disagrees, NOT GIVEN = writer doesn't express an opinion on this.

Covered in: Lesson 5

Group 2: Matching Questions

Matching Headings Type 3

Match headings to paragraphs based on main ideas.
Key Strategy: Focus on main idea, not details. Read first and last sentences. Create YOUR OWN heading first, then match. More headings than paragraphs - some won't be used.

Covered in: Lesson 6

Matching Information Type 4

Match statements to specific paragraphs where the information is found.
Key Strategy: Answers NOT in order. Some paragraphs may not be used; others may be used more than once. Scan for keywords, then verify meaning.

Covered in: Lesson 7

Matching Features Type 5

Match items to categories, people, dates, or places.
Key Strategy: Names and dates are easy to scan for - use them as landmarks. Read all options first. An option may be used more than once.

Covered in: Lesson 7

Matching Sentence Endings Type 6

Match the beginning of sentences with appropriate endings.
Key Strategy: Answers usually in order. Check both grammar AND meaning. More endings than beginnings - some won't be used. Predict the ending before looking at options.

Covered in: Lesson 8

Group 3: Completion Questions

Sentence Completion Type 7

Complete sentences using words from the passage.
Key Strategy: Follow word limits strictly! Check grammar fits. Answers in order. Use exact words from the text - no paraphrasing. Hyphenated words = one word.

Covered in: Lesson 9

Summary Completion Type 8

Complete a summary with words from text or a word bank.
Key Strategy: Summary may cover part of the passage, not all. With word bank: check grammar and meaning. Without: use exact words from text. Answers usually in order.

Covered in: Lesson 9

Note/Table/Flow-chart Type 9

Complete notes, tables, or flow charts with information from the passage.
Key Strategy: Use the structure to predict what type of information is needed. Notes may not be complete sentences. Follow word limits. Look for parallel structures.

Covered in: Lesson 10

Diagram Label Completion Type 10

Label parts of a diagram using information from the passage.
Key Strategy: Understand the diagram first - what does it show? Labels may not be in order. Look for descriptive language in the text that matches diagram positions.

Covered in: Lesson 10

Group 4: Choice Questions

Multiple Choice (Single) Type 11

Choose ONE correct answer from A, B, C, or D.
Key Strategy: Read the stem carefully. Predict answer before looking at options. Eliminate obviously wrong answers. Options contain paraphrasing - don't look for exact matches.

Covered in: Lesson 11

Multiple Choice (Multiple) Type 12

Choose TWO or more correct answers from a longer list.
Key Strategy: Check how many answers required. Options may be in random order. You must get ALL correct answers - partial credit not given. Use elimination.

Covered in: Lesson 11

List Selection Type 13

Select correct items from a given list.
Key Strategy: Similar to multiple choice with multiple answers. Read the question carefully to know how many to select. Items may be in different order than in text.

Covered in: Lesson 11

Group 5: Short Answer

Short Answer Questions Type 14

Answer questions briefly using words from the passage.
Key Strategy: Note the question word (Who? What? When? Where? Why? How?). Follow word limits. Use words from the text. Answers usually in order. Check spelling!

Covered in: Lesson 12
Remember: Each passage typically contains 3-4 different question types. You won't see all 14 types in one test, but you need to be prepared for any of them!

Scoring Rules to Remember

  • 1
    Spelling Counts: Misspelled answers are marked wrong. British and American spellings are both accepted.
  • 2
    Word Limits are Strict: "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS" means 1 or 2 words only. Three words = zero marks.
  • 3
    Hyphenated Words: Words like "well-known" or "check-in" count as ONE word.
  • 4
    Numbers: Can be written as words or figures (e.g., "5" or "five").
  • 5
    Capital Letters: You can write answers in capitals or lowercase - both are accepted.
Pro Tip: If your target is Band 7.0, you can afford to get 8-10 questions wrong. Don't panic if you find some questions difficult - focus on getting the easier ones right first!

Time Management Rules

  • 1
    Maximum 90 Seconds per Question: If you can't find the answer after 90 seconds, mark it and move on. Return later if time permits.
  • 2
    Write Answers Directly: There's no transfer time! Write on your answer sheet as you go, not on the question paper.
  • 3
    Check Your Watch: At 20 minutes, you should be starting Passage 2. At 40 minutes, you should be starting Passage 3.
  • 4
    Never Leave Blanks: With 5 minutes left, if you have unanswered questions, guess! There's no penalty for wrong answers.

What to Do If You're Running Out of Time

Emergency Strategy:
  1. Stop what you're doing
  2. Count how many questions are unanswered
  3. For Multiple Choice: guess B or C (statistically most common)
  4. For T/F/NG: guess TRUE (slightly more common)
  5. For completion: write any reasonable word from the passage
  6. NEVER leave blanks!
