Key words

polite (adjective) — kind and respectful in the way you speak to people. Example: "It's good manners to say 'please' and 'thank you': it's polite."
rude (adjective) — not kind or respectful to other people. Example: "It's rude to shout at a waiter."
formal (adjective) — careful and serious; used at work or with people you don't know. Example: "Use formal English in a job interview."
informal (adjective) — relaxed; used with friends and family. Example: "Text messages to friends are usually informal."
direct (adjective) — saying exactly what you want, with no extra words. Example: "He's very direct: he always says what he thinks."
friendly (adjective) — kind and warm to other people. Example: "The hotel staff were very friendly."
request (noun) — the act of asking for something politely. Example: "I have a small request: could you close the door?"
apologise (verb) — say sorry. Example: "I apologise for being late."
tone (noun) — the feeling in your words, for example happy, kind or angry. Example: "Her words were OK, but her tone was angry."
