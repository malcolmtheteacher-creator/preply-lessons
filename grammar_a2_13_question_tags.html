Now look at this one. A different neighbour says: "You don't usually leave this early, do you?" That's not "isn't it" — it's "do you". Why do you think the little question at the end changes?

Check your idea CLICK
Because it depends on the sentence in front of it. These short questions — called tags — always match the main sentence: whether it's positive or negative, and which auxiliary verb it uses (is, does, can, will...). There isn't just one tag for everything, even though "isn't it" is the one people learn first.
Today: question tags like isn't it? and do you? — matching a positive statement with a negative tag, a negative statement with a positive tag, and choosing the right auxiliary (be, do/does/did, can, will) — so you can chat naturally about weather, people and plans.

Talking About the Weather: "isn't it?" and "aren't you?"

For small talk about the weather, places and people — using is/are/was/were.

Spot the pattern

Three things you might hear as small talk:

  • "It's freezing outside, isn't it?"
  • "You're not from around here, are you?"
  • "The concert was amazing, wasn't it?"

Before you check: when does the little question go negative, and when does it go positive?

Check the pattern CLICK
A positive statement gets a negative tag (isn't, aren't, wasn't, weren't). A negative statement gets a positive tag (is, are, was, were). The pronoun in the tag always matches the subject: "it" stays "it", "you" stays "you".
positive statement + isn't / aren't / wasn't / weren't + pronoun?
negative statement + is / are / was / were + pronoun?

Checking What People Do: "do you?" and "doesn't she?"

For habits, routines and things that happened — using do/does/did instead of be.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday checks:

  • "You don't eat meat, do you?"
  • "She works from home, doesn't she?"
  • "They arrived early, didn't they?"

Same question as before: positive or negative, and which auxiliary this time?

Check the pattern CLICK
Same rule (positive → negative tag, negative → positive tag) — but with an ordinary verb like "eat" or "work", the tag uses do/does/did, never "be". Match the tense too: a past statement needs "did", not "do" or "does".
positive statement (ordinary verb) + don't / doesn't / didn't + pronoun?
negative statement + do / does / did + pronoun?

Plans & Ability: "can't you?" and "won't we?"

For ability, plans and predictions — using can and will.

Spot the pattern

  • "You can swim, can't you?"
  • "It won't rain later, will it?"
  • "We'll arrive on time, won't we?"

Same question again: what's the rule this time?

Check the pattern CLICK
Still the same rule — positive → negative tag, negative → positive tag — but now with a modal (can or will). The tag just repeats the modal from the statement; it never switches to "do" or "be".
positive statement + can't / won't + pronoun?
negative statement + can / will + pronoun?

Your Turn to Talk

Small talk is full of tag questions. Time to use all three types in one real conversation.

The situation

Imagine you've just started chatting with someone new — a neighbour, someone at a bus stop, a colleague on their first day. Talk for one minute. Try to include:

  • a tag about the weather or how something is ("It's..., isn't it?")
  • a tag about something someone does or did ("You don't..., do you?")
  • a tag about ability or a plan ("You can..., can't you?" / "You'll..., won't you?")
