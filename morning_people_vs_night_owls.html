Vocabulary Focus (1-3)

1. chronotype

Click to reveal

Meaning

A person's natural inclination regarding the times of day when they prefer to sleep and when they are most alert or energetic.

Form

Noun (countable) | "a chronotype" / "chronotypes"

Often used with: early/late chronotype, natural chronotype, individual chronotype

Pronunciation

/ˈkrɒnəʊtaɪp/ - KRON-oh-type

Stress on first syllable. From Greek: chronos (time) + type

"Research shows that forcing students to ignore their natural chronotype leads to poorer academic performance."

NOW YOU TRY:

Would you describe yourself as having an early or late chronotype? Has this changed as you've got older?

2. circadian rhythm

Click to reveal

Meaning

The body's internal 24-hour clock that regulates sleep-wake cycles, hormone release, eating habits, and other bodily functions.

Form

Noun (countable) | Usually singular: "the/your circadian rhythm"

Collocates with: disrupt, regulate, reset, natural, internal

Pronunciation

/sɜːˈkeɪdiən ˈrɪðəm/ - sir-KAY-dee-un RITH-um

From Latin: circa (about) + dies (day)

"Jet lag occurs when your circadian rhythm is out of sync with the local time zone."

NOW YOU TRY:

What disrupts your circadian rhythm most - travel, stress, or screen time before bed?

3. to hit one's stride

Click to reveal

Meaning

To reach your optimal level of performance or productivity; to find your rhythm and work at your best.

Form

Idiom (verb phrase) | hit/hits/hitting one's stride

Often followed by time expressions: "by 10am", "in the evening", "after lunch"

Pronunciation

/hɪt wʌnz straɪd/ - hit wunz STRYDE

Register: Neutral to slightly informal. Works in professional contexts.

"I don't schedule important meetings before 11am because I don't really hit my stride until mid-morning."

NOW YOU TRY:

What time of day do you typically hit your stride? What kind of work do you save for that time?

Vocabulary Focus (4-5)

4. groggy

Click to reveal

Meaning

Feeling dazed, weak, and sluggish, especially after waking up or due to lack of sleep. Unable to think clearly.

Form

Adjective | groggy - groggier - groggiest

Collocates with: feel groggy, still groggy, a bit groggy, wake up groggy

Pronunciation

/ˈɡrɒɡi/ - GROG-ee

Register: Informal but widely used. Original meaning: drunk on "grog" (diluted rum)

"I always feel groggy for the first hour after waking up - I can't have serious conversations until I've had coffee."

NOW YOU TRY:

How long does it take before you stop feeling groggy in the morning? What helps?

5. a second wind

Click to reveal

Meaning

A renewed burst of energy or strength after feeling tired; finding fresh energy to continue when you thought you were exhausted.

Form

Noun phrase (countable) | "a second wind" / "get a second wind"

Verbs: get/catch/find a second wind

Pronunciation

/ə ˈsekənd wɪnd/ - a SEK-und wind

From running: when you recover your breath mid-race. Register: Neutral.

"I'm exhausted by 6pm, but around 9pm I get a second wind and can work productively until midnight."

NOW YOU TRY:

Do you ever get a second wind? When does it usually happen, and what triggers it?

Your Turn

Choose 2-3 cards that interest you. Use the vocabulary naturally in your answers.

1. The Productivity Question

Some companies now let employees choose their own hours. Would this work in schools? Should teachers adapt to students' chronotypes, or should students adapt to "real world" schedules?

2. The Fairness Debate

"Night owls are just undisciplined." Do you agree? If chronotype is genetic, is it unfair to judge people who struggle with early starts?

3. Your Best Hours

When do you hit your stride? What tasks do you save for your peak hours, and what do you do when you're groggy or tired?

4. Sleep and Success

Many CEOs brag about sleeping 4-5 hours. Is this admirable discipline or dangerous advice? What does it say about how we value productivity over health?

5. Fighting Your Biology

Have you ever had to work against your natural circadian rhythm? What was it like? Did you adapt, or did you always feel like you were struggling?

6. The Cultural Angle

Some cultures have siestas; others worship the 5am wake-up. How does your culture view early risers vs night owls? Is there a moral judgment attached?
