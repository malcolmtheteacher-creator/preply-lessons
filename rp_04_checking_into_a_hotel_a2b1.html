Checking into a Hotel

You are tired. It was a long day. Now you are at a nice hotel — time to check in, ask for a few things, and be polite.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is late in the evening. You travelled all day, and you are very tired. Now you are at a nice hotel. You will stay here for three nights.

You booked the room online, so that part is easy. But you want a few small things: a room on a higher floor, where it is quiet, and a late checkout on your last day, because your train is in the evening. You do not need these things — but if you ask nicely, you can often get them.

Behind the desk is the receptionist. They are friendly and want to help. Later, there is one problem: your room is cold. But that is fine — you can ask for help, politely.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like hotels? What makes a hotel good for you — the room, the breakfast, or the people?
  2. When you want something (a better table, a quiet room), do you ask, or do you say nothing? Why?

Who's who

You
The Guest 🧳

Tired, but friendly and polite. You want your room, two small things, and — when the room is cold — some help.

The other person
The Receptionist 🛎️

Kind and helpful. They can give you a better room, a late checkout, and a nice welcome — if you are polite.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the polite words: please, thank you, I would like, can I. These small words do a lot of work.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the polite words.

YouYes, I do. I have a booking. My name is Okafor — three nights, please.
ReceptionistOkafor… yes, here it is. A double room, three nights, with breakfast. Can I have your card and your passport, please?

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use in any hotel. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it with a smile. In English, a friendly voice and the words please and thank you do a lot. Speak slowly and clearly. You do not need long sentences — short and polite is perfect.

🛎️ Arriving

Phrase 1

You arrive at the desk. You need to say that you have a room, and your name. Complete it: "I have a ___. My name is Okafor."

"I have a booking. My name is Okafor."
A booking is a room you reserved before (you can also say "a reservation"). Start with your name — the receptionist needs it to find you.
Phrase 2

You want to ask for something extra. Which sentence is polite?

A. "Give me a better room."
B. "Can I ask for something, please?"
C. "I want a different room now."
B — "Can I ask for something, please?"
"Can I…?" and "please" make it polite and soft. A and C sound like an order, not a request — and people help you more when you are polite.

🌟 Asking for things

Phrase 3

You want a room high up, where it is quiet. Say it politely. Complete it: "I would ___ a room on a higher floor, please."

"I would like a room on a higher floor, please."
"I would like…" is the polite way to say "I want". Use it for everything: "I would like a coffee", "I would like a table by the window".
Phrase 4

You want to ask if a late checkout is possible. Put the words in order:

a late checkoutispossible?
"Is a late checkout possible?"
"Is … possible?" is a very useful way to ask for something politely: "Is a bigger room possible?", "Is breakfast at nine possible?"

🌧️ A problem

Phrase 5

Your room is cold. You go back to the desk to tell them. Complete it: "Excuse me, there is a ___ with my room."

"Excuse me, there is a problem with my room."
"Excuse me" gets their attention politely. "There is a problem with…" is the calm, friendly way to start — you are not angry, you just need help.
Phrase 6

Now say what is wrong: the heating is not working. Complete it: "The heating doesn't ___."

"The heating doesn't work."
"… doesn't work" is perfect for any broken thing: "The TV doesn't work", "The shower doesn't work", "The key doesn't work".

💛 Saying thank you

Phrase 7

The receptionist helps you a lot. Which is a warm, friendly thank you?

A. "OK."
B. "That's very kind. Thank you."
C. "Fine."
B — "That's very kind. Thank you."
"That's very kind" tells the person you noticed their help. A and C are cold — they are not wrong, but they are not warm. Warm words make people happy to help you.
Phrase 8

At the end, you want to say a big thank you. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you so much!" / "Thank you very much!"
Add "so much" or "very much" to make your thank you bigger and warmer. A smile helps too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in hotels, shops, cafés — everywhere. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"I would like ___, please." — What do you want to ask for next time you go out? (a coffee, a table, a bag…)

"There is a problem with ___." — Think of a thing that can go wrong. What is it?

"The ___ doesn't work." — Name a thing in a room that can break.

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — arrive → ask for two things → tell them the problem → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your hotel. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the guest 🧳

The receptionist speaks — you answer. Be polite: use please, thank you, and I would like.

ReceptionistGood evening, welcome! Do you have a booking?
ReceptionistHere you are — three nights, with breakfast. Can I have your card, please?
ReceptionistOf course, I can do that. Anything else?
ReceptionistNo problem. Here is your key — room 812.
ReceptionistHello again — is everything OK?
ReceptionistI'm so sorry! Let me move you to another room.

Round 2 — now YOU are the receptionist 🛎️

Change places. You work at the hotel. Be friendly and helpful. Say "Welcome", "Of course", "No problem", "I'm sorry".

GuestHello, I have a booking. My name is Alvarez — two nights, please.
GuestHere you are. I would like a quiet room, please.
GuestThank you. Is a late breakfast possible?
GuestExcuse me — there is a problem. The shower doesn't work.
The Twist

🌙 The hotel is full

One more time — no help this round. Tonight the hotel is very busy. You ask for a room on a higher floor, but the receptionist says: "I'm sorry, we have no high rooms free tonight." What do you say? Stay polite and friendly. Ask for something else — maybe a quiet room, help with your bags, or a high room for tomorrow. Try to leave happy. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said my name and my booking clearly.
  • I used "please" and "I would like" to ask for things.
  • I told them the problem in a calm, polite way.
  • I said "thank you" warmly.
  • I stayed friendly the whole time.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

In 1912, there was a huge ship called the Titanic. It was like a big hotel on the sea — with beautiful rooms, a swimming pool, and lots of staff. People felt safe. They thought nothing could go wrong.

Today, you are at a hotel, and your only problem is a cold room. That is easy to fix — a new key, a phone call, and it is done.

On the Titanic, the problem was much bigger, and no smile could fix it. A cold room is nothing. Be happy it is only that!
