Checking into a Hotel

You are tired. It was a long day. Now you are at a nice hotel — time to check in, ask for a few things, and be polite.

The Scene 🎬

It is late in the evening. You travelled all day, and you are very tired. Now you are at a nice hotel. You will stay here for three nights.

You booked the room online, so that part is easy. But you want a few small things: a room on a higher floor, where it is quiet, and a late checkout on your last day, because your train is in the evening. You do not need these things — but if you ask nicely, you can often get them.

Behind the desk is the receptionist. They are friendly and want to help. Later, there is one problem: your room is cold. But that is fine — you can ask for help, politely.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like hotels? What makes a hotel good for you — the room, the breakfast, or the people?
  2. When you want something (a better table, a quiet room), do you ask, or do you say nothing? Why?

Who's who

You
The Guest 🧳

Tired, but friendly and polite. You want your room, two small things, and — when the room is cold — some help.

The other person
The Receptionist 🛎️

Kind and helpful. They can give you a better room, a late checkout, and a nice welcome — if you are polite.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each, then change. Find the phrases from your toolkit. Listen for the polite words.

ReceptionistGood evening, and welcome! Do you have a booking?
YouYes, I do. I have a booking. My name is Okafor — three nights, please.
ReceptionistOkafor… yes, here it is. A double room, three nights, with breakfast. Can I have your card and your passport, please?
YouHere you are. Can I ask for something? I would like a room on a higher floor, please. I am a light sleeper.
ReceptionistOf course. Let me see… Yes! I can give you room 812, on the eighth floor. It is very quiet up there.
YouThat's great, thank you. One more thing — is a late checkout possible on my last day? My train is in the evening.
ReceptionistNo problem. I can give you two o'clock. I will write it here, on your booking.
YouThank you very much! You are very kind.
ReceptionistYou're welcome. Here is your key — room 812. The lift is behind you.
YouExcuse me, sorry. There is a problem with my room. The heating doesn't work, and the room is very cold.
ReceptionistOh, I am sorry! Let me help you. I can move you to room 814. The heating is good there.
YouThat's very kind. Thank you so much.
ReceptionistYou're welcome. And I will take a little money off, for the problem. Here is your new key. Have a good night!

From memory — don't look up!

2. What is the problem with the first room (812)?

3. How does the receptionist fix the problem? They do two things — what are they?

2. The heating doesn't work, so the room is very cold.
3. The receptionist moves the guest to a different room (814), where the heating works — and takes a little money off, because of the problem.

The Echo 🕰️

In 1912, there was a huge ship called the Titanic. It was like a big hotel on the sea — with beautiful rooms, a swimming pool, and lots of staff. People felt safe. They thought nothing could go wrong.

Today, you are at a hotel, and your only problem is a cold room. That is easy to fix — a new key, a phone call, and it is done.

On the Titanic, the problem was much bigger, and no smile could fix it. A cold room is nothing. Be happy it is only that!

Two questions to talk about

  1. People pay a lot of money for a nice hotel. What do you think you are really paying for — the room, or the good feeling?
  2. When something goes wrong (a cold room, a late train), some people get angry and some stay calm. Which is better? Which are you?
