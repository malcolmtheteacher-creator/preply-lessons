Everyone Has an Accent

Including native speakers!

B1-B2 | 45-50 minutes

What Is a "Normal" Accent?

Quick question: Who speaks English "without an accent"? Americans? British people? Australians? The answer might surprise you...

The truth: Everyone has an accent. There is no "neutral" English. When people say someone has "no accent," they usually mean "an accent similar to mine."
75% of all English speakers in the world are NON-NATIVE speakers NSVRC Research
350+ different languages spoken in the United States alone US Census Bureau
257m people live and work in a country where they were not born UN Migration Report

The Problem: "Accentism"

Dr. Alice Foucart

Psycholinguistics Researcher, Ghent University

"People make judgements about people with foreign accents in jobs, in criminal cases. They think they are less intelligent and less trustworthy. But here's what's important: accents do not reflect intelligence. An accent is simply based on the sounds of the environment where you grew up. Nothing more."

Research Finding

University of Queensland, 2025

"We found that women with a 'standard' English accent were more likely to be hired than women with a non-standard accent - even when their qualifications were identical. This is accent discrimination, and most people don't even realise they do it."

But Here's the Interesting Part...

Native accents are judged too! In the US, Southern accents are often judged as "less intelligent" - unfairly and incorrectly.
Students often prefer non-native teachers Research shows ESL students sometimes understand non-native teachers better than native speakers!
The science says: The more you hear different accents, the easier they become to understand. Accent "problems" are often just about exposure, not intelligence.

So here's the real question: In a world where 75% of English speakers are non-native, who gets to decide what "correct" English sounds like?

Language Focus A: Sharing Your Opinion

These expressions help you share your thoughts - even when you're not 100% sure. Click to explore!

"I'd say..."

+
MEANING

A soft, friendly way to give your opinion. It sounds thoughtful and not too strong. Perfect when you want to share what you think without sounding aggressive.

FORM

I'd say + (that) + clause

OR: I'd say + it's/she's/they're + adjective/noun

PRONUNCIATION

/aɪd seɪ/ - The "I'd" is quick and connected. Don't separate "I" and "would"!

Say it fast: "I'd say" sounds like "EYED-say" - one smooth sound, not "I... would... say"
"I'd say accents make communication more interesting, not more difficult."
"I'd say she's one of the best teachers in the school."
NOW YOU TRY

Complete this sentence with YOUR opinion: "When it comes to learning languages, I'd say..."

"I suppose..."

+
MEANING

Shows you're thinking as you speak. You're considering something, maybe for the first time. It sounds thoughtful and open-minded.

FORM

I suppose + (that) + clause

Often used with "but": "I suppose... but..."

PRONUNCIATION

/aɪ səˈpəʊz/ - The stress is on "POSE". The "su" is very weak - almost like "suh".

The first syllable is weak: "I s'POSE" not "I SUP-pose". Native speakers almost swallow the "su"!
"I suppose it depends on the situation. In business, maybe a clear accent matters more."
"I suppose you're right, but I still think pronunciation is important."
NOW YOU TRY

Think about accent discrimination. Start your answer with "I suppose..." What comes to mind?

"From my experience..."

+
MEANING

You're basing your opinion on something real - your own life. This is powerful because nobody can argue with YOUR experience!

FORM

From my experience, + clause

Also: "In my experience, ..." / "From what I've seen, ..."

PRONUNCIATION

/frɒm maɪ ɪkˈspɪəriəns/ - "From my" often connects: "fruh-my"

Connect "from" and "my": "frum-my experience" flows better than "from... my... experience"
"From my experience, students actually learn better when the teacher is not a native speaker."
"From my experience working with international teams, accent is never the real communication problem."
NOW YOU TRY

From your experience as a teacher, what helps students improve their speaking most?

Language Focus B: More Ways to Share Your View

Two more expressions - plus a pronunciation focus that will help you sound more natural.

"I tend to think..."

+
MEANING

Your general view - not absolute, but your usual opinion. It shows you've thought about this before. It's flexible and intelligent-sounding.

FORM

I tend to think + (that) + clause

Similar: "I generally think..." / "My feeling is that..."

PRONUNCIATION

/aɪ tend tə θɪŋk/ - "tend to" becomes "tend-tuh" - very quick!

The "to" is weak - just "tuh". Say: "I TEND-tuh think" - keep it flowing!
"I tend to think that communication is more important than perfect pronunciation."
"I tend to think children are better at learning accents than adults."
NOW YOU TRY

What do you tend to think about non-native English teachers? Are they as good as native speakers?

"It seems to me that..."

+
MEANING

Your observation or impression. You're sharing how something looks from your perspective. It's humble - you're not claiming to know everything!

FORM

It seems to me (that) + clause

Also: "It looks to me like..." / "The way I see it, ..."

PRONUNCIATION

/ɪt siːmz tə miː ðæt/ - "seems to me" flows together: "seems-tuh-me"

Connect everything: "It-SEEMS-tuh-me-that" - one flowing phrase, not six separate words!
"It seems to me that people worry too much about having a perfect accent."
"It seems to me that we judge accents based on stereotypes, not reality."
NOW YOU TRY

It seems to me that... (Complete this sentence about accents, teaching, or communication)

Pronunciation Focus: Connected Speech

The secret to sounding natural: Native speakers don't pronounce every word separately. They connect words together. Today's expressions all use this!
Weak "to" "to" becomes "tuh"
tend to = "tend-tuh"
seems to = "seems-tuh"
Connected words "I'd say" = one sound
"From my" = "frum-my"
"It seems to me" = flows together

Practice all 5 expressions out loud! Focus on connecting the words and keeping "to" weak. Which one feels most natural to you?
