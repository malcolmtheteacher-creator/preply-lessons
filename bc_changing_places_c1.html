Changing Places

C1 Level · Travel, Holidays & the Environment Focus

How Things Are Changing

Trends and changes are happening all around the world. In this lesson, you'll learn how to describe these changes and talk about places you know.

Look at These Global Trends:

• The rate of obesity is increasing worldwide.

• The influence of religion is collapsing.

• The quality of workmanship in the building trade is rapidly deteriorating.

• Crime is steadily decreasing in most countries.

• Technology and medical care are improving continually.

• People's standard of living is falling year in, year out.

• Housing prices never stop rising.

• The number of fatalities is levelling off across the globe.

Think About These Questions:

Which of these trends have you noticed in your own country?

Are there places where you've seen dramatic changes?

What changes worry you the most? What changes are positive?

Comparative Structures: Comparing Then and Now

Use these structures to describe how things have changed in places you know.

Example Topics to Compare:

Mobile Phones

20 years ago: Expensive, few features, status symbol.

Today: Cheap, many features, everyone has one.

Religious Observance

When you were a child: Very important in the community.

Young people today: Less focused on organized religion.

Hairstyles

In the 1990s: More conservative, traditional.

Today: More experimental, diverse.

Attitudes Towards Women

50 years ago: Limited career opportunities.

Today: Much greater equality and freedom.

Online Shopping

10 years ago: Unusual, risky, limited selection.

Today: Normal, trusted, huge variety.

Practice: Complete These Comparisons

1. Modern communication is __________ than it was 20 years ago.

Show Answer
much faster/more instant — Technology has revolutionized how we communicate.

2. The cost of technology has __________ significantly.

Show Answer
decreased/dropped/fallen — Computers and phones are much cheaper now.

3. Environmental awareness today is __________ than in the 1980s.

Show Answer
much greater/far more developed — People now understand climate change and recycling.

Vocabulary for Describing Trends

Trends Going UP:

increase, rise, grow, expand, improve, climb, soar, surge

Trends Going DOWN:

decrease, drop, fall, decline, shrink, plummet, deteriorate, collapse

Trends STAYING the SAME:

level off, stabilize, plateau, remain constant, stay steady

Adverbs for Speed of Change:

steadily, rapidly, gradually, continuously, dramatically, sharply, slowly, progressively

Example Sentences:

"The population in my city has grown dramatically in the last decade."

"Traffic congestion has steadily increased as more cars flood the streets."

"Traditional crafts have gradually declined as young people move to cities."

"The quality of life has improved significantly with better infrastructure."

Practice: Choose the Right Word

The number of tourists visiting our country has __________ by 50% in the last 5 years.

Show Answer
increased, grown, risen, surged — Any of these works! "Surged" sounds more dramatic.

Environmental pollution in the river has __________ since the new factory closed.

Show Answer
decreased, dropped, fallen — These all work. "Dropped" sounds the most informal.

Your Speaking Task: Describe How a Place Has Changed

Prepare to Talk About a Place:

Choose a place you know well — your hometown, a city you visited, a neighborhood, or a country. Think about how it has changed over time.

Ideas: Your childhood neighborhood, a city you've lived in, a holiday destination you return to, your country as a whole.

Planning Guide (Make Notes Only):

Make brief notes — don't write full sentences!

  • What was the place like in the past?
  • What is it like now?
  • What specific changes have you noticed? (buildings, people, atmosphere, economy, culture)
  • Do you think these changes are positive or negative?

Speaking Time:

Time to describe changes: 10:00

Language Checklist:

When you speak, try to use:

□ At least 3 trend words (increase, decline, improve, etc.)

□ At least 1 adverb to describe speed (dramatically, gradually, steadily, etc.)

□ At least 2 comparative structures (more/less than, different from)

□ Specific examples or details

After You Speak:

If speaking with another student: Listen to their description and ask follow-up questions like:

  • "What do you think caused that change?"
  • "Do you think it will continue to change?"
  • "Was that change surprising to you?"

Reflect on What You've Learned

What place did you choose to describe, and why?

Which trend words or comparatives did you find most useful?

What made it easy or difficult to describe the changes?

Did your classmate mention any interesting changes you didn't expect to hear about?
