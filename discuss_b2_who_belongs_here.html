The Book That Named an Island

"Our Island Story"
H.E. Marshall, 1905 — a children's history of Britain
"The story of a small island that was built by everyone who arrived."
SPEAK: She called it "our" island. But the history she told was the story of wave after wave of outsiders arriving. Look at the pattern:
43 AD
Romans — roads, baths, wine, law. Left after 350 years. Britain kept everything.
400s
Anglo-Saxons — brought the English language itself. "English" = "Angle-ish."
800s
Vikings — gave us "sky", "egg", "window", "husband." Came as raiders, stayed as neighbours.
1066
Normans — French became the court language. Anglo-Saxon "pig" on the farm → French "pork" on the plate.
DISCUSS: What's the pattern here? What happened every time?
Marshall called it "our" island — but whose story was she actually telling? The story of outsiders who became insiders.

Build the Argument

Example — How to connect a fact to the big question

Fact: The Vikings gave English the words "sky", "egg", and "window."

Connection: "So the language people speak every day — the most basic words — came from people who were originally seen as invaders. That suggests belonging isn't about being first. It's about what you leave behind."

SPEAK — together: Take the Norman fact — French became the court language, and we got words like "pork" and "beef" from French. What does this tell us about belonging? Build on each other's ideas.
