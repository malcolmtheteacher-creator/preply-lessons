Never Misspell It Again

B2 Level · 10 essential IELTS words · one trick for each

Why does spelling matter in IELTS?

In IELTS Listening and Reading, a correct answer with a spelling mistake is marked wrong. One letter can cost you a band score.

The same 200 or so words appear again and again — and the same ones get misspelled again and again.

What do they mean?

Before learning to spell a word, it helps to know it well. Click each card to see the definition and an example sentence.

necessary Needed; cannot be avoided.
"Sleep is necessary for good health."
accommodation A place to live or stay.
"Student accommodation is expensive in London."
government The group of people who run a country.
"The government introduced new housing policies."
environment The natural world; or the surroundings.
"Pollution is damaging the environment."
independent Not controlled by others; self-reliant.
"She became financially independent at 25."
privilege A special advantage not everyone has.
"Access to good education is a privilege."
guarantee A firm promise; to promise something will happen.
"Hard work does not guarantee success."
achievement Something accomplished through effort.
"Passing IELTS is a real achievement."
colleague A person you work with.
"I discussed the report with my colleagues."
questionnaire A set of written questions used to gather information.
"Participants completed an online questionnaire."

One trick for each word

A mnemonic (neh-MON-ik) is a memory trick — a phrase, image, or pattern that makes a spelling stick. The stranger the trick, the better it works.

necessary
One C, two Ss — the most common confusion.
A shirt has one Collar and two Sleeves — and so does neCeSSary.
accommodation
Double C and double M — both get dropped.
A good hotel has two Comfortable beds and two Massive pillows — aCCoMModation.
government
People drop the N before -ment. It's always there.
The goverNment will Never lose its N — it governs the N.
environment
Same trap — the N before -ment gets forgotten.
The Nature in the enviroNment will never disappear.
independent
Ends in -ENT, not -ANT. This one catches everyone.
To be independENT, you must ENTer the world on your own terms.
privilege
No D — people write "priviledge" but there is no D.
It's a privileGE, not a privileDGE — only a GEnius gets the privilege.
guarantee
Ends in -TEE. The vowels in the middle (ua) also trip people up.
I guaran-TEE there's a TEE shot at the end — just like in golf.
achievement
I before E — people reverse it to "ei".
ACHieve: Always Come Home — I before E.
colleague
Ends in -LEAGUE. The -ea- in the middle is silent and confusing.
My colLEAGUE plays in the LEAGUE — the league is hiding inside colleague.
questionnaire
Double N in the middle — almost everyone writes one N.
A questioNNaire asks oNe questioN, theN aNother — two Ns, two questions.
