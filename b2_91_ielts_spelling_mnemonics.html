Why does spelling matter in IELTS?

In IELTS Listening and Reading, a correct answer with a spelling mistake is marked wrong. One letter can cost you a band score. The Cambridge Learner Corpus — a database of millions of IELTS scripts — tells us that the same 30 or so words cause the same mistakes, again and again.

What do they mean?

Before learning to spell a word, know it well. Click each card to flip it and see the definition and an example sentence.

🔴 Double Trouble
accommodation A place to live or stay.
"Finding affordable accommodation in the city is difficult."
occurrence An event or incident; the fact of something happening.
"Such an occurrence is extremely rare."
embarrassment A feeling of awkward self-consciousness or shame.
"Being late for an interview is a source of embarrassment."
committee A group of people appointed to make decisions about something.
"The committee will meet on Thursday to discuss the proposal."
successful Having achieved an aim or goal.
"The project was extremely successful."
beginning The starting point of something.
"The beginning of a new chapter is always exciting."
immediately At once; without any delay.
"Please contact us immediately if you have concerns."
apparently As it seems from what is known; seemingly.
"Apparently, the new policy will be announced tomorrow."
recommend To suggest as being suitable or good.
"I would recommend visiting the national museum."
opportunity A time or situation that makes something possible.
"This is a wonderful opportunity to improve your English."
🟡 Silent Dropout
government The group of people who control and run a country.
"The government introduced new housing policies."
environment The natural world; the conditions surrounding something.
"We must protect the environment for future generations."
maintenance The process of keeping something in good condition.
"Regular maintenance of equipment prevents breakdowns."
knowledge Facts, information, and understanding acquired through experience.
"A good education provides both skills and knowledge."
foreign Relating to a country other than one's own.
"She spent a year studying in a foreign country."
🟢 Wrong Ending
independent Free from outside control; self-reliant.
"She became financially independent after university."
different Not the same; unlike in nature or quality.
"People learn in different ways."
definitely Without doubt; certainly.
"I will definitely visit Japan one day."
privilege A special right or advantage not available to everyone.
"Access to education is a privilege not everyone has."
questionnaire A written set of questions used to collect information.
"Participants completed an online questionnaire about their habits."
🔵 Vowel Swap
believe To accept something as true; to have faith in.
"Many people believe the world is getting warmer."
receive To be given or presented with something.
"Did you receive my email about the meeting?"
achievement Something accomplished through effort or skill.
"Finishing the marathon was her greatest achievement."
society People living together in an organised community.
"Modern society faces many environmental challenges."
separate To divide or move apart; existing as a distinct thing.
"Oil and water are difficult to separate."
pronunciation The way in which a word or language is spoken.
"Clear pronunciation is essential for IELTS Speaking."
🟣 Wildcard
rhythm A strong, regular, repeated pattern of sounds or movement.
"The rhythm of the music made everyone want to dance."
colleague A person you work with professionally.
"I had lunch with a colleague from the marketing team."
necessary Needed; impossible to avoid.
"A good night's sleep is necessary for concentration."
guarantee A firm promise that something will happen or be done.
"Hard work does not guarantee success."

One trick for each word

A mnemonic (neh-MON-ik) is a memory device — a phrase, image, or pattern that locks a spelling into your brain. The stranger the trick, the better it works.

🔴 Double Trouble
accommodation
Double C and double M — people always drop one of each.
A good hotel has two Comfortable beds and two Massive pillows — aCCoMModation.
occurrence
Two Cs, two Rs — and it ends in -ENCE, not -ANCE.
It oCCUrred twice — that's why there are two Cs and two Rs. And ENCE is at the end, not ANCE.
embarrassment
Two Rs and two Ss — the most commonly dropped letters.
You go Really Red (RR) and feel So Stupid (SS) — emBARRaSSment.
committee
Three double letters: MM, TT, and EE.
Three couples came to the meeting: MMargaret & MMark, TTina & TTom, and the EEvening went long — comMMiTTeE.
successful
Double C and double S — like the noun "success" inside it.
To be sucCESSful, you need sucCESS — and success already has both the CC and SS.
beginning
Double N before -ING — people write only one.
The two Ns are like bookends: N-I-N — they protect the I in the middle of beginNINg.
immediately
Double M right at the start — people write only one.
IMMediately: MEDI means medicine — it happens right now, like emergency medicine — iMMediately.
apparently
Double P at the start — most people write only one.
Two Ps aPPear, apparently — the word even describes itself.
recommend
One C, two Ms — people either double the C or forget to double the M.
I reCOMmend you remember: one C leads into two Ms — reCoMMend.
opportunity
Double P — people write only one.
You need to push through two doors (PP) to reach an oPPortunity.
🟡 Silent Dropout
government
The N before -ment gets dropped. It's always there.
The goverNment will Never drop its N — it governs the N.
environment
Same trap as government — the N before -ment disappears.
The N stands for Nature — and Nature will Never disappear from the enviroNment.
maintenance
People write "maintainance" — but the I from "maintain" disappears in the noun.
Think: MAIN + TEN + ance — ten things need MAIN maintenance. Not "maintain" — that I hides.
knowledge
People drop the D — it becomes "knowlege".
You KNOW the LEDGE you're standing on — knowLEDGE.
foreign
The GN at the end is silent — people drop it or reverse the vowels.
You cannot foreIGNore the silent GN — it's foreIGN, always there even if you can't hear it.
🟢 Wrong Ending
independent
Ends in -ENT, not -ANT. This catches almost everyone.
To be independENT, you must ENTer the world on your own terms.
different
Two Fs — and it ends in -ENT, not -ANT.
It's diFFicult to be diFFerENT — the two Fs remind you of "difficult", and the ending is -ENT.
definitely
People write "definately" — but the word FINITE is hiding inside.
It's deFINITEly certain — because FINITE is right there in the middle.
privilege
No D — people write "priviledge". It ends in -GE, not -DGE.
It's a privileGE — only a GEnius gets one. No D needed.
questionnaire
Double N before -AIRE — almost everyone writes one N.
A questioNNaire asks one question, then another — two Ns, two questions.
🔵 Vowel Swap
believe
I before E — people reverse it to "beleive".
Don't LIE — and don't reverse the letters. belIEve has I before E, just like the word LIE inside it.
receive
E before I after C — people write "recieve" with I before E.
After C, the E is the boss — reC then EIve. The C gives orders: E comes first.
achievement
I before E — people swap to "acheivment".
Always Come Home — ACH — then I before E: achIEvement.
society
I before E in the middle — people write "soceity" or "socity".
In socIEty, I comes before Everyone else — socIEty.
separate
Two As in the word — people write "seperate" with an E in the middle.
There's A RAT in sepARATe — sep-A-RAT-e. That's how you know both As are there.
pronunciation
People write "pronounciation" — copying the verb "pronounce". The O disappears in the noun.
You PRONOUNCE it, but proNUNciation — the noun hides a NUN inside, not a NOUN.
🟣 Wildcard
rhythm
No standard vowels — just R, H, Y, T, H, M. People invent vowels that aren't there.
Rhythm Has Your Two Hips Moving — the first letter of each word spells RHYTHM.
colleague
Ends in -EAGUE — the silent EA and the GUE ending confuse everyone.
My colLEAGUE plays in the LEAGUE — the whole word LEAGUE is hiding at the end of colleague.
necessary
One C, two Ss — the most common confusion.
A shirt has one Collar and two Sleeves — and so does neCeSSary: 1C, 2S.
guarantee
The UA in the middle and the TEE ending both cause errors.
I guaranTEE there's a TEE at the end — just like in golf. And guaran has a U after the G, like a "guard".

Test yourself — no looking back!

Read the clue, recall the trick, and type the word. If you get it wrong, the correct spelling is shown — use the mnemonic to remember it.

🔴 Double Trouble

1. A hotel with two Comfortable beds and two Massive pillows. What word?

2. Double C, double R — this noun means "an instance of something happening". Ends in -ENCE, not -ANCE.

3. You go Really Red (RR) and feel So Stupid (SS). What is this uncomfortable feeling?

4. Three couples at the meeting: MM, TT, EE. What word?

5. Double C after SU-, double S before -FUL. What adjective means "having achieved your goals"?

6. Two Ns protect the I: N-I-N. Where does everything start?

7. Double M, then MEDI — like medicine — right now, no delay.

8. Two Ps aPPear in this adverb. It seems to be true.

9. One C in the prefix, then double M. What verb means "to suggest something to someone"?

10. You push through two doors (PP) to reach this chance.

🟡 Silent Dropout

11. It govERNs the N — don't drop it before -ment.

12. Nature's N is always inside this word — before -ment.

13. MAIN + TEN + ance — not "maintain-ance". The I from "maintain" hides.

14. You KNOW the LEDGE you're standing on.

15. Silent GN after "fore-" — these two letters make no sound at all. What adjective means "from another country"?

🟢 Wrong Ending

16. You ENTer the world alone — ends in -ENT, not -ANT.

17. It's diFFicult — two Fs, ends in -ENT, not -ANT.

18. The word "finite" (meaning limited) hides inside this adverb. Ends in -ITELY, not -ATELY. What adverb means "certainly, without doubt"?

19. Only a GEnius gets it — ends in -GE, not -DGE.

20. One question, then another — two Ns before -AIRE.

🔵 Vowel Swap

21. Don't LIE — I comes before E. What word means "to think something is true"?

22. After C, E comes before I — the C is the boss.

23. Always Come Home — ACH — then I before E. Something you're proud of.

24. I before E — we live in this together.

25. There's A RAT hiding in the middle of this word. It means to divide.

26. You PRONOUNCe it, but the noun hides a NUN, not a NOUN.

🟣 Wildcard

27. Rhythm Has Your Two Hips Moving — no real vowels.

28. The LEAGUE is hiding at the end of this word for a workmate.

29. One Collar, two Sleeves — 1C, 2S. It means "needed".

30. Think of a golf tee — double E at the end, not just one. What verb means "to promise something will happen"?
