How do you communicate?

How do you feel when you speak in public or give a presentation? Confident? Nervous?

The 4 Ps of Voice

The way you use your voice is just as important as what you say. Learn about the 4 Ps:

POWER (= volume)
How loud or quiet you speak.
Example: Speak louder when you want to emphasise an important point.
PAUSE (= silence)
Stopping between ideas gives the listener time to think.
Example: A good pause is powerful.
PACE (= speed)
How fast or slow you speak.
Example: Slow down for important information. Speed up for excitement.
PITCH (= tone)
How high or low your voice sounds.
Example: Change your pitch to show emotion or ask questions.

Practice: Read this sentence 4 different ways

"We need to change our approach."

Try saying it with a different P each time:

With POWER: Say it loudly and firmly.
With a PAUSE: "We need to change... our approach."
With slow PACE: Say it slowly and deliberately.
With rising PITCH: Say it as a question.

Stress and Meaning

Where you put the stress (emphasis) changes the meaning of a sentence. Look at these examples:

"I didn't eat YOUR cake."
= Someone else's cake? That's fine.
"I didn't EAT your cake."
= I just looked at it.
"I DIDN'T eat your cake."
= It wasn't me!
"I didn't eat your CAKE."
= I ate your sandwich instead.

Practice: Click each sentence to reveal which word to stress

"She speaks very quickly."
Stress "quickly" — this shows HOW she speaks.
"The air conditioning is too cold."
Stress "too" — this shows the PROBLEM.
"The presentation was excellent."
Stress "excellent" — this shows YOUR OPINION.
