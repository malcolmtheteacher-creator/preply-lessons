# Speaking Pathway: Prep Pack Templates

## Purpose
Each prep pack prepares students for a Speaking Pathway lesson with:
1. Target vocabulary (8-12 items) - chunks, collocations, topic words
2. Level-appropriate reading (related to lesson topic)
3. Discussion questions (3 questions to prime thinking)

---

## LEVEL SPECIFICATIONS

### A2 (Lessons 1-20)
**Reading:**
- 200-250 words
- Simple sentences (subject-verb-object)
- Max 1 clause per sentence
- High-frequency vocabulary only
- Present simple, past simple, basic future
- Concrete topics, everyday situations
- Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences each)

**Vocabulary:**
- Single words + simple 2-word collocations
- Definitions in simple English
- Example sentences using basic structures

**Example text style:**
"Maria works in a coffee shop. She starts work at 7am every day. She likes her job because she meets many people. Sometimes the work is hard, but Maria always smiles."

---

### B1 (Lessons 21-40)
**Reading:**
- 300-350 words
- Compound sentences (and, but, so, because)
- Some relative clauses (who, which, that)
- Mix of tenses including present perfect
- Semi-abstract topics with concrete examples
- Can include opinions and reasons

**Vocabulary:**
- 2-3 word collocations and chunks
- Some phrasal verbs
- Definitions can use B1 vocabulary

**Example text style:**
"Working from home has become more common since 2020. Many people enjoy the flexibility because they can manage their own time. However, some workers find it difficult to separate their work life from their personal life, which can cause stress."

---

### B2 (Lessons 41-60)
**Reading:**
- 400-450 words
- Complex sentences with multiple clauses
- Passive voice, conditionals, reported speech
- Abstract concepts with analysis
- Multiple perspectives on issues
- Formal and neutral register

**Vocabulary:**
- Academic collocations
- Formal linking phrases
- Hedging language in definitions

**Example text style:**
"The rise of artificial intelligence has prompted considerable debate among economists regarding its potential impact on employment. While some researchers argue that automation will inevitably lead to widespread job displacement, others maintain that technological advancement has historically created more opportunities than it has eliminated."

---

### C1 (Lessons 61-75)
**Reading:**
- 500-550 words
- Sophisticated sentence structures
- Nuanced arguments with caveats
- Idiomatic expressions
- Near-authentic academic/journalistic style
- Implicit meanings, irony acceptable

**Vocabulary:**
- Low-frequency academic vocabulary
- Sophisticated collocations
- Nuanced synonyms and connotations

**Example text style:**
"The notion that economic growth can be decoupled from environmental degradation remains contentious, with proponents pointing to incremental efficiency gains while critics contend that such optimism fundamentally mischaracterises the systemic nature of ecological limits. What is perhaps most striking about this debate is not the divergence of opinion per se, but rather the extent to which both camps appear to be talking past one another."
