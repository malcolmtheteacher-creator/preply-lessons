Four Urban Environmental Problems

Here are four problems most big cities face. Read each one, then decide which is the most serious in the city you're thinking about.

1. Polluted Air

Diesel engines, factories and heating systems push particles into the air that damage people's lungs. In some European capitals, air quality is so poor on certain days that schools are asked to keep children indoors. The problem is often worst in low-income neighbourhoods next to busy roads.

Why is this problem often worse in poorer areas?

Show Answer
Housing near busy roads and industrial sites is usually cheaper, so poorer families end up living closest to the pollution.

2. Traffic & Congestion

Cars dominate most cities. They take up space that could be used for homes, shops or parks, and drivers sit in traffic for hours — time that could be spent with family or on exercise. Electric cars reduce emissions, but they don't solve congestion or the problem of cities being designed around driving.

The text suggests electric cars are a partial solution only. Why?

Show Answer
They cut emissions, but they still take up space, still cause congestion, and still shape cities around driving instead of walking or cycling.

3. Waste & Recycling

A single city of a million people can produce over a thousand tonnes of rubbish every day. A lot of it ends up in landfill, and even what gets "recycled" is sometimes shipped abroad and dumped. Food waste is a major and often invisible part of the problem.

What does the text mean by saying food waste is "invisible"?

Show Answer
People don't notice it — it's thrown out at home or in restaurant kitchens, so most of us never see how much is being wasted.

4. Loss of Green Space

As cities grow outwards — urban sprawl — parks, small farms and wooded areas get turned into housing estates, shopping centres or car parks. This doesn't just hurt wildlife; it raises city temperatures in summer and reduces the mental-health benefits that come from being near nature.

Name two different consequences of losing green space that the text mentions.

Show Answer
Hotter summers in the city (less shade and cooling from trees), and worse mental health (people lose their contact with nature). Loss of wildlife habitat is a third.

Language for Suggesting Solutions

When we talk about what cities should do, we often use passive structures with modal verbs. This keeps the focus on the action, not on who does it. You'll need this for the speaking task in the final tab.

Structure 1: Modal + be + past participle

Diesel cars should be banned from the city centre.

More trees need to be planted along main roads.

Old buildings could be retrofitted with solar panels.

Public transport ought to be subsidised for low-income residents.

Structure 2: could / can be + past participle + by + -ing

Air quality could be improved by pedestrianising the main shopping streets.

Congestion can be reduced by investing in bike lanes and better buses.

Food waste could be cut by encouraging restaurants to donate unsold meals.

Structure 3: Softer proposals

It would make sense to ban plastic bags.

One option would be to introduce a congestion charge.

A good first step would be to plant more trees in school playgrounds.

Practice

Rewrite using a passive modal: "The government should pay for insulation in old homes."

Show Answer
Insulation should be paid for in old homes. / Old homes should be insulated (at government expense).

Rewrite using "by + -ing": "Planting more trees will improve air quality."

Show Answer
Air quality could be improved by planting more trees.

Which sounds more tentative — "Diesel cars must be banned" or "It would make sense to ban diesel cars"?

Show Answer
"It would make sense to..." — it's a softer, less confrontational way to open a suggestion. "Must be banned" is very strong and direct.

The Pitch

Now deliver the pitch out loud, as if the mayor is listening. Aim for 2 to 3 minutes. Start with the problem, move through your three solutions, and finish with the one thing you'd do first if you had the power.

Useful openers:

"The most urgent issue facing our city is..."

"There are three things I believe need to be done..."

"Of the three, the most realistic starting point would be..."

"Some people argue that... but in my view..."
