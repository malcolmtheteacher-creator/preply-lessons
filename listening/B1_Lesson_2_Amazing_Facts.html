📚 Vocabulary A: Core Words
1. Bacteria
Tiny living things that live everywhere, including in and on your body. Most bacteria are harmless and actually help your body work properly. Some bacteria can cause disease, but many are essential for your health.
NOW YOU TRY: "There are trillions of bacteria living in your gut. They help you..."
2. Cells
The smallest living parts of all living things. Your body is made of billions of cells that work together. Different types of cells do different jobs - for example, nerve cells send messages, and muscle cells help you move.
NOW YOU TRY: "Red blood cells carry oxygen through your body. White blood cells..."
3. Digest
To break down food in your body so your body can use the nutrients. Your stomach and intestines work together to digest food into smaller pieces that your body can absorb and use for energy.
NOW YOU TRY: "It takes about 24-30 hours for your body to digest a full meal. Your..."
4. Atoms
The smallest pieces of matter. Everything in the universe is made of atoms - you, your clothes, your phone, the air around you. Atoms are so small you cannot see them, but they combine to make everything you can see.
NOW YOU TRY: "Atoms are mostly empty space. The rest is made of..."
5. Harmless
Not dangerous and won't hurt you. If something is harmless, you don't need to worry about it or be afraid of it. Many things that seem scary are actually completely harmless.

🎉 Wrap-Up & Summary
What You've Learned Today
Your body is home to trillions of cells that work together to keep you alive and healthy.
90% of the cells in your body are actually bacteria, not human cells - but most are harmless and helpful!
You have about 37 trillion cells in your body, making you incredibly complex on the cellular level.
Atoms are the building blocks of everything, and they're mostly empty space with just energy in the center.
Microscopic dust mites may live in your eyelashes, but they're completely harmless to you.
