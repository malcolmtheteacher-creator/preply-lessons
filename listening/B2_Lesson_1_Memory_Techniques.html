How to Improve Your Memory

Radio Interview with Charles Long about Memorisation Techniques

Let's Get Started!

Have you ever wished you could remember things more easily? Maybe you forget people's names, or struggle to memorise information for exams?

In this lesson, you'll learn about effective memory techniques from Charles Long, a memorisation expert. These proven strategies can help you improve your memory and learn more efficiently.

Before you listen: Think about a time when you had to remember something difficult. What strategies did you use?

Key Topic Overview

Charles Long discusses four main memory techniques:

1. Word Association

Connect new words or information to familiar words, such as your pet's name or a place you know well.

2. Visualisation

Create mental pictures in your mind to help you remember information. The more vivid and unusual, the better!

3. Singing

Put information you want to remember into a song or rhythm. Music helps your brain retain information.

4. Teaching

Explain what you've learned to someone else. Teaching forces your brain to organise and understand the material better.

Listening Task: As you listen to the interview, identify which technique Charles Long considers most effective and why.

Vocabulary Set A (Click to Reveal)

Learn these key words before listening to the interview.

Memorisation
the process of learning something by heart; committing information to memory so you can recall it later without looking at notes
Association
connecting one thing to another in your mind; creating links between new information and things you already know
Visualise
to make a picture in your mind; to create a mental image of something without seeing it in reality
Reinforce
to make something stronger; to repeat or emphasise something to help it stick in your memory
Recall
to remember something; to bring information back from your memory when you need it

Lesson Summary

Here's what we covered in this lesson on memory techniques:

Key Learning Points

  • Word Association: Connect new information to familiar words or personal connections to help retention
  • Visualisation: Create vivid, unusual mental images to make information more memorable
  • Singing: Use music and rhythm to help your brain store information more effectively
  • Teaching: Explain what you've learned to others to deepen your understanding and memory

Vocabulary Review

  • Memorisation - the process of learning by heart
  • Association - connecting things in your mind
  • Visualise - creating mental images
  • Reinforce - making something stronger through repetition
  • Recall - remembering something when needed

Next Steps

To consolidate your learning:

  • Practice using one memory technique every day this week
  • Apply these techniques to learning new English vocabulary
  • Try teaching a friend or classmate one of these techniques
  • Keep a journal of what works best for your memory
