Topic Introduction: Memory and Learning

Have you ever wished you could remember things more easily? Maybe you forget people's names, or struggle to memorise information for exams? The good news is that your memory is a skill you can improve!

In this lesson, you will listen to a radio interview with Charles Long, a memorisation expert. He will share four proven techniques that can help you remember information more effectively. These strategies work for everyone, from students studying for exams to professionals learning new skills.

Did You Know?

Our brains can create new neural pathways at any age. This means you can always improve your memory and learning ability, no matter how old you are. Your memory is like a muscle - the more you exercise it, the stronger it becomes!

Did You Know?

The average person forgets 40-50% of new information within just one hour if they don't actively use memory techniques. But using proven strategies like the ones you'll learn today can increase retention by up to 90%!

Did You Know?

Music helps memory! Studies show that information set to music is remembered better and longer than information presented without music. This is why many people remember song lyrics more easily than telephone numbers.

Vocabulary: Click to Reveal

Click on each word to see its definition and example in context. These words appeared in the interview you just listened to.

Memorisation
The process of learning something by heart; committing information to memory so you can recall it later without looking at notes. Example: "Effective memorisation requires practice and the use of proven techniques rather than just reading the material repeatedly."
Association
Connecting one thing to another in your mind; creating links between new information and things you already know. Example: "By using word association with your pet's name, you create a personal connection that makes information easier to remember."
Visualise
To make a picture in your mind; to create a mental image of something without seeing it in reality. Example: "If you visualise a strange, colourful image of the information you need to learn, your brain will remember it more easily."
Reinforce
To make something stronger; to repeat or emphasise something to help it stick in your memory. Example: "The more you reinforce information by reviewing it in different ways, the stronger your memory of it becomes."
Recall
To remember something; to bring information back from your memory when you need it. Example: "During an exam, you'll need to recall everything you've learned, so it's important to use strong memory techniques."
Technique
A specific method or way of doing something, especially a skill-based approach. Example: "Charles Long shared several memorisation techniques that students can use to improve their learning."
Retain
To keep something in your memory or possession; to hold onto information. Example: "Using music helps your brain retain information better than passive reading."

Lesson Summary

Here's what we covered in this lesson on memory techniques:

The Four Memory Techniques

  • Word Association: Connect new information to familiar words or personal connections (like your pet's name or a place you know) to help retention
  • Visualisation: Create vivid, unusual mental images to make information more memorable - the stranger and more colourful, the better!
  • Singing: Use music and rhythm to help your brain store information more effectively - songs are naturally easier to remember
  • Teaching: Explain what you've learned to others to deepen your understanding and strengthen your memory of the material

Key Vocabulary Review

  • Memorisation - the process of learning by heart
  • Association - connecting things in your mind
  • Visualise - creating mental images
  • Reinforce - making something stronger through repetition
  • Recall - remembering something when needed
  • Technique - a specific method or skill-based approach
  • Retain - to keep information in your memory

Next Steps: Practice These Techniques

To consolidate your learning and see real improvement:

  • Choose ONE memory technique and practice using it every day this week
  • Apply these techniques specifically to learning new English vocabulary
  • Try teaching a friend or classmate one of these techniques - explain it in your own words
  • Keep a learning journal and note which technique works best for different types of information
  • Experiment with combining techniques (e.g., visualisation + association)
