Vocabulary Set A

phenomenon
A thing, fact, or situation that is observed to exist or happen, especially something remarkable or unusual that can be studied scientifically.
validation
Proof or confirmation that something is true, correct, or acceptable; the action of checking or proving the accuracy of information.
hoax
A trick or deceptive scheme that fools people into believing something false; deliberate misinformation spread to deceive.
tampered with
Changed, interfered with, or altered secretly or maliciously; to have deliberately damaged or modified something.
take at face value
To accept something as true without questioning it or looking deeper; to believe the surface appearance without examining the evidence.

Key Takeaways & Summary

Main Concepts Learned

Circular reporting is a self-reinforcing phenomenon where news sources cite each other as validation, creating an illusion of credibility without accessing original evidence.
Misinformation can originate from various sources—deliberate hoaxes, misunderstandings, or unauthorized alterations of data—and spreads through networks rapidly due to confirmation bias and algorithmic amplification.
Verification strategies require identifying primary sources, understanding the author's expertise and potential bias, and cross-referencing claims across multiple independent publications.
Famous cases like the Brazilian aardvark and vaccine-autism controversy illustrate how false information can gain credibility through repetition and citation in respected institutions.

Critical Thinking Skills Developed

Analyzing the structure and flow of information through networks
Distinguishing between primary sources and secondary reporting
Evaluating credibility based on evidence rather than authority alone
Recognizing how cognitive biases influence belief formation
Developing independent verification strategies
