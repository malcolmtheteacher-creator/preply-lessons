👅 English /r/ vs Rolled R

A2 • Italian/Spanish/French/Portuguese/Russian/Polish • Learn the English approximant R

🔍 Let's notice the R problem

Listen to these common mistakes:

❌ "rrrred" with trilled R (like Spanish)"

Should be: "red" (smooth, no trill)

❌ Guttural R (from back of throat)"

Should be: "room" (curled tongue, no vibration)

Why is English /r/ so different from your language's R sound?

🎯 The Problem:

Most languages have a TRILLED or ROLLED R (tongue vibrates against the roof of the mouth).

But English uses an APPROXIMANT /r/: the tongue curls back WITHOUT making contact. NO vibration. NO trill.

🔹 Italian/Spanish: Trilled R (tongue vibrates: "rrr")

🔹 French/Portuguese: Guttural R (back of throat)

🔹 English: Approximant R (tongue curled back, lazy, NO contact)

🛠️ The English /r/ sound explained

✅ ENGLISH /r/ (Correct)

👅
Tongue position: Curled back, NO contact with roof of mouth
Sound quality: Smooth, lazy, continuous (like a sigh)
Examples:
red, room, very, car, three

❌ NOT ENGLISH (Wrong)

👅💨
Trilled R: Tongue vibrates against ridge (Italian/Spanish)
Guttural R: Back of throat (French/Portuguese)
DON'T do:
"rrrrred", "rrrroom", "grrrr"
💡 The key difference:

English /r/ = APPROXIMANT (not a trill, not guttural)

🔹 Tongue CURLS BACK (like you're almost saying a vowel)

🔹 NO tongue vibration (not a trill)

🔹 NO back-of-throat sound

Think: "lazy R" — your tongue just hangs there in a curve.

📍 English /r/ in different positions:

At the START (initial /r/):

🔵 red → RED (curled tongue, smooth sound)

🔵 room → ROOM (no vibration, smooth)

🔵 run → RUN (curled tongue at start)

In the MIDDLE (intervocalic /r/):

🔵 very → VER-ee (smooth /r/ between vowels)

🔵 car → KAR (curled back at end)

🔵 three → THREE (no trill, smooth)

At the END (final /r/):

🔵 car → KAR (lazy /r/ at end)

🔵 door → DOR (curled tongue, no vibration)

🔵 better → BET-ter (smooth /r/)

🚫 Common mistakes to AVOID

❌ rrrred (trilling the R like Spanish)

❌ Guttural R from throat (like French)

❌ Making /r/ sound like a vibration or multiple sounds

❌ Not curling your tongue (makes it sound like /w/ instead)
