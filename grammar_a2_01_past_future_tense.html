Before you check: what two ingredients appear in every bold phrase?

Check the pattern CLICK
was / were + the -ing form (making, watching, raining). It shows the action was still going on at that moment — not finished, not a single quick event.
was / were + verb-ing

The Big Interruption: "...when..."

For a story where something short and sudden interrupts a longer action.

Spot the pattern

  • "I was cooking dinner when the phone rang."
  • "She was walking home when it started to rain."
  • "We were watching the film when the power went out."

Two different verb forms sit on either side of "when". Say what they are before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
The long, in-progress action gets was/were + -ing (was cooking). The short, sudden action that interrupts it gets the past simple (rang). They're joined by when.
was / were + verb-ing + when + past simple

From two ideas to one story

"I was doing the dishes. My phone buzzed."
→
"I was doing the dishes when my phone buzzed."

The background action ("doing the dishes") stays continuous; the short interruption ("buzzed") stays simple past.

Two Things at Once: "While..."

For two long actions happening at the same time in the past.

Spot the pattern

  • "While I was cooking, my sister was laying the table."
  • "While the kids were playing outside, we were tidying the kitchen."
  • "The radio was playing while everyone was chatting in the kitchen."

Say the pattern before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
while + subject + was/were + -ing, subject + was/were + -ing. Both halves are continuous because both actions were going on at the same time — either half can come first.
while + subject + was/were + verb-ing, subject + was/were + verb-ing

From two separate moments to one

"I made lunch. My brother tidied his room."
→
"While I was making lunch, my brother was tidying his room."

Both actions were happening in parallel, so both become continuous once "while" joins them.
