## Challenging someone's ideas [BC, B2]
- Have you considered the fact that…?
- I take your point, but…
- I see where you're coming from, but…
- That's true.
- So how exactly do you see this working?
- I'm very aware of the challenges here, but I'm convinced…
- I'm not so sure.
- You have to look at it from both sides.
- Why don't we try it for a couple of weeks and see?

## Dealing with a problem / confessing a mistake [BC, B2]
- I've got a bit of a problem.
- Can we talk in private?
- What's the matter? / What's wrong?
- I've made a mistake. A big mistake.
- It was such a silly thing to do.
- I'm sure we can work it out.
- Don't worry, these things happen.
- There's a procedure to deal with this.
- Thanks for letting me know.
- I feel so much better now I've told you.

## Discussing advantages and disadvantages [BC, B2]
- On the one hand… but on the other hand…
- I see what you mean, but I'm just not sure…
- There could be an issue with…
- There are lots of advantages to…
- I can also see the disadvantages.
- OK, I'll think about it.

## Giving advice / receiving advice [BC, B2]
- What do you think I should do?
- If I were you, I'd…
- I'm not sure that's a good idea.
- Perhaps you could try…?
- Why don't you try…?
- I'm not too sure about that.

## Persuading someone / asking a favour [BC, B2]
- Have you got a minute?
- I've got a small favour to ask you.
- How would you feel about…?
- I think you'd be brilliant at it.
- I wouldn't usually ask, but…
- Why don't you give it a go?
- There's nothing to lose!

## Refusing offers [EAH, CE, TR]
- No, thanks. / No, thank you.
- I'm fine, thank you. / I'm okay, thanks.
- It's very kind of you, but…
- I appreciate the offer, but…
- It's very tempting, but…
- I really shouldn't.
- I can't this time.
- Not today, thanks.
- No, but thanks for asking.
- Thanks for asking, but I'm afraid I'm busy.
- I'm afraid I can't come, but thank you for the invitation.
- Actually, I think I'm going to pass on it, if you don't mind.
- Let me sleep on it.
- That's very kind. Unfortunately, [reason].
- I'd like to, but [reason].

## Accepting offers [CE, TR]
- Yes, please. / Sure, thanks. / Sounds great.
- That would be nice. / That's very kind of you, thank you.
- Yes, I'd be delighted to. / Yes, I'd love some.

## Making offers [TR]
- Would you like…? / May I offer you…? / Would you be interested in…?
- Can I get you…?
- Do you want…? / How about…? / Are you up for some…?

## Requests (softened) [BC grammar reference, via search]
- Would you mind [-ing]…?
- Do you think you could do me a favour?
- Could you tell me…, please?
- Softened refusals: "Well, I'm not sure." / "Tomorrow night's a bit difficult."

## Agreeing and disagreeing [BC, B1]
- I'm not so sure.
- I think I disagree.
- Don't get me wrong, I like it, but…
- I see what you mean, but…
- True. / That's true.
- How about taking ___ away? / How about ___?
- Maybe you've got a point there.
- I think you're right.
- Yes, definitely.

## Asking a favour / refusing one [BC, B1]
- I'm really sorry about this, but…
- Would you be able to…?
- Is there any chance you could…?
- I'm not really sure if I can.
- I would if I could, but I can't.
- It's been planned for ages.
- I'll make it up to you.
- Name your price.

## Responding to news [BC, B1 — full recap list]
- Oh wow! Congratulations!
- I can't believe it.
- That's wonderful! / Well done. I'm really pleased for you.
- Oh dear. What's happened?
- Oh no, that's awful.
- I'm so sorry. / What a shame.
- Can I do anything to help?
