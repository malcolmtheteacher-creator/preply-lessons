Edexcel A-level English Language · Paper 1 · C1/C2

Language Variation Decoded

Six lessons for the paper about how English varies — from person to person and from century to century — and how to write the two comparative essays that prove you can read the variation.

How this series works. Paper 1 (9EN0/01) gives you 2 hours 15 minutes, four texts, and two 30-mark essays: one comparing two modern voices on identity, one comparing an old text with a new one on change over time. These six lessons build the whole skill set in order: the comparison habit, the identity concepts, written texts, spoken texts, language change, and finally the essays themselves — with model answers at every step. Work in sequence; each lesson tests what came before.

Lesson 1

Two Texts, One Essay

The exam's shape, the four mark strands, and the habit that protects a quarter of every mark: both texts in every paragraph, joined by hinges.

I can: write a sentence that analyses two texts at once.
Lesson 2

The Identity Toolkit

Idiolect, sociolect, dialect, accent, register, mode, accommodation — plus the concept that wins essays: covert prestige, the status of staying loyal to your roots.

I can: weld the seven concepts to real quotes.
Lesson 3

Reading the Writer

How personality gets onto a silent page: graphology, register collisions, minor sentences, allusions as demographic data — and the two-step from feature to person.

I can: reverse-engineer a written voice.
Lesson 4

Hearing the Speaker

Transcripts read fluently: spontaneity features, accent in /slashes/, dialect as identity — and the three questions that turn any interview into analysable performance.

I can: analyse spoken identity, never calling it error.
Lesson 5

English Through Time

An 18th-century advice text beside a modern talk: informalisation, sentence slimming, the authority migration — and the continuities weaker answers always miss.

I can: read old texts calmly and argue change plus continuity.
Lesson 6

The Two Thirty-Mark Essays

Annotating a pair, planning by angle, the five-part comparative paragraph, weak-versus-strong answers, and a timed flight on fresh texts with a full feature key.

I can: write both essays inside 2 hours 15.
