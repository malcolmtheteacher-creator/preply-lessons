IELTS Speaking Grammar Toolkit

Master the 6 Essential Structures for Higher Band Scores

Target: Band 7-8+

Why Grammar Matters in IELTS Speaking

Grammatical Range and Accuracy is worth 25% of your Speaking score. To achieve Band 7+, you must demonstrate:

  • A variety of complex structures used with flexibility
  • Frequent error-free sentences
  • Good control of grammar
Band 6 Answer

"I like my city because it has many parks. The weather is nice. I can do many activities there."

Simple sentences only, limited structure variety

Band 8 Answer

"What I love most about my city is the abundance of green spaces, which allow residents to escape the urban hustle. If I had to choose one thing, I'd say the parks are what make it truly liveable."

Cleft sentence + relative clause + conditional

The 6 Essential Structures

1. Cleft Sentences

Add emphasis to your answers: "What I really enjoy is..." "The thing that matters most is..."

2. Relative Clauses

Add information naturally: "...which is something I've always enjoyed" "...where I spent my childhood"

3. Conditionals

Discuss hypotheticals: "If I had the chance..." "If I were to choose..."

4. Participle Clauses

Sound sophisticated: "Having lived abroad..." "Being someone who..."

5. Wish Structures

Express regret and desires: "I wish I had..." "If only I could..."

6. Combined Practice

Put it all together with timed speaking challenges and real IELTS questions.

Pro Tip: Quality Over Quantity

You don't need to use ALL structures in every answer. Using 2-3 complex structures naturally is better than forcing many structures awkwardly. The examiner notices when grammar sounds rehearsed!

Cleft Sentences - Adding Emphasis

Cleft sentences split information to emphasize a specific part. They're perfect for IELTS Speaking because they help you give longer, more sophisticated answers while highlighting what's important to you.

Structure 1: What-Clefts Most Useful

What + subject + verb + is/was + emphasized information
Compare:
Simple: I really enjoy the peaceful atmosphere.
Cleft: What I really enjoy is the peaceful atmosphere.

What I find interesting is...
"What I find interesting about my job is the variety of challenges I face daily."

What makes it special is...
"What makes my hometown special is the sense of community everyone shares."

What I appreciate most is...
"What I appreciate most about my friends is their unwavering support."

Structure 2: The thing/reason/place/person patterns

The thing/reason/place/person + (that/who/where/why) + clause + is/was...
Examples by type:
The thing that I enjoy most about weekends is having time to read.
The reason why I chose this career is my passion for helping people.
The place where I feel most relaxed is my grandmother's garden.
The person who influenced me most was my high school teacher.

Structure 3: It-Clefts For Strong Emphasis

It is/was + emphasized element + that/who + rest of sentence
Compare:
Simple: My mother taught me the value of hard work.
Cleft: It was my mother who taught me the value of hard work.
More It-Cleft Examples:
It's the atmosphere that makes this cafe my favourite place to study.
It was during university that I discovered my passion for design.
It's only when I travel that I truly appreciate my home country.

NOW YOU TRY: Transform these sentences

Simple: I love the friendly people in my city.
Simple: I chose this career because I want to help people.

Speaking Tip: Use cleft sentences to buy thinking time!

Starting with "What I think is..." or "The thing about this is..." gives you a moment to organize your thoughts while sounding sophisticated.

Relative Clauses - Adding Information Naturally

Relative clauses let you combine information smoothly instead of using choppy short sentences. They're essential for Band 7+ and show you can handle complex sentence structures.

Defining Relative Clauses Essential Information

Noun + who/which/that/where/when + essential information
The clause identifies WHICH one we're talking about:
The teacher who inspired me most was Mr. Chen.
The book that changed my perspective was "Sapiens".
The city where I grew up has changed dramatically.
The moment when I realized my passion was during my first art class.

Non-Defining Relative Clauses Extra Information

Noun, + who/which + extra information, + rest of sentence
The clause adds EXTRA (non-essential) information. Use commas!
My hometown, which is in the south of the country, is famous for its beaches.
My best friend, who I've known since childhood, now lives abroad.
Tokyo, where I spent a year studying, remains my favourite city.

Important: Never use "that" in non-defining clauses!

WRONG: My mother, that is a doctor, works long hours.
CORRECT: My mother, who is a doctor, works long hours.

Advanced Relative Patterns Band 8+

"Which" referring to a whole clause:
I passed my driving test on the first attempt, which surprised everyone.
She decided to quit her job, which I thought was very brave.
Relative clauses with prepositions:
The company for which I work is expanding globally. (formal)
That's the friend with whom I traveled to Japan. (formal)
The project I've been working on is nearly complete. (informal - preposition at end)
"Whose" for possession:
I have a colleague whose expertise in marketing has been invaluable.
The artist whose work I admire most is Frida Kahlo.

PRACTICE: Combine these sentences using relative clauses

1. My grandfather taught me to cook. He was a chef.
2. I visited Barcelona last summer. It was an amazing experience.

Conditionals - Hypotheticals and Speculation

Conditionals are essential for Part 3 of the Speaking test, where you discuss abstract ideas and hypothetical situations. They show you can handle complex time relationships.

Second Conditional Hypothetical Present/Future

If + past simple, ... would/could/might + infinitive
For imaginary or unlikely situations NOW or in the FUTURE:
If I had more free time, I would learn to play the piano.
If I could live anywhere, I would choose somewhere near the sea.
If I were the government, I would invest more in education.

Note: "If I were" (not "If I was")

For Band 7+, use "were" for all persons in second conditionals. "If I were you...", "If she were here..."

Third Conditional Hypothetical Past

If + past perfect (had + past participle), ... would have + past participle
For imagining how the PAST could have been different:
If I had studied harder, I would have gotten into a better university.
If I hadn't taken that gap year, I wouldn't have discovered my passion for photography.
Things might have been different if I had listened to my parents' advice.

Mixed Conditionals Band 8+ Show-off

Past condition - Present result:
If + past perfect, ... would + infinitive (present)
If I had learned Chinese as a child, I would be fluent now.
If she hadn't moved abroad, she would still be working at that company.
Present condition - Past result:
If + past simple (present meaning), ... would have + past participle
If I were more outgoing, I would have made more friends at university.
If I weren't so busy, I would have attended the conference.

PRACTICE: Complete with the correct conditional form

IELTS Question: "If you could change one thing about your education, what would it be?"
"If I _____ (can) go back in time, I _____ (study) a foreign language earlier. If I _____ (do) that, I _____ (be) bilingual now."

Participle Clauses - Sophisticated Speaking

Participle clauses make you sound more sophisticated and natural. They're common in academic English and help you combine ideas elegantly.

Present Participle Clauses (-ing) Background Information

-ing clause (reason/time/result) + main clause
For giving reasons or background:
Being someone who loves nature, I try to spend weekends outdoors.
Having grown up in a small town, I really appreciate the peace and quiet.
Living in a big city, I'm used to the fast pace of life.
For showing simultaneous actions:
I often listen to podcasts while commuting to work.
Walking through the old town, you can really sense the history.

Perfect Participle Clauses (Having + past participle) Prior Experience

Having + past participle + ... main clause
For talking about experiences that led to current situations:
Having lived abroad for several years, I've become much more independent.
Having worked in customer service, I understand the importance of patience.
Having experienced both systems, I can see the advantages of each.

Past Participle Clauses (-ed) Passive Meaning

Past participle + by/with... + main clause
For passive meaning (something done TO the subject):
Inspired by my grandmother's stories, I developed a love for history.
Surrounded by mountains, the village has a very peaceful atmosphere.
Disappointed with the result, I decided to try a different approach.

PRACTICE: Transform using participle clauses

Original: Because I have traveled extensively, I'm quite adaptable.
Original: I am someone who enjoys outdoor activities. I go hiking every weekend.

Wish & If Only - Expressing Regret and Desires

These structures are perfect for talking about regrets, desires, and imaginary situations - common topics in IELTS Speaking Parts 2 and 3.

Wish + Past Simple Present Desires

I wish + subject + past simple (for present meaning)
For things you want to be different NOW:
I wish I had more time to pursue my hobbies.
I wish I were more confident in social situations.
I wish I could speak more languages.

Wish + Past Perfect Past Regrets

I wish + subject + past perfect (had + past participle)
For regretting things in the PAST:
I wish I had studied a different subject at university.
I wish I had taken more risks when I was younger.
I wish I hadn't been so shy as a teenager.

If Only... Stronger Emphasis

If only + same patterns as "wish" (but stronger emotion)
"If only" expresses stronger emotion than "wish":
If only I had more experience in this field!
If only I had known about this opportunity earlier!
If only people were more environmentally conscious!

Would Rather / Would Prefer Preferences

I would rather + infinitive (without "to") / I would prefer to + infinitive
For expressing preferences:
I would rather work independently than in a large team.
I would prefer to live somewhere with a milder climate.
Given the choice, I'd rather travel by train than fly.

PRACTICE: Complete the sentences

Question: "Is there anything you regret about your education?"
"Yes, I wish I _____ (learn) a second language earlier. If only I _____ (start) when I was a child, I _____ (be) fluent by now."

Speaking Practice - Put It All Together

Now it's time to practice using these structures in realistic IELTS Speaking scenarios. Remember: naturalness is key - use 2-3 structures per answer, not all of them!

CHALLENGE 1: Answer with Complex Grammar

IELTS Part 2 Style Question:

"Describe a person who has had a big influence on your life."

Try to include:

A cleft sentence ("What I admire most about them is..."), a relative clause ("...who/which..."), and a conditional ("If it weren't for them, I wouldn't...").

CHALLENGE 2: Part 3 Discussion Question

IELTS Part 3 Question:

"How do you think education will change in the future?"

Try to include:

A conditional ("If technology continues to develop..."), a participle clause ("Having seen how quickly things change..."), and "which" referring to a whole idea ("...which could transform how we learn").

TIMED CHALLENGE: 2-Minute Talk

Topic Card:

Describe a skill you would like to learn
You should say:
- What skill it is
- Why you want to learn it
- How you would learn it
- And explain why this skill is important to you

Grammar Targets:

  • "What I've always wanted to learn is..." (cleft)
  • "...which is something that has always fascinated me" (relative)
  • "If I could dedicate more time to this, I would..." (conditional)
  • "Having seen others master this skill, I believe..." (participle)
  • "I wish I had started learning this earlier..." (wish)
