Part 1 — This is Sarah

Mr Hill's office is on the third floor, with a big window and a view of the river. Two people are waiting.

Mr Hill: Leo, welcome! This is Sarah, our manager. And this is Tom, from our team.

Leo: Nice to meet you, Sarah. Nice to meet you, Tom.

Sarah: Nice to meet you too. How was your flight?

Leo: Long. And late! But I'm here.

Tom: Would you like a coffee, or tea?

Leo: Coffee, please. Black.

Part 2 — Tell us about your company

Mr Hill: So, Leo. Tell us about your company.

Leo: We have a small company. Forty people. Our office is in the city, near the port. We work with companies in Spain, France and Italy.

Sarah: How many customers do you have?

Leo: About two hundred. Small and big.

Tom: And why London?

Leo: Because you are a good company. And because I like rain.

Everyone laughs. Leo relaxes. Say hello, smile, ask questions.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

2. How many customers does Leo have?

1. Forty people. The office is in the city, near the port.

2. About two hundred, small and big.

Talk about it:
  • And you? Tell me about your company or your work: how many people, where, who are the customers?

Part 3 — Let's start in January

Mr Hill: The price is good. The dates are good. Let's start in January.

Leo: January is perfect.

Sarah: We'll send the contract on Monday.

Leo: Thank you. Thank you very much.

Mr Hill: No — thank you, Leo. Now: dinner?

Five thirty. One hour and a half, in English. Leo looks at the river. He did it.

Grammar: we, our / This is… / Let's…

In a meeting you speak for your company, not only for you. That means we and our.

we / our / us. "We have forty people." "Our office is near the port." "Tell us about your company." (we = the people · our = of the company · us = to the people)
Three facts about a company: "We have … people." "We work with companies in …" "Our office is in …" Questions: "How many people do you have?" "Where are your customers?"
This is… to introduce a person: "This is Sarah, our manager." — "Nice to meet you, Sarah." Then a small question: "How was your flight?" "Would you like a coffee?"
Let's… = a decision together: "Let's start in January." "Let's have dinner." "Let's talk about the price."
