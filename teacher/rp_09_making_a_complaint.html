Making a Complaint — teacher sheet

Keep this on a second screen. Not for sharing.

Flow (about 50 min)

  1. Scene and Conversation tabs, then Useful Phrases (about 25 min).
  2. Your Turn step 1: student says the six steps aloud (3 min).
  3. Step 2, run 1: you play the counterpart (5 min). Take notes while you listen.
  4. Feedback (3 min): two things that worked, two phrases to fix.
  5. Step 3, swap: you are the customer (5 min). Model one or two of the target phrases naturally.
  6. Go again, harder: same complaint plus a twist (5 min). Close with the Afterwards ticks.

Your three moves as the counterpart

  • Push back once. Say no or quote a rule the first time they ask, however good their request is.
  • Misunderstand once. Mishear or take the wrong meaning. It makes them rephrase, which is the real skill.
  • Give new information. Offer an option they didn't ask for (voucher, repair, manager).

The counterparts' secrets

Headphones (Dana): gives way if asked about the guarantee for faulty goods, or if the student stays warm for three exchanges. Pushes the manufacturer's helpline first.
Blender (Sam): nervous. Says yes to a replacement once the student offers an easy way for Sam to justify it ("it's clearly faulty, so no one could blame you").
Hotel (Alex): only one quiet room is free, a smaller one. Offers a free breakfast instead first. Gives in if the student stays calm and repeats exactly what was booked.
Their own situation: agree the details together in 30 seconds, then use the three moves.

Levels of difficulty

  • Easier: say yes a little sooner, repeat a question slowly, hint at the next step.
  • Harder: stay firm for two extra exchanges, speak at natural speed, draw a twist right at the start.

While listening

  • Don't interrupt to correct. Write down 2 things that worked and 2 phrases to fix (word choice or tone, not every slip).
  • Say the corrected phrase back once, then have them use it in the next run.
  • Feedback should be mostly positive: pronunciation, tone, creativity.
