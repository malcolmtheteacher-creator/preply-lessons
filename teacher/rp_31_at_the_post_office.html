At the Post Office — teacher sheet

Keep this on a second screen. Not for sharing. Conversation family: 1 · Getting a service.

Flow (about 50 min)

  1. Scene tab, then Useful Phrases: read the toolkit aloud together, then the speed round (about 15 min).
  2. Conversation tab: read it aloud one role each, then the from-memory questions (about 10 min).
  3. Your Turn step 1: student says the steps aloud (3 min).
  4. Step 2, run 1: you play the other person (5 min). Take notes while you listen.
  5. Feedback (3 min): two things that worked, two phrases to fix.
  6. Step 3, swap: you take the student's part and model one or two toolkit phrases naturally (5 min).
  7. Go again, harder: same situation plus a twist (5 min). Close with the Afterwards ticks.

Your three moves as the other person

  • Push back once. Say no, or quote a rule, the first time they ask, however good the request is.
  • Misunderstand once. Mishear or take the wrong meaning. It makes them rephrase or use a repair phrase, which is the real skill.
  • Give new information. Offer an option they didn't ask for.

The other person's secrets

Birthday parcel (Sue): recommends the expensive option first. Gives the cheaper tracked one when asked about timing.
Missed you (Mike): cannot find the parcel on the first try. Finds it once the student gives the date on the card.
Registered letter (Joy): insists on receipts and proof. Offers a cheaper option if the student explains the deadline.
Their own situation: agree the details together in 30 seconds, then use the three moves.

Difficulty

  • Easier: say yes a little sooner, repeat a question slowly, hint at the next step.
  • Harder: stay firm two extra exchanges, speak at natural speed, draw a twist right at the start.

While listening

  • Don't interrupt to correct. Write down 2 things that worked and 2 phrases to fix (word choice or tone, not every slip).
  • Say the corrected phrase back once, then have them use it in the next run.
  • Keep feedback mostly positive: pronunciation, tone, creativity.
