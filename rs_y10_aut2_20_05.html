What is sin, and what is its consequence?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Separation from God through wrongdoing. Its consequence is spiritual death and alienation from God.

What is original sin, and why is it important?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The doctrine that all humans inherit sinfulness from Adam and Eve. It explains why all humans are sinful.

How do Protestants and Catholics differ on salvation?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Protestants emphasise faith alone (sola fide). Catholics believe salvation requires faith + works and participation in sacraments.

What role does the Holy Spirit play in salvation?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The Spirit convicts of sin, enables faith in Jesus, grants spiritual rebirth, sanctifies the believer, and provides assurance of salvation.

Christ as Saviour, Redeemer, and Mediator

Saviour: Jesus saves humans from sin and death. John 3:16 captures this: "God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life."

Redeemer: Jesus redeems (buys back) humanity from slavery to sin. Romans 5:8 says, "God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us." Redemption emphasises Christ's costliness—his death was the price.

Mediator: Jesus mediates between God and humans. He bridges the gap created by sin. 1 Timothy 2:5: "There is one God and one mediator between God and mankind, the man Christ Jesus." He represents both God and humans.

Atonement: How Jesus's Death Saves

What is atonement? Atonement means "at-one-ment"—the act by which Jesus reconciles humans to God through his death. Christians believe Jesus's death makes it possible for humans to be forgiven.

Key scripture: Romans 3:25 says Jesus is "the propitiation for our sins" (KJV). Propitiation means "appeasement" or "satisfaction of justice." Jesus's death satisfies God's justice and enables forgiveness.

Three major atonement theories:

  • Satisfaction Theory (Anselm, 1033–1109): Humans owe God a debt through sin. Jesus's death satisfies that debt. God's honour is restored. Influential in Catholic theology.
  • Moral Influence Theory (Abelard, 1079–1142): Jesus's death demonstrates God's love and calls us to respond with love. It doesn't pay a debt but inspires transformation. Influences liberal Protestantism.
  • Penal Substitution (Calvin, Luther): Jesus bore the penalty of sin in our place. God punished Jesus instead of us. His death satisfies God's wrath. Dominant in Reformed and evangelical theology.

How These Connect: Incarnation, Crucifixion, Resurrection

The Incarnation: The Word (Jesus) became flesh and lived among us (John 1:14). This is necessary because God must become human to die and be raised. Only a divine-human figure can redeem humanity.

The Crucifixion: Jesus died on the cross as the payment for sin. This is where the actual redemption happens.

The Resurrection: Jesus rising proves the victory is complete. Sin and death are defeated. Believers can rise with Christ to new life.

Together: The three events form a unified story. Incarnation makes redemption possible. Crucifixion accomplishes redemption. Resurrection confirms redemption's power.

The Atonement & Different Christian Traditions

CATHOLIC Emphasises Jesus as sacrifice in the Eucharist. Atonement is tied to the sacramental system. Works and grace cooperate in salvation.
REFORMED/EVANGELICAL Stresses penal substitution. Jesus bore our guilt and penalty. Salvation is through faith alone in Christ's work.
ORTHODOX Sees atonement as theosis. Christ's incarnation and victory over death deify human nature. Atonement is transformation, not forensic payment.
LIBERAL Sees atonement as moral influence. Jesus's example of love and justice calls us to transformation. Atonement is about inspiration, not payment.

Scholarly Debate: Which Atonement Theory is Best?

Satisfaction Theory's Strengths

It makes sense that sin requires payment. Honour and justice matter. It respects God's moral character. But critics say it makes God seem like a cosmic accountant.

Moral Influence Theory's Strengths

It emphasises God's love and transformation over debt-payment. Jesus's example is powerful. But critics say it doesn't adequately address the reality of sin and judgment.

Penal Substitution's Strengths

It takes sin and justice seriously. It explains Christ's suffering as bearing our penalty. But critics say it makes God seem violent and arbitrary.

Christus Victor (Ancient View)

Early Church fathers saw Christ's resurrection as victory over Satan and death. Not payment, but triumph. Modern theologians are recovering this view as balanced and biblical.

The Incarnation & Incarnational Theology

Why God Became Human

The Incarnation (God becoming human in Jesus) is essential to atonement. Why?

  • Only God can save: Humans cannot fix the break between God and humanity. Only God's action can restore it.
  • Only a human can represent humanity: Jesus must be human to represent us and take our place. A purely divine figure couldn't die and be resurrected.
  • Union of God and humanity: Jesus unites the divine and human. In him, God and humanity are reconciled.
  • Theosis: Orthodox theology says the Incarnation deifies human nature. God became human so humans could become divine.

Key scripture: Hebrews 4:15 says Jesus "has been tempted in every way, just as we are—yet he did not sin." He is fully human and fully divine, able to understand us and save us.
