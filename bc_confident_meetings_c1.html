Phrases for Confident Contributions

Key Meeting Phrases

Purpose Phrase When to Use
Polite interruption "If I could just jump in for a moment…" When you want to add your point without being rude
Expressing disagreement "That"s an interesting point, but…" When you disagree but want to remain professional
Seeking clarification "I"m not sure I follow. Could you clarify?" When you don"t understand someone"s comment
Returning to an earlier point "I"d like to go back to what was said earlier…" When an earlier topic needs more discussion
Summarizing agreement "Let me just summarise where we are." When clarifying what the group has decided

Diplomatic Language: Softening Structures

In professional meetings, direct statements can sound too harsh. Use softening structures to sound more tactful and professional.

Key Softening Structures

Modal verbs: might / could / would

Direct (harsh): "We need to fix this now."

Softened: "I think we might need to look at this sooner rather than later."

Soft openers: It seems that…, Perhaps we could…, One option might be…

Direct (harsh): "That idea doesn"t work."

Softened: "It seems that approach might have some challenges. Perhaps we could explore an alternative?"

Longer softening phrases:

  • "It might be worth considering…"
  • "One approach could be…"
  • "Would it be possible to…?"
  • "It could be argued that…"
  • "In my view, there"s a case for…"

Rewrite for Diplomacy

Rewrite these statements to sound more diplomatic:

1. "You"re wrong about that."

"I think there might be another way to look at that. Could we consider the alternative perspective?"

2. "That won"t work."

"It"s an interesting idea, but it seems there might be some practical challenges. Perhaps we could build on it further?"

3. "You"ve made a mistake."

"I think there might be a small detail we should clarify. Could we double-check this figure?"
