Flexible Communication at Work

Adapting Your Style to Others' Preferences

⏱️ Lesson time: 3 minutes

How do communication preferences vary at work?

Think about your workplace (or a workplace you know). Do all your colleagues communicate in the same way?

Some people prefer direct, fast-paced communication. Others like time to think and prefer a more measured approach. Some focus on tasks; others focus on relationships.

The key question: How do you adapt your communication style to work better with different people?

Let's explore three workplace characters

In the next sections, you'll meet Anshul, Bella, and Cho. They all work in the same team, but they communicate differently. Your job is to understand their styles and think about how they could adapt to each other.

Key Vocabulary

Click each card to reveal the definition.

Flex To change your behaviour or communication style to adapt to a situation or person.
Direct Straightforward; saying exactly what you mean without hiding anything.
Indirect Suggesting or implying something without saying it clearly; being tactful or careful.
Measured Careful, deliberate, and thoughtful; not rushed.
Task-focused Prioritizing what needs to be done; concentrating on results and goals.
Relationship-focused Prioritizing how people feel; building trust and connection first.
Assertive Confident in expressing your views, needs, and opinions clearly.
Diplomatic Tactful and skilled at managing disagreement without causing offence.

Three Workplace Characters

🟦 Anshul (Direct, Fast-paced, Task-focused)

Communication style:
"Let's get this done. We need results by Friday. I don't need details—just tell me the deadline and the deliverables."
In meetings:
Anshul speaks quickly, interrupts to stay on schedule, and moves immediately to action items. He doesn't ask about people's feelings—he wants facts.
Analysis: Anshul might offend Bella (who's relationship-focused) or frustrate Cho (who needs time to think). He should slow down, ask for input, and acknowledge people's concerns before moving to decisions.

🟪 Bella (Indirect, Measured, Relationship-focused)

Communication style:
"Before we decide, I want to know how everyone feels. Is everyone comfortable with this? Let's build trust first."
In meetings:
Bella listens carefully, asks how others are doing, and suggests decisions gently. She takes time to build consensus and values harmony.
Analysis: Bella might slow down Anshul (who needs speed) or confuse Cho (who needs clarity). She should be more direct about problems, state her opinions clearly, and respect others' need for efficiency.

🟨 Cho (Mostly indirect, Measured, Balanced between task and relationship)

Communication style:
"Let me think about this carefully. I want to understand all the options before I share my thoughts."
In meetings:
Cho listens more than he speaks, asks thoughtful questions, and needs time to process. He's diplomatic and considers both the task and people's feelings.
Analysis: Cho's style is balanced, but he might not be heard by faster communicators like Anshul. He should prepare his ideas in advance and speak up earlier, even if he's not 100% certain yet.

Task 1: Analyse how each character could adapt

For each person, write how they could flex their communication style to work better with the others.

How could Anshul flex?

What should Anshul do differently when working with Bella and Cho?
Anshul could:
• Slow down his pace and ask "What does everyone think?" instead of deciding alone.
• Acknowledge Bella's relationship concerns: "I understand this is important to all of us."
• Give Cho time: "Let's discuss this in two days after you've had time to think."
• Focus less on speed and more on building buy-in.

How could Bella flex?

What should Bella do differently when working with Anshul and Cho?
Bella could:
• Be more direct: "I think we should try option A because..."
• Respect Anshul's timeline: "I understand the deadline. Here's my decision: Yes, let's go."
• Give Cho space to prepare: "What questions do you have? Take time to think."
• Balance relationship-building with efficiency.

How could Cho flex?

What should Cho do differently when working with Anshul and Bella?
Cho could:
• Prepare ideas in advance so he's ready to speak earlier.
• Be more assertive: "I have a different view. Here it is..."
• Acknowledge Anshul's urgency: "I understand we need a decision Friday."
• Speak up sooner, even if he's not 100% sure yet.

Task 2: Describe your own communication style

Think about how you communicate at work. Write or think about your answers:

Reflection Questions

1. Where are you on the three scales? Are you more direct or indirect? Fast-paced or measured? Task-focused or relationship-focused?

2. Describe one situation where you had to flex your style. Who did you communicate with? How did you change? What was the result?

3. Who's it hardest to flex for? Which type of communicator challenges you most?

4. How do you know when to flex? What signals tell you someone needs a different style?

Discussion Questions

Use these questions to think deeper about flexible communication:

1. When does miscommunication happen?
What happens when Anshul (direct and fast) meets Bella (indirect and measured)? Who misunderstands? What can they do?
2. Why is flexing important?
"It's my natural style—why should I change?" How would you answer this question?
3. Who needs to flex more?
Is it the responsibility of direct communicators to flex for indirect ones, or vice versa? Or does everyone share the responsibility equally?
4. What if someone won't flex?
Imagine Anshul refuses to slow down or ask others' opinions. What happens to the team? How does this affect trust?
5. Cultural differences
Are some cultures more direct or indirect? More task-focused or relationship-focused? How does this affect international teams?
6. Technology and flexing
How does communication style change in emails, Slack, video calls, or face-to-face meetings? Do you flex differently?
7. Can you flex too much?
Is it possible to lose your authentic voice if you're always flexing to others? Where's the balance?
