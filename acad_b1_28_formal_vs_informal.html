Register: Matching Your Language to the Context

Register is the level of formality in your language. Academic writing requires formal English, while texts to friends are informal.

When to Use Formal English

  • Academic essays and research papers
  • Professional emails to teachers or employers
  • Formal presentations
  • Official documents

When to Use Informal English

  • Text messages and casual emails to friends
  • Social media posts (usually)
  • Casual conversation
  • Blog posts or personal narratives

Key Formal Alternatives

Informal: "got" → Formal: "obtained / acquired"

I got the data. vs I obtained the data.

Informal: "big" → Formal: "significant / substantial"

That was a big improvement. vs That represented a significant improvement.

Informal: "lots of" → Formal: "numerous / multiple"

There are lots of problems. vs There are numerous problems.

Informal: "said" → Formal: "argued / asserted / claimed"

He said education is important. vs He asserted that education is fundamental.

Informal: "really nice" → Formal: "particularly effective / highly beneficial"

The results were really nice. vs The results were particularly effective.

Informal: "OK" → Formal: "acceptable / satisfactory"

The data was OK. vs The data was satisfactory.

Characteristics of Formal Academic Writing

  • Objective tone: Avoid personal opinions and emotions
  • Third person: Use "the study shows" not "I think"
  • Complete sentences: No sentence fragments or contractions
  • Precise vocabulary: Exact words, not approximate or vague terms
  • Complex sentence structures: Varied, sophisticated sentence patterns
  • Passive voice (when appropriate): "It was found that..." sometimes used in formal writing

Converting Informal to Formal

Rewrite these informal sentences in formal academic English.

Example 1

Informal: "The results were really cool and showed that our method worked."

Formal: "The results demonstrated the efficacy of our methodology." Or: "The results were noteworthy, indicating that the proposed method achieved its intended outcomes."

Example 2

Informal: "Lots of people think climate change is bad for the environment."

Formal: "Numerous scientists and researchers have established that climate change poses significant environmental threats." Or: "It is widely recognised that climate change has substantial negative consequences for the environment."
