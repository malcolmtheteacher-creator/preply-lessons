Encounter: Religious Teachings on Gender Roles

Christianity

Complementarian view: Men and women have different but equally important roles.

"Husbands, love your wives… Wives, submit to your husbands." (Ephesians 5:22–28)

Egalitarian view: Gender should not determine roles or authority in church or home.

"There is neither male nor female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus." (Galatians 3:28)

Women's ordination varies by denomination: The Church of England ordains women priests and bishops. The Roman Catholic Church does not permit women priests. Protestant churches vary widely.

Islam

Spiritual equality: "Indeed, the Muslim men and Muslim women, the believing men and believing women… Allah has prepared for them forgiveness and a great reward." (Qur'an 33:35)

Traditional role framework: Men are described as qawwamun (protectors and providers) — Qur'an 4:34. Women are honoured as mothers, wives, and daughters. Historically, Khadijah (Muhammad's first wife) was a successful businesswoman and merchant.

Modern debate: Muslim scholars and communities discuss how to interpret these roles in contemporary contexts. Some argue for traditional separation; others emphasise equality and women's leadership in mosques and professions.

Judaism

Orthodox Judaism: Men and women have distinct roles. Women are not counted in a minyan (prayer quorum of ten). Blessing: "Blessed are You… for not making me a woman."

Reform and Liberal Judaism: Full equality since the 19th century. The first female rabbi was ordained in 1972. Women lead prayers, serve as rabbis, and have equal ritual status.

Humanism

Gender roles should be based on individual choice and ability, not tradition or doctrine. Equality of opportunity in education, work, politics, and relationships is essential. Religion should not determine someone's life path.
