Reading: Understanding Educational Gaps

To what extent do you agree that most undergraduates don't have a clue about how the real world works? In which areas are young people today seriously lacking essential preparation?

This is a complex question that highlights a common concern about education. Many employers report that graduates lack practical skills in areas like:

  • Financial literacy (managing money, taxes, investments)
  • Basic car and home maintenance
  • Interpersonal and communication skills
  • Project management and organization
  • Adaptability and problem-solving in unexpected situations

However, the responsibility is shared: students themselves must take initiative in developing practical skills through internships, part-time work, volunteering, and self-directed learning.

Comprehension Check

1. What does "seriously lacking" mean in this context?

It means significantly missing or deficient in important skills or knowledge that young people need for success in the real world.

2. According to the text, who bears responsibility for developing practical skills?

The responsibility is shared between schools/universities and the students themselves. Students must take initiative through internships, part-time work, volunteering, and self-directed learning.

3. What are some practical skills that graduates often lack?

Financial literacy, basic maintenance skills, interpersonal skills, project management, and the ability to adapt to unexpected situations.

Expressing Regret About the Past

These phrases allow you to talk about things you wish had been different in your education and life.

I wish someone had Expressing a desire that someone had done something that they didn't
I do regret not Expressing genuine regret about something you failed to do
If only I'd An emphatic way to express a wish about past circumstances
If I'd been Expressing a hypothetical past condition and its implications
I wish I'd Expressing a desire that something had happened in the past but didn't
I massively regret Expressing very strong regret about something

Grammar: Past Regret Structures

Key patterns for expressing regret:

Perfect infinitive (have + past participle) shows a past action that didn't happen:

  • "I wish someone had explained how essential skills like basic car maintenance would be."
  • "I do regret not focusing more on my interpersonal skills."
  • "If only I'd spent more time on practical projects."
  • "I wish I'd learned more about financial management."

These structures require the past perfect or gerund form to show the action was not taken.

Practice: Complete the Regret Phrases

1. "I wish someone how important basic financial literacy would be in my adult life."

Options: a) had taught b) teaches c) would teach

Answer: a) had taught. This uses the past perfect to show something that didn't happen but should have.

2. "I do regret more attention to developing my interpersonal skills at university."

Options: a) not pay b) not paying c) having not paid

Answer: b) not paying. After "regret," you use the gerund form (paying).

3. "If only I'd involved in more teamwork projects during my studies."

Options: a) been b) be c) was

Answer: a) been. After "If only I'd," you need the past participle (been) to form the past perfect.

Your Life Skills Presentation Task

You and your group will collaborate to design a "life skills school-leaving certificate" by identifying and prioritizing essential skills. You have 10 minutes to prepare and present your choices.

Task Objectives:

  • Work with your group to select four crucial life skills
  • Two should be skills you wish you'd developed earlier
  • Two should be skills you believe all graduates need
  • Explain how your chosen skills would have helped your learning
  • Present and justify your selections to the class

Planning Your Presentation

Speaking Tips & Phrases

Use these phrases when presenting your group's choices:

  • "Our group believes that..."
  • "I would argue that [skill] is essential because..."
  • "This skill is particularly important because..."
  • "If I'd developed [skill] earlier, I would have..."
  • "In conclusion, we've prioritized these four skills because..."

Post-Presentation Reflection

What surprised you about the other groups' choices?

Reflect on whether different groups prioritized different skills based on their career interests, cultural backgrounds, or personal experiences. Consider whether there were common themes across all groups or significantly different perspectives.

Which skills do you actually use daily in your current life?

Consider your daily activities: work, relationships, problem-solving, communication. Which of the skills you discussed are you actually using? Which are you still developing? Which would help you most in your current situation?

How can you continue developing these skills after this lesson?

Consider: taking on projects that challenge you, seeking mentorship, participating in workshops, reading relevant books, practicing skills with peers, and reflecting regularly on your progress. Skills development is a lifelong journey that requires intentional effort.
