Key words

miser (noun) — a person who loves money and hates spending it, even on themselves. Example: "He lived in a cold, dark house because heating cost money."
generous (adjective) — happy to give money, time or help to other people. Example: "She paid for everyone's dinner and refused to take anything back."
regret (verb) — to feel sorry about something you did or did not do. Example: "I wish I had called my grandfather more often."
haunt (verb) — to keep appearing in a place or in someone's mind, like a ghost. Example: "That one mistake stayed in his thoughts for years."
legacy (noun) — what a person leaves behind after they die: money, ideas, or the way people remember them. Example: "She wanted to be remembered as someone who helped young teachers."
poverty (noun) — the state of being very poor. Example: "Thousands of children in 1843 worked long hours just to eat."
charity (noun) — help or money given to people in need, or an organisation that gives it. Example: "Two gentlemen came to the office to collect money for the poor."
cherish (verb) — to love and look after something or someone because they are precious. Example: "Keep this memory safe; you will want it when you are old."
transform (verb) — to change completely, usually for the better. Example: "One night changed him from a cold old man into a kind one."
redemption (noun) — being saved from a bad way of living, often by changing yourself. Example: "His second chance came on Christmas morning."
