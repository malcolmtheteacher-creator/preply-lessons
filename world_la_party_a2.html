Always Dancing? Carnival, Samba and Tango

Latin Americans are imagined dancing all night. Music is important, but the stories are deeper.

Key words

1. Rio's carnival is famous.
carnival (n) — a big street festival with music and parades. Example: “Rio's carnival is famous.”
2. They dance every Saturday.
dance (v) — to move to music. Example: “They dance every Saturday.”
3. The parade lasted all night.
parade (n) — a group of people walking through the street. Example: “The parade lasted all night.”
4. I love the rhythm of samba.
rhythm (n) — a regular pattern of beats. Example: “I love the rhythm of samba.”
5. Many people tango in Buenos Aires.
tango (n) — a dance from Argentina and Uruguay. Example: “Many people tango in Buenos Aires.”
6. The samba school is in my neighbourhood.
neighbourhood (n) — a part of a town where people live. Example: “The samba school is in my neighbourhood.”

The stereotype: “All Latin Americans love to party and are born dancers.”

The story

Part 1 — Samba

Samba grew in the late 1800s and early 1900s from Afro-Brazilian music and dance. It began with people from Bahia who moved to Rio de Janeiro. “Pelo Telefone”, from 1917, is often called the first recorded samba. At first, many rich people did not like it. Later, it became a national symbol of Brazil.

Quick check:

1. Where did samba come from?

2. What was the first recorded samba?

1. From Afro-Brazilian music and dance.

2. “Pelo Telefone” (1917).

Discuss:
  • Do you know a samba song?

Part 2 — Carnival and tango

In Rio's carnival, samba schools from many neighbourhoods practise all year for the parades. Tango was born in the late 1800s in working-class and immigrant neighbourhoods in Buenos Aires and Montevideo. In 2009, UNESCO recognised it. The Barranquilla carnival in Colombia is also on UNESCO's list.

Quick check:

1. What is a samba school?

2. Where was tango born?

1. A community group from a neighbourhood that practises for the parade.

2. In Buenos Aires and Montevideo.

Discuss:
  • Would you like to dance in a parade?

Part 3 — Not only dance

For some people, carnival is a time to escape the noise. Many Latin Americans say they cannot dance. The music is also much more than samba and tango: Andean folk, Mexican mariachi, Cuban son, Colombian cumbia and Brazilian bossa nova. Reggaeton, from Puerto Rico and Colombia, is now global.

Quick check:

1. Can all Latin Americans dance?

2. Which new music is global?

1. No, many say they cannot.

2. Reggaeton.

Discuss:
  • What kind of music do you like?

Grammar — like and love + -ing

like / love + verb + -ing: “I love dancing.” “She likes listening to music.”
Negative: don't like / hate: “He doesn't like dancing.”
Question: “Do you like singing?”

1. Choose the word

1. I love to music. (dancing / dance)

2. She likes samba. (listening to / listen to)

3. He doesn't like in crowds. (dancing / dances)
