Key words

lucid (adjective) — aware that you are dreaming while the dream carries on; also clear and easy to understand. Example: "Halfway through, she became lucid and decided to change the ending."
hypnagogic (adjective) — happening in the drowsy state between being awake and falling asleep. Example: "Hypnagogic images can feel startlingly real, then vanish."
consolidate (verb) — to make a memory or a position stronger and more lasting. Example: "Deep sleep seems to help the brain hold on to what we learned that day."
circadian (adjective) — relating to the body's roughly 24-hour cycle. Example: "Night shifts play havoc with your circadian rhythm."
conflate (verb) — to treat two different ideas as if they were one. Example: "Critics said the book had mixed up a link and a cause, treating them as the same thing."
unfalsifiable (adjective) — impossible to prove wrong by any observation or test. Example: "If every possible dream confirms the theory, the theory may be untestable."
introspection (noun) — examining your own thoughts and feelings from the inside. Example: "Dream research still depends heavily on what sleepers report about their own minds."
archetype (noun) — a figure or pattern that appears again and again across stories and cultures. Example: "The wise old guide is a familiar archetype in myths."
latent (adjective) — present but hidden, or not yet developed. Example: "Freud looked for the latent meaning behind the story the dreamer remembered."
overstate (verb) — to make something seem bigger, surer or more important than it really is. Example: "It would be easy to overstate what a single study shows."
