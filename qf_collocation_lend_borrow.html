🔄 Lend vs Borrow

Master the direction | A2 Level

Let's see what you notice...

Read this sentence. What's wrong with it?

Can you borrow me your pen?

What's the correction?

✓ Correction

Can you lend me your pen?

📌 Why?

LEND = you give to me (direction goes away from you). BORROW = I take from you (direction comes toward me).

Think about direction: LEND → away, BORROW ← toward. "Can you lend me £10?" means the direction is away from you (you give it).

These are perspective verbs. From different speakers' viewpoint, the same action has different verbs.

📚 Direction Matters: LEND vs BORROW

✓ LEND ➡️ (give)

➡️

I lend you my book

Can you lend me £10?

She lends him her car

They lend us money

Direction: away from lender

Object: who receives

✓ BORROW ⬅️ (take)

⬅️

I borrow your book

Can I borrow £10 from you?

He borrows her car

We borrow money from them

Direction: toward borrower

Object: what is taken

Practice: LEND or BORROW?

1. Can I __________ your umbrella? I'll return it tomorrow.

2. My bank __________ me money to buy a house.

3. I often __________ books from the library.

4. Will you __________ me your phone charger for an hour?

💡 Memory Tip: The same situation has two different verbs! "Can you LEND me £10?" = "Can I BORROW £10 from you?" Both mean the same thing, but the verb changes based on WHO is speaking.
