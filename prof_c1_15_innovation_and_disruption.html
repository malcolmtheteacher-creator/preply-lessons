6 Pitch Moves for Innovation

Click each card to see the move and examples.

1. The Problem First

Never start with your solution. Start with the problem it solves. Vividly.

Wrong: "We've built a platform that uses AI to..."

Right: "Every day, sales managers waste two hours manually updating CRM. They're doing data entry instead of coaching their teams. That's a $10M productivity loss in a 500-person sales org."

Why it works: The problem makes people lean forward. Your solution then becomes inevitable.

2. The "Why Now?"

Why is this the right moment for this idea? What has changed?

Bad: "Electric cars are the future."

Good: "Five years ago, battery costs were $500/kWh. Today they're $100/kWh. That's the moment when electric cars become cost-competitive. That's now."

Why it works: You're not imagining the future. You're reading the present. You're riding a real wave, not surfing a hype cycle.

3. The Market Reality

Who exactly will use this, and how many of them are there?

Vague: "The global market for this is huge."

Specific: "There are 50,000 independent dentists in the US. Each one does three root canals a week. That's 7.8M procedures annually. If we capture just 5%, that's $35M in addressable market."

Why it works: You're showing you've done the work. You're not guessing. You're specific enough to be believable.

4. The Moat

What stops someone from copying you immediately? Your advantage.

Weak: "We have a better product."

Strong: "Our advantage is that we've built a proprietary data set of 10 million X-rays. No competitor can get that without spending five years and $50M. We've got a moat that's years deep."

Why it works: You're showing you've thought about why you can own this market. Not just win, but own.

5. Handling Scepticism

You'll get pushback. How you handle it matters more than the pushback itself.

Defensive: "That's not true! We've already solved that."

Confident: "That's a fair concern. Here's how we're addressing it. [Specific approach]. That's why we're moving fast on [timeline]."

Why it works: You're not dismissing the concern. You're showing you've thought about it and have a plan. Investors fund founders who think through problems, not founders who deny they exist.

6. The Ask

Every pitch ends with a clear, specific ask. Not "funding." Something concrete.

Vague: "We're raising money."

Specific: "We're raising $2M in seed funding. We're using it to hire two engineers and do a pilot with five enterprise customers. By this time next year, we'll have proven the model and be ready to scale."

Why it works: You're telling them exactly what their money does. You're painting a picture of what happens if they say yes.

Your Pitch: Develop and Deliver

Build and deliver a 3-minute pitch for a genuine innovation or disruptive idea in your field. 3 minutes prep, 3 minutes pitch. Use the 6 moves as your structure.

Your innovation: Pick something real. A product you think should exist. A process change. A business model shift. It should be disruptive — it challenges the current way things are done.
The structure (follow these 6 moves):
  • 1. The Problem: What is broken today?
  • 2. Why Now: What has changed that makes this the moment?
  • 3. The Market: How big is the opportunity? (specific numbers)
  • 4. The Moat: What's your sustainable advantage?
  • 5. Handling Scepticism: What will people doubt? Address it directly.
  • 6. The Ask: What specifically do you need?

The Tank: 6 Investor Challenges

After you pitch, the investors push back. You have 45 seconds to respond. Click "Next challenge" to reveal each one.

Challenge 1

Investor: "This already exists. I can name three companies doing exactly this."

Your task: You're not the first. How do you show why you're different? Why now is your moment?

0:45

Challenge 2

Investor: "The market isn't ready for this. People don't want their habits disrupted."

Your task: Show why they're wrong. What evidence do you have that the market is shifting?

0:45

Challenge 3

Investor: "What's your revenue model? Where does the money actually come from?"

Your task: You have a business model. Explain it clearly. Show how you actually make money.

0:45

Challenge 4

Investor: "This relies on [major assumption]. What if that assumption is wrong?"

Your task: Every pitch has hidden assumptions. Show you've stress-tested yours. What happens if you're wrong?

0:45

Challenge 5

Investor: "Why are you the right person to do this?"

Your task: Show your unfair advantage. What's your unique position to execute this idea?

0:45

Challenge 6

Investor: "I'll give you a decision right now. But I need you to tell me: what's the biggest risk, and what are you doing about it?"

Your task: Final question. Show you're thinking like an investor. Name your biggest risk and your mitigation.

Innovation Pitch Language Bank

Problem Statement

"Today, [specific situation] forces [people] to spend [time/money] on [low-value thing]."

"The cost of this problem is [quantified impact]."

"People have adapted, but the underlying problem remains unsolved."

Why Now

"Five years ago, [precondition] cost $X. Today it costs $Y. That's the moment."

"Three things have shifted: [thing 1], [thing 2], [thing 3]. Now the equation changes."

"The market is shifting. We're riding that wave, not predicting it."

Market Sizing

"There are [number] potential customers. Each one has [problem]. Today they spend [amount] trying to solve it."

"If we capture just [realistic %], that's a $[X]M market."

"The addressable market is [specific number]."

The Moat

"Our sustainable advantage is [specific thing] that takes competitors [time/money] to replicate."

"We've built [specific asset] that creates a barrier."

"Our network effects / data set / relationships create a moat that's [years/dollars] deep."

Handling Scepticism

"That's a fair concern. Here's how we're addressing it: [specific approach]."

"You're right that [objection] is a risk. Here's our mitigation: [plan]."

"We've stress-tested that assumption. Here's what we found: [evidence]."

The Ask

"We're raising $[X]M. We're using it to [specific 2-3 milestones]. By [time], we'll [concrete outcome]."

"Your investment goes to: [allocation]. That gets us to [milestone]."

"With this funding, here's what becomes possible: [vision]."
