Year: 1861

Founder: Louis Pasteur

Key Idea: Diseases caused by microscopic organisms (germs), not bad air

Fact 7
Laws of Thermodynamics
Click to reveal details...

Year: 1850s-1870s (developed over time)

Core Concept: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed (conservation of energy)

Key Law: First Law: Energy is conserved; Second Law: Entropy always increases

Memory Anchor: ENERGY NEVER DIES, JUST GETS MESSY - Imagine a phoenix burning (energy transferred) but the ashes never disappear (entropy). Energy transformation = thermodynamic laws.
Fact 8

Year: 1900 (Planck's quantum hypothesis)

Founder: Max Planck

Key Idea: Energy comes in discrete packets called "quanta," not continuous waves

Memory Anchor: PLANCK'S PIXEL WORLD (1900) - Imagine reality as a pixelated video game where energy jumps from pixel to pixel. No smooth transitions, just discrete quantum jumps. 1900 = easy to remember!

Elaboration: Why These Discoveries Matter

Understanding the connections between these discoveries helps deepen memory. Here's how they relate:

The Story of Modern Science

1687: Newton's Laws - The foundation of physics. Everything that moves follows these rules. Without Newton, we couldn't predict planetary motion or build machines.
1859: Darwin's Evolution - Explains how life on Earth diversified. Bridges physics and biology by showing life adapts to environmental laws.
1861: Germ Theory (Pasteur) - Revolutionized medicine and public health. Showed diseases aren't magic, but caused by microscopic life following biological laws.
1869: Periodic Table (Mendeleev) - Organized all matter into predictable patterns. Showed that atoms follow mathematical rules, bridging Newton's physics and chemistry.
1905: Relativity (Einstein) - Revealed that Newton's laws only work at normal speeds. At extreme speeds/gravity, spacetime bends. Energy and matter are interchangeable (E=mc²).
1900-1927: Quantum Mechanics & Thermodynamics - At tiny scales (atoms), energy behaves as particles, not waves. At large scales, energy always increases in disorder (entropy). These describe the hidden rules of the universe.
1912: Plate Tectonics (Wegener) - Applied physics to Earth itself. Continents move due to internal heat and pressure—proving physics operates even on planetary scales.
1927: Big Bang (Lemaître) - Combined relativity, thermodynamics, and observations to explain the universe's beginning. The ultimate application of physical laws to all of existence.
1953: DNA Structure (Watson & Crick) - Shows how life encodes information at the molecular level. Combines chemistry, physics, and biology. Explains how evolution works at the atomic scale.

The Web of Connections

Why memorize these together? Each discovery is a building block. Newton gave us mechanics. Darwin showed life changes. Chemistry (Periodic Table) revealed matter's nature. Einstein showed matter and energy are one. Quantum mechanics explained atoms. DNA connected chemistry to life. Big Bang connected physics to the universe. This isn't random facts—it's the story of how humans understood reality.
