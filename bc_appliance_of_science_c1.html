The Appliance of Science

Manufacturing Processes

Opening Questions

How do manufacturers put the candy coating on sweets like M&Ms?

How do water treatment plants ensure the water they process is safe to drink?

Why is it difficult to recycle plastic bottle caps?

True or False — Answer Reveal

Question 1: How do manufacturers coat M&Ms?

A: The sweets are placed in a coating pan (a machine that resembles a cement mixer), which rotates like a clothes dryer and sprays melted sugar over them.

B: The sweets are placed in a mould made out of starch and then hand-painted.

Answer: A is correct. The coating pan rotates and sprays melted sugar over the sweets, similar to how a cement mixer works.

Question 2: How is water made safe to drink?

A: Water is treated with chlorine to disinfect it and then filtered to remove large particles of foreign matter like mud or algae.

B: Water is screened for large particles, which are then filtered and finally treated with chlorine to kill any remaining bacteria or organisms.

Answer: B is correct. The process involves screening, filtering, and then treating with chlorine in that order for effective water treatment.

Question 3: Why are plastic bottle caps difficult to recycle?

A: Bottle caps are too small to justify recycling them.

B: Bottle caps melt at 100 degrees higher than plastic bottles, so it is more expensive to process them through the recycling system.

Answer: B is correct. The different melting temperatures make it costly and impractical to recycle caps with bottles in the same process.

Using Passive Structures

When describing manufacturing processes, use passive structures to focus on what is being done to the product rather than who is doing it.

Potato Crisps: Expanded Description Using Passives

1. First, the potatoes are washed with drum or flotation washers to remove extraneous matter.

2. The potatoes are then trimmed to remove decay, bruises, and other imperfections.

3. The potato slices are dried by blower fans.

4. The slices are fried for about 1.5 to 3 minutes at between 160 and 190 degrees Celsius.

5. The crisps are sent on a conveyor belt to a rotating drum where salt and flavours are added.

6. Finally, the crisps are cooled and packed into bags.

Notice: Each step uses a passive verb (are washed, are trimmed, are dried, etc.) to describe the manufacturing process. This structure is typical in technical descriptions.

Stress and Pauses in Fluent Speech

When speaking about manufacturing processes, natural pauses (marked with //) help listeners follow your explanation. Stressed words are emphasised.

Ice Cream Making with Pauses (// = pause)

First // the ingredients are weighed out // and mixed together. //

Then // the mix is pasteurised, // which involves heating it to a high temperature to ensure that harmful bacteria are killed off. //

The mix is then left // for at least 4 hours to allow the fat to cool //and form into crystals.

It is usually at this point that any flavours, //purees, // or colourings are added. //

The next stage // is for any fruit particles, //biscuit pieces, //or chocolate shavings to be added to the mix.

Finally, // the ice cream is packaged and put into a blast freezer at -40 degrees Celsius, //where more of the water content is frozen to make it firmer.

Key points:

  • Pause after topic words like "First," "Then," "Finally"
  • Pause before major process steps (verbs)
  • Stress important nouns like "blast freezer"
  • Natural pacing helps listeners understand the sequence

How is jam made? (Reference process)

Jam Making Steps

Fruit arrives at the plant and undergoes quality inspection (colour, ripeness, taste).
The fruit is funnelled into pipes for cleaning and crushing. Water sprays clear surface dirt, and paddles push fruit through small holes to remove stems.
The fruit is sent to cooking vats for pasteurisation (heating to 100°C and immediate freezing to 0°C).
The fruit is pumped into cooking kettles where it is mixed with sugar and pectin. The mixture is cooked and cooled three times, with optional flavourings added.
The jam is sent to filling machines and poured into pre-sterilised jars.
Metal caps automatically seal the jars, and pre-printed labels are affixed by machine.
The finished jars are packed and sent to customers.
