Explaining Emotions

Understanding and expressing your feelings in English

How to Talk About Your Feelings

Essential Phrases

Use these expressions to talk about yourself:

I can't stand…

Meaning: Something you strongly dislike

I can't stand waiting in queues or being interrupted when I'm working.
If I don't…, it'll get on top of me later.

Meaning: Something will overwhelm you or become a problem

If I don't exercise regularly, it'll get on top of me later and I'll feel stressed.
Right now…is a looming deadline.

Meaning: Something worrying or negative is approaching

Right now finishing my project is a looming deadline, and I feel anxious about it.
I get great satisfaction out of…

Meaning: Something that makes you feel rewarded or fulfilled

I get great satisfaction out of helping others and seeing them succeed.
I'm up for…

Meaning: You are ready to do or try something

I'm up for trying a new challenge or going on an adventure.

Emotion Adjectives

Strong Emotion Words

Here are powerful adjectives to describe how you feel:

  • Blissful: Extremely happy and peaceful
  • Ecstatic: Overwhelmingly joyful and excited
  • Furious: Extremely angry
  • Heart-breaking: Causing deep sadness
  • Irritating: Annoying or frustrating
  • Tedious: Boring and tiring

How These Words Work

📖 Context Examples ▼

Blissful: After the stressful exam, I felt blissful sitting on the beach.

Ecstatic: When she said yes to the proposal, he felt absolutely ecstatic.

Furious: He was furious when someone cut in front of him in traffic.

Heart-breaking: The ending of that movie was absolutely heart-breaking.

Irritating: The sound of loud chewing is really irritating to me.

Tedious: Filling out the same forms over and over is tedious work.

What About Moving Countries?

How would you feel if you had to move country?

Think about the emotions involved:

You might feel: nervous, excited, sad (leaving friends), hopeful (new opportunities), anxious (learning a new culture), or exhilarated (adventure). Different emotions can happen at the same time!

Key Learning Points

  • Use can't stand for things you strongly dislike
  • Get on top of means something will overwhelm you
  • Looming deadline describes negative future events
  • Satisfaction and up for show positive feelings
  • Strong adjectives like blissful, ecstatic, furious add emotional depth
  • Context matters: the same event can cause different emotions
