Alternative Sports

Alternative or extreme sports are growing in popularity, especially among young people. They include activities like skateboarding, parkour, BMXing, surfing, and rock climbing. These sports are often seen as more exciting or challenging than traditional sports.

What makes them different?

  • Often done individually rather than in teams
  • Require creativity and personal style
  • May involve higher levels of risk
  • Often performed in urban environments or nature
  • Appeal to people who want something different from traditional school PE

Deeper Discussion

Think about these questions and prepare your answers:

Physical Education & Sports Culture

  • What should the main goal of PE lessons be - to make students fit, to teach them teamwork, or to help them find a sport they enjoy?
  • Do you think traditional sports like football or basketball are becoming outdated for young people?
  • How has social media changed young people's interest in alternative sports?

Safety & Risk

  • Is it the school's responsibility to keep students completely safe, or should they learn to take calculated risks?
  • What measures could schools take to teach alternative sports safely?
  • Can extreme sports ever be completely safe? Should that stop schools from teaching them?

Inclusion & Opportunity

  • Are traditional school sports inclusive for all students, or do some feel excluded?
  • What types of students might prefer alternative sports to traditional team sports?
  • Should schools offer more variety in PE lessons?

Personal Reflection

  • What sport would interest you most if it was offered at school?
  • What stops you from trying new or extreme sports?
  • How important is "having confidence in yourself" for trying something new and challenging?
