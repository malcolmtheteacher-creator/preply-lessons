A friend tells you about a bad week. While they talk, you check your phone, plan your reply and think about your own problems. You nod in the right places. Did you really listen? A French philosopher said that real attention is so rare that it is a kind of gift.

Meet Simone Weil

Read one part at a time. After each part, close your eyes and answer the question before you check.

I

A serious child

Simone Weil was born in Paris in 1909. Her brother André became one of the great mathematicians of the century. Simone was brilliant too, and very serious. As a small child during the First World War, she is said to have refused sugar because soldiers at the front had none. According to a well-known story, she later came first in a university philosophy exam, ahead of Simone de Beauvoir.

Why is Simone Weil said to have refused sugar as a child?

Because French soldiers at the front had none, and she wanted to share their hardship.
II

The philosopher in the factory

From 1931 Weil taught philosophy in secondary schools, and she gave much of her salary to unemployed workers. But she felt she could not write about workers without sharing their lives. So in 1934 she took a year off and worked on machines in factories near Paris, including Renault. The work was exhausting, and she was often afraid. In her diary she described how hard work without respect can crush a person.

Why did Weil go to work in factories?

Because she felt she couldn't truly write about workers' lives without sharing them herself.
III

A short life, a long echo

In 1936 she went to Spain to support the fight against fascism in the civil war, but she burned her leg badly in hot cooking oil and had to return. In 1942 she reached London to work for the Free French. She was ill, and it is said she refused to eat more than people in occupied France were given. She died in England in 1943, aged 34. Most of her books came out after her death; Camus helped to publish several.

Why were most of Weil's books published only after her death?

She died young, in 1943, and most of her writing was published later, some of it with Camus's help.
Talk: Weil gave up a comfortable job to feel what factory workers felt. Is that admirable, or too extreme? Would you do something similar to understand others?

Key words

Each word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means. Then reveal and compare.

1. She had lost her own job once, so she felt real empathy for her worried colleague.
empathy (n) — the ability to understand and share the feelings of another person. Example: "Good nurses need skill, but they also need empathy for their patients."
2. The children were so attentive during the story that nobody moved.
attentive (adj) — listening or watching carefully, with your whole mind. Example: "The waiter was attentive but never annoying."
3. Even when he was old and ill, he kept his dignity and thanked everyone who helped him.
dignity (n) — the sense that a person deserves respect and has value. Example: "Every worker deserves to be treated with dignity."
4. Her generosity is famous: she always gives her time to anyone who needs it.
generosity (n) — the quality of happily giving time, money or help to others. Example: "Thanks to the neighbours' generosity, the family had enough food all winter."
5. He looked distracted during dinner, checking his phone every few minutes.
distracted (adj) — unable to concentrate because your mind is on something else. Example: "I was distracted by my thoughts about work and missed my stop."
6. People across the country sent food and money in solidarity with the flood victims.
solidarity (n) — support and unity between people who share a problem or a goal. Example: "The workers stopped work in solidarity with their colleague who had lost his job."
7. From the perspective of a small child, the long car journey felt like for ever.
perspective (n) — a particular way of seeing or thinking about something. Example: "Living abroad gave me a new perspective on my own country."

The idea: Attention as generosity

1
StopPut your own plans, worries and phone aside.
2
WaitDon't rush to judge, advise or fix.
3
SeeLook at the person as they really are, not as you expect.
4
AskAsk the question love asks: "What are you going through?"

See it in action

Here is the idea in action. Maya's friend says, "I didn't get the job." One reply: "Oh no! The same thing happened to me last year, let me tell you..." Now the talk is about the speaker. Another reply: "You must be so disappointed. What happened?" Then Maya waits, without fixing anything, and lets her friend find the words. The second reply costs nothing but attention. For Weil, that is exactly why it is so rare, and so generous.

Your turn

Now you: explain Weil's idea of attention to a friend who has never heard of it. Speak for about a minute. Use the starters if they help.

Simone Weil believed that...Real attention means...For example, when a friend...She called attention generosity because...
Simone Weil believed that paying real attention to another person is one of the rarest things we can do. Real attention means putting yourself aside, waiting, and seeing the person as they really are. For example, when a friend shares bad news, you ask what they're going through instead of talking about yourself. She called attention generosity because it gives the other person something precious: your whole mind.

Connect

Compare

Marx and Simone Weil: Marx wrote about factory workers mostly from books, libraries and his desk. Weil went into the factory and worked on the machines herself. Does living an experience give you a better right to write about it?
Marx studied the whole economic system and its history; Weil wanted to feel the work in her own body. Her experience gave her details about fear, tiredness and dignity that theories can miss, but theories can show a bigger picture that one worker cannot see.
Arendt and Simone Weil: Arendt said great harm can grow when people stop thinking. Weil said goodness begins with real attention. Are thinking and attention the same thing, or different?
They are close: both mean not living on autopilot. Arendt's thinking is a conversation with yourself about what you do; Weil's attention is turning your whole mind towards another person. One looks inward, the other outward.

Remember earlier thinkers

No looking back. Answer from memory first, then check.

From Hannah Arendt

What did Arendt mean by "the banality of evil"?

That terrible harm can be done by ordinary people who don't think about what they are doing, not only by monsters.
From Jean-Paul Sartre

What did Sartre mean by "existence comes before essence"?

That humans have no fixed nature or plan at birth: we exist first, then make ourselves through our choices.
From Søren Kierkegaard

What did Kierkegaard mean by 'the leap'?

Big personal choices, like faith or love, can't be proved first. When reasons run out, you have to choose and commit, and that is how you become yourself.
From Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz

What did Leibniz mean by 'the best of all possible worlds'?

That God looked at every possible world and chose the best one. Even with its evil, a world with free people is better than any other.

Final review

From memory, out loud. Then check.

1. Why did Simone Weil call attention "the rarest and purest form of generosity"?

Because giving someone your full, patient attention, and seeing them as they really are, is rare and is a real gift.

2. Why did Weil take a year off to work in factories?

To share and understand workers' real lives, not just write about them from outside.

3. What question did Weil say love asks another person?

"What are you going through?"

4. What does 'empathy' mean? Give an example.

Understanding and sharing another person's feelings. For example: feeling for a worried colleague because you've been in the same situation.
