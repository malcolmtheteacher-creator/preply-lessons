Nikola Tesla

The inventor who imagined the electric world — and died poor and almost forgotten.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A boy with a special mind

Nikola Tesla was born in 1856 in a small village called Smiljan. It is in Croatia today. His family were Serbs, and his father was a priest.

Tesla had a very special mind. He could do difficult sums in his head very fast. He could see a whole machine in his mind before he built it. He loved one big idea: a new kind of electricity called "alternating current", or AC.

In 1884, he travelled to the United States. He had almost no money. He went to work for Thomas Edison, the most famous inventor in America. But Edison liked his own kind of electricity, not Tesla's new idea. The two men did not agree, and soon Tesla left. For a time he was so poor that he dug holes in the ground for money. But he did not stop believing in his dream.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was special about Tesla's mind?

2. Why did Tesla leave Edison?

1. He could do difficult sums in his head very fast, and he could see a whole machine in his mind before he built it.

2. Edison liked his own kind of electricity, not Tesla's new idea (AC). The two men did not agree, so Tesla left.

Talk about it:
  • Tesla came to a new country with no money, but he believed in his dream. Is it good to believe in a dream like this?

Part 2 — The electricity war

Tesla made a new motor that worked with AC electricity. AC was better than Edison's electricity for one big reason: it could travel a very long way along thin wires. A rich businessman, George Westinghouse, liked Tesla's idea and bought it.

Now there was a big fight: Tesla's electricity (AC) against Edison's electricity (DC). People called it the "War of the Currents". Edison said AC was dangerous, but Tesla was right. In the 1890s, engineers used Tesla's AC to take power from the great waterfall at Niagara Falls and send it to a city. It worked! Tesla was sure the world would use his electricity, and now it did.

Tesla had many more ideas. He made big machines that shot electric lights into the air. He worked on radio. In 1898, he showed people a small boat that he could control from far away, with no wires — like magic. People could not believe their eyes.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why was Tesla's electricity (AC) better?

2. What did Tesla show people in 1898?

1. It could travel a very long way along thin wires. (Edison's electricity could not.)

2. A small boat that he could control from far away, with no wires.

Talk about it:
  • Tesla showed people ideas that were like magic. Do you like new technology, or does it sometimes worry you?

Part 3 — The big dream and the sad end

Tesla had a very big dream. He started to build a huge tower called Wardenclyffe. He wanted to send electricity through the air to all the world — free power for everybody! He believed wireless power was going to change everything.

But it was too difficult. His rich helper, J. P. Morgan, stopped giving money. The tower was never finished, and later people took it down. After this, Tesla had less and less money. He became a little strange: he was afraid of germs, and he loved the pigeons (birds) in the park. He lived alone in a hotel.

Tesla died in 1943, in a hotel room in New York. He was poor, and most people had forgotten him. But the story did not end there. Many years later, people remembered him again. Now we know that Tesla saw the future before everyone else. Today his name is famous — there is even a famous car company called Tesla!

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did Tesla want his big tower to do? Why did it fail?

2. How did people feel about Tesla after he died?

1. He wanted to send electricity through the air to all the world, with no wires. It failed because it was too difficult and his helper, J. P. Morgan, stopped giving money.

2. At first they forgot him. But many years later, they remembered him again and saw that he was a genius who knew the future.

Talk about it:
  • Tesla died poor and forgotten, but now he is famous. Is this a happy ending or a sad one?

Grammar: 'going to' and 'would' (the future in the past)

This story is about a man who dreamed about the future. To say what he thought in the past about the future, we use two forms.

was/were going to = a plan or a strong belief in the past: "Tesla believed his electricity was going to change the world."
would = a prediction in the past about the future: "Tesla was sure the world would use his electricity."
We use these forms when we tell a story in the past and we talk about what someone thought would happen next.

1. Choose the right form

1. Tesla believed his new motor (going to / change) the world.

2. He was sure the world one day (use) his electricity.

3. Tesla thought the big tower (going to / send) free power to everybody.

1. was going to change

2. would use

3. was going to send

2. Tell a short part of the story

Put the verbs in the right form ('was/were going to' or 'would'). Then read it out loud.

Tesla had a big dream. He was sure that one day people (have) free electricity. He believed his tower (going to / work). He told everyone that wireless power (change) the whole world. But sadly, the dream (never / come) true in his life.

would have · was going to work · would change · would never come

We use these forms to say what Tesla, in the past, thought about the future.
