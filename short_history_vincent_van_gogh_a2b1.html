Vincent van Gogh

A painter the world did not want — and then could not stop loving.

Today: tell the story of Vincent van Gogh, and talk about fame that comes too late. Grammar: "I wish" and "If only" for regret about the past.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A late start

Vincent van Gogh was born in 1853 in the Netherlands. For many years, he could not find his place in life. He tried different jobs — he sold art, he taught, and he worked for the church. But he was not happy, and every job ended badly.

Then, when he was twenty-seven, he made a big decision. He decided to become a painter. He had almost no money and very little training, but he worked very hard. He learned to draw and then to paint.

One person always believed in him: his younger brother, Theo. Theo lived in Paris and sold art. He sent Vincent money, paint, and kind letters, month after month. Without Theo, Vincent could not have painted at all. The two brothers wrote hundreds of letters to each other.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What jobs did Vincent try before he became a painter? How old was he when he started painting?

2. How did Theo help his brother?

1. He sold art, he taught, and he worked for the church — but every job ended badly. He started painting when he was twenty-seven.

2. Theo sent him money, paint, and kind letters every month, so Vincent could paint.

Talk about it:
  • Vincent found the right job only after many years and many failures. Is it ever too late to start something new?

Part 2 — Hard work and hard times

Now Vincent painted all the time. In about ten years, he made around two thousand pictures! Some of them are very famous today, like Sunflowers and The Starry Night. His colours were bright and strong, and they grew more beautiful every year.

In 1888, Vincent moved to the south of France, to a town called Arles. He loved the light and the colours there. He rented a small house and painted it yellow — people call it the "Yellow House". He had a dream: a place where artists could live and work together. He asked another painter, Paul Gauguin, to come and live with him.

But the two men were very different, and they argued a lot. Vincent was also becoming very ill in his mind. After one bad argument, he had a breakdown — a very difficult time for his health — and Gauguin left. The next year, Vincent went to a special hospital to rest and get better. Even there, he kept painting beautiful pictures. But sadly, almost nobody was buying his work.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How many pictures did Vincent make? What are two famous ones?

2. What was the "Yellow House", and what was his dream?

1. About two thousand pictures in around ten years. Two famous ones are Sunflowers and The Starry Night.

2. It was a small house in Arles that he painted yellow. His dream was a place where artists could live and work together.

Talk about it:
  • Vincent painted some of his best pictures when his life was very hard. Why do you think difficult times can sometimes help people create?

Part 3 — Fame that came too late

In 1890, Vincent moved to a small village near Paris, to be closer to Theo. He was still painting every day. But he was very ill and very tired, and in July of that year, he died. He was only thirty-seven years old. The sad truth is that he died thinking he was a failure. In all his life, he sold almost no paintings.

Theo was very, very sad. He died only six months later. For a moment, it looked like the whole story would end here — two brothers gone, and a room full of paintings that nobody wanted.

But one woman changed everything. Her name was Jo, and she was Theo's wife. She kept all of Vincent's paintings and all the brothers' letters. Many people told her to forget them. Instead, she worked for years to show his art to the world. She sent his paintings to art shows, and she published the letters so people could know him. Slowly, people began to love his work. Today, Vincent van Gogh is one of the most famous painters in the world, and his paintings sell for millions. It is very sad: the man who could not sell one painting became famous — but only after his death.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did Vincent feel about his life when he died?

2. Who made Vincent famous after his death, and how?

1. He died thinking he was a failure, because he sold almost no paintings in his life.

2. Jo, Theo's wife. She kept his paintings and letters, sent his art to shows, and published the letters, so the world learned to love his work.

Talk about it:
  • Vincent became famous only because one person did not give up on him. How important are the people who believe in us?

Grammar: I wish / If only

This is a story full of regret — things we wish were different. We use I wish and If only to talk about this.

To talk about a sad situation now, we use the past simple: "I wish he was famous when he was alive."
To talk about a regret in the past (something we wanted to be different), we use had + past participle: "I wish people had bought his paintings." "If only Theo had lived longer."
If only means almost the same as I wish, but it is stronger.

1. Complete the sentence

1. I wish people (buy) his paintings when he was alive.

2. If only Theo (live) long enough to see his brother become famous.

1. had bought (a regret about the past — had + past participle)

2. had lived

2. Write your own regret

Finish these sentences about the story. Then read them out loud.

1. I wish Vincent happy in his life.

2. If only the world his pictures sooner.

Example answers — your words can be different:

1. was / had been (e.g. "I wish Vincent was happy in his life.")

2. had loved / had seen (e.g. "If only the world had loved his pictures sooner.")
