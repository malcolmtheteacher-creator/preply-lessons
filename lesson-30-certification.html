The hall, again

Imagine you are standing in the doorway of a great hall. It is a still morning. The roof is whole, the columns are tall, and the long tables are laid for a banquet that will never happen. It is the hall from the oldest story in this course, where a poet once stood in the door and a roof fell behind him.

You are not here to mourn. You are here to walk. Because that is what the poet did, and what this whole course has taught you to do. Order and place. If you can walk through a place, you can walk through anything you have put in it.

What did the poet's story teach at the beginning of the course?
That remembering where things are, in a place we know, lets us recall them in order.
Talk about it. What is the most surprising thing you have learned about your own memory in this course?

You begin at the front door, where you learned how memory works and how a picture sticks. You pass the body, the building and the chain. You cross into the counting house, where numbers became pictures, then people, then scenes. You enter the market square, with its names, cards, talks and maps. You climb to the library, with its words, verbs, lists and formulas.

Then the tower, where you learned to go fast, to keep dates, to pack three digits into a syllable, to join palaces, and to give ideas a body. And finally the great hall, with its speeches, its books, its competitions, its habits. It is a long walk, and you have taken it.

Name the six wings of the house in order.
The Front Door, the Counting House, the Market Square, the Library, the Tower, the Great Hall.
Talk about it. Which wing was your favourite? Which would you like to go back to?

A last word, as old as the first. The Roman teachers said that memory is a treasure-house of ideas. A house is no use unless you live in it. What you have now is a method, and a method grows with use.

So this last room has three jobs. To walk the whole house once. To test you on a mixture of everything. And to help you plan what to do in the next thirty days, so that what you have learned becomes a habit.
