Giving a Quote

You have seen the house. Now the customer wants one thing: the price. Time to say your number clearly, say what is included, and answer "that's a bit expensive" — without giving your work away.

Part 2 of a set. ← Part 1: The First Enquiry (the phone call)

The Scene 🎬

You run a small cleaning business. Last week a new customer phoned you. Today you are at their house. You have walked around: three bedrooms, two bathrooms, a big kitchen, a friendly dog. Now you are standing in the kitchen, and the customer says: "So — how much?"

This is the moment many people find hard. Saying a price out loud feels strange, especially in another language. Some people say the number very quietly. Some say "sorry" before the price. Some give a discount before the customer asks for one!

You do not need to do any of that. Say the price clearly, say what the customer gets for it, and stay calm when they say "hmm, that's a bit expensive". Most customers say that. It is normal. Today you practise what to say next.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. When you buy something and the price feels high, what do you say? Do you ask for a lower price, or do you just walk away?
  2. Is it easy or difficult for you to say a price out loud? Why do you think that is?

Who's who

You
The Cleaner 🧽

You own the business. You know your price and you know it is fair. You explain it clearly, you are kind, and you do not say sorry for your price.

The other person
The Customer 🏠

They like you and they want a cleaner. But they have a budget, and they will ask for a better price. They are polite — but they will try!

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Watch what the cleaner does when the customer says "that's a bit expensive". They do not drop the price straight away. They explain, they wait, and then they offer a small deal. Watch how, not just what.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for how the price is said: clearly, and only once.

YouIt's eighteen pounds an hour. For a house this size, I need three hours a week. So that's fifty-four pounds a week.
CustomerOK. And what do I get for that?
YouThat includes the kitchen, both bathrooms, all the floors, and dusting in every room.
CustomerAnd the oven? And the windows?

CustomerHmm. I like you, but it's a bit over my budget. Is that the best price you can offer?
YouThe price is good for this area. I bring all my own products, and I always come on the same day.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases for giving a price. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say the number and stop. "It's eighteen pounds an hour." Then wait. Do not say "sorry", do not say "but", do not say "is that OK?" A clear price, said once, with a calm voice, sounds like a professional. A price said three times, quietly, sounds like a question.

💷 Saying the price

Phrase 1

The customer asks "how much?" You want to say your price per hour, clearly and simply. Complete it: "It's eighteen pounds ___ ___."

"It's eighteen pounds an hour."
"An hour" = for each hour. Short and clear. Then add the total: "So that's fifty-four pounds a week." Say the number, then stop and wait — do not fill the silence.
Phrase 2

The customer asks "what do I get for that?" You want to list what is in the price. Which one is the clear, professional way?

A. "Everything, more or less. You know, the normal things."
B. "That includes the kitchen, the bathrooms, and all the floors."
C. "What do you want me to do?"
B — "That includes the kitchen, the bathrooms, and all the floors."
"That includes…" tells the customer exactly what they get. A list of three or four things sounds like a plan. A is vague, and C makes the customer do your job. The opposite is just as useful: "The oven is extra."

🛡️ When they say "too expensive"

Phrase 3

The customer says "it's a bit over my budget". You do not want to drop the price at once. You want to say, calmly, that the price is normal. Put the words in order:

for this area.is goodthe price
"The price is good for this area."
Calm, friendly, and not a fight. Then add one reason why: "I bring my own products", "I always come on the same day". You are not arguing — you are explaining.
Phrase 4

The customer asks for a much lower price — fifteen instead of eighteen. You want to say "no" softly, not with a hard "no". Which one is polite but clear?

A. "No. That's not possible."
B. "OK, fine, fifteen then."
C. "I'm not sure if I can do that."
C — "I'm not sure if I can do that."
A soft "no" that keeps the door open. A sounds cold, and B gives your money away in one second! After this phrase, you can offer something smaller — see the next phrase.

🤝 Offering a small deal

Phrase 5

You can lower the price a little — but only if the customer gives you something too: every week, not "sometimes". Complete it: "___ you book every week, I ___ do seventeen."

"If you book every week, I can do seventeen."
"If you…, I can…" — you give something, they give something. This is how a good deal works. Other examples: "If you pay on the day, I can do…", "If you book two hours more, I can…"
Phrase 6

The customer wants sixteen, you want eighteen. You want to suggest a price between the two. Say it out loud first, then check.

"Maybe we can meet in the middle."
"Meet in the middle" = a price between your number and their number. Customers say this too: "Could we meet in the middle?" — now you know what it means, and you can say "yes" or "seventeen is the best I can do".

✅ Closing the deal

Phrase 7

You have agreed a price. Before you finish, you want to say all the details back, so there are no surprises later. Complete it: "So, just to ___ — three hours every Thursday, seventeen an hour."

"So, just to confirm — three hours every Thursday, seventeen an hour."
"Just to confirm" = let's check we agree. Say the day, the hours, the price, and what is extra. Then send the same thing by email. A customer who hears it AND reads it never says "but you said…"
Phrase 8

The customer says "yes, perfect". Now you want to close warmly — short and happy. Which one?

A. "It's a deal! Thank you very much."
B. "OK then. Bye."
C. "Are you sure? You can still say no."
A — "It's a deal! Thank you very much."
"It's a deal" closes the conversation with a smile. Then say the next step: "I'll see you on Thursday." B is cold, and C opens the door again — never do that after a "yes"!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work for any price — a cleaning job, a car, a room to rent. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"It's ___ an hour." — What is your real price? Say it out loud, clearly, once.

"That includes ___." — What is in your normal price? List three or four things.

"If you ___, I can ___." — What small deal can you offer? What do you want back?

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole quote out loud — the price → what's included → what's extra → "the price is good for this area" → the small deal → confirm → "it's a deal!" When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Change places. A cleaner has just looked at your office. You have a budget. Be polite — but try for a better price!

CleanerThat includes all the desks, the kitchen, the toilets, and the floors. The windows are extra.

The Twist

💷 "My last cleaner was cheaper"

One more time — no help this round. You are the cleaner again. You say your price, and the customer says: "My last cleaner did it for twelve pounds an hour." Do not drop your price. Stay friendly, say why your price is good, and offer one small deal — or say "seventeen is the best I can do" and wait. Speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said my price clearly, once, and I did not say "sorry".
  • I said what is included, and what is extra.
  • When they said "too expensive", I stayed calm and gave a reason.
  • I said a soft "no" and then offered a small deal with "If you…, I can…".
  • I confirmed all the details and closed with "It's a deal!"

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Bigger Picture 🔭

Here is a strange thing about prices. When you say "sorry, it's eighteen pounds an hour", the customer hears: "even she thinks it's too much." When you say "it's eighteen pounds an hour" and stop, the customer hears: "this is what it costs."

The words are almost the same. The difference is the "sorry", and the silence after the number. Many people who work for themselves lose money every week — not because customers say no, but because they drop the price before anyone asks.

A fair price, said clearly, with a friendly face, is not rude. It is the most honest thing in the conversation.
