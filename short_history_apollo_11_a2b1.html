The Moon Landing

The day people first walked on the Moon.

Today: tell the story of the first Moon landing, and talk about space. Grammar: the future with will and going to.

Key words — match them first

Before we read, let's learn the important words. Match each word (1–8) to its meaning (A–H). Say your answers out loud — "I think 1 is D" — then check.

Words

  1. a rocket
  2. an astronaut
  3. the Moon
  4. to launch
  5. to land
  6. a spaceship
  7. to walk
  8. famous

Meanings

  • A. to send a rocket up into space
  • B. a person who travels in space
  • C. a vehicle that flies in space
  • D. a tall machine that flies up into space with fire and smoke
  • E. known by very many people
  • F. the big round light in the night sky that goes around the Earth
  • G. to come down safely onto the ground
  • H. to move on your feet, step by step

1 = D (a rocket) · 2 = B (an astronaut) · 3 = F (the Moon) · 4 = A (to launch)

5 = G (to land) · 6 = C (a spaceship) · 7 = H (to walk) · 8 = E (famous)

Now talk — out loud

Before we read:
  • Which words are about space? Which word means "very well known"?

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A big promise

In 1961, the American president, John F. Kennedy, made a big promise. He said that America would put a man on the Moon before 1970.

This was very difficult. No country had ever done it before. Many people did not think it was possible. But thousands of people in America started to work very hard to make the promise come true.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did President Kennedy promise in 1961?

2. Was this an easy thing to do? Why / why not?

1. He said America would put a man on the Moon before 1970.

2. No, it was very difficult. No country had done it before, and many people did not think it was possible.

Talk about it:
  • Kennedy made a very big promise. Is it a good idea to promise something difficult? Why / why not?

Part 2 — The journey to the Moon

Eight years later, in July 1969, a rocket called Apollo 11 went to the Moon. There were three astronauts: Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin and Michael Collins.

On 20 July 1969, a small spaceship landed on the Moon. Armstrong and Aldrin were inside it. The third astronaut, Michael Collins, did not land on the Moon. He waited in another spaceship above the Moon.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was the name of the rocket, and how many astronauts were on it?

2. Who landed on the Moon, and who did not?

1. The rocket was called Apollo 11. There were three astronauts.

2. Armstrong and Aldrin landed on the Moon. Michael Collins did not — he waited in a spaceship above the Moon.

Talk about it:
  • Michael Collins went all the way to the Moon, but he did not walk on it. How do you think he felt?

Part 3 — The first steps, and home again

Armstrong and Aldrin walked on the Moon. When Armstrong stepped onto the Moon, he said the famous words: "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." About 650 million people watched on TV.

Then the astronauts came home. They came home safely on 24 July 1969. Kennedy's promise came true.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What famous words did Armstrong say? How many people watched on TV?

2. When did the astronauts come home? Did Kennedy's promise come true?

1. "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." About 650 million people watched on TV.

2. They came home safely on 24 July 1969. Yes, Kennedy's promise came true.

Talk about it:
  • 650 million people watched the same moment on TV. Why do you think it was so important to so many people?

Grammar: will & going to

We use will and going to to talk about the future.

will = a promise, or something we think is true: "America will put a man on the Moon."
going to = a plan we have already, or something we can see is coming: "The astronauts are going to land on the Moon tomorrow."
In a story from the past, "will" often becomes "would": Kennedy said America would put a man on the Moon.

1. will / would

1. Kennedy said America (put) a man on the Moon. (a past promise)

2. "I think Apollo 11 (be) a great success!" (now)

3. He promised the astronauts (come) home safely. (a past promise)

1. would put

2. will be

3. would come

2. going to

These are the plans for the day of the landing. Use going to.

1. The rocket (launch) in the morning.

2. Armstrong and Aldrin (walk) on the Moon.

3. Michael Collins (wait) in the spaceship.

1. is going to launch

2. are going to walk

3. is going to wait

Task B · Space (3 minutes)

Task C · Something amazing (3 minutes)
