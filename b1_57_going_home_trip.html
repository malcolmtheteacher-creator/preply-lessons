Going Home: A Trip Back

B1+ Level · Past Tenses, Storytelling & Travel Talk

Warm-up

You are going home soon. Even though the trip is in the future, we mostly talk about going home using the past tense — because home is full of memories.

First Questions

1. When was the last time you went home?

How long ago? How long did you stay?

2. What was the first thing you did when you arrived?

Did you eat something? See someone? Smell something familiar?

3. What did you miss most while you were away?

A food? A place? A person? A sound?

4. Did anything surprise you the last time you were home?

Something changed? Something new? Something gone?

What we'll do today:
  • Use used to to describe how your hometown was before
  • Use the past simple and past continuous to tell a short travel story
  • Talk about what you're packing and why — tied to past memories

The Way It Was

When we describe how a place was in the past — especially how it was different from today — we love using used to.

How It Works

"There used to be a small market on this corner."
"We used to play in the street after school."
"It didn't use to be so crowded."
"Did you use to live near the centre?"

The Pattern

+  subject + used to + verb (base form)

−  subject + didn't use to + verb (base form)

?  Did + subject + use to + verb (base form)?

Fix the Sentence

Each sentence has one mistake. Find it.

1. "There used to being a beautiful old tree in our garden."

used to be — we use the base form of the verb, not -ing.

2. "I didn't used to like olives, but now I love them."

didn't use to — no -d after "use" in the negative.

3. "Did your grandmother used to tell you stories?"

use to — in questions, "did" already shows the past, so "use" has no -d.

A Story From Home

To tell a good travel story, you need two past tenses working together: past simple (for the main events) and past continuous (for the background).

The Two Tenses Together

"I was walking through the old market when I saw my old neighbour."
"While we were eating dinner, the lights went out."
"The sun was setting as we arrived at the house."

The Trick

Past continuous = the long action in the background (was/were + -ing).

Past simple = the short action that interrupts or happens in that moment.

Choose the Right Tense

1. I down the street when I my old teacher.

was walking … saw

2. While my grandmother , I at the kitchen table.

was cooking … sat (or was sitting — both can work if the sitting was a long action too).

3. We at the airport when it .

landed … was raining — the raining was the background to landing.

Tell Your Own Mini-Story

Think of one small moment from your last trip home. Tell it in 4-5 sentences using both past tenses. Start when you're ready.

Story starters: "Last time I went home…" / "I'll never forget the time I…" / "I remember one evening…"
Speaking time: 2:00

Packing Your Suitcase

Even what you pack tells a story. Most of the things in your bag have a reason from the past — gifts you bought because you remembered, clothes you missed wearing, foods you can't find anywhere else.

Useful Vocabulary

Click each card to see the meaning.

a souvenir A small gift you bring back to remember a trip.
homesick Feeling sad because you miss your home.
to catch up with To meet someone you haven't seen for a long time and share news.
to crave To want something very strongly — often food.
jet-lagged Tired after a long flight, because of the time difference.
to settle in To feel comfortable in a new (or returned) place.
a hand-me-down Something (often clothes or a gift) passed from one person to another.
to lose track of time To enjoy something so much you don't notice the hours passing.

Show & Tell — Three Things

Pretend you're already packing. Choose three things you're definitely taking. For each one, explain:

  • What it is
  • Who it's for — or who gave it to you
  • A short memory connected to it (use past tense!)
Example: "I'm taking a small notebook for my cousin. She used to write little stories when we were kids. The last time I saw her, she asked me to bring something to write in — so this is for her."
Quick speaking task: Choose one item now and describe it in 3-4 sentences. Use at least one used to sentence and one past simple sentence.

Discussion

Bigger questions. Take your time and give longer answers.

Conversation Questions

1. Has your hometown changed a lot in the last 10 years?

What used to be there? What is there now? Do you like the changes?

2. What is the strangest thing about visiting home after living somewhere else?

The food? The pace of life? How people see you now?

3. Is there a smell, a sound, or a taste that always means "home" to you?

Can you describe it? When did you first notice it?

4. Do people change when they move away from home? In what way?

Have you changed? Do your family say you have?

5. Some people say "you can never really go home again". What do you think they mean?

Do you agree? Why or why not?

6. If you had to describe your country in three words to someone who had never been, what would they be?

And which word would you put first?

To stretch your English:
  • Try to use at least one used to sentence per answer.
  • When you tell a story, mix past simple and past continuous.
  • Don't be afraid of long answers. The teacher is listening, not waiting to interrupt.
