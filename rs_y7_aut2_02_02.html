Encounter

Let's build the Trinity concept from the ground up.

KEY TERM

Trinity

The Christian doctrine that God is one being in three persons: the Father, the Son (Jesus Christ), and the Holy Spirit. Each is fully God, but there is only one God.

The three persons of the Trinity

God the Father

The creator of all things. Transcendent, all-powerful, eternal.

God the Son (Jesus)

God incarnate — God made flesh. Born of Mary, lived on Earth, died and rose again.

God the Holy Spirit

The presence of God in the world today. Guides believers, gives spiritual gifts, empowers the church.

Scripture sources

"As soon as Jesus was baptized, he went up out of the water. At that moment heaven was opened, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and alighting on him. And a voice from heaven said, 'This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased.'" Matthew 3:16-17

Why it matters: All three persons are present at Jesus's baptism — Jesus is being baptized, the Holy Spirit descends, and God the Father speaks.

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. Through him all things were made." John 1:1-3

Why it matters: "The Word" is Jesus. He is described as with God and as God, suggesting both unity and distinction.

"In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. Now the earth was formless and empty, darkness was over the surface of the deep, and the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters." Genesis 1:1-2

Why it matters: God creates (Father), the Spirit of God is present (Holy Spirit). The divine action involves multiple persons.

The Shield of the Trinity (Scutum Fidei)

Medieval Christians created a visual diagram to explain the Trinity:

The Father is God
The Son is God
The Holy Spirit is God
But:
The Father is not the Son
The Son is not the Holy Spirit
The Holy Spirit is not the Father
And:
There is one God

The Shield of the Trinity became famous across the Christian world. It's a visual way of saying: three in one, one in three — a mystery, not a logical contradiction.

Why all analogies fail

Theologians have tried to explain the Trinity using analogies. Every one breaks down:

Water: Ice, Liquid, Steam

The idea: Water exists in three forms.

Why it fails: Water is only one of these at a time. But the Trinity says each person is fully God at the same time.

Egg: Shell, White, Yolk

The idea: Three parts, one egg.

Why it fails: The three parts make up the whole. But in the Trinity, each person is the whole God, not a part.

Sun: Light, Heat, Radiation

The idea: One sun radiates three things.

Why it fails: These are effects of the sun, not distinct persons who act separately.

What theologians say instead

Christian theologians accept that the Trinity is a mystery — something beyond human reason to fully comprehend. If you fully understand it, it's not the Trinity.

Not all Christians accept the Trinity the same way. Unitarians (a small Christian group) reject it and believe God is singular. But the Trinity has been mainstream since the Council of Nicaea in 325 CE.

Good analogies capture:
• Three distinct realities that are somehow one
• Each part is full and complete, not partial
• They're co-equal

Common pitfalls:
• Partialism: Three parts that make up the whole
• Modalism: Three modes or aspects of the same thing
• Arianism: The Son is subordinate to the Father
