2. Encounter

Let's build the Trinity concept from the ground up.

KEY TERM

Trinity (noun)

The Christian doctrine that God is one being in three persons: the Father, the Son (Jesus Christ), and the Holy Spirit. Each is fully God, but there is only one God. This is foundational to Christian theology and has been debated by theologians for 2,000 years.

The three persons of the Trinity

God the Father

The creator of all things. Transcendent, all-powerful, eternal. The "first person" of the Trinity.

God the Son (Jesus)

God incarnate — God made flesh. Born of Mary, lived on Earth, died and rose again. The "second person" of the Trinity.

God the Holy Spirit

The presence of God in the world today. Guides believers, gives spiritual gifts, empowers the church. The "third person" of the Trinity.

Scripture sources: evidence for the Trinity in the Bible

"As soon as Jesus was baptized, he went up out of the water. At that moment heaven was opened, and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and alighting on him. And a voice from heaven said, 'This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well pleased.'" Matthew 3:16-17 (The Baptism of Jesus)

Why it matters: All three persons are present at Jesus's baptism — Jesus is being baptized, the Holy Spirit descends, and God the Father speaks. This is one of the clearest "proof texts" for the Trinity.

"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. Through him all things were made." John 1:1-3 (The Incarnation)

Why it matters: "The Word" is Jesus. He is described as with God and as God, which suggests both unity and distinction.

"In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. Now the earth was formless and empty, darkness was over the surface of the deep, and the Spirit of God was hovering over the waters." Genesis 1:1-2 (Creation)

Why it matters: God creates (Father), the Spirit of God is present (Holy Spirit). The divine action involves multiple persons.

The Shield of the Trinity (Scutum Fidei)

Medieval Christians created a visual diagram to explain the Trinity. It shows the logical relationships:

The Father is God
The Son is God
The Holy Spirit is God
But:
The Father is not the Son
The Son is not the Holy Spirit
The Holy Spirit is not the Father
And:
There is one God

The Shield of the Trinity became so famous that it appears in churches, prayer books, and theological writings across the Christian world. It's a visual way of saying: three in one, one in three — a mystery, not a logical contradiction.

Why all analogies fail

Theologians have tried to explain the Trinity using analogies. Every single one breaks down. Here are the most famous:

Water: Ice, Liquid, Steam

The idea: Water exists in three forms, but it's always H₂O.

Why it fails: Water is only one of these at a time. Ice isn't liquid. But the Trinity says each person is fully God at the same time.

Egg: Shell, White, Yolk

The idea: Three parts, one egg.

Why it fails: The three parts make up the whole. But in the Trinity, each person (Father, Son, Spirit) is the whole God, not a part of God.

Sun: Light, Heat, Radiation

The idea: The sun radiates three things, but it's one sun.

Why it fails: Light, heat, and radiation are effects of the sun, not distinct persons. The Trinity's three persons are not effects — they're real persons who act separately.

What theologians say instead

Christian theologians accept that the Trinity is a mystery — not a contradiction, but something beyond human reason to fully comprehend. As one theologian put it: "If you fully understand the Trinity, it's not the Trinity — it's a false god you've invented."

Important note: Not all Christians accept the Trinity in the same way. Unitarians (a small Christian group) reject the Trinity and believe God is singular. But the Trinity has been the mainstream Christian position since the Council of Nicaea in 325 CE.

Trinity Detective: Which person is being described?

Read these Bible passages. For each, identify which person of the Trinity (Father, Son, or Holy Spirit) is being emphasized:

1. "He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation. For in him all things were created."

Which person?

The Son (Jesus). He is described as the image of God and creator.

2. "Your Father in heaven is not willing that any of these little ones should be lost."

Which person?

The Father. Explicitly called "Your Father in heaven."

3. "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor."

Which person?

The Holy Spirit. The Spirit anoints (empowers) the speaker (Jesus).

Good analogies capture:
• Three distinct realities that are somehow one
• Each part is full and complete, not partial
• They're co-equal, not hierarchical

Common pitfalls:
• Partialism: Three parts that make up the whole (like egg parts)
• Modalism: Three modes or aspects of the same thing (like water states)
• Arianism: The Son is subordinate to the Father (historically declared heretical)

The truth is: the Trinity is probably beyond human analogy. But trying to explain it teaches you something about theology.
