The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A poor boy on the grasslands

His name was not Genghis Khan at first. He was born about 1162, and his name was Temüjin. He was born on the cold, open grasslands near Mongolia. His people did not live in towns. They were riders who moved their tents from place to place, with their animals. Different tribes lived there, and they often fought each other.

When Temüjin was about nine, a different tribe killed his father with poison. After this, his own tribe left the family alone. They thought a mother and her small children were too weak to help. Life was very hard. His mother kept the children alive with fish and wild food. Later, an old friend caught Temüjin and made him a slave. But one night Temüjin ran away and hid in a river until the men stopped looking for him.

Temüjin learned one big lesson: one person alone was nothing. You needed friends you could trust. So he made friends slowly and carefully. Men who were loyal to him and fought well got a good place in his group — it did not matter what family they came from. Men who turned against him got no second chance.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What two bad things happened to Temüjin's family when he was about nine?

2. How did Temüjin choose the men around him? Why was this different?

1. A different tribe killed his father with poison, and then his own tribe left the family alone to live by themselves.

2. He chose men who were loyal and fought well, not men from rich or important families. This was different because most leaders only trusted their own family.

Talk about it:
  • Temüjin had a very hard life as a child. Do you think a hard start can make someone a stronger person? Why?

Part 2 — One leader of all the tribes

For more than twenty years, Temüjin fought the other tribes of the grasslands. Sometimes they were his friends, but often they were his enemies. He won because he was clever, not only brave. He put his soldiers into clear groups — tens, hundreds and thousands — so orders moved fast. He also used a clever trick: his riders pretended to run away in fear, the enemy chased them, and then his riders turned around and beat them.

In 1206 he did something no one had done before. He brought all the fighting tribes of Mongolia together under one leader. The chiefs gave him a new name: Genghis Khan, which means something like "great ruler". He made new laws to keep the people together. The laws said everyone must be loyal. They stopped people from stealing animals or taking women. They kept messengers and traders safe. And, surprising for that time, the laws let people follow any religion they liked.

Then Genghis Khan looked at other lands. His army could ride very fast and live for days on dried meat and horse milk. No other army could move like this. He turned south to the rich lands of China, and west to central Asia. The big conquests began.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Name two things that made Genghis Khan's army better than the others.

2. What did his new laws do? Say one thing.

1. Any two: he put his soldiers into clear groups of tens, hundreds and thousands; he used the trick of pretending to run away; his riders were very fast and could live on dried meat and horse milk.

2. Any one: the laws said everyone must be loyal; they stopped stealing animals and taking women; they kept messengers and traders safe; they let people follow any religion.

Talk about it:
  • Genghis Khan made good laws but was very hard in war. Can a leader be a good lawmaker and a cruel fighter at the same time? What do you think?

Part 3 — The world he left behind

One story shows the kind of leader he became. In 1218, Genghis Khan sent traders, and then messengers, to a rich empire in central Asia. But the local ruler killed his traders, and the king there cut off the head of his messenger and sent it back. Genghis Khan was very angry. His army came down on the empire and destroyed it in only a few years. They surrounded the big cities for a long time, until the people had no food. Many, many people died. Cities that fought back were destroyed completely.

But that is only half of the story. When Genghis Khan died in 1227, he ruled the biggest land empire in all of history. It went from the sea in the east, near China, far to the west. His sons and grandsons made it even bigger. Across this huge empire there was a strong peace, kept by Mongol soldiers. For the first time, a trader could travel the whole long Silk Road in safety. Goods, ideas and inventions moved between East and West like never before.

His mark on the world is huge. He had so many children and grandchildren that, even today, millions of men may come from his family. Some people call him a hero and the father of their country. Other people call him a cruel man who killed too many. The truth is, he was both: the poor, lonely boy who became the most famous and most powerful conqueror in history.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did Genghis Khan attack the rich empire in central Asia?

2. What good thing did the Mongol peace bring?

1. The empire killed his traders and cut off the head of his messenger. He was very angry, so his army destroyed the empire.

2. It made the lands safe. A trader could travel the whole Silk Road safely, so goods and ideas could move between East and West like never before.

Talk about it:
  • Genghis Khan brought war and death, but also peace and safe roads. When we think about a person from history, how do we decide if they were "good" or "bad"?

Grammar: could & was able to

This is a story about what people could do, and about the hard things one man was able to do. In English we use two ways to talk about ability in the past.

could = a general ability in the past. Something you could do many times, any time.
"Temüjin could ride a horse very well." "The Mongol army could move very fast."
was able to / managed to = one success. One hard thing that happened one time, often with difficulty.
"One night, he was able to escape from the men." "After many years, he managed to unite the tribes."
The easy rule:
• Many times, general → could ("He could ride all day").
• One time, one success → was able to / managed to ("That day, he was able to win").
• For "no" / "not", could not (couldn't) is fine for both: "No army could stop him."

1. could or was able to?

Read the help in brackets. Write could or was able to in the gap. Say your answers out loud, then check.

1. As a boy, Temüjin ride and shoot very well. (general — many times)

2. One night, he escape from the men who held him. (one hard success)

3. The Mongol riders live for days on dried meat. (general — many times)

4. After twenty years, he bring all the tribes together. (one hard success)

1. could (general ability — many times)

2. was able to / managed to (one hard success — not "could")

3. could (general ability — many times)

4. was able to / managed to (one hard success after many years)

2. Finish the sentences about the story

Write could, couldn't or was able to. Then read each sentence out loud.

1. No other army move as fast as the Mongols. (general — "no")

2. The king (not) save his cities from the army. (one failure)

3. With the new laws, traders travel the Silk Road safely. (general — many times)

1. could ("no army could move…" — general, with "no")

2. couldn't / wasn't able to (one failure)

3. could / were able to (general ability after the laws)
