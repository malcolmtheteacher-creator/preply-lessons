The Middle East and North Africa: One Region, Many Worlds

Deserts, big cities, old libraries and the sea. There are many countries and many cultures.

Talk first:
  • Which countries do you know in this region?

  • Is it one place or many places?

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. The Sahara is a big desert.
desert (n) — a very dry place with sand or stones and little rain. Example: “The Sahara is a big desert.”
2. Many people live on the coast.
coast (n) — the land next to the sea. Example: “Many people live on the coast.”
3. The Nile is a famous river.
river (n) — a long line of water that flows to the sea. Example: “The Nile is a famous river.”
4. People speak many languages in this region.
language (n) — the words people speak in a country. Example: “People speak many languages in this region.”
5. There was a famous library in Baghdad.
library (n) — a place with many books. Example: “There was a famous library in Baghdad.”
6. Cairo is a huge city.
city (n) — a very big town. Example: “Cairo is a huge city.”
Talk it through:
  • Which word is new for you? Say a sentence with it.
The stereotype: “The Middle East and North Africa is one place, and everyone is the same.”

The story

Part 1 — Many countries

The region has about twenty countries, from Morocco to Iran. About 500 million people live there. People speak Arabic, Persian, Turkish, Kurdish and other languages. Iranians are not Arab, and Turkish people are not Arab.

Quick check:

1. About how many people live in the region?

2. Are Iranian people Arab?

1. About 500 million.

2. No, they are Persian.

Discuss:
  • Which country do you want to visit?

Part 2 — Old ideas

People wrote for the first time in Mesopotamia, about 5,000 years ago. It was a very early place for cities and farming. Later, there were big libraries in Baghdad and Cairo. The scholars were famous for medicine and mathematics. The old university in Fez was founded by a woman, Fatima al-Fihri.

Quick check:

1. Where was writing first used?

2. Who founded the university in Fez?

1. In Mesopotamia.

2. A woman, Fatima al-Fihri.

Discuss:
  • What was your favourite school subject?

Part 3 — Many faiths, many cities

Most people in the region are Muslim. There are also Christians, Jews and other groups. Most Muslims in the world are not in this region. Two out of three people live in cities, not in the desert. In Egypt, most people live near the Nile.

Quick check:

1. Do most people in the region live in the desert?

2. Where do most Egyptians live?

1. No, they live in cities.

2. Near the Nile.

Discuss:
  • Is your home in a city or a town?

Grammar — was and were

Was + I / he / she / it: “Cairo was a big city.”
Were + you / we / they: “The libraries were famous.”
Negative: wasn't / weren't. Questions: Was it …? Were they …?

1. Choose the word

1. The library in Baghdad very big. (was / were)

2. The scholars very clever. (was / were)

3. The streets busy. (was / were)

1. was

2. were

3. were

2. Make your own

1. Yesterday I was ______.

2. My parents were ______.

3. It wasn't ______ last week.

1. ...at home. / ...at work.

2. ...in the garden. / ...at school.

3. ...sunny. / ...cold.
