Formal Collocations for Business Writing

These word combinations are commonly used in formal business emails.

Common Collocations

1. Excellent + ___________

Answer: Excellent service
Example: "We expected excellent service from our long-standing supplier."

2. __________ supplier

Answer: Reliable supplier
Example: "We had always believed them to be a reliable supplier."

3. Raise ____________

Answer: Raise concerns
Example: "I need to raise concerns about the quality of your service."

4. __________ to inform s/o

Answer: Write to inform someone
Example: "I am writing to inform you of a serious problem with your service."

5. Long-standing__________

Answer: Long-standing relationship
Example: "This is disappointing given our long-standing relationship."

6. _______ s/o's attention to s/t

Answer: Draw someone's attention
Example: "I must draw your attention to a critical issue with your delivery."

7. Timely ___________

Answer: Timely delivery
Example: "We require timely delivery of our orders."

8. ___________ change

Answer: Significant change
Example: "There must be a significant change in your service quality."

Non-Defining Relative Clauses

Use these clauses to add extra information in a formal way. Note: They use commas and are often important for formal business writing.

Correct vs Incorrect

Example 1 (Correct)

"The staff member I spoke to, who was rather rude, could not provide a delivery date."

The clause "who was rather rude" adds extra information about the staff member. Note the commas!

Example 2 (Incorrect)

"Alice Riley said she would deal with the matter immediately, that she didn't."

This is incorrect. You cannot use "that" after a comma. Use "but" instead: "…immediately, but she didn't."

Example 3 (Incorrect - Missing Commas)

"It is disappointing that your services, which were recommended very highly did not meet our expectations."

The clause "which were recommended very highly" needs commas on both sides.

Correct: "…your services, which were recommended very highly, did not meet our expectations."

Example 4 (Correct)

"Several items that we ordered did not arrive, which caused a number of problems."

Here "which" refers to the whole first clause and adds important information.

Rules for Non-Defining Relative Clauses

  • Use commas: Always put commas before and after the relative clause
  • Relative pronouns: Use "who" (people), "which" (things), "where" (places)
  • Cannot use "that": Use "which" or "who" instead in formal writing
  • Information type: These clauses give extra information that isn't essential to the sentence

Examples in Business Writing

  • "Our photocopier, which we purchased last month, has already broken down twice."
  • "The contract, which was agreed upon last year, is clearly not being honoured."
  • "The delivery dates, which were promised in writing, have not been met."
  • "The manager, who promised to resolve this immediately, has not responded."

Problem Scenarios

Choose one of these scenarios to write about in your email.

Scenario 1: Photocopier Breakdown

Your Situation:

Your company purchased a new photocopier three months ago. It has broken down three times already. You want a complete replacement.

Problem: Equipment failure

Desired outcome: Full replacement

Scenario 2: Poor Refurbishment Work

Your Situation:

Your office was refurbished last month. The quality of work is unsatisfactory. Additionally, the company hasn't finished tiling one of the new bathrooms.

Problem: Poor quality work and incomplete project

Desired outcome: Remedial work and completion

Scenario 3: Distribution Contract Issue

Your Situation:

Your factory has a contract with a distribution company. They have repeatedly failed to deliver orders on time. This is affecting your business.

Problem: Late deliveries affecting operations

Desired outcome: On-time delivery commitment

Scenario 4: Defective Product Delivery

Your Situation:

Your company ordered 500 units of a product. 200 units arrived defective. The supplier has been slow to respond to your complaints.

Problem: Defective goods and poor customer service

Desired outcome: Replacement or refund

Scenario 5: Billing Error

Your Situation:

You have been over-charged for the last three months. The billing department has ignored your enquiries. You want the over-charges refunded immediately.

Problem: Billing error and poor communication

Desired outcome: Refund and correction of account

Writing Task: Email to Resolve a Problem

Your Task

Write an email to someone from another organization explaining a problem in your workplace and asking them to solve it.

Step 1: Choose Your Problem

Select one of the scenarios above, or use a real problem from your own workplace. Describe:

  • What is the problem?
  • When did it start?
  • How serious is it?
  • What outcome do you want?

Step 2: Plan Your Email Structure

Your email should have four paragraphs:

Paragraph 1: Introduction and Purpose

State what you're writing about and why. Use formal language like "I am writing to inform you of…" or "I must draw your attention to…"

Paragraph 2: Background Information

Explain the situation in detail. Include dates, products/services involved, and what was expected. Use non-defining relative clauses to add information.

Paragraph 3: The Problem and Impact

Describe the problem clearly. Explain how it affects your business. Use formal collocations like "raise concerns" or "draw attention to."

Paragraph 4: Request for Resolution

State clearly what you want them to do. Be polite but firm. Mention a deadline if appropriate.

Step 3: Use Formal Language

  • Greeting: "Dear [Name]," or "Dear Sir or Madam,"
  • Formal collocations: "excellent service," "raise concerns," "long-standing relationship"
  • Non-defining clauses: Add extra information: "the photocopier, which we purchased in January, has already broken down"
  • Formal phrases: "I must inform you," "I would appreciate," "I look forward to your response"
  • Closing: "Yours faithfully," or "Yours sincerely," followed by your name

Email Template

Dear [Name],

I am writing to inform you of [state the problem]. [Additional background sentence using a relative clause].

[Background information about the situation, including dates and context]. We had been expecting [state expectations].

Unfortunately, [describe the problem and its impact]. This has [explain consequences]. I must therefore raise concerns about [issue].

I would appreciate [state your request clearly]. I look forward to receiving [timeline]. Please contact me if you need any further information.

Yours faithfully,
[Your name]

Success Criteria

Your email is successful if it includes:

  • ✓ Relevant information clearly describing the problem
  • ✓ Clear organization with four paragraphs, each with a specific purpose
  • ✓ Appropriately formal style throughout
  • ✓ Appropriate variety of vocabulary and structures (collocations, relative clauses)
  • ✓ Good grammatical control with no errors that cause misunderstanding
  • ✓ Clear request for resolution with specific outcome desired

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • ❌ Using informal language or contractions ("can't," "they're")
  • ❌ Forgetting commas in non-defining relative clauses
  • ❌ Using "that" instead of "which" in formal writing
  • ❌ Not being specific about what you want
  • ❌ Sounding angry or emotional rather than professional
