Good presentations often:

  • Organize information into clear parts
  • Use words that connect ideas ("First...", "Next...", "Finally...")
  • Use body language and visuals to guide attention

Key Words for Presentations

Click each card to reveal the definition.

Structure The way something is organized or arranged. A presentation structure is the basic pattern you use to arrange your ideas.
Transition A word, phrase, or action that connects one idea to the next. It helps the audience move smoothly from one part to another.
Signal A sign or indication that tells the audience something important. In presentations, signals help audiences understand which ideas are most important.
Signpost A verbal marker that guides your audience. Words like "First," "Second," "In conclusion" are signposts that mark the direction of your talk.
Key point The most important information or idea. In a presentation, key points are the main ideas you want your audience to remember.
Outline A summary showing the main ideas and how they are organized. An outline is a simple plan that shows the structure of your presentation.
Summary A short version of the most important information. A summary at the end reminds your audience of the main ideas.
Audience The people listening to or watching your presentation. Understanding your audience helps you choose the right information and language.

Three Presentation Structures

All effective presentations have three main parts. Here are three common patterns you can use:

Problem / Solution / Outcome

Start with a problem, show how to solve it, explain the positive result

Rationale / Change / Benefits

Explain why something was done, describe what changed, show the advantages

Past / Present / Future

Show what was true before, describe the current situation, predict what comes next

The power of three: All presentations have three parts: "Tell them what you are going to tell them" → "Tell them" → "Tell them what you told them"

The 3 V's of Transition Signals

Good presenters use three types of signals to help audiences follow:

Type What Your Audience Experiences Example
Verbal Words that connect ideas and mark the path "First... next... finally..." or "In conclusion..."
Vocal Your voice changes — pause, speed up, slow down, change volume or tone Pause after saying something important. Speak loudly for key points. Slow down for difficult ideas.
Visual What they see — gestures, slides, movement, focus points Point to the screen. Show a new slide. Move to a different part of the room. Make eye contact.

Listen and watch for these signals:

Verbal: "Now let me explain the second reason why..." (word signals: now, second, why)
Vocal: The presenter pauses for 3 seconds after saying something important, then continues more slowly. (the pause and change in speed signal importance)
Visual: The presenter holds up three fingers while saying "There are three main reasons." (the gesture shows: attention, numbers, structure)
