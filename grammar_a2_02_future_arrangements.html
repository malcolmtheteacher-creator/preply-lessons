Listen in on this conversation

Anna: Are you free on Saturday?

Ben: Actually, no — I'm helping my dad move house that day.

Anna: Oh, okay. What about Sunday?

Ben: Sunday's good. Want to get lunch?

Look at what Ben says. He doesn't say "I help my dad" or "I will help my dad" — he says "I'm helping my dad move house that day." Why do you think English uses this form, with a day mentioned, for something that hasn't happened yet? Say your idea out loud before you check.

Check your idea CLICK
Because it's already arranged — it's in Ben's diary, and it involves his dad. The little word "that day" also shows it isn't happening right now. English uses am/is/are + verb-ing for exactly this: real plans that are already fixed, usually with a time and often with someone else.
Today: the present continuous for fixed plans — am/is/are + verb-ing + a time — so you can say your plans, ask about someone else's plans, and politely change or turn down a plan.

Fixed Plans: "I'm meeting her at six"

For real plans already in the diary — a time, and usually another person.

Spot the pattern

Three real plans:

  • "I'm meeting Sara at six."
  • "We're having dinner with the Alvarez family on Saturday."
  • "He's flying to Berlin on Monday morning."

Before you check: what's the same in all three sentences?

Check the pattern CLICK
am / is / are + verb-ing, plus a time or day (six, Saturday, Monday morning). The time word is doing a lot of work — without it, these sentences could just mean "right now."
am / is / are + verb-ing (+ time or day)

Why the time word matters

"I'm having lunch."
(right now, no time given)
→
"I'm having lunch with my boss tomorrow."
(a future plan — clear because of "tomorrow")

Without a time word, "I'm having lunch" usually means right now. Add a time word like "tomorrow," "on Saturday" or "at six," and the same structure becomes a fixed plan for the future.

A Fixed Plan or Just an Idea?

You already know "going to" for ideas and intentions. Here's how it's different from today's form.

Spot the difference

  • "I'm going to learn Spanish this year." (an idea — no fixed time, nobody else involved yet)
  • "I'm having a Spanish lesson at five." (a fixed plan — a time, and probably a teacher too)

Which one is already arranged, and which one is still just a plan in your head? Say your idea before you check.

Check the difference CLICK
"Going to learn Spanish this year" is a decision or intention — no diary, no fixed time. "Having a Spanish lesson at five" is already booked in — a real time, today or soon, usually with someone else.
am / is / are + going to + verb = an idea or intention (no fixed time yet)
am / is / are + verb-ing (+ time) = a fixed plan (already arranged, often with someone else)
