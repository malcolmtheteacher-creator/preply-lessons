Thinking about memorable meals

Think about a really good meal you have had. It could be at a restaurant, at a friend's house, or at home. Consider these questions:

When was the last time you went out for a really good meal?
Where did you go?
What did you have?
Why was the meal good?

Was it the food, the company, the atmosphere, or something else?

Click to reveal: How to use "so" and "such"

Using "So" — We use "so" to emphasise adjectives:

so + adjective

Example: "The restaurant was so good!"

Using "Such a" — We use "such a" to emphasise nouns or adjectives + nouns:

such a + noun

Example: "It was such a surprise!"

Listening actively to your conversation partner

Active listening means showing the person you are listening and interested. Read this conversation between Maria and Dwayne about a memorable meal:

Maria:

"...one of the highlights of the trip for me was this meal we had one night in a little town by the sea."

Dwayne:

"Oh yeah?"

Maria:

"I really wanted to try this dish called 'fruits de mer'. It's basically a plate of different kinds of seafood on a bed of ice."

Dwayne:

"Sounds good. What seafood did you have?"

Maria:

"Loads! There were prawns, mussels, clams and a big crab – so much – it took Simon and I such a long time to finish it, but it was so good."

Dwayne:

"Right, wow…"

Maria:

"Well, we decided to share a dessert, so I ordered a lemon tart and you know what?"

Dwayne:

"Go on."

Active listening phrases (Click each card to reveal the purpose)

"Oh yeah?"
Shows interest and encourages the speaker to continue
"Sounds good."
Responds positively + asks a follow-up question to show interest
"Right, wow…"
Shows you're impressed and paying attention
"Go on."
Encourages the speaker to continue their story
