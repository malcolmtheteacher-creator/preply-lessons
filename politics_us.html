US: Polarisation & Checks and Balances

How the system works, hot topics, and the debate style.

The words for today's story

polarisation — division into opposing groups.
checks and balances — system that stops one part of government from having too much power.
filibuster — long speech to delay a vote.
healthcare — medical system and insurance.
debate — formal argument.
turnout — percentage of people who vote.
media — news and information sources.
trust — belief that institutions are reliable.

The reading

Part 1 — The system

The US has three branches: Congress makes laws, the President executes them, the courts interpret them. Checks and balances mean each can limit the others. Polarisation has grown: the two main parties disagree more strongly than in the past.

Part 2 — Hot topics

Healthcare and guns are frequent debates. Opinions are often split along party lines. Media on both sides amplify differences.

Part 3 — How people argue and participate

Debates can be loud and personal. Voting turnout varies by election. Trust in institutions has fallen for some groups.

Grammar — Contrast (Although, Despite, However)

Use these to show differences between the system and real politics.

Practice: Although the system has checks and balances, polarisation makes compromise hard.

Going deeper

Polarisation comes from media, geography, and social sorting. Tips: Listen for the actual policy, not the party label. Ask questions before judging.
