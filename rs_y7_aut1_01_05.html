2. Encounter

Let's look at what Britain actually looks like, according to the 2021 Census — the most recent official count of the UK population. The answers might surprise you.

The UK Census happens every 10 years and asks 28 million households about their religion, language, ethnicity, job, housing, and more. It's the largest survey in Britain. The 2021 Census was the first to ask about religion in a new way, which is why the numbers look quite different from 2011.

How Has This Changed? (2011 to 2021)

What happened in those ten years? Here's the comparison:

Christianity

2011: 59.3% | 2021: 46.2% | Change: −13.1 percentage points

Nearly 7 million fewer people identified as Christian in a single decade.

No Religion

2011: 24.7% | 2021: 37.2% | Change: +12.5 percentage points

The biggest growth. Nearly 3.5 million more people said "no religion."

Islam

2011: 4.8% | 2021: 6.5% | Change: +1.7 percentage points

Modest but steady growth. Why? Immigration, higher birth rates, and younger Muslim population.

Other traditions

2011: 11.2% combined | 2021: 9.2% combined | Change: −2 percentage points

Hinduism, Sikhism, Judaism, and Buddhism remain relatively stable.

Why Is This Happening? (Multiple Causes)

These changes don't have one simple cause. Religious change is complex. Here are the main factors:

1. Secularisation

Secularisation means the decline of religion's influence in society. Britain is increasingly secular — religion is becoming less central to life, institutions, and culture. Fewer people attend church regularly. Fewer see religion as essential to morality. This is especially true in younger generations.

2. Generational Decline

It's not that people are changing FROM Christian TO non-religious. It's that older generations (who grew up when church was normal) are dying, and younger generations (who grew up with other options) are not becoming Christian. If you didn't learn Christianity as a child, you're less likely to adopt it as an adult.

3. Immigration and Diversity

Islam, Hinduism, and Sikhism have grown partly through migration from South Asia and Middle East — communities bringing their own religions with them. These aren't new British religions; they're established global religions that are now present in Britain in larger numbers.

4. The Rise of "Spiritual but Not Religious"

Not everyone who says "no religion" is an atheist. Some say they believe in God or spirituality but don't follow any organised religion. They're "spiritual but not religious." The 2021 Census didn't capture this clearly, but surveys suggest 10-15% of Brits fit this description.

KEY TERM

Pluralism

A society with many different religions and worldviews coexisting. Modern Britain is religiously pluralistic — no single religion dominates. This is different from the 1950s, when Christianity was overwhelming.
