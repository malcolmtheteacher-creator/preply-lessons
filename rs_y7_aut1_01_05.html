Encounter

Let's look at what Britain actually looks like, according to the 2021 Census — the most recent official count of the UK population.

The UK Census happens every 10 years and asks 28 million households about their religion, language, ethnicity, job, housing, and more. It's the largest survey in Britain.

What does this tell us? Christianity is still the largest religion. But "no religion" is now the second-largest group. Together they make up 83% of Britain.

How Has This Changed? (2011 to 2021)

Christianity

2011: 59.3% → 2021: 46.2% — −13.1 percentage points. Nearly 7 million fewer people identified as Christian in a single decade.

No Religion

2011: 24.7% → 2021: 37.2% — +12.5 percentage points. The biggest growth. Nearly 3.5 million more people said "no religion."

Islam

2011: 4.8% → 2021: 6.5% — +1.7 percentage points. Modest but steady growth through immigration, higher birth rates, and a younger Muslim population.

Other Traditions

2011: 11.2% combined → 2021: 9.2% combined — −2 percentage points. Hinduism, Sikhism, Judaism, and Buddhism remain relatively stable.

Why Is This Happening?

1. Secularisation

Secularisation means the decline of religion's influence in society. Britain is increasingly secular — religion is becoming less central to life, institutions, and culture. Fewer people attend church regularly. This is especially true in younger generations.

2. Generational Decline

Older generations (who grew up when church was normal) are dying, and younger generations are not becoming Christian. If you didn't learn Christianity as a child, you're less likely to adopt it as an adult.

3. Immigration and Diversity

Islam, Hinduism, and Sikhism have grown partly through migration from South Asia and the Middle East — communities bringing their religions with them.

4. "Spiritual but Not Religious"

Not everyone who says "no religion" is an atheist. Some believe in God or spirituality but don't follow any organised religion. Surveys suggest 10–15% of Brits fit this description.

KEY TERM

Pluralism

A society with many different religions and worldviews coexisting. Modern Britain is religiously pluralistic — no single religion dominates.
