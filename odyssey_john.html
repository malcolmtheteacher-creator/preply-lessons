The words for today's story

1. The barons rebelled against John.

2. Magna Carta was a charter of liberties.

dysentery (n) — a serious stomach illness.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — Lackland

John, Henry II's youngest son, was nicknamed "Lackland". He became king in 1199 after Richard's death. By 1204 he had lost Normandy to Philip II of France.

He quarrelled with Pope Innocent III over the Archbishop of Canterbury. England was placed under an interdict from 1208, and John was excommunicated in 1209.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did John lose in 1204?

2. What happened during his quarrel with the Pope?

1. Normandy.

2. England was placed under an interdict, and John was excommunicated.

Discuss:
  • Why might losing land make a king raise taxes?

Part 2 — Runnymede

After another defeat in France in 1214, John's barons rebelled. In June 1215, at Runnymede, near Windsor, he agreed to a charter later called Magna Carta. It promised, among other things, that no free man would be imprisoned or punished "except by the lawful judgement of his peers or by the law of the land".

Within months, John had the Pope annul it, and civil war began.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did John agree to Magna Carta?

2. What did the famous clause promise?

1. At Runnymede, near Windsor.

2. No free man would be punished except by lawful judgement or the law of the land.

Discuss:
  • Why did the barons want written promises?

Part 3 — The legacy

In 1216 John lost part of his baggage train while crossing the Wash — legend says the crown jewels; what was actually lost is uncertain. He died of dysentery in October 1216.

Magna Carta was reissued under his son and became a symbol of the idea that rulers must obey the law. Four copies of the 1215 charter survive.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did John die?

2. Why is Magna Carta important?

1. Of dysentery in 1216.

2. It became a symbol that rulers must obey the law.

Discuss:
  • Why does a document from 1215 still matter?

Grammar — Relative Clauses

To pack a lot of information into one sentence — who someone was, where something happened — we use relative clauses.

How it works:
• who for people, which for things, where for places, whose for possession.
• Non-defining clauses add extra information with commas: "Edward, who was deeply religious, rebuilt Westminster Abbey."
• Defining clauses identify which one — no commas.

1. Choose who, which, where or whose

Complete each sentence about King John. Use the word in brackets.

1. Magna Carta, was sealed in 1215, limited royal power.

2. John, barons rebelled, had lost Normandy.

3. Runnymede is the meadow the charter was agreed.

1. which

2. whose

3. where

2. Join the sentences

1. The barons rebelled. They demanded rights. (who)

2. The Pope annulled the charter. He supported John. (who)

3. Four copies survive. They were made in 1215. (which / that)

1. The barons, who demanded rights, rebelled.

2. The Pope, who supported John, annulled the charter.

3. Four copies, which were made in 1215, survive.

Going deeper

Magna Carta was a peace treaty between a king and rebels, not a democratic constitution. But its idea — that the ruler is under the law — inspired later documents, including in America.

Worth noticing: most of Magna Carta's 63 clauses dealt with medieval rights; only a few remain in English law today.
Learning from the past — for today: Write it down. Agreements in writing protect everyone. When something important is agreed — at work, in a rental, in a team — get it in writing.
Dig into it — talk these through:

  • Is Magna Carta overrated?
  • What written agreement protects you?
