Explaining a Partially Covered Claim

B2 Level · Insurance · Customer Claims

Insurance · Dewey 368

First Thoughts

A customer has called about a house claim after a pipe burst and damaged their kitchen. Some of the damage is clearly covered — the water damage to the floor, for example. Some of it is not — the laptop that was on the counter, because they had no contents cover for portable electronics.

They are expecting a full payout. You have to explain, in plain English, why they will not receive one — without sounding like you are hiding behind small print.

Take a moment with each question below. Share as much detail as you like — specific examples, what you would actually say, what tripped you up last time.

1. How do you feel when you have to read a section of a policy out to an upset customer? What makes that moment hard?

3. Is there a difference between ‘following policy’ and ‘treating the customer fairly’? Where do those two things pull against each other?

4. Think of a time you felt badly treated by an insurer. What, specifically, did they do wrong?

Key Vocabulary

Eight words and phrases you will actually use in this conversation. Click each card to reveal the meaning, then say the word aloud and try to use it in a sentence from your own experience.

a policyThe contract between the customer and the insurer — what is covered, what is not, and how much is paid out.
an exclusionSomething specifically not covered by the policy. Often written in the small print.
a claimA request from a customer to be paid for a loss that they believe is covered.
an excessThe amount the customer has to pay themselves before the insurer pays anything. Often called a ‘deductible’ in the US.
buildings coverInsurance for the structure of a home — walls, roof, fitted kitchen, plumbing.
contents coverInsurance for the things inside a home — furniture, electronics, clothes, valuables.
to settle (a claim)To complete and pay out a claim, or to agree a final amount.
a like-for-like replacementA replacement of the same type and similar value as the item that was lost or damaged.

Key Language

Useful chunks of language for this conversation. Read each phrase aloud and think about when you would actually reach for it.

Opening — listening first

  • “Before I look at the policy in detail, can you walk me through what happened and what has been damaged?”
  • “I am really sorry this has happened — let me take you through what I can do and what will need a bit more of a conversation.”
  • “Thank you for calling us straight away — that actually helps us move things along faster.”

Explaining what is covered

  • “The good news is that the damage to the floor and the units falls within your buildings cover, so that part we can move on today.”
  • “We will send an approved contractor to quote for the repair — you will not need to find anyone yourself.”
  • “Your policy covers a like-for-like replacement, which means we match what you had, not necessarily the cheapest option.”

Delivering the harder news

  • “There is one part I need to talk through with you carefully, because it is a bit of a grey area.”
  • “I am afraid the laptop is not going to be covered on this claim — let me explain why, and what your options are.”
  • “Your contents cover has an exclusion for portable electronics unless they are listed separately, and unfortunately the laptop was not listed.”

Offering a way forward

  • “What I can do today is settle the covered portion quickly so you are not out of pocket on the main repair.”
  • “Going forward, I would recommend we add your laptop and other portable items to your policy so you are covered next time.”
  • “If you disagree with my decision, you have the right to a formal review — I can set that up for you now if you would like.”

The Scenario

The situation: A customer calls your claims line. Pipe burst Tuesday night. Water damage to kitchen floor and units (covered under buildings). A laptop on the counter was destroyed (not covered — portable electronics were not listed on contents). Customer’s excess is £250. You have already confirmed the buildings portion is valid.

Your task: Handle the call. Settle what you can, explain what you cannot, and leave the customer feeling heard — even though they are not getting everything they expected.
