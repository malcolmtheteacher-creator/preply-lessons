What is Bravery?

Bravery is not the absence of fear — it's acting despite being afraid. Here are some key character adjectives related to bravery. Click each card to reveal the definition.

brave
courageous
fearless
daring
bold
determined

Vocabulary Key

brave: able to face danger or pain without showing fear

courageous: having or displaying courage; willing to take risks

fearless: lacking fear; not afraid

daring: adventurous and willing to take risks

bold: confident and willing to take risks or stand out

determined: firmly committed to doing something, even if it's difficult

Reflection Questions

1. What does bravery mean to you? Is it about not being afraid, or is it about doing something even when you are afraid?
2. Can you think of a brave person? Who is someone you know (in real life, from history, or from a film) who you think is brave? Why?
3. Have you ever felt brave? Tell me about a moment when you had to be brave.

Brave Moments — Reading & Grammar

Read the short scenario and answer the comprehension questions. Then complete the grammar exercise.

Scenario: The School Presentation

Marco was terrified of speaking in public. For years, he avoided presentations and group discussions. One day, his teacher asked him to present his research project to the class. His hands shook and his heart raced as he walked to the front. But he started speaking anyway. He spoke about his topic with passion. His classmates listened carefully. When he finished, they applauded. Marco felt proud and relieved. He had done something he thought was impossible.

Comprehension Questions

1. What was Marco afraid of?
Click to Reveal
Marco was afraid of speaking in public / was terrified of giving presentations.
2. What physical signs of fear did Marco show?
Click to Reveal
His hands shook and his heart raced. (He was nervous and anxious.)
3. How did Marco feel after the presentation?
Click to Reveal
He felt proud and relieved. He had done something he thought was impossible.

Grammar: Past Simple — Fill in the Blanks

Complete the sentences using the past simple form of the verbs in brackets.

1. Sofia (be) scared of water, but last summer she (learn) to swim.

Show Answer
was / learned — "Sofia was scared of water, but last summer she learned to swim."

2. They (feel) nervous before the interview, but they (speak) well.

Show Answer
felt / spoke — "They felt nervous before the interview, but they spoke well."

3. I (not want) to climb the mountain, yet I (decide) to try anyway.

Show Answer
didn't want / decided — "I didn't want to climb the mountain, yet I decided to try anyway."

4. She (have) doubts about herself, but she (achieve) her goal.

Show Answer
had / achieved — "She had doubts about herself, but she achieved her goal."

Your Brave Story

It's your turn to share a brave moment from your own life. Use the sentence starters to help you.

Speaking Activity — Tell Your Story

Use these sentence starters to structure your answer:

The bravest thing I ever did was...
I felt scared because...
But I decided to... because...
After that, I felt...
That moment taught me...
Tip: Use the past simple (I did, I felt, I decided) to tell your story. Be honest and personal — there is no perfect answer.

Role-Play Scenario

Situation: You witnessed someone being treated unfairly. You want to defend them, but you are nervous.

Your task: Describe to a friend what happened and why you decided to speak up (or why you didn't).

• What did you see happen?

• Why was it unfair?

• How did you feel in that moment?

• What did you do? (Did you speak up? Ask for help? Stay silent?)

• Would you do the same thing again?

Follow-Up Questions

Was that action brave, or just normal? When does helping someone become a brave act?
Is trying something new brave? Is it brave to try a new sport, eat a new food, or speak in English even if you might make mistakes?

Rank These Situations

Order them from 1 (most brave) to 5 (least brave). Click each one and explain your choice.

  • _
    A firefighter runs into a burning building to save someone.
    Why Rank This Way?
    This is extremely brave because the person faces real danger and risk to save another life.
  • _
    A student admits to a friend they made a mistake, even though they were embarrassed.
    Why Rank This Way?
    This is brave because it requires vulnerability and honesty, but the danger is emotional rather than physical.
  • _
    Someone tries a new hairstyle they love, even though other people might judge it.
    Why Rank This Way?
    This is moderately brave because it requires confidence to be different, but the consequences are usually minor.
  • _
    A doctor tells a patient difficult news about their health.
    Why Rank This Way?
    This requires courage because the doctor must be honest in a difficult situation, but it's part of their professional duty.
  • _
    A child asks a question in class even though they are shy.
    Why Rank This Way?
    This is brave for a shy person because they overcome their fear of attention, but the actual risk is low.
Reflection: Is bravery always physical? Can "small" acts of bravery be as important as "big" ones? What makes something brave for one person but not for another?
