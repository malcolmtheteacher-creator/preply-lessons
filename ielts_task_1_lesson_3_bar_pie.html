How Bar & Pie Charts Differ from Line Graphs

The Key Shift: Line graphs show CHANGE OVER TIME. Bar and pie charts show COMPARISON BETWEEN CATEGORIES. This changes how you write about them.

Bar Chart: Water Usage by Sector

Pie Chart: Same Data as Proportions

What Changes in Your Writing?

Line Graphs Bar/Pie Charts
Focus on TRENDS (rose, fell, fluctuated) Focus on COMPARISONS (higher than, the largest)
Describe change: "increased from X to Y" Describe difference: "X was twice as high as Y"
Time language: "over the period", "by 2020" Comparison language: "whereas", "compared to"
Overview: main trends Overview: biggest/smallest, main patterns
Common Mistake: Students often write "Agriculture increased to 70%" for bar charts. But nothing "increased" - you're comparing categories at ONE point in time. Say: "Agriculture accounted for 70%" or "Agriculture was the highest at 70%".

What Makes a Good Bar Chart Overview

INCLUDE:

  • Which category was highest/lowest ACROSS all groups
  • Overall patterns (one country consistently higher/lower)
  • Where differences were biggest/smallest

SAVE FOR BODY PARAGRAPHS:

  • Specific dollar amounts
  • Individual data points without comparison

Pie Chart Strategy: Describing Proportions

Pie Chart = 100% Total Pie charts show how a whole is divided into parts. Your language should reflect PROPORTIONS: "accounted for", "made up", "represented", "comprised".

Energy Sources 2000

Energy Sources 2020

The Special Challenge: Two Pie Charts

When you have two pie charts showing different times, you DO describe change - but the language is different from line graphs.

WEAK: Line graph language (wrong for pie charts)

"Oil increased from 40% to 35%." - This is contradictory! Oil didn't increase.

"Coal rose while renewables fell." - Imprecise and potentially confusing.

STRONG: Proportion language

"Oil's share declined from 40% to 35%, while renewables grew from a negligible 2% to account for 18% of the total."

"Coal remained the dominant source, though its share fell from 45% to 30%."

Proportion Vocabulary

Percentage Alternative Expressions
100% all / the entire amount / the whole
75% three quarters / the vast majority
66% two thirds / the majority
50% half / equal proportions
33% one third / a third
25% a quarter / one in four
10-15% a small proportion / a minority
< 5% a negligible amount / a tiny fraction

Practice: Rewrite Using Proportion Language

The sentence below uses weak language. Rewrite it properly.

Weak: "In 2000, coal was 45% and oil was 40%. Gas was 10% and nuclear was 3%. Renewables was 2%."

Model: "In 2000, fossil fuels dominated the energy mix, with coal accounting for nearly half (45%) and oil representing two-fifths of the total. Gas made up a tenth, while nuclear and renewables together comprised only 5%."

Notice: Groups related items, uses fractions, avoids repetitive "was X%"

Decision: How to Organize Your Answer

With bar charts comparing multiple categories across groups, you must decide: organize by CATEGORY or by GROUP?

Participation in Activities by Age Group (%)

Which Organization is Better?

Option A: By Activity

"Swimming was most popular among young people (65%) but less so for older groups (30%)...

Reading showed the opposite pattern, with 70% of over-65s participating compared to only 25% of 18-30s..."

Option B: By Age Group

"Among 18-30 year olds, swimming was the most popular activity at 65%, followed by gaming at 55%...

The 31-50 age group showed different preferences, with cycling leading at 45%..."

Both Can Work - But Consider This:

Option A (By Activity) is often better when:

  • You want to highlight how ONE thing varies across groups
  • There are clear patterns (young vs old preferences)
  • Fewer activities to discuss

Option B (By Age Group) works when:

  • Each group has distinctly different patterns
  • You want to paint a picture of each group's behavior
  • The question specifically asks about groups
Best Practice: For THIS chart, Option A is slightly better because the interesting story is HOW activities differ by age (swimming = young, reading = old). Organizing by activity makes these contrasts clearer.

Full Practice: Pie Charts (Two Time Periods)

Household Waste Disposal 2000

Household Waste Disposal 2020

Write a Complete Answer (150+ words)

Include: Introduction, Overview, Body Paragraph 1 (2000 data), Body Paragraph 2 (2020 data and changes)

0 words / 150 minimum

INTRODUCTION

The pie charts compare methods of household waste disposal in 2000 and 2020.

OVERVIEW

Overall, there was a significant shift away from landfill towards more sustainable methods, particularly recycling. While landfill remained the largest category in both years, its dominance decreased substantially.

BODY 1

In 2000, landfill was by far the most common disposal method, accounting for nearly two-thirds (62%) of all household waste. Incineration made up approximately a quarter (25%), while recycling represented only a small fraction at 8%. Composting was negligible, comprising just 5%.

BODY 2

By 2020, the picture had changed dramatically. Landfill's share fell by nearly half to 35%, though it remained the largest category. Most notably, recycling increased fourfold to account for almost a third (32%) of waste disposal. Composting also saw significant growth, tripling to 15%, while incineration declined slightly to 18%.

Key Takeaways

Bar Charts - Remember:
  • Focus on COMPARISONS between categories/groups
  • Use comparative language: "higher than", "twice as much", "the largest"
  • Identify which differences are most significant
  • Group related categories in body paragraphs
Pie Charts - Remember:
  • Use PROPORTION language: "accounted for", "comprised", "made up"
  • Vary expression: use fractions (a quarter), not just percentages
  • For two pies: describe change in SHARE, not just amounts
  • Always note the dominant category and any surprising findings
Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Using "increased/decreased" for single-time bar charts
  • Describing every single bar or slice (select what's significant)
  • Repeating "was X%" for every data point
  • Forgetting to compare - just listing numbers separately
  • Putting specific percentages in the overview
