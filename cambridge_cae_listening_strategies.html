C1 Advanced - Exam Strategy

The CAE Listening Test

Test Format at a Glance

Duration: Approximately 40 minutes

Questions: 30 questions total

Key Advantage: You hear each recording TWICE

The Four Parts

1

Three Short Extracts

  • 3 unrelated recordings
  • 2 questions per extract
  • 6 questions total
  • Focus: gist, opinion, attitude
2

Sentence Completion

  • Monologue (3-4 minutes)
  • 8 gaps to complete
  • Write 1-3 words per gap
  • Focus: specific information
3

Multiple Choice (Long)

  • Interview or discussion
  • 6 questions
  • 4 options (A-D) per question
  • Focus: opinion, detail, inference
4

Multiple Matching

  • 5 short monologues
  • 2 tasks (5 questions each)
  • Match speakers to statements
  • Focus: opinion, feeling, attitude

Why You Hear Twice

Cambridge gives you two listens because the texts are complex and you need to process nuanced opinions, not just facts. Use this to your advantage:

  • First listen: Get the gist and answer what you can confidently
  • Second listen: Confirm answers and catch what you missed

The Real Challenge

The CAE Listening test is NOT about hearing every word. It tests whether you can:

What Examiners Actually Test

  • Understand paraphrased information (answers rarely use exact words from the recording)
  • Identify speaker attitude and opinion beyond just facts
  • Avoid distractors designed to trap you
  • Follow changes of mind or contrasting views

The Difference Between Pass and Fail

Unsuccessful candidates often choose answers that contain words they heard. Successful candidates understand that the correct answer usually paraphrases what the speaker said using different vocabulary.

Decision 1: Use the Reading Time

Before each part, you get time to read the questions. This is NOT wasted time - it is your strategic advantage.

1
Read Questions
2
Underline Keys
3
Predict Content
4
Listen Actively

Step 1: Read Questions Before Listening

  • Use every second of reading time - do not daydream
  • Read ALL questions and options for the upcoming part
  • For Part 2, read the whole sentence (not just the gap)
  • For Parts 1, 3, 4, read all options A-D carefully

Step 2: Underline Key Words

  • Focus on content words: nouns, verbs, adjectives
  • Mark question words: why, what, how, who
  • Note negative words: not, never, rarely, hardly
  • Highlight contrast words: but, however, although, despite

Step 3: Predict What You'll Hear

  • What topic will this be about?
  • What type of information do you need? (opinion, fact, reason)
  • For Part 2: What grammar fits the gap? (noun, verb, adjective?)
  • For Part 4: What feelings or opinions might speakers express?

Practice: Which Key Words Would You Underline?

Look at this Part 3 question and select the most important words to underline:

Question: "According to Sarah, what surprised her most about working remotely?"

Click on the words you would underline:

Best words to underline:

  • Sarah - tells you WHOSE opinion to listen for
  • surprised - the emotion/reaction you need
  • most - superlative = you need the MAIN thing
  • working remotely - the topic context

Why NOT the others:

  • "According to" - standard phrasing, not useful
  • "what" / "about" - grammatical words, not content

Reading Time by Part

Part 1 ~45 seconds before each extract
Part 2 ~45 seconds for all 8 sentences
Part 3 ~70 seconds for all 6 questions
Part 4 ~30 seconds for each task

Decision 2: Listen for Paraphrase, Not Exact Words

The #1 Trap in CAE Listening

If you hear the EXACT words from an answer option, it is often a distractor. The correct answer typically paraphrases what the speaker said using different vocabulary.

How CAE Paraphrases

You Hear in Recording Correct Answer Says
"I was absolutely thrilled" She was happy / delighted / pleased
"It wasn't what I expected at all" He was surprised / taken aback
"I've been doing this for ages" She has considerable experience
"The whole thing was a disaster" It didn't go well / was unsuccessful
"I had my doubts at first" He was initially skeptical / uncertain

Common Paraphrase Patterns

Emotion/Attitude Words

  • "happy" = delighted, thrilled, pleased, over the moon, chuffed
  • "worried" = concerned, anxious, apprehensive, uneasy
  • "angry" = frustrated, annoyed, irritated, furious
  • "sad" = disappointed, dejected, downhearted, disheartened

Degree/Quantity Words

  • "a lot" = considerably, substantially, significantly, extensively
  • "a little" = slightly, somewhat, marginally, to some extent
  • "very" = extremely, incredibly, remarkably, exceptionally
  • "always" = consistently, invariably, without fail

Strategy Summary

When listening, don't wait to hear exact words from the options. Instead:

  1. Understand the MEANING of what the speaker says
  2. Find the option that expresses the SAME IDEA differently
  3. Be suspicious of options that use exact words from the recording

Part-by-Part Strategies

Part 1: Three Short Extracts (6 questions)

Focus: Gist, opinion, attitude, feeling

  • Each extract is independent - reset your focus between them
  • Listen for HOW speakers feel, not just WHAT they say
  • Pay attention to tone of voice and intonation
  • Questions often ask "What does the speaker think/feel about..."
  • Both questions for each extract play together - read both before listening

Common Part 1 Question Types

  • What is the speaker's attitude towards...?
  • What do the speakers agree about?
  • Why does the speaker mention...?
  • What does the speaker imply about...?

Part 2: Sentence Completion (8 questions)

Focus: Specific information, details

  • Write 1-3 words ONLY - never more
  • GRAMMAR MATTERS: Check singular/plural, verb tense, word form
  • The answer must fit grammatically into the sentence
  • Answers come in ORDER - follow the recording sequence
  • Spelling must be correct (British or American accepted)
  • You often hear the exact words - but check they fit grammatically

Grammar Check Examples

If the sentence reads: "Sarah works as a "

You need a NOUN (job title): "researcher" NOT "researching"

If the sentence reads: "The main was the cost"

You need a SINGULAR NOUN: "problem" NOT "problems"

Part 3: Multiple Choice Long Text (6 questions)

Focus: Opinion, attitude, detail, inference, purpose

  • BEWARE OF DISTRACTORS - speakers often mention all options
  • Listen for qualifiers: "but", "however", "although", "actually"
  • The speaker may mention something then reject or modify it
  • Questions follow the order of the recording
  • If two options seem correct, one probably has a subtle difference
  • Pay attention to WHO says WHAT (in conversations)

Distractor Alert Words

When you hear these, the speaker may be about to change direction:

"but actually...", "on second thought...", "having said that...", "mind you...", "then again...", "to be honest..."

Part 4: Multiple Matching (10 questions)

Focus: Opinions, feelings, experiences across 5 speakers

  • You complete TWO tasks while listening to the SAME 5 speakers
  • Each speaker matches ONE option per task (no repeats within a task)
  • Cross out options as you use them
  • Listen for the MAIN point each speaker makes
  • Speakers may mention multiple topics - identify their FOCUS
  • Options are paraphrased - do not expect exact words

Part 4 Strategy

First listen: Focus on Task 1 primarily, note Task 2 if possible

Second listen: Confirm Task 1, focus on Task 2

The speakers talk about similar themes, so distinguishing between them requires careful attention to each speaker's specific perspective.

Decision 3: Beware of Distractors

What Are Distractors?

Distractors are wrong answer options that are deliberately designed to trick you. They usually contain words or ideas that you DO hear in the recording - but they are not the correct answer.

How CAE Plants Wrong Answers

Type 1: Mentioned but Rejected

The speaker mentions something but then says it's NOT the case.

Speaker: "I thought the main problem would be the budget, but actually it turned out to be finding qualified staff that was the real challenge."

Trap option: "budget constraints" (mentioned but NOT the answer)

Correct option: "recruitment difficulties" (paraphrase of the real answer)

Type 2: Change of Mind

The speaker expresses one view, then changes their opinion.

Speaker: "At first I was really enthusiastic about the project. But after a few months, I have to admit I've become quite disillusioned with the whole thing."

Trap option: "She feels positive about it" (initial feeling, not final)

Correct option: "She has become disappointed" (current feeling)

Type 3: Other Person's View

The speaker mentions what someone ELSE thinks, not their own view.

Speaker: "My colleagues think we should expand into new markets, but I believe we should consolidate what we have first."

Question: "What does the speaker think the company should do?"

Trap option: "expand into new markets" (colleagues' view)

Correct option: "focus on current operations" (speaker's view)

Signal Words That Indicate Direction Change

but however actually although on the other hand having said that mind you then again to be honest in reality the thing is nevertheless

Practice: Identify the Distractor

Read this transcript excerpt and identify which option is the distractor:

Interviewer: "What was the most challenging aspect of writing your first novel?"

Author: "Well, everyone assumes it must be the research, and yes, that took time. And of course, finding a publisher was stressful. But honestly? The hardest part was maintaining self-discipline day after day. Writing is such a solitary activity."

Question: What does the author say was most difficult about writing her novel?

Correct Answer: C - "maintaining self-discipline" is paraphrased as "staying motivated"

Distractors explained:

  • A: Research is mentioned but dismissed ("everyone assumes... that took time" - not the main challenge)
  • B: Finding a publisher is mentioned ("was stressful") but not identified as the hardest part
  • D: "solitary activity" is mentioned as context, but not as the difficulty itself - the difficulty is self-discipline

Decision 4: Use the Second Listen Strategically

You hear every recording twice. This is a major advantage - but only if you use both listens strategically.

First Listen: Answer What You Can

  • Focus on getting the overall meaning
  • Answer questions you feel confident about
  • Make a mark (? or *) next to uncertain answers
  • Do NOT stress about missed answers - you get another chance
  • Keep moving - do not get stuck on one question

Second Listen: Check and Complete

  • Verify your confident answers are correct
  • Focus especially on marked questions
  • Listen for details you might have missed
  • Use context to help with uncertain answers
  • Make final decisions - do not leave blanks

Do Not Panic If You Miss Something

Missing information on the first listen is NORMAL. The test is designed to be challenging. Trust that the second listen will help, and keep your focus on the current question rather than worrying about what you missed.

Strategic Focus by Part

Parts 1, 3, 4: Multiple Choice/Matching

First Listen Second Listen
  • Eliminate obviously wrong options
  • Note which options seem possible
  • Choose if you're confident
  • Confirm your choices
  • Distinguish between similar options
  • Catch nuances you missed

Part 2: Sentence Completion

First Listen Second Listen
  • Write what you hear quickly
  • Do not worry about spelling yet
  • Leave blanks if needed - move on
  • Check spelling carefully
  • Verify grammar fits
  • Fill in any blanks

The "Move On" Rule

If you're stuck on a question:

  1. Make your best guess
  2. Mark it with a symbol (?)
  3. Move on immediately
  4. Return on the second listen

Missing the next question while worrying about the previous one means losing TWO marks instead of one.

Common Trap Words

When you hear these, be alert - a distractor may follow:

"I thought..." (past belief) "People say..." (others' views) "It seems like..." (appearance vs reality) "Initially..." (first impression) "You might think..." (common assumption) "Some argue..." (not speaker's view) "At first glance..." (surface level) "Many believe..." (general opinion)

Final Reminders

  1. Use reading time wisely - it's your strategic advantage
  2. Listen for paraphrase - correct answers rarely use exact words
  3. Beware of distractors - mentioned does not mean correct
  4. Use both listens - first to answer, second to verify
  5. Never leave blanks - always make your best guess
