Discussion Questions

Read these questions. Would you classify each situation as ethical or unethical?

1. A manager is giving a reference for a cousin. The cousin is not suitable for the job, but the manager gives a positive reference anyway. Is this ethical?

No. This is unethical. Giving false references is dishonest and could harm the employer.

2. An employee uses the office photocopier to photocopy personal documents while working. Is this unethical?

Potentially. It depends on company policy and the extent of personal use. Even small personal use can be unethical without permission.

3. An employee takes a long lunch break without informing their manager. Is this unethical?

Yes. This is unethical because it breaks the employment contract and shows dishonesty.

4. A supplier sends an expensive gift to a buyer. The buyer accepts it and hides it from their employer. Is this ethical?

No. This could be seen as bribery or corruption. The employee should declare the gift to their employer.

Modal Verbs for Solutions

Modal verbs help us discuss obligations, permissions, and possibilities when solving problems. Notice the different uses below.

Permission and Obligation

should / shouldn"t — Give advice or express what is right

He shouldn"t take office equipment home.

We should report this immediately to management.

must / mustn"t — Show obligation or strict prohibition

Employees mustn"t accept expensive gifts from suppliers.

Companies must have clear ethical policies.

can"t / couldn"t — Show something is impossible or not allowed

We can"t simply ignore this behaviour.

She couldn"t have known about the fraud.

don"t have to / doesn"t have to — Show something is not necessary

You don"t have to report this immediately, but you should.

They don"t have to accept the offer, but it might be wise to.

could / might — Show possibility or options

This situation could result in disciplinary action.

He might lose his job over this.

Real Workplace Scenarios

Read these four scenarios from a multinational sportswear company. Each involves an ethical dilemma. What is the ethical issue in each case?

Scenario 1: Hidden Information

Ito is responsible for advertising a social responsibility campaign. But a newspaper has published articles about human rights abuses in the company"s factories. At an office party, Ito overhears a colleague, Laura, telling someone she supplied information to the newspaper about poor working conditions.

Ethical issues: Whistleblowing, loyalty, transparency, corporate responsibility

Scenario 2: Family Favouritism

Gan works in HR and has agreed to give a reference for his cousin Lee, who is applying for a job. Gan also has access to the interview questions. Lee has asked his cousin for a copy to help him prepare.

Ethical issues: Conflict of interest, unfair advantage, nepotism, integrity

Scenario 3: Accepting Gifts

Eola is a buyer. A supplier sends her an expensive hamper to her home as a gift. When her son sees it and tears open the wrapper, the hamper is partially consumed before she reports it to her manager.

Ethical issues: Bribery, conflicts of interest, professional boundaries

Scenario 4: Misusing Resources

Claudia works as PA to the Finance Director. The office photocopier doesn"t require a pin code. Her son needs to photocopy 600 pages of scripts for a youth club play. Claudia is considering using the office photocopier for this personal project.

Ethical issues: Misuse of company resources, theft, honesty

Analysing the Dilemmas

For each scenario, consider: How serious is the issue? What could happen next? What should the person do?

Evaluation Framework

Ask yourself these questions for each scenario:

Key Questions

  • How serious is each ethical issue?
  • What immediate action should be taken?
  • What are the wider implications of the unethical behaviour?
  • Should there be a warning, or does this require dismissal?
  • What would you do in this situation?

Speaking Task — Analysis

Choose one scenario above. Use the language below to discuss your analysis.

Example responses:

"The employee shouldn"t have accepted the gift."

"The company must investigate this immediately."

"There might be wider implications we haven"t considered."
