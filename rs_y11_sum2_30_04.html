What are the main causes of crime according to religious perspectives?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Christianity: sin nature, free will, rebellion against God, lack of moral training. Islam: deviation from Shariah law, societal breakdown, moral corruption. Sociology: poverty, lack education, poor socialization.

What are the four purposes of punishment?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Retribution (just desert; offender punished for wrongdoing), Deterrence (prevent future crime), Reform (rehabilitation of offender), Protection (society protected from dangerous offender).

What does retribution mean as a purpose of punishment?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Offender deserves punishment proportionate to crime. "Eye for an eye" principle. Expresses moral condemnation of wrongdoing. Gives offender what they deserve. Qisas in Islamic law.

What is deterrence and how might it work?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Punishment should deter offender (specific deterrence) and others (general deterrence) from committing crime. Certainty and severity of punishment assumed to prevent crime. Criticized as utilitarian, not addressing justice.

What is reform or rehabilitation as a purpose of punishment?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Help offender change behavior and reintegrate into society. Prison education, therapy, vocational training. Assumes people can change. More hopeful than retribution alone. Modern emphasis in progressive countries.

What is protection as a purpose of punishment?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Incapacitation: removing dangerous offender from society to protect public. Long sentences or life imprisonment for serious violent crime. Removes ability to harm others, at least in that environment.

What is the Roman Catholic teaching on capital punishment?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Evangelium Vitae (1995): opposition to death penalty. Sanctity of life includes murderers. Alternatives to execution available in modern societies. Forgiveness and redemption possible.

What is Islamic law on capital punishment (Qisas and Diyah)?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Qisas = retaliatory justice (life for life). Victim family can demand death of murderer. Diyah = blood money; victim family may accept compensation instead. Both embedded in Shariah law.

What does the Lord's Prayer teach about forgiveness?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: "Forgive us our debts as we also have forgiven our debtors." Jesus emphasizes forgiveness. Matthew 18:22 - forgive "seventy times seven" (unlimited). Parable of unmerciful servant (Matthew 18:23-35).

What does Surah 42:40 teach about forgiveness in Islam?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: "The recompense for an injury is an injury equal to it; but those who forgive and make amends will have their reward from Allah." Forgiveness is superior to retaliation. Shows strength and virtue.

What is Shariah law and how does it address crime?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Islamic legal system based on Quran, Sunnah, Ijma (consensus), Qiyas (analogy). Hudud crimes (theft, zina, apostasy) have fixed severe punishments. Tazir crimes (lesser offenses) have discretionary punishment by judge.

Crime, Punishment & Justice

Causes of Crime (Religious Perspectives)

Christian view: Sin (rebellion against God), free will (choice to do wrong), lack of moral/spiritual formation. Humans capable of good or evil. Crime expresses broken relationship with God and others.

Islamic view: Deviation from Shariah guidance, moral corruption, weakness of faith. Crime violates both divine law and community harmony. Lack of religious education and leadership.

Sociological factors: Poverty, lack education, poor family structure, peer pressure, substance abuse, mental illness. Religious perspectives often neglect these.

Four Purposes of Punishment

Retribution: Offender deserves punishment equal to harm caused. "Just desert." Expresses moral condemnation. Qisas in Islamic law.

Deterrence: Prevent offender reoffending (specific) and deter others (general). Assumes rational choice; punishment makes crime unattracted.

Reform: Rehabilitate offender; help change behavior and reintegrate society. Prison education, therapy. More hopeful; assumes people can change.

Protection: Incapacitate dangerous offender; remove from society to protect public. Long sentences or life imprisonment. Pragmatic goal: keep society safe.

Christian Teaching on Capital Punishment

Traditional view: State has authority to punish crime, including death (Romans 13:3-4). Life is sacred but justice requires proportionate punishment.

Modern Catholic view: Pope John Paul II (Evangelium Vitae 1995) opposed capital punishment. Sanctity of life includes murderers. Modern societies have alternatives.

Protestant diversity: Some support death penalty for murder (retribution). Others, including many Protestants, oppose it.

Trend: Increasing Christian opposition to capital punishment worldwide.

Islamic Law & Punishment (Shariah)

Hudud crimes: Theft, robbery, zina (sexual relations outside marriage), murder, apostasy. Fixed severe punishments prescribed in Quran/Sunnah.

Qisas: Retaliatory justice. Murderer can be executed at victim family request. However, victim family can accept Diyah (blood money) instead.

Tazir crimes: Lesser offenses; discretionary punishment by Qadi (judge). Allows flexibility based on circumstances.

Modern application: Varies by country. Some Muslim-majority countries apply strict Shariah; others less rigorous.

Apply: Case Study Analysis

Example: Desmond Tutu & Reconciliation in South Africa

Bishop Tutu led Truth and Reconciliation Commission (1995-2002) after apartheid. Emphasis on forgiveness, confession, and healing rather than punishment. Perpetrators confessed; victims forgave. Foundation: Christian theology of forgiveness and reconciliation.

Contrast with: retributive justice (punishment), which might have meant imprisonment or execution of perpetrators.

Key Terms & Concepts

Essential Vocabulary

Crime: Action that violates law and harms others or society.

Punishment: Consequence imposed for wrongdoing by legitimate authority.

Retribution: Offender deserves punishment proportionate to harm caused. "Just desert."

Deterrence: Punishment prevents future crime. Specific (offender reoffends) vs general (deters others).

Rehabilitation: Help offender change behavior and reintegrate into society. Reform.

Protection: Incapacitate dangerous offender to protect public. Removal from society.

Capital Punishment: Death penalty for serious crime (murder). Controversial; Christians increasingly opposed.

Qisas: Islamic retaliatory justice. Life for life, eye for eye, tooth for tooth.

Diyah: Islamic blood money; compensation accepted in lieu of retaliation by victim family.

Shariah: Islamic law based on Quran, Sunnah, Ijma, Qiyas. Includes Hudud (fixed) and Tazir (discretionary) punishments.

Reconciliation: Restoration of relationships; offender and victim/society restored to harmony.

How does Islamic law offer mercy through Diyah when Qisas demands death?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Victim family can choose Diyah (blood money/compensation) instead of Qisas (death). Allows for forgiveness and restoration while respecting justice. Shows flexibility in Islamic law.

What biblical foundation supports Christian forgiveness?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Jesus teaching in Matthew 18:22 (forgive seventy times seven), Matthew 18:23-35 (parable of unmerciful servant), Luke 6:37 (forgive and be forgiven). Lord Prayer: "forgive as we forgive others."

What is the difference between Hudud and Tazir crimes in Shariah?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Hudud = major crimes (theft, zina, murder, apostasy) with fixed severe punishments in Quran/Sunnah. Tazir = minor offenses; judge has discretion in punishment. Allows for flexibility and mercy.
