What are sociological causes of crime?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Poverty, inequality, poor education/unemployment, family breakdown/abuse, peer pressure/gangs, substance abuse, mental illness. Not all poor commit crimes, but poverty is risk factor.

What are Christian views on crime causes?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Sin — free will to choose evil. Temptation — Satan/evil influences. Lack of moral teaching. Separation from God. But also recognize poverty/hardship as factors.

What are Islamic views on crime causes?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Disobedience to Allah, temptation by Satan (Shaitan), lack of Qur'an/Hadith education, poverty (Q17:16), free will — humans choose to sin.

What are purposes of punishment?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Deterrence (stop future crime), Rehabilitation (reform offender), Retribution (justice/proportional response), Protection (public safety), Restorative justice (restore relationships/victims).

What is Christian view on punishment?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Forgiveness and redemption — offenders can repent/change. Rehabilitation over retribution. Love sinner, hate sin. Death penalty increasingly rejected (incompatible with 'Thou shalt not kill'). Reconciliation with victims.

What is Islamic justice (Shariah)?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Hudud: fixed severe punishments (theft: cut hand, adultery: stone/100 lashes). Qisas: 'eye for eye' justice. Ta'zir: discretionary punishments. Purpose: deter crime, uphold justice.

What is capital punishment?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Death penalty for serious crimes. Traditionally: some Christians supported (retribution). Modern: Pope John Paul II and most Christian ethicists oppose as violating human dignity.

What do Islamic scholars teach about death penalty?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Islamic law permits death penalty for serious crimes (murder, apostasy). Qisas allows death penalty. However, modern scholars debate effectiveness and compatibility with modern justice.

What is Christian teaching on forgiveness?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Jesus: 'Forgive seventy times seven' (Mt 18:22). Forgiveness breaks revenge cycle. Jesus forgave those who crucified him. Doesn't mean no justice — victims deserve support, offenders accountability.

What is restorative justice?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Focus on repairing harm to victims, offender responsibility, community involvement, rehabilitation. Victim-offender mediation, community courts. Both religions support where safe.

Christian Justice

Emphasize rehabilitation and forgiveness. Jesus came 'not to condemn but to save' (Jn 3:17). Capital punishment rejected because life is God's image. Victims deserve support. Offenders deserve redemption chance.

Islamic Justice (Shariah)

Hudud (fixed punishments), Qisas (proportional), Ta'zir (discretionary). Clear consequences deter crime. Justice protects society. Very high proof standard (4 witnesses for adultery).

Causes of Crime

Sociological: poverty, education, family breakdown. Christian: sin, temptation, separation from God. Islamic: disobedience, Shaitan, ignorance of Qur'an. Understanding causes helps prevention.

Restorative vs. Retributive

Retributive: 'eye for eye' — proportional punishment. Restorative: heal victim, offender responsibility, community healing. Victim-offender mediation. Both religions can support restorative where safe.

Death Penalty Case Study

Christian: Historically accepted (medieval), now mostly rejected. Pope John Paul II called for abolition. Sanctity of life incompatible with state killing. Islamic: Qur'an permits (5:45). Very high proof (4 witnesses). Can be forgiven by victim/family (Diyya). Modern debate: prison more effective?

Difference: Hudud vs. Ta'zir?

Answer: Hudud = fixed severe punishments prescribed by Qur'an (theft, adultery). Ta'zir = discretionary punishments for other crimes. Hudud requires very high proof (4 witnesses).

Why Christians increasingly oppose death penalty?

Answer: Sanctity of life — God's image. Jesus taught forgiveness. Once executed, innocence can't be restored. Rehabilitation/redemption more consistent with gospel.

What is Diyya?

Answer: Financial compensation paid by offender to victim/family as alternative to Qisas (death). Allows mercy and reconciliation instead of demanding death.

Watch Out

Don't oversimplify Shariah — high proof standard exists. Forgiveness ≠ no justice — offenders need accountability. Understanding causes ≠ excusing crime. Know modern Christian views — not all support death penalty.
