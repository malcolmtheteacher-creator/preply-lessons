Day of the Dead: Mexican Halloween?

Skulls, costumes and 2 November. Many people call it “Mexican Halloween”. It is not.

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. The family built an altar at home.
altar (n) — a table with photos and gifts for people who died. Example: “The family built an altar at home.”
2. They put food on the altar as an offering.
offering (n) — something you give to show respect. Example: “They put food on the altar as an offering.”
3. The marigold is the flower of the dead.
marigold (n) — a bright orange flower. Example: “The marigold is the flower of the dead.”
4. They honour their grandparents.
honour (v) — to show respect. Example: “They honour their grandparents.”
5. People paint a skull on their faces.
skull (n) — the bones of the head. Example: “People paint a skull on their faces.”
6. Families spend the night in the cemetery.
cemetery (n) — a place where dead people are buried. Example: “Families spend the night in the cemetery.”

The stereotype: “The Day of the Dead is just Mexican Halloween.”

The story

Part 1 — A family festival

The Day of the Dead (Día de Muertos) is on 1 and 2 November. Families build an altar at home. They put photos, candles and orange marigolds on it. They also put the favourite food and drink of the people who died. Many families clean the graves and spend the night in the cemetery. The mood is warm and happy, not scary.

Quick check:

1. When is the Day of the Dead?

2. Is the mood scary?

1. 1 and 2 November.

2. No, it is warm and happy.

Discuss:
  • Which food would you put on an altar?

Part 2 — Old and new

The festival mixes old Indigenous traditions, for example from the Aztecs, with the Catholic days of All Saints and All Souls. The famous skeleton lady, La Catrina, was a joke at first. An artist called José Guadalupe Posada drew her about 100 years ago. In 2008, UNESCO put the festival on its heritage list.

Quick check:

1. Who drew La Catrina?

2. What did UNESCO do in 2008?

1. José Guadalupe Posada.

2. It put the festival on its heritage list.

Discuss:
  • Do you know a festival that mixes old and new traditions?

Part 3 — Not Halloween

Halloween is about scary costumes and fun. The Day of the Dead is about memory and family. But the two festivals have changed each other. Mexico City's big parade began in 2016. A James Bond film showed a parade in 2015, and there was no real parade then. After the film, the city made one.

Quick check:

1. What is Halloween about?

2. When did the Mexico City parade begin?

1. Scary costumes and fun.

2. In 2016.

Discuss:
  • Is it OK to call it “Mexican Halloween”? Why not?

Grammar — was and were

was = I / he / she / it: “La Catrina was a joke at first.”
were = you / we / they: “The parades were big.”
Negative: wasn't / weren't.
