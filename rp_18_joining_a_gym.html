Joining a Gym

New year, new you — or so the salesperson hopes. Time to ask the right questions and hold your ground.

New to English? Try the easier A2/B1 version →

The Scene 🎬

You've walked past the new gym on the corner for three weeks now, telling yourself "next Monday". Today, on a whim, you actually go in. Glossy floors, a wall of running machines, someone lifting something enormous in the corner — and a bright, friendly face heading straight for you with a clipboard.

You want a few honest answers: how much it really costs, what you're tying yourself into, and whether it suits what you actually want — not what looks good on the poster. The trouble is, the person greeting you is paid to sign you up today, and they're very, very good at it.

So this isn't about being talked into anything. It's about staying friendly, getting the facts, and walking out with either a fair deal or your dignity — ideally both.

Warm up — talk for a minute or two

  1. Have you ever joined a gym — or signed up for a class, an app, a course — full of good intentions? Be honest: how long did it last, and what killed it?
  2. Some people are brilliant at saying "no thanks" to a pushy salesperson; others cave every time. Which are you — and what's the last thing you got talked into that you didn't really want?

Who's who

You
The Newcomer 👟

Curious but cautious. You'd genuinely like to get fitter — but you've been burned before, and you're not signing anything until you understand exactly what you're getting.

The other voice
The Membership Consultant 📋

Warm, quick, and quietly relentless. Every objection has a smooth answer ready. There's a "today only" deal — there's always a "today only" deal.

When you're the newcomer

"I'd like to get a feel for the place before I decide."
💷 Ask the facts
"Could you give me a rough idea of the monthly cost?"
"What kind of commitment am I looking at?"
🤝 Negotiate
"Is there any wiggle room on that?"
"Is there a cheaper plan with fewer extras?"
🎯 Say what you want
"I'm mainly looking to get back into some kind of routine."
"I'm not sure I'd use everything a premium membership throws in."
🛑 Resist the pressure
"I'd rather not rush into anything."
"Is that offer really only available today?"
📝 Leave on your terms
"Let me take the details away and have a think, and I'll come back to you."
"Thanks for the tour. That's been really useful."

When you're the consultant

"Welcome! Have you been to a gym before?"
"We have a special offer, today only."
"It's twelve months, and then it rolls monthly."
"I can take thirty pounds off if you sign up today."
"Let me show you round."

When something changes

"Can I cancel at any time?"
"Is there a joining fee?"
"Could I try a class first?"
"Could you put the offer in writing?"

Whenever you need a moment, or you missed something

"Sorry, I didn't quite catch that. Could you say it again?"
"Do you mean…?"
"Just so I've got this right, you're saying…"
"Could you go a bit slower, please?"
"Give me a second to think."
"How do you say that in English? I mean…"

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one role each, then swap. Spot as many phrases from your toolkit as you can. Listen for how the newcomer keeps smiling while saying, in effect, "not so fast".

ConsultantHi there, welcome in! First time? You've picked a great day — we've got a bit of an offer on. Are you looking to join?
YouMaybe — I'm just looking into my options at the moment, to be honest. I thought I'd come in and get a feel for the place before I commit to anything.
ConsultantAbsolutely, no pressure at all. Well, let me show you around. What are you hoping to get out of it — weight loss, strength, general fitness?
YouNothing dramatic, really. I'm mainly looking to get back into some kind of routine — a bit fitter, a bit stronger. I'm not training for a marathon or anything.
ConsultantPerfect, that's most people. So this is the main floor, free weights over there, classes run all day — spin, yoga, the lot. Now, memberships: our premium plan is normally forty-five a month, but signed up today it's thirty-two.
YouRight — and could you give me a rough idea of what the cheaper plans look like? I'm not sure I'd use everything a premium membership throws in.
ConsultantOf course. There's a standard plan at twenty-six — off-peak only, so no evenings. But honestly, for a few pounds more, premium's the one everyone ends up wishing they'd taken.
YouPossibly. Before we get into which plan, though — what kind of commitment am I actually looking at? Is it a rolling month, or am I tied in for a year?
ConsultantTwelve months on the discounted rate, so that's the one that locks in your saving. There's a rolling monthly option, but it works out pricier over the year.
YouThat's useful to know — I'd want to weigh those two up properly. And I take it there's a joining fee on top? Is there any wiggle room on that?
ConsultantThere's normally a thirty-pound joining fee, yes. Tell you what — sign up today and I can wave that for you. But it does have to be today; that's the deal I'm allowed to offer.
YouI appreciate that, genuinely. I'd rather not rush into a year-long contract on the spot, though. Out of interest — is that offer really only available today?
ConsultantOfficially, yes. Between us, these promotions do tend to come round again — but I can't promise which one, or when.
YouThat's fair enough, and thanks for being straight with me. Look — let me take the details away and have a proper think, and I'll come back to you. Could you write down the plans and the prices for me?
ConsultantCourse I can. No hard feelings — better you join and actually stick at it than sign up in a panic and vanish by February. Here, I'll pop my card in too.
YouPerfect — you've been really helpful, and not at all the shark I was braced for. I'll be in touch either way.

From memory — no peeking back up!

1. The newcomer walks in wanting "a bit fitter, a bit stronger" — nothing more. Why do they make a point of saying they're "not training for a marathon", and how does that shape which plan makes sense for them?

2. Three times the consultant nudges the newcomer towards the premium, twelve-month plan. Pick ONE of those nudges and explain the pressure tactic hidden inside it.

3. "Is that offer really only available today?" The newcomer asks it politely, but it isn't an innocent question. What are they actually testing — and what does the consultant's answer reveal?

1. By naming a modest goal out loud, the newcomer quietly resists being sold a bigger, pricier package than they need — it's a defence, not just small talk. It points them towards the cheaper or off-peak plan, since they don't need every premium extra.
2. Any of: "for a few pounds more, premium's the one everyone ends up wishing they'd taken" (social pressure + fear of regret); "that's the one that locks in your saving" (framing a year-long tie-in as a smart move); "signed up today it's thirty-two… but it has to be today" (artificial urgency). Each dresses up the consultant's goal as the customer's benefit.
3. They're testing whether the "today only" deadline is real or just a pressure tactic. The consultant's honest answer — the promotions "do tend to come round again" — confirms the urgency was manufactured, which frees the newcomer to walk away and think.

The Echo 🕰️

You're worrying about thirty-two pounds a month and whether you'll actually turn up. Spare a thought for the Spartans, who took fitness rather more seriously. In ancient Sparta, physical training wasn't a New Year's resolution — it was the whole point of being a citizen, drilled from childhood in a brutal state programme called the agogē.

No "today only" offers, no off-peak discount, no quietly skipping leg day. You trained, or you weren't really a Spartan. Twenty-five centuries later, we've turned the same instinct into a business — glossy floors, monthly direct debits, and a friendly face with a clipboard.

The Spartans never had to be talked into it. You, on the other hand, just have to survive the sales pitch.
