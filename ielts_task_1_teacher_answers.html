Section 2: What's Significant? ▼

Student selects 4 from 8 options. CORRECT answers:

Overall smartphone ownership increased dramatically across all age groups
WHY: Overview-level observation - the BIG picture
Young adults (18-34) consistently had highest ownership throughout
WHY: Describes consistent pattern/relationship between groups
The gap between age groups narrowed significantly by 2022
WHY: Describes CONVERGENCE - relationship change over time
The 65+ group showed fastest RATE of growth (5% to 60%)
WHY: Focuses on RATE of change (12x vs 2x growth)

INCORRECT answers:

In 2016, the 35-49 group had 75% ownership
WHY: Random mid-point with no significance
The 18-34 group started at 40% in 2010
WHY: Just isolated starting point, no pattern
Small dip in 50-64 ownership between 2018-2019
WHY: Minor fluctuation, not significant trend
By 2020, three groups had over 80% ownership
WHY: Just counting, not analyzing
Teaching Tip: Ask: "Which ones help an examiner understand the graph WITHOUT looking at it?" Answers 1-4 describe PATTERNS. Answers 5-8 just list data.

Section 3: Overview Language ▼

Three overviews shown. CORRECT: Overview C

Overview A: "Internet usage grew from 100 million to 2.5 billion..."
Contains specific numbers. Rule: Overview = NO specific data
Overview B: "The chart shows internet users in different regions..."
Just repeats title. Band 5 mistake - no analysis
Overview C: "Overall, internet usage increased dramatically across all regions, with Asia experiencing most significant growth..."
Describes trend, compares regions, shows relationship change - NO numbers
Teaching Tip: Tell student: "After you write your overview, check: Does it contain ANY numbers? If yes, delete them and rewrite without numbers. The overview should ONLY describe patterns, not data."

Section 4: Organization Strategy ▼

Energy consumption chart. Four strategies shown.

BEST: Strategy B - By source type (fossil fuels vs clean energy)
Creates natural comparison: declining fossil fuels vs growing renewables. Shows the STORY: energy transition
Why This Works: Allows contrasting language: "While fossil fuels declined, renewables increased"
Strategy A - By time period (1990-2005, then 2005-2020)
Repetitive - mention each source twice. Only works if dramatic turning point at specific year
Strategy C - By trend direction (declining vs growing)
Also good! Similar to Strategy B. Nearly as effective
Strategy D - One source per paragraph
Creates 5 short paragraphs. Too fragmented, hard to compare
Teaching Moment: Ask: "What's the STORY this graph tells?" Answer: "Shift from fossil to renewables" Then: "Which organization tells that story best?"

Section 5: Rates of Change ▼

Upgrade weak sentences to strong ones:

WEAK: "Asia increased to 2.5 billion. Europe increased to 700 million."
STRONG: "Asia increased 25-fold to reach 2.5 billion, while Europe saw more modest growth of approximately five times to 700 million."
Compares RATES (25x vs 5x), not just final numbers
WEAK: "Renewable energy was 50 units in 1990. It was 400 units in 2020."
STRONG: "Renewable energy increased eightfold from 50 to 400 units, representing the fastest growth among all energy sources."
Uses "eightfold" (rate) + adds context "fastest growth" (comparative analysis)
Vocabulary to Teach:
  • doubled, tripled, quadrupled (2x, 3x, 4x)
  • increased X-fold (25-fold, eight-fold)
  • at a faster/slower rate than
  • the rate of growth/decline

Section 6: Full Practice - House Prices ▼

Step 1: Identify Significant Features - TEACHING TRANSCRIPT

How to Guide This Step:

Teacher: "Look at the graph for 30 seconds. Don't write anything yet - just look. What do you notice?"

[Wait for student response]

Teacher: "Good start. Now let me ask you: Which city was ALWAYS the most expensive?"

Student: "London"

Teacher: "Correct. So that's significant - write that down: 'London consistently highest.'"

Teacher: "Now look at the START and END. Did all cities go up or down?"

Student: "All went up"

Teacher: "Good. But did they go up by the SAME amount? Look at the steepness of each line."

Student: "No... London went up more"

Teacher: "Exactly! So write: 'All increased, but at different rates. London fastest.'"

Teacher: "Now look at the GAP between the lines. At the start in 2000, what was the gap between London and Manchester?"

Student: "About 90,000"

Teacher: "And in 2020?"

Student: "About 320,000"

Teacher: "So the gap got BIGGER. Write: 'Gap between cities widened.'"

Strong observations (comparative, analytical):
  • All cities increased, but London grew fastest
  • London consistently most expensive throughout
  • Manchester showed steepest proportional growth
  • Gap between cities widened over time
Weak observations (too specific):
  • "In 2010, London was 280,000" (random mid-point)
  • "Edinburgh had second highest" (obvious, no analysis)

Step 2: Overview - WRITING TOGETHER

Teaching Transcript:

Teacher: "Now we write the overview. Remember: NO NUMBERS. Let's build it sentence by sentence."

Teacher: "First sentence - what happened overall to ALL cities?"

Student: "They all increased"

Teacher: "Good start. Let's make it more sophisticated. Type this: 'Overall, all four cities experienced substantial increases in house prices over the two decades'"

[Student types]

Teacher: "Now we need to add: were the increases the same?"

Student: "No, different"

Teacher: "Right! Add this after 'decades': comma, 'though at markedly different rates.' Read the whole sentence."

Student: "Overall, all four cities experienced substantial increases in house prices over the two decades, though at markedly different rates."

Teacher: "Perfect! That's sentence 1. Now sentence 2: What about London specifically?"

Student: "It was highest?"

Teacher: "Yes, and was it highest only at the end, or...?"

Student: "Always highest"

Teacher: "Exactly. Type: 'London consistently had the highest prices throughout'"

[Student types]

Teacher: "Now contrast - what about the cheapest city? Which was it?"

Student: "Manchester"

Teacher: "Good. Add: comma, 'while Manchester remained the most affordable despite showing the steepest proportional growth.' The word 'despite' is important - it shows Manchester was cheap BUT grew fast. Read the whole overview."

Student: [Reads complete overview]

Teacher: "Excellent. Now check: did you use ANY numbers?"

Student: "No"

Teacher: "Correct. That's a Band 7+ overview."

Final Overview: "Overall, all four cities experienced substantial increases in house prices over the two decades, though at markedly different rates. London consistently had the highest prices throughout, while Manchester remained the most affordable despite showing the steepest proportional growth."

Step 3: Body Paragraphs - WRITING TOGETHER

Body 1 Teaching Transcript:

Teacher: "We're grouping by price level. Body 1 = expensive cities. Which two?"

Student: "London and Edinburgh"

Teacher: "Correct. Let's start with London. First sentence: give the starting figure and say it was highest. Type: 'In 2000, London had the highest average house price at approximately 180,000 pounds'"

[Student types]

Teacher: "Good. Now in the SAME sentence, add Edinburgh's starting point. Add comma and type: 'followed by Edinburgh at 120,000.' Read it."

Student: "In 2000, London had the highest average house price at approximately 180,000 pounds, followed by Edinburgh at 120,000."

Teacher: "Perfect! That's starting figures for both. Now sentence 2: describe what happened to London. Start with 'Over the next two decades' - this shows time period. What happened to London?"

Student: "It increased to 520,000"

Teacher: "Yes, but let's use better vocabulary. Type: 'Over the next two decades, London's prices surged dramatically, reaching around 520,000 by 2020'"

[Student types]

Teacher: "Now add the RATE of change. Calculate: 180,000 to 520,000 - that's about 3 times bigger, right?"

Student: "Yes, nearly three times"

Teacher: "So add: comma, 'representing nearly three-fold growth.' This shows you can calculate rates, not just list numbers. Now Edinburgh - it went from 120,000 to 280,000. Type a new sentence: 'Edinburgh also saw substantial increases, climbing to 280,000'"

[Student types]

Teacher: "And the rate? 120 to 280 - that's more than double. Add: comma, 'more than doubling over the period.' Read the whole paragraph."

Student: [Reads Body 1]

Teacher: "Excellent. You've given start figures, end figures, rates of change, and compared the two cities. That's Band 7+ content."

Body 1 Final Version: "In 2000, London had the highest average house price at approximately 180,000 pounds, followed by Edinburgh at 120,000. Over the next two decades, London's prices surged dramatically, reaching around 520,000 by 2020, representing nearly three-fold growth. Edinburgh also saw substantial increases, climbing to 280,000, more than doubling over the period."
Body 2 Teaching Transcript:

Teacher: "Body 2 = cheaper cities. Birmingham and Manchester. Start with their beginning prices. Type: 'Birmingham and Manchester, which started at similar levels of around 100,000 and 90,000 respectively'"

[Student types]

Teacher: "Good. 'Respectively' means in that order - Birmingham 100k, Manchester 90k. Now add what happened: comma, 'showed more moderate growth patterns.' This contrasts with Body 1 where we said 'surged dramatically.'"

[Student adds]

Teacher: "New sentence. Give ending figures. Type: 'Birmingham rose steadily to 240,000, while Manchester's increase was more gradual, reaching 200,000 by 2020.' Notice 'while' - that's comparison."

[Student types]

Teacher: "Last sentence - link back to overview. We said London was always most expensive. Reinforce that. Type: 'Despite these increases, both cities remained significantly cheaper than London throughout the entire period.' Read the whole paragraph."

Student: [Reads Body 2]

Teacher: "Perfect! You've maintained the comparison throughout. That's high-level coherence."

Body 2 Final Version: "Birmingham and Manchester, which started at similar levels of around 100,000 and 90,000 respectively, showed more moderate growth patterns. Birmingham rose steadily to 240,000, while Manchester's increase was more gradual, reaching 200,000 by 2020. Despite these increases, both cities remained significantly cheaper than London throughout the entire period."
Organization Logic: Groups by price level (high vs low) allows price comparisons throughout. Alternative (by growth rate) also works.

Step 4: Checklist Explanation

How to check each item:
  • No numbers in overview: If student wrote "520,000" = WRONG
  • Identifies main trends: Not just "shows house prices" = WRONG
  • Grouped logically: Not 4 short paragraphs (one per city) = POOR
  • Starting AND ending figures: "Rose to 520,000" missing start = INCOMPLETE
  • Made comparisons: "London increased. Edinburgh increased." = NO comparison
  • Rates of change: "180,000 to 520,000" without "tripled" = Just amounts
  • Varied vocabulary: Using "increased" 5+ times = Too limited
  • 150 words: Quick count - aim for 150-180

Section 2: Bar Chart - Finding the Story ▼

Monthly spending in Japan, UK, Brazil. Select 3 from 6. CORRECT:

Housing was the largest expense category in all three countries
Identifies common pattern across all countries - comparative analysis
Brazil consistently had lower spending across all categories
Describes Brazil's relationship to others, not just "Brazil spent X"
The gap between countries was largest for housing costs
Analyzes WHERE biggest differences occur - sophisticated observation
Japanese people spent $1,200 on housing
Specific number without context/comparison - just listing
The UK had second-highest spending in most categories
Vague, adds little value - not wrong but weaker
Food spending ranged from $300 to $500
Just states range without explaining significance
Teaching Moment: All correct answers use COMPARATIVE language: "in all countries", "compared to", "gap between". This separates Band 7 from Band 6.

Section 3: Pie Chart - Rewrite Weak Sentences ▼

Common mistake:

"Oil increased from 40% to 35%"
"Increased" but number went DOWN!
"Oil's SHARE declined from 40% to 35%"
"Share" makes clear it's proportion, not absolute amount

Sentence Rewrites:

Weak: "Renewables was 5% in 2000. It was 15% in 2020."

Better versions:
  • "The proportion of renewable energy tripled from 5% to 15%"
  • "Renewable energy's share increased threefold, from 5% to 15%"
  • "Renewables accounted for 5% in 2000, rising to 15% by 2020"
Uses rate of change (tripled) + proportion language (share/proportion/accounted for)
Weak: "Coal was 30% and then 20%."

Better versions:
  • "Coal's share fell by one-third, from 30% to 20%"
  • "The proportion of coal declined from 30% to 20%"
  • "Coal accounted for 30% in 2000, but this proportion dropped to 20% by 2020"
"Fell by one-third" = sophisticated (10% drop from 30% = 33% reduction)
Challenge Students (Pie Charts): "When describing proportions in pie charts, never use 'was' or 'increased' alone. Always add: share/proportion/percentage/accounted for." This forces academic vocabulary for describing parts of a whole.

Section 4: Organization Decision ▼

Activity participation by age group. Organize by ACTIVITY or AGE GROUP?

ANSWER: Option A (by activity) is better for THIS chart
The STORY is: "How does each activity vary across age groups?"
Activity Patterns:
  • Swimming: Popular with young, declines with age
  • Reading: Stable across ages
  • Cycling: Peaks in middle age
  • Gaming: Dramatically higher in young people
Decision Framework: Ask: "What's the interesting pattern?"
  • If: "Activity X varies by age" → organize by activity
  • If: "Young vs old have totally different preferences" → organize by age
For THIS chart: Each activity has distinct age pattern, so organize by activity.

Section 5: Full Bar Practice - News Sources ▼

Overview: "Overall, there is a clear generational divide in news consumption, with younger people relying primarily on digital platforms while older demographics favor traditional media."
Body 1 (Digital dominance in young): "Among 18-24 year-olds, social media was by far the most popular news source at approximately 65%, followed by online news at 40%. In contrast, traditional media had minimal appeal to this group, with TV at just 15% and newspapers virtually non-existent at under 5%. The 25-34 age group showed a similar but slightly less extreme pattern, with social media at 50% and online news at 45%."
Body 2 (Traditional media in older): "The trend reversed sharply for older demographics. The 45-64 group favored TV at 60%, with online sources and social media each around 30%. Among those 65+, TV dominated at approximately 75%, while social media usage was negligible at under 10%."
Alternative Organization: Could organize by SOURCE TYPE instead:
  • Body 1: Social media and online (digital)
  • Body 2: TV and newspapers (traditional)
Both work! Choice depends on which story to emphasize.

Section 6: Full Pie Practice - Waste Disposal ▼

Complete Model Answer (178 words):

"The two pie charts illustrate how waste disposal methods changed between 2000 and 2020.

Overall, there was a significant shift away from landfill toward more environmentally sustainable disposal methods, particularly recycling and composting.

In 2000, landfill accounted for nearly two-thirds of all waste disposal, at 64%, making it by far the dominant method. Incineration represented approximately a quarter at 25%, while recycling and composting were relatively minor at 8% and 3% respectively.

By 2020, the waste disposal landscape had transformed considerably. Landfill's share had fallen by nearly half to 35%, while incineration remained relatively stable at 22%. The most dramatic changes occurred in sustainable methods: recycling increased more than fourfold to reach 32%, while composting tripled to account for 11% of waste disposal.

These figures demonstrate a clear trend toward more environmentally responsible waste management, with combined recycling and composting rising from just 11% to 43% over the twenty-year period."

Key Techniques:
  • Proportion language: "accounted for", "represented"
  • Fractions: "nearly two-thirds", "approximately a quarter"
  • Change: "fell by nearly half", "fourfold", "tripled"
  • Synthesis: "combined recycling + composting = 43%"

Section 2: Passive Voice Activity ▼

Question 1: "Workers pick the coffee beans"

ANSWER: The coffee beans are picked
Plural subject "beans" needs "are"
The coffee beans pick (active voice, incorrect)
The coffee beans is picked (agreement error)

Question 2: "Machines dry the beans in the sun"

ANSWER: The beans are dried in the sun
Passive form of "dry" is "dried"
The beans dry in the sun (could work but sounds like beans dry themselves - not standard for process descriptions)
The beans are dry in the sun ("dry" as adjective, not passive verb)

Question 3: "The factory packages the final product"

ANSWER: The final product is packaged
Singular "product" needs "is"
The final product packages (active, doesn't make sense)
The final product are packaged (agreement error)
Teaching Note: Students struggle with is/are. Remind them:
  • Singular (product, bean, paper) = is
  • Plural (products, beans, papers) = are

Section 3: Process Practice - Recycled Paper ▼

Overview: "Overall, the diagram illustrates an eight-stage process for producing recycled paper, beginning with collection from recycling bins and ending with the cutting of dried sheets."
  • States number of stages (8)
  • Identifies start (collection)
  • Identifies end (cutting)
  • NO detailed description
Body 1 (Collection & Preparation): "At the first stage, used paper is collected from recycling bins. Following this, it is sorted by type to separate different grades of paper. The sorted paper then is shredded into small pieces before being mixed with water to create a pulp."
  • All passive: "is collected", "is sorted", "is shredded"
  • Sequencing: "At the first stage", "Following this", "then"
  • Groups stages 1-4 (preparation)
Body 2 (Processing & Finishing): "Once the pulp is formed, any ink is removed through a cleaning process. At this point, the pulp may be bleached if white paper is required. In the final stages, the cleaned pulp is rolled into thin sheets, which are then dried and cut to the appropriate size."
  • "Once" = good transition between paragraphs
  • "May be bleached" = passive with modal (conditional step)
  • "In the final stages" = clear signposting
  • Groups stages 5-8 (finishing)
Organization Logic: Split at pulp formation:
  • Body 1 = Turning paper into pulp (breaking down)
  • Body 2 = Turning pulp into paper (building up)

Section 5: Map Practice - Greenfield Village ▼

Overview: "Overall, Greenfield Village was transformed from a small rural settlement into a developed suburban area, with the northern natural areas being replaced by residential and commercial facilities."
  • Describes overall change (rural → suburban)
  • Identifies where most change occurred (northern)
  • Uses passive: "was transformed", "being replaced"
  • NO specific buildings listed
Body 1 (Removals & Major Changes): "The most significant changes occurred in the northern part of the village. The forest in the northwest was cleared and replaced by a car park. Similarly, the farmland in the northeast was developed into a large housing estate containing 150 homes, compared to the original 12 houses."
  • Location phrases: "northern part", "northwest", "northeast"
  • Passive: "was cleared", "was replaced", "was developed"
  • Numbers OK in body (150 vs 12 houses)
  • "Similarly" = good linking
Body 2 (New Facilities): "In the centre of the village, a shopping center was constructed, with a school built to the east of it. The river in the south remained unchanged, although a marina was added alongside it."
  • Location phrases: "centre", "to the east of", "south", "alongside"
  • Shows what DIDN'T change: "remained unchanged"
  • Adds development to unchanged feature (marina by river)
Organization Logic: Body 1 = Major transformations (forest → car park, farmland → housing)
Body 2 = New additions (shopping, school, marina)

Alternative (also OK): Body 1 = Northern changes, Body 2 = Central/southern changes

Common Student Questions

Quick answers for frequent questions

How many words should I write? ▼

ANSWER: 150 minimum, but 170-190 is ideal
  • 150 = bare minimum (risky)
  • 170-190 = safe zone
  • 200+ = wasting time for Task 2
Tell them: Count to 170 words in practice, then stop. That's your natural length.

Can I give my opinion? ▼

NO. Never.
  • "I think this is concerning..." = WRONG
  • "In my opinion..." = WRONG
  • "This suggests..." = WRONG
Just describe what you see
Task 1 = Describe data. Task 2 = Give opinions

What if I don't understand the topic? ▼

Doesn't matter. You're not tested on topic knowledge.

Example: Graph shows "Semiconductor exports"

  • Student doesn't know what semiconductors are
  • Doesn't matter! Just describe: "Product A exports increased..."

Tell them: IELTS doesn't fact-check. Focus on describing the visual, not explaining the topic.

Should I write a conclusion? ▼

NO. The 4 paragraphs are:
  • 1. Overview
  • 2. Body 1
  • 3. Body 2
  • 4. (That's it - no conclusion)
Note: Some teachers say "conclusion optional" but it's waste of time. Overview = your conclusion.

How do I know which features are significant? ▼

Ask yourself:
  • What's the HIGHEST/LOWEST?
  • What CHANGED THE MOST?
  • What's the OVERALL TREND?
  • Are there any EXCEPTIONS?
  • Do any lines/bars CROSS or CONVERGE?

If it's one of these → significant
If it's random middle value → skip it

Can I use "we can see" or "the chart shows"? ▼

Overview - NO:
  • "We can see that Asia increased..." = AVOID
  • "The chart shows that..." = AVOID
Overview - YES:
  • "Asia increased..." = Direct
  • "Housing was highest..." = Direct
Body - OK occasionally:
  • "The chart indicates that..." = Acceptable
  • "As shown in the graph..." = Acceptable

But still better without. Just state facts directly.
