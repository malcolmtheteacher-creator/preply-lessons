Check your idea CLICK
"Are you...?" already has the verb be, so it just moves to the front. "Know" and "live" are ordinary action verbs, not forms of "be" — English can't just move them, so it borrows a helper word, do, to build the question instead. Two different tools for two different jobs.
Today: two ways to ask yes/no questions — with do/does and with be — so you can ask real questions when you meet someone new.

Do You...? "Do you like tea?"

For questions about actions and habits — with any ordinary verb.

Spot the pattern

Three real questions:

  • "Do you like tea?"
  • "Does she work here?"
  • "Do they live near you?"

Before you check: what comes first, and what happens to the main verb — like, work, live?

Check the pattern CLICK
Do (I/you/we/they) or Does (he/she/it) comes first, then the subject, then the verb in its base form — never "likes", "works" or "lives" in the question. "Does" already carries the "-s" for he/she/it, so the main verb goes back to plain form.
Do  +  I / you / we / they  +  base verb ...?
Does  +  he / she / it  +  base verb ...?

From statement to question

"He watches football."
→
"Does he watch football?"

"Does" arrives at the front and takes the "-s" with it — that's why "watches" becomes plain "watch".

Short answers — just do/does, nothing more

  • "Do you like tea?" — "Yes, I do." / "No, I don't."
  • "Does she work here?" — "Yes, she does." / "No, she doesn't."

Are You...? "Are you ready?"

For questions with be — feelings, jobs, places, descriptions.

Spot the pattern

Three real questions:

  • "Are you ready?"
  • "Is he your brother?"
  • "Are they at home?"

What's different here compared with the "Do you...?" questions? Say it before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
No "do" or "does" at all. Am / Is / Are is already the main verb, so it just swaps places with the subject and moves to the front. There's nothing left to add.
Am  +  I  +  ...?
Is  +  he / she / it  +  ...?
Are  +  you / we / they  +  ...?

From statement to question

"The bus is late."
→
"Is the bus late?"

"Is" simply jumps to the front of its own sentence — no extra helper word needed.

Short answers — just am/is/are, nothing more

  • "Are you ready?" — "Yes, I am." / "No, I'm not."
  • "Is he your brother?" — "Yes, he is." / "No, he isn't."

Which One?

Real life doesn't tell you which type to use. Here's the trick.

Look at these two statements:

  • "He is hungry."
  • "He wants a sandwich."

One already contains a form of "be". The other doesn't. Which is which — and what does that tell you about how to turn each one into a question?

Check the trick CLICK
"He is hungry" already has "is" — just move it: "Is he hungry?"
"He wants a sandwich" has no "be" word — you must add "does": "Does he want a sandwich?"
The question to ask yourself every time: is there already an am/is/are in this sentence?
Already has am/is/are? → move it to the front: Am/Is/Are + subject + ...?
No am/is/are? → add Do/Does + subject + base verb ...?
