Assessment: Does Evil Disprove God?

YEAR 8 · UNIT 2: THE PROBLEM OF EVIL

What You'll Do

You will be assessed on your understanding of the problem of evil, the major theodicies, and your ability to evaluate arguments and form your own reasoned position.

Assessment Task 1: Knowledge Retrieval Test

Answer these questions to show you understand key concepts from Unit 2:

Question 1: Define "theodicy" and explain why it matters in religious studies.
Question 2: What is the inconsistent triad, and why is it a problem for theists?
Question 3: Explain the difference between the logical problem of evil and the evidential problem of evil. Give an example of each.
Question 4: State and briefly explain the free will defence. What type of suffering does it address?
Question 5: What does "sabr" mean in Islam, and how does it relate to suffering?
Question 6: In Buddhism, what is the First Noble Truth, and how does it differ from Christian theodicy?

Assessment Task 2: Comparison Essay

Write an essay (300-400 words) comparing TWO responses to the problem of evil. Choose from:

  • Christianity (free will or soul-making) vs. Islam (divine testing)
  • Christianity vs. Buddhism
  • Islam vs. Humanism

Essay Structure

  1. Introduction: State which two worldviews you're comparing and what aspect of the problem of evil they address.
  2. First Perspective: Explain how the first worldview answers the problem. What are its strengths?
  3. Second Perspective: Explain how the second worldview answers the problem. What are its strengths?
  4. Analysis: Compare the two approaches. Which aspects of each do you find most convincing? Why? What are the weaknesses of each?
  5. Conclusion: Which response seems more adequate to you, and why?

Assessment Task 3: Evaluation Essay

Write an essay (400-500 words) evaluating whether any of the theodicies we've studied adequately answer the problem of evil.

Essay Structure

  1. Introduction: State whether you think the problem of evil has been adequately answered. What criteria would a "good" theodicy need to meet?
  2. Evidence for Theodicy Success: Present the strongest arguments from the theodicies that suggest the problem HAS been solved. Which ones work best?
  3. Evidence Against Theodicy Success: Present the strongest objections to theodicies. What remains problematic? Describe types of suffering that seem hard to justify.
  4. Your Position: Weigh the evidence. Do you think belief in God remains reasonable despite suffering? Why or why not?
  5. Conclusion: Summarize your position and reflect on what you've learned about how worldviews address fundamental human questions.

Assessment Task 4: Creative Response

Choose one of the following creative responses:

Option A: Imagined Dialogue

Write a dialogue between a Christian, a Muslim, a Buddhist, and a humanist discussing suffering. Each person should articulate their worldview's response authentically, and the conversation should show genuine engagement with different perspectives.

Option B: Personal Reflection

Write a personal essay (300-400 words) reflecting on a time when you or someone you know faced significant suffering. Which theodicy (if any) resonates with that experience? How has learning about different worldviews perspectives affected your thinking about suffering?

Option C: Visual Argument

Create a poster or infographic comparing how Christianity, Islam, Buddhism, and Humanism answer the question "Why do good people suffer?" Include symbols, quotes, and key concepts from each worldview.

Mark Scheme

Knowledge Retrieval (30 marks)

2-3 marks per answer. Award marks for accurate, clear definitions and explanations of key concepts.

Comparison Essay (30 marks)

15 marks per worldview: Accurate explanation of position, identification of strengths, relevant examples. Award bonus marks for sophisticated analysis of limitations.

Evaluation Essay (30 marks)

10 marks each for: Clear statement of position with criteria; evidence for theodicy success; evidence against theodicy success. Award bonus marks for nuanced, reasoned judgment and reference to specific theodicies.

Creative Response (10 marks)

Award marks for: authentic representation of worldviews, engagement with multiple perspectives, clear communication, thoughtful reflection.

Reflection Questions

Before submitting, ask yourself:
  • Have I accurately represented each worldview's position?
  • Have I identified both strengths and limitations of each theodicy?
  • Is my own position clearly stated and reasoned?
  • Have I used examples from the lessons to support my arguments?
  • Have I considered suffering that seems hardest to justify?
  • Is my writing clear and well-organized?
