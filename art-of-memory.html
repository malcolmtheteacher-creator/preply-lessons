The Art of Memory

A course in the oldest memory technique in the world. Each lesson is a room with a true story at the door, a method you can learn in an evening, and practice that proves it works. Written in clear English for learners and for anyone who wants a better memory.

Step through the door →

Two and a half thousand years ago, a poet walked out of a banquet hall a moment before the roof fell in. The guests inside could not be recognised. The poet could name every one of them, because he remembered where each person had been sitting. That story is the seed of everything in this course: we remember places far better than lists, so we put what we want to keep in a place.

You will build a house in your head and fill it, room by room. The first five rooms teach the basics: how memory works, how to make a picture stick, and how to use your body, a building and a story as places for what you want to keep. Then numbers, people, cards, speeches, words, formulas and ideas, and finally the habits that keep it all alive. Take one room at a time. Each is about an hour.

How every room works

1 · The StoryA short true story, or a scene, that shows the idea at work. Questions after each part.
2 · The MethodThe technique in clear steps, with a worked example and diagrams.
3 · Try ItYou build the pictures yourself. Models appear only after you have tried.
4 · DrillFlash cards, a quick quiz and a timed memory challenge.
5 · Look BackQuestions from earlier rooms, what you can now do, and one thought to keep.

Before you start

  • You need a pen and paper, and about an hour for each room.
  • You need a few places you know very well. A home you lived in, a street you walk, a school.
  • Say things out loud when a box asks you to. Speaking is part of remembering.
  • Your ticks and notes are saved on this device only.
