Why Do We Travel?

✈️ The average person will travel to 12 countries in their lifetime

Meet Sarah, a 26-year-old marketing manager from London. Last year, she took 15 days of vacation. She went to Italy for 5 days, Spain for 4 days, and Greece for 6 days. She stayed in nice hotels, ate at restaurants every meal, and took hundreds of photos. Each trip cost about £1,500. Total vacation spending: £4,500 (about $5,600 USD).

When you ask Sarah, "Why do you travel?", she says: "I work hard all year. I need a break. I want to see famous places, try new food, and relax on the beach. When I'm traveling, I forget about work completely."

Now meet Tom, also 26 years old, also from London, also works in marketing. Last year, Tom also took 15 days of vacation. But his experience was completely different.

Tom went to Thailand for 15 days straight. He stayed in cheap hostels (£8 per night), ate street food (£2 per meal), and took buses instead of taxis. He spent most of his time hiking in the mountains and talking to local people. Total cost: £600 (about $750 USD) — that's 7 times cheaper than Sarah!

When you ask Tom, "Why do you travel?", he says: "I don't want a break from my life — I want to experience something completely different. I want to challenge myself, meet people who think differently than me, and learn about other cultures. The less comfortable I am, the more I learn."

🌍 Researchers studied why people travel and found 3 main types of travelers

Dr. Anna Kowalski, a tourism psychologist at the University of Vienna, studied 10,000 travelers from 25 countries. She found three completely different travel personalities:

Type 1: The "Relaxers" (like Sarah) - 45%

  • Want comfortable hotels, good food, and familiar experiences
  • Prefer beach resorts, cruise ships, or popular tourist cities
  • Take lots of photos to show friends and family
  • Main goal: rest and escape from work stress
  • Average spending: $3,000-5,000 per week

Type 2: The "Explorers" (like Tom) - 35%

  • Want adventure, challenges, and new experiences
  • Prefer backpacking, hiking, volunteering, or living with locals
  • Less interested in photos, more interested in memories and learning
  • Main goal: personal growth and understanding other cultures
  • Average spending: $500-1,500 per week

Type 3: The "Non-Travelers" - 20%

  • These people don't like traveling at all!
  • They prefer to stay home or visit the same place every year
  • Reasons: "Too expensive," "Too stressful," "I'm happy at home"
  • Main goal: save money and avoid the stress of traveling
😰 Here's something surprising: 48% of people say they need a vacation to recover from their vacation!

Dr. Kowalski explains: "For many 'Relaxer' type travelers, the vacation is almost as stressful as work. They spend months planning every detail, they worry about money, they stress about getting the perfect photos for Instagram, and when they come home, they're exhausted. Meanwhile, 'Explorer' type travelers often come home with more energy because they had real experiences, not just a break from routine."

The research also found that people who travel to learn something new are 34% happier six months after their trip compared to people who only travel to relax.

Think About Your Travel Style:

  • Are you more like Sarah (comfortable hotels, relaxation) or Tom (cheap hostels, adventure)?
  • Do you take lots of photos when you travel? Why or why not?
  • What's the longest trip you've ever taken?
  • After a vacation, do you feel rested or tired?

5 Phrases for Talking About Travel

Click each phrase to see examples and practice using it!

1. "I've always wanted to go to..."
Formality: Neutral (common in conversation)
Meaning: You have a dream destination — a place you really want to visit someday. This shows you've been thinking about it for a long time.
Example:
"I've always wanted to go to Japan to see the cherry blossoms."
"I've always wanted to go to Iceland to see the Northern Lights."
NOW YOU TRY:
Where have you always wanted to go? Tell your teacher: "I've always wanted to go to _______"
(Example: New Zealand, Egypt, Norway, Brazil, Morocco)
2. "I'm not really a beach person"
Formality: Informal
Meaning: You don't enjoy beach vacations much. This is a polite way to say you prefer other types of travel. You can also say: "I'm not really a city person" or "I'm not really an adventure person."
Example:
"I'm not really a beach person — I prefer mountains and hiking."
"I'm not really a city person — I like quiet places in nature."
NOW YOU TRY:
What type of vacation are you NOT interested in? Say: "I'm not really a _______ person"
(Example: beach, city, mountain, camping, cruise, backpacking)
3. "I could spend hours just..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: There's an activity you love so much while traveling that time passes quickly. You enjoy it so much that you don't get bored.
Example:
"I could spend hours just walking around new cities."
"I could spend hours just sitting in cafes and watching people."
"I could spend hours just looking at art in museums."
NOW YOU TRY:
What activity could you do for hours while traveling? Say: "I could spend hours just _______"
(Example: exploring markets, taking photos, trying local food, hiking, swimming)

More Phrases for Travel & Holidays

4. "I'm dying to..."
Formality: Informal (shows strong desire)
Meaning: You REALLY want to do something — you're extremely excited about it. This is much stronger than "I want to." Be careful: "dying to" is an expression and doesn't actually mean death!
Example:
"I'm dying to visit New York City — I've wanted to go for years!"
"I'm dying to try authentic Italian pizza in Italy."
"I'm dying to go on a safari in Africa."
NOW YOU TRY:
What travel experience are you really excited about? Say: "I'm dying to _______"
(Example: visit Paris, see the pyramids, try sushi in Japan, go scuba diving)
5. "I can't imagine living without..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: Something is so important to you that life would feel empty without it. For travelers, this often refers to the importance of travel itself in their life.
Example from the story:
"I can't imagine living without traveling — I need to see new places every year."
"I can't imagine living without taking at least one big trip per year."
"I can't imagine living without experiencing different cultures."
NOW YOU TRY:
Can you imagine living without traveling? Or is there something about travel that's essential for you? Say: "I can't imagine living without _______" OR "I can imagine living without traveling — I prefer _______"
(Example: exploring new cities, having adventures, relaxing at home, saving money)
