YEAR 8 · UNIT 4: PEACE & CONFLICT

Assessment: Peace or Conflict?

What have you learned about religion, peace, and conflict?

Knowledge Check

Answer the following questions to consolidate what you've learned:

Question 1: Define "Just War theory" and name two of its six criteria.
Question 2: What is the difference between "greater jihad" and "lesser jihad" in Islam?
Question 3: What does "ahimsa" mean in Buddhism, and why is it important?
Question 4: What is the Khalsa in Sikhism?
Question 5: Name one religious peacemaker and explain how their faith motivated their work.

Comparing Traditions

Religion approaches peace and conflict in different ways. Complete this comparison task:

Question 6: Compare Christian and Islamic approaches to military conflict. How are they similar? How do they differ?

Hint: Think about Just War theory and the rules for jihad. What conditions must be met in each tradition?

Question 7: Buddhist and Sikh approaches to violence seem opposite. Are they? Explain.

Hint: What do they both value? What situations does each one address?

Remember: When comparing traditions, look for both similarities and differences. Sometimes traditions that seem opposite actually share values (like protecting the innocent), but apply them in different ways.

Evaluating the Evidence

Now for the challenging part: thinking critically about the big question.

Question 8: Is religion a force for peace or conflict? Make an argument.

Remember: This isn't a yes/no question. The evidence shows religion can be both. Your job is to analyse the evidence and make a reasoned argument.

Structure your answer:

  • State your position clearly
  • Give at least two pieces of evidence from what you've learned
  • Address the counterargument (what would someone who disagrees say?)
  • Explain why your position is stronger
Question 9: What do you think is the most important lesson from this unit?

This is your chance to reflect on what you've learned. There's no single right answer.

Challenge extension: Can you think of a modern conflict that's often described as "religious"? Apply the REASOM framework to show how religion is involved, but so are economics, history, politics, and other factors. What does this teach us about how to address the conflict?
