Emailing Your Teacher

Master formal email writing: greetings, requests, and professional tone

Email Structure

The Five Parts

1. Greeting: Formal opening with teacher's name

2. Purpose: State why you're writing in the first sentence

3. Details: Provide necessary information or explain the situation

4. Request/Close: Ask clearly for what you need or thanks

5. Sign-off: Formal closing with your name

Greetings

Starting Formally

Dear Prof./Dr. [Last Name], — Most formal (for university)

Dear Mr./Mrs./Ms. [Last Name], — Standard formal

Hello [Name], — Slightly less formal, still professional

Avoid: "Hi," "Hey," "What's up" — too casual for teachers

Common Requests

Asking Politely

Asking a question: "Could you clarify what you mean by...?"

Asking for help: "I'm struggling with the assignment. Could you give me some guidance?"

Asking for an extension: "I'm having difficulty finishing on time. Would it be possible to have an extension?"

Asking to meet: "Would you have time to meet during office hours to discuss...?"

Asking to resubmit: "May I have the opportunity to revise my assignment?"

Sign-offs

Closing Formally

Best regards, — Professional and warm

Kind regards, — Formal and respectful

Yours sincerely, — Very formal (UK)

Thank you, — When expressing gratitude

Avoid: "XOXO," "Cheers," "Later" — too casual

Important Rules

Email Etiquette

✓ Use a clear, descriptive subject line

✓ Keep emails concise and well-organised

✓ Use proper spelling and grammar

✓ Write during school hours (not at 2 AM)

✓ Allow time for response (don't expect instant replies)

✓ Be polite and respectful even if frustrated

Essential Vocabulary

Clarify Make something clearer or easier to understand
Extension More time to complete an assignment
Concise Brief and to the point; not wordy
Formal Following proper rules and conventions
Guidance Advice or direction on how to do something
Reciprocal Mutual; given or felt by both sides
Appreciate Feel thankful or recognise the value of something
Revise Make changes to improve something

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: Fix the Email

"Hi! I don't get the homework. Can u explain it? Thanks!"

Rewrite this more formally.

"Dear [Teacher], I'm having difficulty understanding the homework assignment. Could you clarify the requirements? Thank you. Best regards, [Your name]"

Exercise 2: Write a Request

You need an extension because you're sick. Write the email.

"Dear [Teacher], I am writing to request an extension on the assignment due [date]. I have been unwell and need a few more days to complete the work. Would an extension until [date] be possible? Thank you for understanding. Best regards, [Your name]"

Exercise 3: Thank Your Teacher

Your teacher gave you helpful feedback. Write a thank you email.

"Dear [Teacher], I wanted to thank you for the detailed feedback on my essay. Your comments have helped me understand where to improve. I really appreciate your time and guidance. Best regards, [Your name]"

Complete Email Examples

Subject: Question about Essay Assignment

Dear Dr. Smith,

I am writing to ask for clarification on the essay assignment due next week. Could you explain what you mean by "critical analysis"?

Should I include personal opinions or focus only on academic sources?

Thank you for your help. I appreciate your time.

Best regards,
[Your name]
Subject: Request for One-Week Extension

Dear Mrs. Johnson,

I am writing to request an extension on the project due March 26. I have faced unexpected family circumstances that prevent me from completing the work on time.

Would it be possible to submit the project by April 2 instead?

I sincerely appreciate your consideration.

Kind regards,
[Your name]

Key Takeaways

✓ Always use formal greetings with your teacher's name

✓ State your purpose in the first sentence

✓ Be specific when asking for help or extensions

✓ Keep emails concise and well-organised

✓ Use proper spelling and grammar

✓ End with a professional sign-off and your name
