The Longitude Prize

The Longitude Prize is offering £10 million to help solve a major world problem.

Questions about the Prize

Why is it called the Longitude Prize?

It's named after the historical Longitude Prize from the 1700s, which was a major innovation challenge that helped advance navigation and science.

What do you have to do to win it?

You must develop a solution to one of six key scientific problems facing the world today and demonstrate that your solution works.

What is the prize?

£10 million will be awarded to the winner to help implement and develop their solution.

What are the six key scientific problems?

The problems cover areas like food security, clean water, disease prevention, energy, and environmental sustainability. Can you think of which specific problems would be selected?

Background

The original Longitude Prize (1714) was created by the British government to solve the problem of determining longitude at sea, which was critical for navigation. This new prize follows that tradition by addressing major challenges facing humanity.

Major Global Problems

The Longitude Prize considers six key scientific problems facing humanity. Read about each and think about which one you think is most pressing.

Problem A: Food Security

Ensuring that all people have access to safe, nutritious food. Climate change and population growth make this increasingly difficult.

Problem B: Clean Water Access

Providing clean drinking water to billions of people worldwide. Contaminated water causes disease and limits economic development.

Problem C: Disease Prevention

Developing ways to prevent deadly diseases like malaria, tuberculosis, and other infectious diseases that kill millions annually.

Problem D: Sustainable Energy

Creating reliable, clean energy sources that can power the world without damaging the environment or causing climate change.

Problem E: Education and Opportunity

Providing quality education to all children, regardless of their location or economic status, to create equal opportunities.

Problem F: Climate Change

Developing technology and solutions to combat global warming and adapt to climate change impacts on food, water, and ecosystems.

Your Arguments

Think of arguments for why each problem deserves to win the Longitude Prize. Consider:

  • How many people does this problem affect?
  • How urgent is this problem?
  • What would be the impact of a solution?
  • Is this a solvable problem?

Discussion Task

Timer for Discussion

5:00

Instructions

Step 1: Prepare

You will receive information about one of the six problems. Read about your problem and think of arguments for why it deserves the £10 million Longitude Prize.

Step 2: Present

Present your problem to the group. Explain why you think it's the most pressing problem facing humanity.

Step 3: Discuss

Listen to the other problems presented by other students. Discuss each one and compare them.

Step 4: Decide

As a group, decide which problem should be awarded the Longitude Prize. You must agree on a single choice and explain your decision to the class.

What to Include in Your Presentation

1. Problem Overview

Clearly state what the problem is and why it exists.

2. Impact

Explain how many people are affected and in what ways.

3. Urgency

Explain why this problem needs to be solved now, not later.

4. Potential Solution

Describe what a winning solution might look like.

5. Global Benefit

Explain how solving this problem would benefit humanity worldwide.

Useful Language Reminders

  • Use expressions like "The main issue is…" and "In my view…"
  • When you agree, say "You're absolutely right" or "That's a good point"
  • When you disagree, use polite language: "I see your point, but…"
  • Ask for clarification: "Could you explain that further?"

Success Criteria

Your discussion was successful if you:

  • ✓ Clearly presented a specific problem
  • ✓ Gave strong arguments using discussion expressions
  • ✓ Listened to and engaged with others' ideas
  • ✓ Used language to agree, disagree, and ask for clarification
  • ✓ Reached a group decision and explained it
