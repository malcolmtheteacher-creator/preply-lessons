The Longitude Prize

The Longitude Prize is named after the historic challenge to solve maritime navigation. Today, it offers £10 million to the person or team who can solve a major world problem.

Why is it called the Longitude Prize?

In the 18th century, the Longitude Prize was offered to anyone who could solve the problem of measuring longitude at sea. Ships were getting lost because they could not determine their exact position. Today the name is reused for solving modern world problems.

Who decides what the prize will be awarded for?

The public votes on which of six key scientific problems facing the world today should be solved. The problem chosen gets the £10 million prize.

Six Problems for the Longitude Prize

These are the major scientific challenges considered for the prize:

Antibiotic Resistance

Bacteria are becoming resistant to antibiotics. This means diseases that were once treatable are becoming deadly again. We need new ways to fight bacterial infections.

Sustainable Food Production

The world population is growing. We need to produce enough food to feed everyone while protecting the environment and not destroying ecosystems.

Clean Water Access

Billions of people lack access to clean, safe water. This causes disease and prevents economic development. We need affordable solutions to provide clean water to all.

Zero-Carbon Flight

Aviation contributes significantly to climate change. We need to develop aircraft that fly without producing carbon emissions.

Restoring Movement

Millions of people are paralysed due to spinal cord injuries. We need medical breakthroughs to restore movement and allow people to walk again.

Early Diagnosis of Dementia

Dementia is diagnosed only after significant cognitive damage. We need a way to detect dementia early so treatment can begin before major symptoms appear.

Language for Presenting a Problem

Starting a discussion:

  • The key issue is...
  • One major concern is...
  • The main problem facing us is...

Giving your opinion:

  • I would argue that...
  • In my opinion, the most important problem is...
  • I believe this problem needs urgent attention because...

Language for Agreeing and Disagreeing

Agreeing:

  • I agree with you completely
  • That is a really valid point
  • You are absolutely right

Disagreeing politely:

  • On the other hand...
  • I see your point, but I think...
  • That is interesting, however...
  • What do you think about...?

Practice Sentences

Complete: "The key issue facing our planet is..."

Accept any reasonable completion about a global problem. Example: "...climate change and its effects on food production."

How would you respond politely to a different opinion?

Use phrases like: That is an interesting point, however I would argue that... or I see what you mean, but on the other hand...
