Food Quiz

Test your knowledge about food and cooking terms.

Which type of water is usually served with meals in fancy restaurants?
Click to Reveal
Still or sparkling water. Still water has no bubbles; sparkling water has bubbles (carbonated).
What is a risotto?
Click to Reveal
An Italian rice dish made by slowly stirring rice with broth, butter, and cheese until creamy. Popular variations include mushroom risotto and seafood risotto.
What is gazpacho?
Click to Reveal
A cold Spanish soup, usually made from tomatoes, peppers, onions, and cucumbers. Perfect for hot weather!
What does "sweet tooth" mean?
Click to Reveal
A strong desire or preference for sweet foods. Someone with a sweet tooth loves desserts and sweets.
Name three types of fish.
Click to Reveal
Salmon, cod, tuna, trout, mackerel, sea bass, or halibut are all common types of fish used in cooking.
Where does manoushe (or manooshe) come from?
Click to Reveal
The Levantine region (Lebanon, Syria, Palestine). It's a flatbread topped with zaatar (thyme and sesame) or cheese.
What are three ways to cook a steak?
Click to Reveal
Rare, medium, and well-done. You can also have medium-rare or medium-well. This refers to how cooked the inside is.

Expressing Opinions & Making Decisions

These phrases help you plan and explain your menu choices.

Phrase 1: Explaining a Change of Plan

"I was going to make a mushroom risotto, but then I remembered..."

Use this when you change your mind about a dish because of something you just thought of.

Phrase 2: Multiple Benefits

"Not only would it be fresh from the market..."

Use "not only... but also" to list several good reasons for choosing a dish.

Phrase 3: Safe Choice

"I reckon that's a safe bet"

"Reckon" (British) means think or believe. A "safe bet" is something that's very likely to work or be popular.

Phrase 4: Giving a Reason

"I thought since it's a bit cold, I could do a pea soup"

Use "since" to explain your reason for choosing a warm or seasonal dish.

Phrase 5: Explaining Why Something Won't Work

"I don't think garlic bread will go down well because..."

Use this to politely disagree and give your reason why a dish might not be liked.

Phrase 6: How Flavours Work Together

"I think the herb topping would really complement the soup"

Use "complement" when one dish enhances or pairs beautifully with another.

Phrase 7: Making a Final Decision

"Let's just stick to the soup and plain bread"

Use "stick to" when you want to keep it simple or settle on something.

Let's Discuss

Think and share your thoughts on these:

  • Have you watched cooking reality shows? Which ones? What makes them interesting to you?
  • What's the difference between cooking at home and being a professional chef?
  • How important is presentation when you serve food to guests? Why?
  • Tell us about a meal that really impressed you. What made it special?
  • If you could cook with any famous chef, who would it be? What would you ask them?
  • How does food connect to culture? Can you share a traditional dish from your culture?
  • What's the best meal you've ever had? Where were you? Who were you with?
  • Do you think eating is just about nutrition, or is it about something more? Explain your thinking.
Keep going: These are deep questions. Take time to explain your ideas clearly. Use the phrases you learned to express your opinions!
