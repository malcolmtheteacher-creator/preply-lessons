Reporting Questions: "She asked where I lived"

For retelling wh-questions people asked you — no question mark, no "do/does/did", just normal word order.

Spot the pattern

Three questions, reported later:

  • "Where do you live?" → "She asked where I lived."
  • "What are you cooking?" → "He asked what I was cooking."
  • "How much did the tickets cost?" → "She asked how much the tickets had cost."

Before you check: where did "do", "are" and "did" go, and what order are the words in now?

Check the pattern CLICK
"Do/does/did" simply disappear. The rest goes back to normal statement order — subject before verb — and the verb often shifts one step into the past, because "asked" is already past.
wh-word + subject + verb (statement order) — never do/does/did
This is the single biggest trap in reported questions: the word order of a question ("Where do you live?") is NOT kept. It flips back to statement order, exactly like "I live here."

From question to report

"Where are you going?"
→
He asked where I was going.

Reporting Yes/No Questions: "He asked if I was coming"

When the original question has no question word at all, English needs to add "if" or "whether".

Spot the pattern

Three yes/no questions, reported later:

  • "Are you coming to the party?" → "She asked if I was coming to the party."
  • "Do you like spicy food?" → "He asked whether I liked spicy food."
  • "Have you finished the report?" → "My boss asked if I had finished the report."

What word had to be added that wasn't in the original question at all? Say it before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
if or whether. A yes/no question has no wh-word to introduce the reported clause, so English inserts one — then the word order goes back to normal statement order, exactly like with wh-questions.
if / whether + subject + verb (statement order) — never do/does/did
"if" and "whether" both work in almost every case here. "Whether" sounds a little more formal, and it's the only choice before "or not": "She asked whether or not I was coming."

Reporting Requests & Orders: "He told me to wait"

For instructions, requests and warnings — no exact words, just told/asked/warned + person + to + verb.

Spot the pattern

Three instructions, reported later:

  • "Please wait here." → "He told me to wait there."
  • "Don't be late!" → "She told me not to be late."
  • "Could you close the window?" → "She asked me to close the window."

What always sits between "told/asked" and "to"? Say it before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
A person (me, him, her, us...). "Told" and "asked" here always need someone in between — you can't say "told to wait", it has to be "told me to wait". For a negative instruction, "not" goes right before "to": told me not to be late.
told / asked / warned + person + (not) to + base verb
"told" and "asked" always need a person straight after them. Skipping the person ("told to wait outside") is the single most common mistake with this structure.

A few more reporting verbs, kept simple

  • "I'll call you tomorrow." → He promised to call me tomorrow.
  • "Shall I carry that bag?" → She offered to carry the bag.
  • "Would you like to come to dinner on Saturday?" → They invited me to come to dinner on Saturday.
  • "Don't touch that pan, it's hot!" → She warned me not to touch the pan.

Notice: promise and offer usually go straight to "to + verb" with no person; invite and warn work like told/asked, with a person in between.
