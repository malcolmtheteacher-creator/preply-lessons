Understanding Past Participles

What is a Past Participle?

The past participle is the THIRD FORM of a verb.

Every verb has 3 forms:

  1. Base form (infinitive) - walk, go, eat
  2. Past simple - walked, went, ate
  3. Past participle - walked, gone, eaten ← THIS ONE!

Two Types of Verbs

1. REGULAR VERBS - Easy! Just add -ed

The past simple and past participle are THE SAME!

Base Form Past Simple Past Participle
work worked worked
learn learned learned
watch watched watched
play played played

2. IRREGULAR VERBS - You must memorize these!

The past participle is DIFFERENT from the past simple.

Base Form Past Simple Past Participle
go went gone
eat ate eaten
see saw seen
do did done
be was/were been
drive drove driven
write wrote written

How to Use Past Participle with Past Perfect

Formula: HAD + PAST PARTICIPLE

Regular Verb Example:

I had worked there for 5 years before I left.

→ had + worked (past participle)

Irregular Verb Examples:

She had gone home before the party started.

→ had + gone (past participle of "go")

I had never eaten sushi before I visited Japan.

→ had + eaten (past participle of "eat")

By age 10, I had already learned to swim.

→ had + learned (past participle of "learn")

Common Irregular Past Participles

Base Past Participle Example with Past Perfect
be been I had been to Paris twice before.
see seen I had never seen snow before.
do done I had done my homework before dinner.
make made She had made a mistake.
know known I had known him for years.
take taken He had taken the test before.
speak spoken I had never spoken English before.

Remember!

Past Participle = The 3rd form of the verb

Past Perfect = HAD + Past Participle

✅ Regular verbs: just add -ed
✅ Irregular verbs: you must memorize them!
