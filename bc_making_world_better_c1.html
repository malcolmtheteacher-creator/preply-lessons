Understanding Gerunds

A gerund is the -ing form of a verb used as a noun. It's essential for speaking about actions, habits, and ideas.

When to Use Gerunds

1. As the subject of a sentence:

Smoking is not allowed in public places.

Teaching is a rewarding profession.

2. After a preposition:

I'm terrified of flying.

She's interested in learning a new language.

3. After certain verbs (avoid, enjoy, consider, imagine, etc.):

The driver couldn't avoid hitting the cat.

I enjoy reading books before bed.

4. In set phrases:

It's no good sitting there and feeling sorry for yourself.

It's worth considering all options before deciding.

Practice: Sentence Rewriting

Rewrite these sentences using gerunds. Keep the meaning the same:

1. You do realise that you have to work weekends in this job, Ms Jackson?

Rewrite using "entails": You do realise that this job entails working weekends?

Other similar structures:

  • "...involves working weekends"
  • "...requires working weekends"
  • "...means working weekends"

2. We were able to buy a new car when we won the football pools.

Rewrite using "meant": Winning the football pools meant we were able to buy a new car.

Hesitation Devices for Natural Speech

When speaking, it's natural to pause and think. Use these hesitation devices to sound more fluent and give yourself time to organise your thoughts:

Well, ...

Use to show you are considering what to say next.

Example: "Well, I think that making education more accessible would help a lot of people."

I mean, ...

Use to clarify or rephrase what you just said.

Example: "It's hard to get a good job these days. I mean, without proper qualifications, it's really difficult."

You know, ...

Use to suggest the listener is already familiar with something or to seek agreement.

Example: "You know, pollution is getting worse every year. It affects our health."

Actually, ...

Use to correct yourself or add new information.

Example: "Most animals are well-treated in research. Actually, there are strict regulations about this."

Right, ...

Use to confirm understanding or to move to the next point.

Example: "Right, so the main idea is that we need to be more sustainable."

Anyway, ...

Use to get back to the main topic or move the conversation forward.

Example: "There are lots of small things we could change. Anyway, the biggest improvement would be..."

Practice Tip: Try recording yourself saying a few sentences about a world problem. Notice how you naturally pause and think. Use these devices to fill those pauses smoothly.

Check Your Understanding

What is a gerund and when would you use it?

A gerund is the -ing form of a verb used as a noun. You use it: as the subject of a sentence ("Reading is fun"), after prepositions ("interested in learning"), after certain verbs ("avoid smoking"), and in set phrases ("it's worth trying").

How would you rephrase "You must avoid making mistakes" using a gerund?

"Avoiding mistakes is important" or "You can't avoid making mistakes." The structure depends on context, but the gerund "avoiding/making" keeps the same meaning.

What is a hesitation device and why use one?

A hesitation device (like "well," "I mean," "you know") is a natural filler that gives you time to think while speaking. It makes your speech sound more fluent and natural instead of awkward silences.

Which hesitation device would you use to clarify something you just said?

"I mean" is used to clarify or rephrase. Example: "It's hard to find good jobs these days. I mean, without proper training, it's really competitive."
