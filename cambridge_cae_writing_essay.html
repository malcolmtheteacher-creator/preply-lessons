What Examiners Really Want

The CAE essay is not just about expressing your opinion. It tests your ability to discuss multiple perspectives, organize ideas coherently, and use sophisticated language appropriately.

The Task: Write 220-260 words responding to a statement, addressing 2 points from the prompt AND adding your own relevant idea.

The 4 Marking Criteria

Content

Have you addressed all parts of the task? Both given points + your own idea?

25%

Communicative Achievement

Is it appropriate for the target reader? Is the register suitable?

25%

Organisation

Is it well-organized with clear paragraphs and effective linking?

25%

Language

Is there a range of vocabulary and grammatical structures?

25%

Band 3 vs Band 5: What's the Difference?

Both responses below answer the same question: "Should schools teach financial literacy?"

Band 3 Response

"I think schools should teach financial literacy because it is very important. Many young people don't know about money. They don't know how to save or spend wisely. Also, parents are busy and can't always teach this. So schools should do it. In my opinion, this is a good idea."


Problems:

  • Simple vocabulary ("important", "good idea")
  • Basic sentence structures
  • Repetitive linking ("Also")
  • No clear paragraphing
  • Only one perspective

Band 5 Response

"While it is often argued that financial education should be the responsibility of parents, there are compelling reasons why schools should incorporate this vital skill into their curriculum. Not only would this ensure equal access to financial knowledge, but it would also prepare students for the economic challenges they will inevitably face as adults."


Strengths:

  • Complex structures (concession + main point)
  • Sophisticated vocabulary ("compelling", "incorporate", "inevitably")
  • Varied linking ("While", "Not only...but also")
  • Multiple perspectives acknowledged
The Key Insight: The difference isn't about having better ideas - it's about HOW you express and organize those ideas.

Decision 1: Understand the Task Format

Every CAE essay task follows the same format. Understanding this format is your first strategic decision.

The Standard Format

"With increasing automation, many traditional jobs are disappearing. Some people believe this is a positive development, while others are concerned about the impact on society."

Write an essay discussing two of the points below and include your own idea:

  • Employment opportunities
  • Quality of life
  • Economic inequality

Breaking Down the Requirements

You MUST:
  1. Respond to the statement (show you understand the issue)
  2. Discuss exactly TWO of the three given points
  3. Add your OWN relevant idea (not from the notes)
  4. Write 220-260 words
Common Mistakes:
  • Discussing all three points (choose only TWO)
  • Forgetting to add your own idea
  • Writing too much or too little
  • Not addressing the statement directly

Decision 2: Structure Your Essay

A clear, logical structure is essential for a Band 5 essay. Follow this 5-paragraph model:

Paragraph 1: Introduction 35-45 words

Purpose: Introduce the topic and establish your position

Structure:

  • Hook or context (1 sentence)
  • Restate the issue in your own words (1 sentence)
  • Thesis statement - your overall position (1-2 sentences)

Model: "In recent years, technological advancement has fundamentally transformed the workplace. While automation undoubtedly offers numerous benefits, its impact on employment and society requires careful consideration."

Paragraph 2: First Point from Notes 50-60 words

Purpose: Develop the first point with reasoning and examples

Structure:

  • Topic sentence introducing the point
  • Explanation or reasoning
  • Example or evidence
  • Link to overall argument

Model: "One significant consideration is the effect on employment opportunities. While automation may eliminate certain jobs, it simultaneously creates new roles in technology and innovation. For instance, the tech industry now employs millions of people in positions that did not exist two decades ago."

Paragraph 3: Second Point from Notes 50-60 words

Purpose: Develop the second point with contrast or addition

Structure:

  • Linking phrase connecting to previous paragraph
  • Topic sentence for second point
  • Development with reasoning
  • Supporting example

Model: "Furthermore, the impact on quality of life cannot be overlooked. Automation has the potential to free workers from repetitive, dangerous tasks, allowing them to pursue more fulfilling activities. Nevertheless, this transition must be managed carefully to prevent social disruption."

Paragraph 4: Your Own Idea 45-55 words

Purpose: Add a new perspective not mentioned in the notes

Structure:

  • Clear transition signaling a new idea
  • Introduce your point
  • Brief development
  • Connect to overall discussion

Model: "Beyond these considerations, I would argue that education systems must adapt to prepare future generations. Schools should focus on developing skills that complement rather than compete with automation, such as creativity, critical thinking, and emotional intelligence."

Paragraph 5: Conclusion 30-40 words

Purpose: Summarize and give final opinion

Structure:

  • Summary phrase
  • Restate main points briefly
  • Final position/recommendation

Model: "In conclusion, while automation presents both opportunities and challenges, its success depends on how society manages this transition. With proper planning and education, we can harness its benefits while minimizing negative consequences."

Decision 3: Use Sophisticated Language

Band 5 essays demonstrate a wide range of vocabulary and grammatical structures. Here's how to upgrade your language.

Linking Devices by Function

Function Basic Sophisticated
Addition Also, And Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, What is more
Contrast But, However Nevertheless, Nonetheless, On the other hand, Conversely
Cause/Result So, Because Consequently, As a result, Therefore, Hence
Example For example For instance, To illustrate, A case in point is
Concession Although While, Whilst, Despite the fact that, Notwithstanding

Complex Grammatical Structures

Click: Inversion Structures

What it is: Changing normal word order for emphasis

Examples:

  • "Not only does automation increase efficiency, but it also reduces costs."
  • "Rarely has society faced such a transformative technological shift."
  • "Only by investing in education can we prepare for this change."

Click: Cleft Sentences

What it is: Splitting a sentence to emphasize a particular element

Examples:

  • "It is education that holds the key to adapting to automation."
  • "What concerns many people is the potential for job losses."
  • "The reason why this matters is that it affects everyone."

Click: Mixed Conditionals

What it is: Combining different time references in conditional sentences

Examples:

  • "If governments had invested in retraining earlier, workers would be better prepared today."
  • "Had we anticipated these changes, we would not be facing such challenges now."

Band 3 vs Band 5 Language Comparison

Band 3 Language

  • "This is very important because..."
  • "Many people think that..."
  • "Also, another point is..."
  • "In my opinion, I think..."
  • "This is a good/bad thing"

Band 5 Language

  • "This issue merits consideration because..."
  • "It is widely acknowledged that..."
  • "Furthermore, a compelling argument is..."
  • "From my perspective..."
  • "This development has profound implications"

Decision 4: Address the Reader Appropriately

The CAE essay requires a semi-formal register. You need to sound educated and thoughtful, not casual or overly academic.

Register Guidelines

Too Informal

  • "Loads of people reckon..."
  • "It's kind of obvious that..."
  • "You know what I mean?"
  • "This stuff is really cool"
  • Using contractions: "don't", "won't"

Appropriately Semi-Formal

  • "Many people argue..."
  • "It is evident that..."
  • "This point requires clarification"
  • "This development is significant"
  • Full forms: "do not", "will not"
Avoid Contractions: In formal writing, always use full forms: "cannot" not "can't", "do not" not "don't", "it is" not "it's".

Engaging the Reader Without Being Casual

Purpose Phrases
Acknowledge different views "While some may argue...", "Although it is often claimed...", "Despite differing opinions..."
Present your opinion "From my perspective...", "I would contend that...", "It seems reasonable to suggest..."
Emphasize importance "It is essential to consider...", "This issue merits attention because...", "The significance of this cannot be understated"
Make recommendations "It would be advisable to...", "A sensible approach would be...", "One solution might be to..."

Full Practice Task

"Social media has transformed how people communicate and share information. While some see this as progress, others believe it has had negative effects on society."

Write an essay discussing two of the points below and include your own idea:

  • Personal relationships
  • Access to information
  • Mental health

Write your essay in 220-260 words.

Step-by-Step Planning

Your Planning Template

My position: What is your overall view? (balanced / mostly positive / mostly negative)
Point 1 (from notes): Which point? What will you say about it?
Point 2 (from notes): Which point? How does it relate to Point 1?
My own idea: What new perspective can you add? (e.g., privacy, democracy, education)
Conclusion: What is your final position/recommendation?

Write Your Essay

Word count: 0 / 220-260

Self-Assessment Checklist

  • I have addressed the statement in my introduction
  • I have discussed exactly TWO points from the notes
  • I have added my OWN relevant idea
  • My essay has 5 clear paragraphs
  • I have used varied linking devices
  • I have used at least one complex structure (inversion, cleft, conditional)
  • I have avoided contractions
  • My register is semi-formal throughout
  • I have a clear conclusion with my final position
  • My word count is between 220-260

Model Answer with Annotations

Click to reveal model answer

Paragraph 1 - Introduction:
In the digital age, social media platforms have fundamentally altered human interaction. While these technologies offer unprecedented connectivity, their broader impact on society remains contentious. This essay will examine both the opportunities and challenges presented by this transformation.

Paragraph 2 - Point 1 (Personal relationships):
One significant consideration is the effect on personal relationships. Although social media enables people to maintain connections across vast distances, it has arguably diminished the quality of face-to-face interaction. For instance, many young people now prefer texting to conversation, potentially limiting their development of interpersonal skills.

Paragraph 3 - Point 2 (Access to information):
Furthermore, access to information has been revolutionized. Never before has knowledge been so readily available to such a wide audience. Nevertheless, this abundance comes with risks, as misinformation can spread rapidly, making it increasingly difficult to distinguish fact from fiction.

Paragraph 4 - Own idea (Privacy):
Beyond these considerations, I would argue that privacy concerns deserve attention. The vast amounts of personal data collected by social media companies raise serious questions about surveillance and individual autonomy in the modern era.

Paragraph 5 - Conclusion:
In conclusion, while social media has brought undeniable benefits in connectivity and information access, these advantages must be weighed against potential harm to relationships and privacy. A thoughtful, critical approach to these platforms is essential for harnessing their potential while mitigating their risks.
