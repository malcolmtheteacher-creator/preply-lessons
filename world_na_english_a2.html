Same Language, Different Words?

Americans and Canadians speak English. But their words and spelling can be different.

Key words

1. The spelling of “colour” is British.

The stereotype: “Americans and Canadians speak exactly the same English.”

The story

Part 1 — Webster's dictionary

After independence, the dictionary maker Noah Webster wanted the USA to have its own English. In his 1828 dictionary, he used simpler spellings: “color”, “center”, “defense”. Many changes stayed. That is one reason why American spelling is different from British spelling.

Quick check:

1. Who made a dictionary in 1828?

2. What is one American spelling?

1. Noah Webster.

2. color, center or defense.

Discuss:
  • Which spelling do you use?

Part 2 — Canadian English

Canadian English mixes the two. Canadians write “colour” and “centre” like the British, but say “truck” and “gas” like Americans. A “toque” is a winter hat. A “double-double” is a coffee with two creams and two sugars. Some Canadians say “eh?” at the end of a sentence.

Quick check:

1. What is a toque?

2. What is a double-double?

1. A winter hat.

2. A coffee with two creams and two sugars.

Discuss:
  • Do you know a special word from your area?

Part 3 — Other languages

In Quebec, French is the main language of work, signs and schools. In the USA, more than 40 million people speak Spanish at home. There are also many Chinese-, Vietnamese- and Arabic-speaking communities. Before Europeans came, hundreds of Indigenous languages were spoken. Many are in danger now, but some are coming back.

Quick check:

1. What is the main language in Quebec?

2. How many Americans speak Spanish at home?

1. French.

2. More than 40 million.

Discuss:
  • Which languages are spoken in your city?

Grammar — the same as and different from

the same as: “Canadian spelling is the same as British spelling.”
different from: “American spelling is different from British spelling.”
similar to: “Canadian English is similar to American English.”

1. Choose the word

1. American spelling is British spelling. (different from / the same as)

2. Canadian “colour” is British “colour”. (the same as / different from)

3. Canadian English is American English in some words. (similar to / different)

1. different from

2. the same as

3. similar to
