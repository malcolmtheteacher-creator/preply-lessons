The Flat Box

A furniture company asked its customers to do something strange: carry the furniture home in pieces and build it themselves. Why did millions of people say yes?

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then answer the questions before you read the next part.

Part 1 — A very young businessman

In 1943, a seventeen-year-old boy from Sweden started a small company. His name was Ingvar Kamprad. He called it IKEA. The name came from his own initials and the first letters of his family's farm and village.

At first, Ingvar did not sell furniture. He sold small things, like pens and picture frames, and people ordered them by post. In 1948, he began to sell furniture. He used a catalogue with pictures and prices, and customers sent their orders.

But there was a problem. Customers wanted to touch and see the furniture before they bought it. So in the 1950s, IKEA opened a showroom in the small town of Älmhult, and in 1958 it opened its first big store there.

  • Would you start a company at seventeen? What would you sell?

Part 2 — The problem with big furniture

Furniture is big and heavy. A finished table needs a lot of space in a warehouse, and a lot of space in a truck. Delivery is expensive, and the furniture often arrives damaged. All this makes the price higher.

In 1956, the story says, a young IKEA designer called Gillis Lundgren had a simple idea. He wanted to put a table into his car, but the table did not fit. So he took the legs off. Suddenly, the table was flat, and it fitted easily.

Kamprad liked the idea very much. IKEA began to sell furniture in flat boxes. The customer took the box home and assembled it with a small tool and a page of instructions.

True or false? Click your answer.

1. Finished furniture is easy and cheap to move.

2. The designer took the legs off a table to fit it in his car.

3. In a flat box, the customer must put the furniture together.

Talk about it
  • What is good about furniture in a flat box? What is bad?
  • Do you think the table story is true? Why does the story work so well for the company?

Part 3 — Why it worked

The flat box was a clever bet, because it made things cheaper in many places. A flat box takes less space in the warehouse and in the truck. It breaks less often. And the customer does the last job — carrying it home and building it — so IKEA does not need to deliver it.

All this means IKEA can save money, and it can give the customer a lower price. People did not mind building the furniture, because it was much cheaper.

There was a risk. Some people hated the instructions and the missing screws. But many customers felt proud when the furniture stood in their room. Today IKEA has stores in dozens of countries. One of its simplest products, the Billy bookcase, has sold more than a hundred million.

The lesson is clear: if you ask customers to do some work, give them a good reason, like a much lower price.

Something that started in the past and continues now: has been growing, have been waiting

The story of IKEA is a story of things that started a long time ago and are still going on. For this we use the present perfect continuous.

Form: have / has + been + verb-ing → IKEA has been selling furniture since 1948. / Customers have been building their own furniture for many years.

for + a length of time: for sixty years, for a long time, for two hours. since + a starting point: since 1958, since Monday, since I was a child.

Questions: How long have you been working here? / How long has the company been growing?

Be careful: if you say when something finished or happened, use the past simple: IKEA opened its first store in 1958. (not has been opening in 1958)

Remember: for and since are different. for ten years ✓ / since ten years ✗
