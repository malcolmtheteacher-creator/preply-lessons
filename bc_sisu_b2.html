When you want to stop

Everyone reaches a moment when the easy thing to do is give up — the last kilometre of a long run, a difficult project that drags on for months, a conversation that is going nowhere. Some people stop there. Others find a way to keep going. Let us start with you.

Two runners

Imagine two people running their first marathon. At kilometre 32, both are exhausted and in pain. One thinks, “This is too much — I have done enough,” and walks off the course. The other thinks, “I have nothing left — but I will take one more step, and then one more,” and finishes hours later, slowly, but on their own two feet.

A word with no easy translation

Here is the idea behind a famous Finnish word. Read it, then we will talk about it.

In Finland, there is a word that Finns are very proud of: sisu. It is hard to translate into English with a single word. People reach for “grit”, “determination”, “inner strength” or “guts” — but none of them is quite right.

Sisu is not ordinary determination. It is the strength you find after your ordinary strength has run out. It is what makes a person keep going when every reasonable part of them says it is time to stop. The Finns sometimes describe it as a “second wind” of the will — a hidden reserve of energy that appears only when things are at their hardest.

The word became famous during the bitterly cold Winter War of 1939, when a small Finnish population resisted a far larger army in freezing conditions. Foreign journalists could not understand how they kept fighting, and the Finns answered with one word: sisu. But sisu is not only for war or for extreme situations. Finns talk about it in everyday life too — finishing a hard task, getting through a long, dark winter, or simply not complaining when things are difficult.

Importantly, sisu is not the same as being stubborn. Stubbornness means refusing to change for no good reason. Sisu means choosing to continue towards something that matters, even when it costs you. It is quiet, not loud. People with sisu do not talk about how strong they are — they simply keep going.

Sisu is not the strength to start. It is the strength to continue when starting is long behind you.

Grammar focus — verbs of effort: -ing or to?

When we talk about not giving up, we use a lot of verbs followed by another verb: “I kept going”, “I managed to finish”, “I refused to stop”. The tricky part is that some verbs are followed by the -ing form (gerund) and others by to + verb (infinitive). This is one of the most common B2 mistakes — and an easy one to fix.

1. The two patterns

After certain verbs we use the -ing form; after others we use to + infinitive. There is no deep logic — they are learned as groups.

verb + -ing: keep, keep on, carry on, give up, avoid, can't help, feel like, finish
verb + to: manage, refuse, decide, learn, try, want, hope, promise, agree

-ing: She kept running even though her legs hurt.

to: She managed to run the whole way.

After a preposition, always use -ing: “He succeeded in finishing.” “She insisted on carrying on.”

2. Side by side

verb + -ingverb + to
I kept going.I decided to go on.
He gave up smoking.He refused to give up.
They carried on working.They promised to work harder.
She avoided giving up.She learned to endure it.

Practice B — make one sentence

Join the ideas using the verb in brackets, in the correct form.

1. It was hard. She continued anyway. (carry on) → She ____ even though it was hard.

2. He was in pain. He still crossed the line. (manage) → Despite the pain, he ____ the line.

3. Everyone advised him to stop. He said no. (refuse) → He ____, although everyone advised him to.

4. The task was boring. She did not stop. (keep) → She ____ at the task until it was done.

1. She carried on (working) even though it was hard.

2. Despite the pain, he managed to cross the line.

3. He refused to stop, although everyone advised him to.

4. She kept working at the task until it was done.

C · When should you quit? (4 minutes)

Sisu is about not giving up. But sometimes the wise thing really is to stop — to leave a job, end a project, or change direction.

How do you tell the difference between giving up too early and refusing to quit when you should? Talk it through with an example from work or life.
