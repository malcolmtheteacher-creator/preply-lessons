Why Physics Matters for Epicureanism

This might surprise you: Epicureanism is fundamentally a physics course. Not in the modern sense of equations and experiments, but in the ancient Greek sense — physika, inquiry into the nature of things.

To understand why people suffer from fear and irrational desire, Epicurus believed you had to understand the physical nature of reality. His ethical advice makes no sense without his physics. And his atomism (inherited from Democritus, refined by Lucretius) is the key to everything.

Today we ask: What is the universe made of? How did it form? Is there purpose in nature? The answers are more revolutionary than you might expect.

Atomism: The Epicurean Foundation

Democritus, Epicurus, and Atomism

Epicurus didn't invent atomism. Around 440 BCE, the philosopher Democritus developed atomic theory: the universe is made of atoms (atomos = "uncuttable") and void (empty space).

Epicurus adopted Democritus's atomism wholesale — almost. He kept the fundamental insight but made one crucial modification that we'll explore in Lesson 7. The key point: Epicurus used inherited physics to build his own ethics.

⚛️
A Note on "Atoms":
Ancient atoms are NOT like modern atoms. They're not made of protons and electrons. For Democritus and Epicurus, atoms are tiny, invisible, eternal, indivisible particles. Think of them as the smallest possible units of matter that cannot be broken down further.

The Two Fundamentals

The entire universe consists of exactly two things:

⚫

ATOMS

Eternal, indivisible, solid particles. All material things.

◯

VOID

Empty space. Without it, atoms couldn't move.

Nothing Else Exists

This statement is radical. No Forms (Plato's perfect abstract ideas). No God crafting the universe. No purpose imposed from outside. No spirits, no supernatural forces.

Just atoms moving in void, colliding, combining, and separating. That's it. Everything in existence — your body, your mind, the Earth, the stars — is made of atoms rearranged.

Pause and consider: What are the implications of a universe made only of atoms and void? If there's no designer, no purpose built in, no divine plan — what follows?

Key implications:

  • No cosmic purpose: Nothing happens "for a reason" in a purposive sense. Atoms move according to their nature, not toward a goal.
  • No divine judgment: The universe doesn't care about morality. You won't be punished by gods in an afterlife.
  • No hidden order: What you see is what you get. Nature follows mechanical rules, not mysterious intentions.
  • Human freedom becomes possible: If the universe isn't designed, then human choices aren't predetermined.

Properties of Atoms

Atoms are eternal and indestructible. They have three essential properties:

The Three Properties of Atoms
1. Shape (Schema)

Atoms differ in shape — some are smooth, some rough, some hooked. Different shapes combine differently, creating different materials.

2. Size (Megethos)

Atoms vary in size. Smaller atoms can slip between larger ones. This variation explains the diversity of matter.

3. Weight (Rhope)

Atoms have weight and naturally move downward. This "tendency to fall" explains why atoms collide and interact.

No Creation or Destruction

Here's the key logical move: atoms are eternal. They cannot be created from nothing. They cannot be destroyed into nothing.

"Nothing comes to be from what is not. Nothing passes away into what is not."

This rules out creation (God making something from nothing) and destruction (things vanishing entirely). The universe has no beginning and no end. It just is — eternal atoms in eternal void.

The sum total of things has always been such as it is now, and such it will ever be. For there is nothing into which it can change. For outside the whole there is nothing which could enter into it and bring about the change.

— Epicurus, Letter to Pythocles (as reported by Diogenes Laërtius)

Question: If the universe is eternal (no creation) and made only of atoms moving in void (no design), what does this mean for the classical arguments for God's existence? What "problems" does this physics solve?

Problems dissolved by atomic physics:

  • The First Cause problem: "Who/what made the universe?" — Answer: The universe is eternal; there is no first cause.
  • Design argument: "The order in nature proves a designer" — Answer: Order emerges naturally from atoms moving and colliding, not from design.
  • Teleology: "Things exist for a purpose" — Answer: Things exist because atoms combined that way; no purpose required.
  • Creation myth: "God created the world" — Answer: The world is eternal; nothing was created.

The theological consequence: If the universe is eternal and self-explanatory, there's no need for God as creator. This is why Epicureanism was so feared by religious authorities.

Why This Physics Matters for Ethics

The Chain of Reasoning

Here's how Epicurus connects physics to ethics:

  1. Physics: The universe is atoms and void. Eternal. No design. No creator.
  2. Consequence: There is no divine punishment or reward in an afterlife.
  3. Consequence: You are not fated or predetermined. Free will is real (more on this in Lesson 7).
  4. Consequence: Your happiness depends only on you, not on cosmic forces or divine will.
  5. Ethical conclusion: Pursue ataraxia (freedom from fear and pain). Don't worship gods for protection or favor. Cultivate friendship and simple living.

Attacking Fear

Epicurus's primary target was fear. Most human suffering, he believed, comes from irrational fear:

  • Fear of divine punishment: If gods are made of atoms (like everything else), they can't influence your life. They're busy in the intermundia, unconcerned with human affairs.
  • Fear of fate: If there's no designer, there's no predetermined destiny. You're free.
  • Fear of chaos: Atoms follow natural laws. The universe isn't random or capricious — it's mechanical and comprehensible.

When you are young, train yourself in physics. Then as you grow older, you will be freed from fear and superstition. For nothing terrible happens to the wise.

— Lucretius, paraphrasing Epicurus

A Philosophy of Freedom

By establishing a materialist physics with no design or divine intervention, Epicurus created space for human autonomy. You are not a puppet of the gods. You are not fated. You are free to choose your own path.

This freedom is what makes ethics possible. You can cultivate friendships. You can practice virtue. You can seek ataraxia. Not because the universe demands it (it doesn't demand anything). But because you, freely, recognize that this is the path to happiness.
