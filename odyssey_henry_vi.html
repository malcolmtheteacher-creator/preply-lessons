The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. He became king as an infant of nine months.
an infant (n) — a baby.
2. In 1453 he fell into a stupor.
a stupor (n) — a state of near-unconsciousness.
3. He suffered a mental breakdown.
a breakdown (n) — a collapse of mental health.
4. Richard of York was made Protector.
a protector (n) — someone who rules for an unfit king.
5. He decided to found Eton and King's College.
to found (v) — to start an institution.
6. He was gentle and pious.
pious (adj) — religious.
7. The court split into factions.
a faction (n) — rival groups.
8. He was restored to the throne in 1470.
to restore (v) — to bring back.
9. He died in the Tower of London.
the Tower (n) — the royal fortress and prison.
10. The red rose became a symbol of Lancaster.
a rose (n) — a flower; here, a badge.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The baby king

Henry VI became king of England in 1422 at nine months old, and after his grandfather's death was proclaimed king of France. Regents governed for him. He grew up gentle and pious and founded Eton College and King's College, Cambridge.

But England slowly lost its French lands; by 1453 only Calais remained.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How old was Henry when he became king?

2. What did he found?

1. Nine months.

2. Eton College and King's College, Cambridge.

Discuss:
  • What made a gentle king struggle in his time?

Part 2 — The stupor

In 1453 Henry fell into a mental breakdown and for over a year did not recognise anyone, not even his newborn son. Richard, Duke of York, was made Protector. When Henry recovered, the rivalry between York and the queen, Margaret of Anjou, broke into war in 1455 — later called the Wars of the Roses.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened in 1453?

2. Who became Protector?

1. Henry had a breakdown and recognised no one for over a year.

2. Richard, Duke of York.

Discuss:
  • How should a country handle a leader who is too ill to rule?

Part 3 — Lost and restored

In 1461 Henry was deposed by York's son, Edward IV. He was briefly restored in 1470, but in 1471 Edward returned, Henry's son was killed at Tewkesbury, and Henry died in the Tower — almost certainly murdered.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who deposed Henry in 1461?

2. How did Henry die?

1. Edward IV.

2. In the Tower in 1471, almost certainly murdered.

Discuss:
  • Why couldn't Edward IV let Henry live?

Grammar — The Passive Voice

Historians often care more about what was done than who did it — or they simply do not know who did it. That is the job of the passive.

How it works:
• Form: be + past participle: "The king was crowned at Westminster."
• Add by only when the doer matters: "The castle was built by Norman masons."
• Reporting passives hedge a legend: "He is said to have…", "It was believed that…"

1. Make it passive

Complete each sentence about Henry VI. Use the word in brackets.

1. Eton College (found) by Henry in 1440.

2. France (lose) bit by bit.

3. York (make) Protector in 1454.

1. was founded

2. was lost

3. was made

2. Choose the right passive form

1. Henry (depose) in 1461.

2. He (restore) in 1470.

3. He (kill) in the Tower, probably on Edward's orders.

1. was deposed

2. was restored

3. was killed

Going deeper

Henry VI was perhaps the wrong man for his time — gentle when the age demanded strength. His educational foundations are his lasting legacy.

Worth noticing: the name "Wars of the Roses" became popular much later, partly thanks to Shakespeare and Walter Scott.
Learning from the past — for today: Institutions outlast individuals. Building something that serves others — a school, a scholarship, a community group — can be a leader's greatest legacy.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Was Henry VI a failure?
  • Why do institutions outlast kings?
  • What would you like to build?
