Phrases for Apologising

These phrases help you sound sincere when apologizing. Pay attention to the stress pattern.

Phrase 1: "I am sorry to hear that"

Use this to show sympathy when someone tells you about their problem. Stress the word HEAR.

"I am sorry to HEAR that our photocopier broke down so soon. Let me help resolve this immediately."

Phrase 2: "I must apologise"

A formal way to take responsibility. Stress APOLOGISE.

"I must APOLOGISE for the delay in our service. We take full responsibility for this situation."

Phrase 3: "I do apologise again"

Emphasizes sincerity by adding "do." Stress APOLOGISE.

"I do APOLOGISE again for the inconvenience this has caused to your business."

Phrase 4: "I'm so sorry"

A strong, genuine expression. Stress SORRY.

"I'm SO sorry that our staff member was rude to you. That's not the service we want to provide."

Phrase 5: "I promise it won't happen again"

A commitment to improvement. Stress PROMISE.

"I PROMISE it won't happen again. We're implementing new quality checks to prevent this."

Role Play Scenarios

Practice making complaints with a partner. Take turns being the customer and the customer service officer.

Scenario A: Photocopier Breakdown

Customer:

Your company ordered a new photocopier and in the first week it has broken down three times. You want a replacement.

Customer Service Officer:

You work for the photocopier company. You can offer the customer the newest model. Remember to sound sympathetic!

Scenario B: Restaurant Staff Party Damage

Restaurant Manager:

A company had a staff party at your restaurant. They were very rude to your staff and were fighting with each other. They damaged three tables, five chairs, and many glasses and plates. You want an apology and payment for the damages.

Company Manager:

You are the manager of the large company. Your staff party was a disaster. You will pay for the damages and should sound apologetic.

Scenario C: Catering Company Cleanup

Office Manager:

You hired a catering company for a staff lunch. The lunch was fine but the caterers did not clean up. You hired cleaners yourself and want payment for them.

Catering Customer Service:

You work for the catering company. You have recently had new employees. You will pay for the cleaners and should take responsibility.

Scenario D: Bus Breakdown and Conference Miss

Office Manager:

You hired a bus company to take employees to a conference. The bus broke down and everyone missed the conference. You want a full refund.

Transport Company Customer Service:

You work for the bus company. You have had mechanical problems lately and are getting new buses. Promise this won't happen again and offer a full refund. Sound sincere.

Scenario E: Printing Error Refund

Customer:

You hired a printing company to print 1000 copies of your company's new brochure. They printed a large number of pages upside down. You want a refund.

Printing Company Customer Service:

You work for the printing company. You have had printer problems and are getting new equipment. Promise your customer this won't happen again and offer a full refund.

Instructions

Choose one of the five scenarios above. Role play the conversation with an imaginary partner (or a real one if possible).

Your role: You can be either the person making a complaint OR the person responding to it. Practice both if you have time.

What to focus on:

  • If complaining: Explain the problem clearly and explain what you want
  • If responding: Sound sympathetic and sincere in your apology
  • Use the apology phrases from the previous section
  • Pay attention to word stress to sound genuine

Tips for Sounding Sincere

Use appropriate stress and intonation

Stress the key words in apology phrases. This shows you care about what you're saying.

Take responsibility

Use phrases like "I take full responsibility" or "This is our fault" to sound sincere.

Offer solutions

Always offer something concrete to fix the problem (replacement, refund, new training, etc.)

Use "look" phrases appropriately

Examples: "Look, I understand your frustration" or "This looks like a serious problem"

Review Questions

1. When was the last time someone complained to you? What was it about?

Think about how the person expressed their complaint and how you responded. Did you sound sympathetic?

2. Can you think of a situation with a bad service/product when you didn't complain? What happened?

Would you handle it differently now after this lesson?

3. Do you always complain when you don't like a product or service? When do you decide not to complain?

Consider factors like the severity of the issue and the company's reputation.

4. Have you ever handled a complaint from someone?

What strategy did you use? How could you improve your response?

5. How can we be polite while making a complaint?

Use respectful language, explain clearly, avoid being aggressive, and listen to the other person's perspective.
