Making a Difference: Volunteering

B1 Level · World & Society

Helping Others & Community

Volunteering is giving your time to help others without payment. It benefits not only the people you help, but also yourself—building confidence, new skills, and connections. Whether it's helping at a local food bank, teaching English, or cleaning up beaches, volunteering creates real change.

Let's talk about helping others:

• Have you ever volunteered? What did you do?

• What causes matter most to you? (Environment, poverty, education, animals, etc.)

• Do you think young people should volunteer?

• What's stopping people from volunteering?

• Would you volunteer abroad? Why or why not?

Volunteering Vocabulary

Volunteer Someone who works without payment to help others or a cause
Charity An organization that helps people in need; also the act of giving help
Fundraising Collecting money to support a cause or organization
Community Service Work done to benefit the local community without payment
Non-profit An organization that uses money to help people, not to make profit
Donate To give money or goods to help others or a charity
Campaign A coordinated effort to raise awareness or collect funds for a cause
Awareness Understanding and knowledge about an issue or problem

Types of Volunteering

Local Volunteering

Food banks, homeless shelters, animal rescues, school tutoring, nursing homes, environmental cleanups. Help people and places close to home.

Online Volunteering

Teach languages online, help with website design, translate documents, provide virtual mentoring. No travel required.

Abroad (Voluntourism)

Work in different countries—teaching, building, community projects. Combine travel with helping others.

Activism & Campaigns

Environmental campaigns, human rights advocacy, fundraising events, social media awareness. Change systems and mindsets.

Volunteering Debates

Gap Year Volunteering: Many young people take a "gap year" to volunteer abroad before university. Is this a good idea? Benefits: personal growth, helping others, building skills. Concerns: voluntourism might not provide real help, expensive, disruptive.

Voluntourism Debate: Some volunteer projects prioritize tourism over impact. Are short-term volunteer trips helpful or just feel-good tourism?

Reflection Questions

1. Would you take a gap year to volunteer? What appeals or concerns you?

Consider your goals, interests, financial situation, and what kind of impact matters to you.

2. Is it better to volunteer locally or internationally?

Both have value. Local volunteering builds community; international work expands perspective.

3. What makes volunteering authentic and impactful?

Real impact comes from understanding the community's needs and working alongside them.

4. How can students balance studies with volunteering?

Start small—even a few hours monthly can make a difference without overwhelming your schedule.

Remember: Small acts of kindness matter. You don't need to change the world alone. Even one person helped, one skill taught, one tree planted—these all count. Volunteering is about showing up and doing what you can.
