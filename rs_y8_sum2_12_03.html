Christian Pilgrimage — Lourdes and Iona

Not all pilgrimages are the same. In this lesson, you'll compare two very different Christian pilgrimage sites — Lourdes in France and Iona in Scotland — and consider why Christians still make these journeys today.

What is Hajj?
Hajj is the annual Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca. It is one of the Five Pillars of Islam and is compulsory for all Muslims who are physically and financially able to undertake it. It occurs once per year during the Islamic month of Dhul-Hijjah.
🔒
Name two rituals of Hajj and explain their meaning.
Tawaf: walking seven times around the Kaaba (the sacred cube structure) to show devotion and circling God's house. Wuquf: standing on the plain of Arafat at sunset to reflect on human weakness and seek God's mercy.
🔒
Why do pilgrims wear Ihram?
Ihram is sacred dress (simple white robes for men) that symbolises spiritual purification and equality. Everyone wears the same basic clothing, removing worldly status and showing humility before God.
🔒
Is Hajj optional or compulsory for Muslims?
Hajj is compulsory for all Muslims who are physically fit and financially able. It is one of the Five Pillars, the core duties of Islamic faith. However, a Muslim's faith is not considered weakened if they cannot do it due to genuine hardship.

Christian Pilgrimage: A Different Path

Unlike Hajj, Christian pilgrimage is not compulsory. Christians choose to make pilgrimages as acts of faith, seeking spiritual renewal and encounter with the sacred. Two major pilgrimage sites reveal different expressions of Christian devotion.

Lourdes, France

History: In 1858, a young girl named Bernadette Soubirous reported seeing visions of Mary (the mother of Jesus) at a grotto in Lourdes, southwest France. The Church investigated and recognised these as genuine visions.

What pilgrims do:

  • Bathe in the healing spring water, believed to have miraculous properties
  • Join candlelit processions in the evening
  • Attend Mass at the basilica
  • Seek healing from physical or spiritual illness

Character: Catholic focus, emphasises healing miracles, deeply emotional and devotional atmosphere. Around 5 million pilgrims visit annually, making it one of Europe's most important pilgrimage sites.

Iona, Scotland

History: Founded in 563 AD by St Columba, an Irish monk, Iona became the centre of Celtic Christianity and a major spiritual community. The island was a refuge for learning during Europe's Dark Ages.

What pilgrims do:

  • Stay at the Iona Community for spiritual retreat
  • Walk the island's sacred sites
  • Participate in simple worship and reflection
  • Engage in service and community work

Character: Ecumenical (welcoming to all Christian traditions), emphasises simplicity, contemplation and spiritual growth. Less focused on healing miracles, more on deepening relationship with God and others.

Why Christians Go on Pilgrimage

Pilgrimage: A journey undertaken for spiritual or religious reasons, often to a place considered sacred or holy.
  • Draw closer to God: The journey is seen as a physical expression of spiritual seeking. Walking the path mirrors the inner journey of faith.
  • Seek healing: Some pilgrims hope for miracles or recovery from illness, particularly at sites like Lourdes where healing is emphasised.
  • Strengthen faith: Meeting other pilgrims and experiencing sacred spaces reinforces personal belief and sense of Christian community.
  • Follow Jesus's example: Jesus spent time in places of prayer and solitude. Pilgrimage reflects his spiritual practices.
  • Reflect and pray: Pilgrimage provides time away from daily life to pray, think deeply about faith, and experience God's presence.

Debate: Is Pilgrimage Still Relevant?

Pilgrimage in the modern world raises important questions. Explore both sides of this debate.

Arguments For Pilgrimage's Relevance

✓ Spiritual Renewal
Pilgrimage offers a break from busy modern life. The physical act of travelling creates space for spiritual reflection that daily routines don't allow. Many pilgrims report profound personal transformation after their journeys.
✓ Community and Belonging
Walking with thousands of other pilgrims—or joining a small retreat community—creates deep bonds. This sense of shared faith strengthens individual belief and connects Christians across denominations and cultures.
✓ Physical Journey, Spiritual Meaning
The body and mind are connected. Walking a pilgrimage path mirrors the Christian spiritual journey—the effort, the struggles, the arrival at sacred places—making abstract faith tangible and meaningful.

Arguments Against Pilgrimage's Relevance

✗ God Is Everywhere
Christian theology teaches that God is omnipresent—present in all places equally. This raises the question: why spend money and time travelling to a specific location when you can pray and worship anywhere?
✗ Money Could Help Others
The cost of pilgrimage (especially international travel) could be given to those in poverty or used for charity work. Jesus emphasised caring for the vulnerable, which some argue is a more direct Christian responsibility than pilgrimage.
✗ Online Worship and Connection Exist
In the 21st century, Christians can worship together, listen to teachings, and feel part of a community online. This makes pilgrimage seem less necessary for spiritual growth or fellowship.
✗ Faith Should Be Internal, Not Location-Based
Some argue that true faith is internal—about personal relationship with God—rather than dependent on visiting holy sites. Pilgrimage might suggest that faith needs external validation or miraculous confirmation.

Key Terms

Pilgrimage
A journey undertaken for spiritual or religious reasons, often to a place considered sacred or holy.
Lourdes
A town in southwest France where Bernadette Soubirous reported visions of Mary in 1858. It is now a major Catholic pilgrimage site known for healing miracles and attracts millions of pilgrims annually.
Iona
A small Scottish island founded as a monastic community by St Columba in 563 AD. Today, the Iona Community welcomes pilgrims for spiritual retreat and ecumenical fellowship.
Bernadette Soubirous
A French peasant girl who, in 1858, reported seeing visions of Mary at a grotto in Lourdes. The Church investigated and recognised her visions, leading to Lourdes becoming a major pilgrimage site.
St Columba
An Irish monk who founded the monastic community on Iona in 563 AD. He was a key figure in spreading Celtic Christianity throughout Scotland and Northern Britain.
Ecumenical
Relating to or representing different branches of Christianity. An ecumenical approach welcomes members of all Christian denominations.
Miracle
An event that appears to break the laws of nature and is attributed to divine or supernatural intervention. At Lourdes, many pilgrims report miraculous healing.
Retreat
A period of withdrawal from everyday life for spiritual reflection, prayer, and renewal. Iona offers retreat experiences for Christian pilgrims seeking contemplation.
