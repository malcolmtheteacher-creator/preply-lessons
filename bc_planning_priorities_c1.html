Setting SMART Goals

Not all goals are equal. The best workplace goals are SMART — they give you a clear target and help you stay focused.

What does SMART mean?

S — Specific
Your goal should clearly state WHAT you want to achieve and WHY. Avoid vague language.
M — Measurable
You should be able to track progress. Include numbers, dates, or clear indicators of success.
A — Achievable
Your goal should be realistic given your time and resources. Impossible goals are demoralising.
R — Relevant
Your goal should matter to your work or career. It should align with your priorities.
T — Time-bound
Set a deadline. When do you need to complete this goal? By when?

Practice: Identify Problems

Is this a SMART goal? "Get a promotion by next year"

No. This goal is not SMART. It is not specific (what type of promotion?), not measurable (what steps lead to promotion?), and not clear about what you need to do. It's also not relevant unless you explain why.

Is this a SMART goal? "Complete the advanced customer service training module and apply for the senior representative position by the end of June"

Yes. This goal is SMART. It is specific (complete training and apply for role), measurable (training module completion), achievable (assuming reasonable time), relevant (related to promotion), and time-bound (by end of June).

The Priority Matrix (Eisenhower Matrix)

Not everything that seems urgent is truly important. The Priority Matrix helps you see the difference.

Understanding the Four Quadrants

Quadrant 1: Urgent + Important

DO NOW
Crisis situations, tight deadlines, serious problems. These demand immediate attention.

Example: Fix a critical IT system failure

Quadrant 2: Not Urgent + Important

SCHEDULE
Long-term goals, planning, professional development. These are most valuable but easy to postpone.

Example: Work on the quarterly report

Quadrant 3: Urgent + Not Important

DELEGATE
Tasks that seem urgent but don't move your goals forward. Pass them to others when possible.

Example: Attend an optional webinar

Quadrant 4: Not Urgent + Not Important

ELIMINATE
Time-wasters that neither advance goals nor respond to deadlines. Cut them out.

Example: Organise your desk

Practice: Categorise Tasks

Where does "Respond to 20 emails" belong?

Quadrant 1 or 3 depending on the context. If the emails are from key clients or contain urgent problems, they are Quadrant 1 (urgent + important). If they are routine administrative emails, they might be Quadrant 3 (urgent but not important).

Where does "Work on the quarterly report" belong?

Quadrant 2 (not urgent + important). This is strategic work that needs scheduling and planning. It should be a regular part of your week.

Key Vocabulary

Verbs and Phrases

  • Prioritise: decide what matters most and rank tasks by importance
  • Delegate: assign a task to someone else
  • Procrastinate: delay doing something you should be doing
  • Break down: divide a large task into smaller, manageable steps
  • Schedule: plan when to do something; set a time for it

Nouns Related to Work

  • Deadline: the date or time by which something must be completed
  • Overdue: past the deadline; late
  • Backlog: accumulation of uncompleted tasks or work
  • Workload: the amount of work you have to do
  • Contingency: a backup plan for if something goes wrong
  • Time-sensitive: work that must be done by a specific time

Adjectives for Urgency

  • Urgent: requiring immediate attention
  • Critical: essential; will have serious consequences if not done
  • Routine: normal, regular, expected work
  • Pressing: demanding immediate attention
