  • "Get a promotion by next year" — Outside your direct control. No specific action steps.

SMART Goals Framework

Effective goals follow the SMART criteria:

  • Specific — Clear and well-defined
  • Measurable — You can track progress
  • Achievable — Realistic and attainable
  • Relevant — Aligned with broader objectives
  • Time-bound — Has a clear deadline

Good Example of a SMART Goal:

"Earn a promotion to senior customer service representative by completing the 2 required training modules before June and applying for the role at the end of next quarter."

This is specific (senior rep role), measurable (2 modules, application), achievable (within control), relevant (career development), and time-bound (June deadline, next quarter application).

The Urgency-Importance Matrix

Use this framework to prioritise your tasks effectively:

High Urgency + High Importance

DO FIRST

Critical deadlines, urgent problems, crisis situations

Low Urgency + High Importance

SCHEDULE

Long-term goals, planning, skill development

High Urgency + Low Importance

DELEGATE

Interruptions, some meetings, some emails

Low Urgency + Low Importance

ELIMINATE

Time-wasting activities, social media, low-value tasks

Where would you place these tasks?

1. Complete quarterly report (due Friday)

High Urgency + High Importance → DO FIRST

2. Develop leadership skills through online course

Low Urgency + High Importance → SCHEDULE (Plan regular time each week)

3. Attend non-essential team social event

High Urgency (socially) + Low Importance → DELEGATE or DECLINE

Timed Speaking Task

Task: Explain your personal to-do list for the next working day, breaking down tasks into sub-tasks and explaining your prioritisation strategy.

Time remaining: 10:00

Structure for your 10-minute talk:

  1. Opening (1 min): "Tomorrow I have X main tasks to accomplish..."
  2. Task 1 (2 mins): Describe the task, its importance, urgency, and sub-steps
  3. Task 2 (2 mins): Repeat for second task
  4. Task 3 (2 mins): Repeat for third task
  5. Strategy (2 mins): Explain your overall prioritisation and timeline
  6. Closing (1 min): Summarize why this approach is effective for you

Useful language:

  • "My first priority is... because it's both urgent and important."
  • "To accomplish this, I'll need to..."
  • "The key sub-tasks are..."
  • "This will take approximately... hours."
  • "I'll schedule time for... before tackling..."
  • "In terms of time management, I believe..."

Key Strategies for Success

  • Make lists regularly: Write down everything you need to do to clear mental clutter
  • Use the Urgent-Important matrix: Not all tasks deserve immediate attention
  • Break big goals into small steps: This makes them feel achievable and manageable
  • Set realistic deadlines: Account for unexpected problems and dependencies
  • Review and adjust: Prioritisation is not fixed—adapt as circumstances change
  • Protect time for important work: Long-term goals often get neglected for urgent tasks
