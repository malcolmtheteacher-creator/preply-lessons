The Vikings

People from the north who attacked, traded, and travelled very far — much further than you might think.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The attack from the sea

The Vikings came from the north of Europe — from the lands we now call Norway, Sweden and Denmark. They were very good sailors.

In the year 793, Viking ships arrived at a small island in England called Lindisfarne. There was a quiet monastery there — a place where religious men called monks lived and prayed. The Vikings attacked it. They took the gold, hurt the monks, and then sailed away fast. This raid shocked everyone. Many people say this was the start of the "Viking Age".

The Vikings could attack like this because of their special boats, called longships. These ships were long, fast, and light. They could cross the open sea, but they could also go up small rivers. The Vikings did not have maps. Instead, they used the sun, the stars, and the wind to find their way.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did the Vikings do at Lindisfarne in 793?

2. Why were the longships so good?

1. They attacked the monastery, took the gold, hurt the monks, and sailed away. Many people say this was the start of the Viking Age.

2. They were long, fast and light. They could cross the open sea but also go up small rivers.

Talk about it:
  • The people who wrote about the Vikings were the people they attacked. How might this change the story we have today?

Part 2 — Not only fighters

But the Vikings were not only fighters. The same people also traded, travelled, and made new homes in faraway places.

They sold furs and other things in markets very far away. They built towns — for example, Dublin in Ireland started as a Viking town. Many Viking families did not want to fight. They wanted to settle: to make farms and live in a new country. They settled in parts of Britain and Ireland, and also on the islands of Iceland and Greenland.

And they travelled even further. Around the year 1000, a Viking called Leif Erikson sailed west across the sea. He reached North America! This was about 500 years before Columbus. For a long time, people thought this was only a story. But later, people found a real Viking camp in Canada — so we know it is true.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What other things did the Vikings do, apart from fighting?

2. Who was Leif Erikson, and what did he do?

1. They traded (sold things in faraway markets), built towns like Dublin, and settled (made farms and homes) in Britain, Ireland, Iceland and Greenland.

2. He was a Viking who sailed to North America around the year 1000 — about 500 years before Columbus. People later found a real Viking camp in Canada.

Talk about it:
  • We remember the Vikings as fighters, but we forget they were also travellers and farmers. Why do you think this happened?

Part 3 — Gods, stories, and the end

The Vikings had their own gods. The most famous are Odin and Thor, the god of thunder with his big hammer. We know about their gods because of their stories, called sagas. People told these stories for many years, and later wrote them down.

Here is one surprising thing. Many people think Vikings wore helmets with horns. This is not true! It is a myth. Real Vikings did not wear horns in battle. This idea came much later, from old paintings and a famous opera. It is a good example of a story that is not true, but that everybody believes.

The Viking Age ended in the year 1066. A Viking king from Norway attacked England, but he lost a big battle and died. After this, the big Viking attacks stopped. But the Vikings did not really disappear. They had already settled and married in many countries. They simply became part of the people of Europe.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How do we know about the Viking gods? And what is the truth about horned helmets?

2. When did the Viking Age end? What happened to the Vikings?

1. We know from their stories, called sagas. Horned helmets are a myth — real Vikings did not wear them; the idea came later from paintings and an opera.

2. It ended in 1066, when a Viking king lost a battle in England and died. The Vikings did not disappear — they had already settled and become part of Europe.

Talk about it:
  • The horned helmet is not real, but everybody knows it. Why do you think some false stories become so popular?

Grammar: relative clauses (who / which / that)

We use relative clauses to give more information about a person, a thing, or a place. They join two ideas into one sentence.

who = for people: "the Vikings who attacked Lindisfarne."
which or that = for things: "the ships which crossed the sea," "the gold that they took."
We use these words to add information without starting a new sentence.

1. Choose who, which or that

1. The Vikings were people came from the north.

2. The longship was a boat could go on rivers and on the sea.

3. Leif Erikson was a sailor reached North America.

4. A saga is a story the Vikings told for many years.

1. who (people — the Vikings)

2. which / that (a thing — the boat)

3. who (a person — a sailor)

4. which / that (a thing — a story)

2. Join the two sentences

Make one sentence with who, which or that. Then read it out loud.

1. Lindisfarne was a monastery. It stood on a small island. →

2. Odin and Thor were gods. The Vikings prayed to them. →

1. "Lindisfarne was a monastery which/that stood on a small island."

2. "Odin and Thor were gods who the Vikings prayed to." (or: "…gods that the Vikings prayed to.")

Task C · A famous group or person (3 minutes)

Think of a famous group, country, or person that people think they "know". What is the simple idea people have? Is the real story bigger or different? Tell me about it.

Not sure what to talk about? Choose one:

  • Pirates — people think they were only bad men who looked for gold. But many were poor sailors who ran away to find a better life.
  • Genghis Khan — people remember a man who killed many people. But he also built roads and helped people who travelled to trade.
  • Cowboys — in films they fight with guns. But real cowboys were workers who looked after cows all day.
  • Your country — many people from other countries have one simple idea about it. What is it? What is the real story that you know?
  • A job people judge — think of a job, like a police officer or an influencer, that has a reputation which is only half true.
