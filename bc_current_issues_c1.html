Key Debate Phrases

Use these expressions to participate effectively in a debate:

Politely Interrupting

  • "I'm sorry to interrupt, but..."
  • "If I could just come in here..."
  • "I'm afraid I have to stop you there."
  • "Could I just say something here?"
  • "Excuse me, but..."

Expressing Your Opinion

  • "As far as I'm concerned..."
  • "From my point of view..."
  • "My view is that..."
  • "To my mind..."
  • "I'm in favour of..."
  • "I'm afraid I don't see it like that."

Disagreeing Respectfully

  • "But there's another aspect to this."
  • "I can see what you're saying, but..."
  • "That's all very interesting, but..."
  • "I doubt very much that..."
  • "I couldn't disagree more."
  • "That argument doesn't make sense."

Presenting Facts and Logic

  • "The real point is..."
  • "The truth of the matter is actually..."
  • "The fact is..."
  • "Moving on to..."
  • "I take your point, but..."

Acknowledging Other Views

  • "That's one way to look at it, but..."
  • "So you're saying...?"
  • "I don't think that's really the point."

Discussion Topics

Choose a topic that interests you. You can take either the "for" or "against" position:

Video games are a waste of time.

For: They prevent people from being productive or learning useful skills.

Against: They develop problem-solving, teamwork, and strategic thinking.

Smartphones have made our lives easier.

For: They connect us, provide instant information, and increase efficiency.

Against: They create addiction, reduce face-to-face interaction, and cause health issues.

Animals shouldn't be used for medical research.

For: It's unethical and alternative methods exist.

Against: It saves human lives and many medicines wouldn't exist without it.

Children need to learn how to use gadgets at an early age.

For: Technology skills are essential in the modern world.

Against: It limits physical activity, outdoor play, and social development.

Education is outdated and not essential to do well in life.

For: Experience and practical skills matter more than formal qualifications.

Against: Education provides essential knowledge, opportunities, and critical thinking skills.

Check Your Understanding

Which phrase would you use to politely interrupt someone during a debate?

Any of these are correct: "I'm sorry to interrupt, but...", "If I could just come in here...", "Could I just say something here?", or "Excuse me, but...". These phrases show respect while allowing you to participate.

How can you disagree with someone's opinion respectfully?

Use phrases like: "I can see what you're saying, but...", "That's one way to look at it, but...", "I take your point, but...", or "I doubt very much that...". These acknowledge their view while presenting an alternative perspective.

What makes a strong argument in a debate?

A strong argument: (1) is clear and specific, (2) provides reasons or evidence, (3) addresses possible counter-arguments, (4) uses appropriate debate language, and (5) shows respect for other viewpoints.

How do you express your personal opinion in a debate?

Use phrases such as: "As far as I'm concerned...", "From my point of view...", "My view is that...", "To my mind...", or "I'm in favour of...". These frame your ideas as your perspective, not absolute truth.

Reflect on Your Debate

After the debate, consider:

  • Did you convince your partner with your arguments?
  • Did you change your mind about any aspect of the topic?
  • Which debate phrases were most helpful?
  • What would you do differently next time?
