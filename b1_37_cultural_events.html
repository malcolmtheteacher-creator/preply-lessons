Identifying Distracting Information

In listening tests, speakers often include extra information that can mislead you.

Example: Finding the Actual Location

Show Analysis
Speaker\'s response:
"Well, due to the event\'s popularity, the MLG tournament isn\'t at the theatre this year, as it usually is. Instead you have to head towards the famous Two Bridges Conference Centre. Next to that, off Bristol Road, you\'ll find Central Hall, which is a lot larger than the theatre. That\'s the venue. Use the conference centre carpark."

Distracting information:
• The theatre (where it USUALLY is, but NOT this year)
• Two Bridges Conference Centre (mentioned but is just a landmark)
• Central Hall being larger than the theatre (comparison, but not the answer)

Correct answer: Central Hall (the actual venue)

Key phrase: "That\'s the venue." — This tells you the answer!

Listening Tip:

Listen for marker phrases like "That\'s the venue," "The location is," or "It\'s at..." to find the actual answer, not just any place mentioned.

Understanding the Table Structure

A typical cultural events table has these columns:

Event Date/Time Performers Details
Jazz Festival Summer Local schools A celebration
Rock Concert Half-term Famous bands Evening show
Folk Performance Teatime The north-east Less familiar music

What Type of Information Fits Each Column?

Show Guide
Event column: Names of performances, festivals, or competitions
Example: "Jazz Festival," "Rock Concert," "Football Match"

Date/Time column: When the event happens
Example: "Summer," "Half-term," "Teatime," "Morning," "Evening"

Performers column: Who performs or participates
Example: "Local schools," "Famous bands," "Unknown artists," "Semi-professional"

Details column: Additional information about the event
Example: "A celebration," "Evening show," "Less familiar," "Female performers"
