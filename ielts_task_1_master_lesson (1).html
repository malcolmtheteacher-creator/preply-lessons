Task 1 Overview & Strategy

What is Task 1? You have 20 minutes to write at least 150 words describing visual information: graphs, charts, tables, diagrams, or maps. This is worth 33% of your Writing score (Task 2 is 67%).

The 20-Minute Time Plan

Minutes 1-2

ANALYZE

What type? Main trend? Time period? Units?

Minutes 3-4

PLAN

4 paragraphs. Group logically. Note key data.

Minutes 5-17

WRITE

Intro (1 min) + Overview (2 min) + Bodies (9 min)

Minutes 18-20

CHECK

Word count. Tenses. Obvious errors.

The 3 Biggest Mistakes:

1. NO OVERVIEW - Without this, you cannot get Band 6+. The overview is MANDATORY.

2. Describing EVERYTHING - Select the most significant features only. You don't need every data point.

3. Giving OPINIONS - Just describe what you see. Never speculate about why!

The Perfect 4-Paragraph Structure

Every Task 1 answer uses this structure:

Para 1: Introduction (1 sentence)

Para 2: Overview (2-3 sentences, NO data)

Para 3: Body 1 (3-5 sentences with data)

Para 4: Body 2 (3-5 sentences with data)

Paragraph 1: Introduction (1 sentence)

Formula: The + [graph type] + shows/illustrates + [what] + [where/when]

GOOD EXAMPLE

Question: The graph shows fish and meat consumption in Europe between 1979 and 2004.

Your introduction: The line graph illustrates the amount of fish and various types of meat consumed per person in a European country over a 25-year period from 1979 to 2004.

Perfect! Paraphrased the question, identified graph type, clear time period.

BAD EXAMPLE

This graph shows meat and fish in Europe.

Too short, copied words directly, missing detail.

Paragraph 2: Overview (2-3 sentences)

CRITICAL: This is the most important paragraph. Give the "big picture" WITHOUT specific data.

What to include: Highest/lowest, overall trends, main differences, most notable feature

GOOD OVERVIEW

Overall, it is clear that chicken consumption increased dramatically over the period, while beef, lamb, and fish all declined. By the end of the period, chicken had become the most popular meat, overtaking beef.

Perfect! Main trends stated, comparisons made, NO specific numbers.

BAD OVERVIEW

In 1979, people ate 220 grams of beef and 140 grams of chicken.

These are details for body paragraphs, not overview!

Paragraphs 3 & 4: Body Paragraphs

NOW you use specific data! Include numbers, percentages, dates. Make comparisons.

Organizing strategies:

  • Group by category (all meats in one, fish in another)
  • Group by trend (increasing items vs decreasing items)
  • Group by time (first half vs second half of period)

Master All 6 Graph Types

Type 1: Line Graphs (Change Over Time)

UK Energy Consumption (1980-2020)
Line Graph Strategy:

Introduction: "The line graph illustrates..."

Overview: Describe overall trends (increased/decreased/fluctuated/remained stable)

Body 1: Starting points and highest figures with data

Body 2: Changes throughout period, ending figures

Tenses: Past tense if dates are in the past

Type 2: Bar Charts (Comparing Categories)

Weekly Spending by Age Group (2023)
Bar Chart Strategy:

Introduction: "The bar chart compares..."

Overview: Identify highest and lowest categories

Body 1: Highest categories with specific figures

Body 2: Lowest categories with comparisons

Language: Use comparatives (higher than, the most)

Type 3: Pie Charts (Showing Proportions)

Global Energy Sources (2023)
Pie Chart Strategy:

Introduction: "The pie chart shows the proportion of..."

Overview: Largest and smallest segments

Body 1: Largest segments with percentages

Body 2: Smaller segments with comparisons

Language: Percentages, fractions (one-third, majority, a quarter)

Type 4: Tables (Multiple Data Points)

Table Strategy:

Introduction: "The table shows/compares..."

Overview: Highest/lowest values, biggest/smallest changes

Body 1: Group highest figures or biggest changes

Body 2: Group lowest figures or smallest changes

CRITICAL: Don't describe every cell - select key data!

Band 9 Model Answer

Meat and Fish Consumption (1979-2004)

INTRODUCTION: The line graph illustrates the amount of fish and three different types of meat consumed per person per week in a European country over a 25-year period from 1979 to 2004.

OVERVIEW: Overall, it is clear that beef consumption declined substantially over the period, while chicken consumption increased dramatically. Fish consumption remained relatively low and stable throughout, and lamb consumption also decreased moderately.

BODY 1: In 1979, beef was the most popular meat, with consumption standing at approximately 220 grams per person per week, compared to around 150 grams for lamb and just 140 grams for chicken. Fish consumption was considerably lower at roughly 60 grams. Over the following decade, beef consumption declined gradually to around 170 grams by 1989, while chicken consumption rose steadily to approximately 200 grams, overtaking lamb which had fallen to about 140 grams.

BODY 2: The trend continued throughout the 1990s and into the early 2000s. By 2004, chicken had become by far the most consumed meat at nearly 250 grams per person per week, having increased by over 100 grams since 1979. In contrast, beef consumption had plummeted to just under 120 grams, while lamb had decreased to approximately 60 grams, matching fish consumption which had remained relatively stable at around 50 grams throughout the entire period.

[195 words]

Why This is Band 9:
  • Task Achievement: All requirements covered, clear overview, key features well-selected
  • Coherence & Cohesion: Logical organization, clear paragraphing, excellent linking
  • Lexical Resource: Wide vocabulary range (declined, substantially, plummeted, remained stable)
  • Grammar: Variety of complex structures, accurate tenses, minimal errors
✓

Task 1 Checklist

Before you submit, check:
  • At least 150 words (aim for 170-190)
  • 4 paragraphs: Intro + Overview + Body 1 + Body 2
  • Overview paragraph with NO specific data
  • Specific data and figures in body paragraphs
  • Comparisons between categories or time periods
  • Correct verb tenses (usually past tense)
  • Paraphrased the question (didn't copy)
  • NO opinions or speculation about WHY
  • Clear, varied vocabulary
  • No major grammar errors
