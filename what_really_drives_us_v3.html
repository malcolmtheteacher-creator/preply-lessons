What Really Drives Us

C2 | Analytical Discussion | 45 minutes

Source: The Diary of a CEO - Tony Robbins (January 2026)
Watch the full interview

The Hook

Same door. Same food. Same moment.
Two completely different lives.
Thanksgiving Day, Los Angeles, 1971

An 11-year-old boy answers a knock at the door. A stranger stands holding two bags of groceries and a frozen turkey. The family has no money. No food. Just crackers and peanut butter.

The boy's father sees the stranger and slams the door: "We don't take charity."

The stranger leans in. The door bounces off his shoulder. He says quietly: "Don't make your family suffer because of your ego."

The father's veins pulse. He takes the food. Slams it on the table. Never says thank you.

The Father's Focus

"I can't even feed my family. I'm worthless."

He spiralled. Repeated the story of his failure for years. Never recovered.

The Boy's Focus

"Strangers care. If someone I've never met would do this, I have to care about strangers too."

At 17, he fed two families. Today, his organisations have provided over one billion meals.

That boy was Tony Robbins. He argues that three decisions shape every moment of your life:

  1. What do you focus on? You don't experience life - you experience the life you focus on.
  2. What does it mean? Is this the end or the beginning? An insult or a challenge?
  3. What will you do? And this depends entirely on the first two.
Discussion: Why do you think the father and son interpreted the same event so differently? What factors might shape how someone focuses on an experience?

Language Focus A

be driven by (something)

▼
Meaning

To be fundamentally motivated by; to have as one's core driver

Form

be + driven by + noun/gerund

Register

Analytical, introspective. Common in psychology and business.

Common collocations
fear of failure a need for validation pure curiosity unresolved trauma external pressure intrinsic motivation
"I realised I wasn't driven by ambition - I was driven by fear of being seen as worthless."
"Most people think they're driven by success, but underneath, they're driven by the need for significance."
  1. What do you think most politicians are really driven by?
  2. Someone who works 80-hour weeks but already has enough money - what might they be driven by?
  3. Is it better to be driven by fear of failure or desire for success? Why?

settle for (something)

▼
Meaning

To accept something less than what you truly want, often without acknowledging the compromise

Form

settle for + noun

Register

Direct, often confrontational. Implies resignation.

Common collocations
second best mediocrity the safe option good enough less than you deserve a compromise
"Most people want love, but they've been hurt, so they settle for connection instead."
"She didn't choose that career - she settled for it because the alternative felt too risky."
  1. Why do you think so many people settle for jobs they don't love?
  2. In relationships, what's the difference between compromise and settling for less?
  3. Is there ever wisdom in settling for "good enough"?

turn (X) into (Y)

▼
Meaning

To transform or convert one thing into another - implying agency and choice

Form

turn + noun + into + noun

Register

Versatile. Powerful in narrative - shows transformation.

Common collocations
adversity into opportunity setback into fuel weakness into strength pain into purpose failure into a learning experience
"The question that changed everything: how do you turn your worst day into your best day?"
"She turned what could have been career-ending into the foundation of her consulting business."
  1. How did Robbins turn his childhood poverty into something valuable?
  2. Can any negative experience be turned into a positive, or are some things just destructive?
  3. What would it take for someone to turn a job loss into an opportunity?

Language Focus B

underneath it all

▼
Meaning

At the deepest level; when you strip away surface explanations and reach the true cause

Form

Usually sentence-initial or as a pivot point

Register

Intimate, analytical. Signals you're revealing a deeper truth.

What lies underneath?
insecurity a fear of rejection unmet needs a craving for control deep-seated resentment genuine love
"He said he left because of the salary, but underneath it all, he just didn't feel valued."
"Underneath it all, the deepest fear everyone has is that they're not enough."
  1. When someone constantly posts about their perfect life on social media, what do you think is going on underneath it all?
  2. A CEO who micromanages everything - underneath it all, what might be driving that behaviour?
  3. What was going on underneath it all with Robbins' father when he slammed that door?

that's where (X) comes from

▼
Meaning

Used to identify the source or origin of a quality, behaviour, or outcome

Form

That's where + noun + comes from

Register

Explanatory, connecting. Links cause and effect in storytelling.

Things that come from somewhere
the restlessness that relentless drive her resilience his inability to trust the need to overachieve all that energy
"Find something you care about more than yourself. That's where all the energy comes from."
"She grew up with four different fathers. That's where her need for control comes from."
  1. Robbins now feeds a billion people. Where does that drive come from?
  2. Many high achievers had difficult childhoods. Where do you think their relentless ambition comes from?
  3. Someone who can never trust a partner - where might that come from?

Your Turn

Select 2-3 cards. Discuss each for 5+ minutes. Push beyond your first answer.

The Reframe Test

Robbins and his father experienced identical circumstances but extracted opposite meanings. Is this reframing genuinely available to everyone? Can you turn any experience into something valuable - or are some things simply destructive?

The Significance Trap

People driven by significance often achieve everything and feel nothing. Yet significance is a human need. How do you pursue excellence without being driven by the need to prove you're enough?

The Settling Question

Most people settle for connection instead of love, safety instead of growth. But maybe settling is rational - love is risky, growth is painful. When is settling for something actually wisdom rather than cowardice?

The AI Meaning Crisis

If AI displaces millions of jobs, people lose not just income but identity. Underneath it all, work provides significance and purpose. What could replace it? Or is tying identity to labour where human misery comes from?

Push vs. Pull

Robbins distinguishes "push" motivation (forcing yourself) from "pull" motivation (caring about something beyond yourself). He claims push has limits; pull is unlimited. Do you buy this? What are you pulled by?

The Pain Paradox

Robbins' drive to end suffering comes from his own suffering. Many exceptional people are driven by early wounds. Does this mean pain is necessary for meaning? If you could remove all suffering from a child's life, would you?

Deep Dive

Choose one challenge. Speak for the full time. Use at least 3 of today's expressions naturally.

The Audit

Analyse yourself honestly. What are you actually driven by? Where are you settling for something without admitting it? Underneath it all, what are you afraid of?

The Defence

Argue against Robbins. Maybe the father was right to slam the door - dignity matters. Maybe reframing trauma is a luxury. Maybe "turning your worst day into your best day" is toxic positivity. Make the case.

The Origin

Tell the story of where your drive comes from. Trace it back. What happened? Have you turned that origin story into something useful - or does it still control you?

The Prescription

A friend is successful but empty - driven by significance, settling for connection, unable to feel satisfied. Based on what we've discussed, what would you actually tell them?
