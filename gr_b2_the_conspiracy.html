How This Works

Every conspiracy theory starts with real facts — then takes a creative leap. You're going to do exactly that... and then destroy your own argument.

Step 1 — The Facts
Four real, verified facts appear on screen. They seem unrelated.
Step 2 — Build the Theory
Work together. You have 3 minutes to connect all four facts into one convincing conspiracy theory. The more creative, the better.
Step 3 — Destroy it
Now switch sides. Find every logical fallacy, weak link, and alternative explanation. Pick the theory apart — no mercy.

There are 3 rounds, each with different facts. The language here is about argumentation — persuading, linking, qualifying, and debunking.

Language Debrief

Conspiracy theories rely on specific language patterns. Which of these did you use today?

Connecting unrelated things:
"What if... isn't a coincidence?"
"The timing is suspicious — just weeks after..."
"It can't be a coincidence that X happened right when Y..."
Sounding convincing without evidence:
"They don't want you to know..."
"If you look closely enough..."
"It's been well documented that..." (when it hasn't)
Hedging (useful in real life!):
"I'm not saying it's true, but..."
"All I'm saying is..."
"You have to admit it's suspicious..."
Debunking and calling out fallacies:
"That's correlation, not causation."
"You're cherry-picking the evidence."
"Just because A happened before B doesn't mean A caused B."
"There's a much simpler explanation..."
"That's a massive leap in logic."
