Introducing: The Six Thinking Hats

The Six Thinking Hats is a decision-making framework developed by Edward de Bono. It helps individuals and teams explore different perspectives systematically rather than chaotically.

Why This Works

Instead of everyone arguing from their own viewpoint, the Six Hats method asks everyone to think in the same direction at the same time. This:

  • Reduces conflict and ego-driven arguments
  • Ensures all perspectives are considered
  • Makes meetings more productive
  • Leads to more creative and balanced decisions

The Six Thinking Hats

Click each hat to learn about its role and key questions:

🤍 White Hat - Facts & Data

Neutral, objective. Focus on available information.

Key Questions:

  • What information do we have?
  • What information do we need?
  • What are the facts?

Example: "The data shows sales increased 15% last quarter."

❤️ Red Hat - Emotions & Intuition

Feelings, hunches, gut instinct. No justification needed.

Key Questions:

  • How do you feel about this?
  • What does your intuition tell you?
  • What's your gut reaction?

Example: "I have a bad feeling about this partnership."

🖤 Black Hat - Caution & Risks

Critical judgment. Identifies weaknesses and dangers.

Key Questions:

  • What could go wrong?
  • What are the risks?
  • Why might this not work?

Example: "If we expand too quickly, we might overstretch our resources."

💛 Yellow Hat - Benefits & Value

Optimistic, positive. Explores benefits and value.

Key Questions:

  • What are the benefits?
  • What's the best-case scenario?
  • Why will this work?

Example: "This could open up a whole new market for us."

💚 Green Hat - Creativity & Ideas

Creative thinking. New ideas, alternatives, possibilities.

Key Questions:

  • What are the alternatives?
  • What if we tried something completely different?
  • How can we solve this problem creatively?

Example: "What if we partnered with a competitor instead?"

💙 Blue Hat - Process & Control

Manages the thinking process. Overview and control.

Key Questions:

  • What's our agenda?
  • Which hat should we use next?
  • What have we decided so far?

Example: "Let's summarize our findings before moving on."

Idiomatic Expressions with "Think"

These 10 expressions are commonly used in professional and everyday English. Click each one to reveal its meaning and an example:

1. put your thinking cap on
Start thinking seriously about how to solve a problem
"We've got a budget crisis - everyone needs to put their thinking caps on."
2. think something through
Consider an idea or plan and its possible consequences logically
"Before we launch, let's think this through properly."
3. think it over
Consider an idea carefully before making a decision
"I'll think it over and get back to you tomorrow."
4. can't think straight
Unable to think clearly, confused or overwhelmed
"I'm so tired I can't think straight anymore."
5. think twice
Pause and think again before taking action
"You should think twice before quitting your job."
6. think big
Be ambitious; aim for large goals
"Don't limit yourself - think big!"
7. think outside the box
Think creatively, not limited by traditional expectations
"We need to think outside the box to solve this."
8. think for yourself
Form your own opinions independently
"Don't just follow the crowd - think for yourself."
9. think on your feet
Think quickly and react spontaneously
"In negotiations, you have to think on your feet."
10. think ahead
Plan for the future; anticipate what's coming
"Good managers always think ahead."

Use the Six Thinking Hats to explore this decision from all angles.

Group Activity

In your group, assign each person a different hat. Take turns presenting the 4-day work week from your hat's perspective.

Discussion Questions

After everyone has spoken:

  1. What new perspectives did you discover?
  2. Which hat was most difficult to wear? Why?
