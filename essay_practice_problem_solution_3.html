Your task — about 40 minutes

More and more people are migrating to cities in search of a better life, but city life can be extremely difficult. Explain some of the difficulties of living in a city. How can governments make urban life better for everyone?

Band 8 model answer (~260 words)

As economic opportunity draws ever more people into urban areas, many discover that city living brings serious hardships. This essay will outline the main difficulties city dwellers face and suggest how governments might make urban life more bearable for all.

The problems of city life are numerous. The most pressing is the high cost of living, particularly housing: as demand outstrips supply, rents rise until ordinary workers are forced into cramped or distant accommodation. Overcrowding brings further difficulties, including chronic traffic congestion, overstretched public transport and high levels of air pollution, all of which damage health and quality of life. Newcomers may also experience isolation, since the anonymity of a large city can be profoundly lonely for those far from family and friends.

Fortunately, governments can take meaningful action. To address housing, authorities should invest in affordable public housing and regulate rents so that essential workers are not priced out. Congestion and pollution can be tackled together by expanding reliable, low-cost public transport and by discouraging private car use through measures such as congestion charging, as London has done successfully. To combat isolation, local governments can fund community centres, parks and events that help residents form the social connections a healthy city depends on.

In conclusion, although rapid urbanisation creates real difficulties, from unaffordable housing to loneliness and pollution, these are not inevitable. With sustained investment in housing, transport and community life, governments can transform overcrowded cities into places where everyone, not merely the wealthy, is able to thrive.
