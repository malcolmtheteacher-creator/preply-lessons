Understanding Opinion Essays

In an opinion essay, you must clearly state YOUR position and defend it with arguments.

Today's Question:

Some people believe that university education should be free for all students. Others think that students should pay for their own higher education.

To what extent do you agree or disagree with free university education?

Essay Structure for Opinion Essays

Introduction (2-3 sentences)

Paraphrase the question + State your opinion clearly

~40-50 words
Body Paragraph 1 (4-5 sentences)

Your MAIN argument + Explanation + Example

~90-100 words
Body Paragraph 2 (4-5 sentences)

Your SECOND argument + Explanation + Example

~90-100 words
Conclusion (2 sentences)

Restate your opinion + Final thought

~30-40 words
Total: 250-280 words. You have 40 minutes. Spend 5 minutes planning, 30 minutes writing, 5 minutes checking.

Key Decisions for This Essay

Before writing, you need to decide:

  1. What is your position? Agree, disagree, or partially agree?
  2. What are your 2 main arguments?
  3. What examples can you use?
For this practice essay, we'll take the position: "I strongly agree that university education should be free."

Our Arguments:

Argument 1: Equal opportunity

Free education removes financial barriers, allowing talented students from poor backgrounds to succeed.

Argument 2: Economic benefit

Educated citizens contribute more to the economy through higher taxes and innovation.

Model Essay (Band 8)

Read this model essay carefully. Notice the structures highlighted.

Question:

To what extent do you agree or disagree with free university education?

INTRO The question of whether higher education should be funded by the state has sparked considerable debate in recent years. I strongly believe that university education should be free for all students, as this would create a more equitable society and benefit the economy.
BODY 1 The primary argument in favour of free education is that it removes financial barriers for talented individuals from disadvantaged backgrounds. Were tuition fees to be abolished, students would no longer need to take on crippling debt to pursue their academic goals. For example, in countries such as Germany and Norway, where university education is free, social mobility has significantly increased, with more working-class students entering professional careers than ever before.
BODY 2 Furthermore, the provision of free higher education can be seen as an investment rather than a cost. Graduates typically earn higher salaries and therefore pay more taxes over their lifetimes, effectively repaying the government's investment. Additionally, a more educated workforce drives innovation and economic growth, which benefits society as a whole. Countries with high rates of university attendance tend to be more competitive in the global economy.
CONCLUSION In conclusion, I firmly believe that the advantages of free university education far outweigh the costs. Were governments to invest in their citizens' education, both individuals and society would reap significant long-term benefits.

Band 8 Features Used:

  • Nominalization: "The question of whether...", "The provision of free higher education"
  • Advanced conditional: "Were tuition fees to be abolished", "Were governments to invest"
  • Complex noun phrases: "talented individuals from disadvantaged backgrounds"
  • Passive voice: "can be seen as an investment"
  • Cohesive devices: "Furthermore", "Additionally", "In conclusion"
Word count: 268 words. This is within the ideal range (250-280).

Write Your Introduction

The introduction has 2 jobs: (1) Paraphrase the question, (2) State your opinion.

Question:

To what extent do you agree or disagree with free university education?

Sentence 1: Paraphrase the Question

Use this frame (nominalization):

The [noun: question/issue/debate] of whether [topic rephrased] has [sparked/generated/led to] [considerable/significant] [debate/discussion/controversy].

Example: "The question of whether higher education should be funded by the state has sparked considerable debate."

0 words

Sentence 2: State Your Opinion

Use one of these frames:

I [strongly/firmly] believe that [your position], as this would [reason 1] and [reason 2].
In my view, [your position] because [brief reason].
