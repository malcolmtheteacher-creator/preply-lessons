The Hidden Difficulty

Part 1 looks simple - just choose A, B, C, or D. But here is the catch: all four options often fit grammatically. The examiners are testing whether you know which word naturally goes with other words in English. This is called collocation.

Three Core Challenges

1. Similar Meanings
All options might mean roughly the same thing, but only one naturally combines with the surrounding words.

Example: "make" vs "do" - both relate to actions, but only one works with specific nouns.
2. Fixed Expressions
English has thousands of fixed phrases where only one word fits.

Example: "take into account" NOT "bring into account"
3. Preposition Combinations
Verbs and adjectives have specific prepositions that follow them.

Example: "depend ON" NOT "depend from"
4. Context Clues
Sometimes the answer depends on understanding the whole text, not just the sentence.

The tone, topic, and logical flow all matter.

Band Score Comparison

Band C Candidate

  • Translates from native language

Band A Candidate

Common Mistake

Do not choose an answer just because it "sounds natural" in your language. English collocations often differ from other languages. For example, in many languages you "make a photo" but in English you "take a photo".

Decision 1: Read for Gist First

The Golden Rule

Never look at the options until you have read the entire text. Understanding the topic, tone, and flow will help you predict what type of word fits each gap.

1
Read the title - It tells you the topic and helps you activate relevant vocabulary.
↓
2
Read the whole text quickly - Ignore the gaps for now. Just understand what it is about.
↓
3
Note the register - Is it formal, informal, academic, journalistic? This affects word choice.
↓
4
Then tackle each gap - Now you have context to make informed decisions.

Practice: Read for Gist

Exercise: What is this text about?

The art of bonsai has been practised in Japan for centuries, though its origins actually (1) ______ in China. Today, enthusiasts around the world (2) ______ great pleasure from cultivating these miniature trees, which require patience and careful attention. Unlike regular gardening, bonsai demands that practitioners (3) ______ a deep understanding of how plants grow and respond to their environment.
Topic: Bonsai (miniature tree cultivation)

Register: Informative/educational, semi-formal

Key Context Clues:
  • Historical origin mentioned (China, Japan)
  • Words like "practised," "cultivating," "patience" suggest careful, skilled activity
  • Comparison with "regular gardening" - bonsai is more demanding

Prediction for gaps:
(1) Needs a verb about origins/beginning - probably "lie" or similar
(2) Needs a verb collocating with "pleasure" - probably "take" or "derive"
(3) Needs a verb about gaining/developing - probably "develop" or "acquire"

Why This Works

When you understand the text first, you can often predict the type of word needed before seeing the options. This prevents you from being tricked by distractors that fit grammatically but not contextually.

Decision 2: Spot the Collocation Type

Know Your Enemy

Most Part 1 questions test specific collocation patterns. If you can identify which type you are dealing with, you will know where to look for clues.

The Four Main Types

Type 1: Verb + Noun Collocations

Certain verbs naturally pair with certain nouns.

Correct Incorrect
make a decision do a decision
take a photo make a photo
do homework make homework
tell a lie say a lie
The company decided to ______ drastic measures to cut costs.
Answer: B - take
We say "take measures" (also "take action", "take steps"). This is a fixed verb + noun collocation. You cannot "make measures" or "do measures" in English.

Type 2: Adjective + Noun Collocations

Some adjectives only work with specific nouns.

Adjective Correct Use Incorrect Use
high high price, high temperature high person
tall tall building, tall person tall price
big big problem, big mistake big temperature
heavy heavy rain, heavy traffic heavy price
There is a ______ chance that the project will be delayed.
Answer: B - high
We say "high chance" (also "high probability", "high risk"). For chances and probabilities, we use "high/low" not "big/small". Similarly, we say "strong possibility" not "big possibility".

Type 3: Verb + Preposition Combinations

Many verbs require specific prepositions.

Verb Correct Preposition
depend on
consist of
result in / from
insist on
apologize for
The success of the campaign will ______ on how well we communicate our message.
Answer: A - depend
"Depend on" is the correct combination here. While "rely on" also works grammatically, "depend on" is more natural when describing a causal relationship with outcomes. "Base" would need "be based on" and "count on" means something slightly different (to trust/expect).

Type 4: Phrasal Verbs

The particle (up, down, off, etc.) completely changes the meaning.

Phrasal Verb Meaning
take up start a hobby/activity
take over assume control
take in understand/deceive
take on accept responsibility/employ
After retirement, she decided to take ______ painting as a way to stay creative.
Answer: A - up
"Take up" means to start a new hobby or activity. "Take on" would mean to accept a challenge or responsibility. "Take over" means to assume control. "Take in" means to understand or to be deceived.

Decision 3: Eliminate Wrong Options

The Power of Elimination

Even if you cannot immediately identify the correct answer, you can often eliminate 2-3 wrong options. This dramatically increases your chances of selecting the right answer.

Common Confusion Pairs

⚠ MAKE vs DO
MAKE
Creating/producing:
make a decision, make a mistake, make progress, make an effort, make money, make a suggestion, make an appointment, make a complaint, make arrangements
DO
Completing tasks/activities:
do homework, do research, do business, do damage, do good, do harm, do your best, do someone a favour, do the shopping
⚠ SAY vs TELL
SAY
No direct object needed:
say hello, say goodbye, say sorry, say a word, say something, say nothing

Pattern: say + (words/nothing)
TELL
Needs a person as object:
tell someone, tell a story, tell a lie, tell the truth, tell the difference, tell the time, tell a joke

Pattern: tell + person/story/lie
⚠ TAKE vs BRING
TAKE
Away from the speaker:
take a photo, take action, take measures, take into account, take place, take part, take advantage, take responsibility
BRING
Towards the speaker:
bring to an end, bring about (cause), bring up (raise/mention), bring together, bring to light, bring to someone's attention

Elimination in Action

Example Question

The investigation ______ to light several previously unknown facts about the case.

Options: A. took B. brought C. made D. got

A
took - We say "take place" but NOT "take to light". Eliminate.
B
brought - "Bring to light" is a fixed expression meaning "reveal". Keep!
C
made - No expression "make to light" exists. Eliminate.
D
got - No expression "get to light" exists. Eliminate.

Answer: B - brought
"Bring to light" is a fixed expression meaning to reveal or discover something.

Your Turn: Practice Elimination

It is important to ______ the difference between correlation and causation in scientific research.
Answer: B - tell

Elimination process:
- "Say the difference" - incorrect, "say" does not collocate with "difference"
- "Tell the difference" - correct! Fixed expression meaning "distinguish"
- "Speak the difference" - incorrect, makes no sense
- "Talk the difference" - incorrect, makes no sense

"Tell the difference" is a fixed expression meaning to recognize or distinguish between things.

Elimination Strategy Summary

  • Cross out options that do not form valid collocations
  • Check if fixed expressions apply
  • Consider meaning differences between similar words
  • Even eliminating one option increases your odds from 25% to 33%

Decision 4: Check Your Answer Fits

The Final Check

Before confirming your answer, run it through three checks: Grammar, Meaning, and Register. An answer must pass all three to be correct.

The Three Checks

1. Grammar Check
  • Does the word fit grammatically?
  • Correct verb form (tense, agreement)?
  • Right word class (noun/verb/adj)?
  • Countable/uncountable correct?
2. Meaning Check
  • Does it make sense in context?
  • Does it fit the overall message?
  • Is the logical connection right?
  • Does it match the tone?

3. Register Check

Register refers to the level of formality. CAE texts are usually formal or semi-formal.

Informal Formal (CAE-appropriate)
get obtain, acquire
start commence, initiate
ask request, inquire
think about consider, contemplate

Common Traps to Avoid

⚠ Trap 1: The "Almost Right" Answer

One option might seem perfect but has a subtle problem. Always re-read the complete sentence with your chosen answer.

The company's profits have ______ dramatically over the past year.
Answer: B - risen
"Rise" is intransitive (no object) - profits rise by themselves. "Raise" is transitive (needs an object) - you raise prices. "Arose" means "came up" and "aroused" means "awakened/stimulated".
⚠ Trap 2: The "Sounds Good" Trap

An option might sound natural in everyday speech but be wrong in the specific collocation context.

The new policy will ______ into effect next month.
Answer: A - come
"Come into effect" is a fixed expression. While "go into effect" is used in American English, "come into effect" is the standard British English form tested in CAE. "Get into effect" and "enter into effect" are not valid collocations.
⚠ Trap 3: The "Translation" Trap

Do not translate from your native language. English collocations are often completely different.

Scientists have ______ important discoveries about climate change.
Answer: A - made
We say "make a discovery" NOT "do a discovery". Many languages use their equivalent of "do" with discoveries, but English uses "make". Similarly: make progress, make an attempt, make a contribution.

Final Check Routine

Before moving to the next question, always:

  • Read the complete sentence with your answer inserted
  • Check it sounds natural with the words before AND after the gap
  • Verify it fits the overall meaning of the paragraph
  • Confirm the register matches the text style

The Psychology of Decision Making

Every day, we (1) ______ countless decisions, from trivial choices about what to eat to significant ones that shape our future. Psychologists have long been fascinated by how humans (2) ______ to conclusions, often without being fully aware of the mental processes involved.

Research has (3) ______ to light some surprising findings about decision making. For instance, people often (4) ______ more attention to potential losses than equivalent gains, a phenomenon known as loss aversion. This tendency can (5) ______ us to make choices that, on closer inspection, are not in our best interest.

Another key finding is that having too many options can actually (6) ______ the quality of our decisions. When faced with overwhelming choice, people often experience decision fatigue and may (7) ______ up giving up entirely. Understanding these psychological patterns can help us (8) ______ better decisions in both our personal and professional lives.
1 Every day, we ______ countless decisions...
Answer: B - make
"Make a decision" is the correct collocation. We do not "do decisions" or "take decisions" (though "take a decision" exists in very formal British English, "make a decision" is standard). "Have decisions" is not a valid collocation.
2 ...how humans ______ to conclusions...
Answer: B - come
"Come to a conclusion" is the correct expression. While "reach a conclusion" is also correct, the preposition "to" here specifically requires "come". "Arrive at a conclusion" would need "at" not "to". "Get to conclusions" is not standard.
3 Research has ______ to light some surprising findings...
Answer: B - brought
"Bring to light" is a fixed expression meaning to reveal or discover. We do not say "take to light", "come to light" would need a different structure (e.g., "findings have come to light"), and "put to light" is not valid.
4 ...people often ______ more attention to potential losses...
Answer: B - pay
"Pay attention" is the correct collocation. While "give attention" exists, the natural collocation is "pay attention to something". "Put attention" and "take attention" are not valid English expressions.
5 This tendency can ______ us to make choices...
Answer: A - lead
"Lead someone to do something" is the correct pattern. "Bring" would need a different structure. "Take" does not fit here. "Drive" can be used ("drive someone to do something") but "lead" is more natural in this academic context.
6 ...too many options can actually ______ the quality of our decisions.
Answer: B - reduce
"Reduce the quality" is the most natural collocation. "Lower" typically collocates with prices, standards, or expectations. "Decrease" and "lessen" are less commonly used with abstract nouns like "quality". "Reduce" fits best in formal/academic writing.
7 ...people often ______ up giving up entirely.
Answer: A - end
"End up doing something" means to eventually do something, often unexpectedly. "Finish up" means to complete something. "Wind up" also works but is slightly more informal. "Close up" means to shut/seal something.
8 ...can help us ______ better decisions...
Answer: B - make
Again, "make a decision" is the correct collocation. This reinforces the importance of knowing key collocations - they often appear multiple times in different forms throughout a test.

Key Takeaways

Your Part 1 Strategy Summary

Follow this checklist every time you approach a Multiple Choice Cloze question.

The 4-Decision Process

  • 1
    Read for Gist First - Understand the whole text before looking at options. Identify topic, tone, and register.
  • 2
    Spot the Collocation Type - Is it verb+noun, adjective+noun, verb+preposition, or a phrasal verb?
  • 3
    Eliminate Wrong Options - Cross out options that do not form valid collocations. Use your knowledge of confusion pairs.
  • 4
    Check Your Answer Fits - Verify grammar, meaning, and register. Read the complete sentence with your answer.

Common Traps to Avoid

  • Translate from your native language
  • Look at options before reading the text
  • Spend too long on one question
  • Leave any answers blank

  • Read the entire text first
  • Look at words before AND after the gap
  • Use elimination when unsure
  • Check your answer in context
