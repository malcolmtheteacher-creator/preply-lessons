Understanding Problem/Solution Essays

In this essay type, you identify CAUSES or PROBLEMS, then propose realistic SOLUTIONS.

Today's Question:

In many cities, traffic congestion is a growing problem that affects quality of life and economic productivity.

What are the main causes of this problem? What measures could governments take to address it?

Essay Structure for Problem/Solution Essays

Introduction (2-3 sentences)

State the problem + Say you'll discuss causes and solutions

~40-50 words
Body Paragraph 1: PROBLEMS/CAUSES (4-5 sentences)

2-3 causes with explanations

~90-100 words
Body Paragraph 2: SOLUTIONS (4-5 sentences)

2-3 solutions that ADDRESS the causes

~90-100 words
Conclusion (2 sentences)

Summary + Call to action or prediction

~30-40 words
Your solutions should MATCH your problems! If Problem 1 is "too many cars," Solution 1 should address cars (e.g., "better public transport").

Model Essay (Band 8)

Notice how each solution DIRECTLY addresses a cause mentioned earlier.

Question:

What are the causes of traffic congestion? What solutions can governments implement?

INTRO Traffic congestion has become an increasingly pressing issue in urban areas worldwide, causing significant economic losses and environmental damage. This essay will examine the primary causes of this problem and propose practical solutions.
PROBLEMS The rapid increase in private car ownership is arguably the main cause of traffic congestion. As living standards rise, more families can afford vehicles, leading to overcrowded roads during peak hours. Additionally, inadequate public transport infrastructure forces many commuters to rely on personal vehicles. In numerous cities, bus and train networks are either unreliable or fail to cover suburban areas effectively, leaving residents with no viable alternative to driving.
SOLUTIONS Several measures could effectively address these issues. Were governments to invest heavily in efficient public transport, commuters would have practical alternatives to driving. Cities like Singapore and Tokyo have demonstrated that excellent metro systems can significantly reduce traffic. Furthermore, the implementation of congestion charges in city centres can discourage unnecessary car journeys, as has proven successful in London and Stockholm.
CONCLUSION In conclusion, while traffic congestion stems primarily from increased car ownership and poor transport infrastructure, these problems can be mitigated through strategic investment and pricing policies. Governments must act decisively before cities become paralysed by gridlock.

Band 8 Features Used:

  • Nominalization: "The rapid increase in...", "the implementation of..."
  • Advanced conditional: "Were governments to invest heavily..."
  • Passive voice: "can be mitigated"
  • Real examples: Singapore, Tokyo, London, Stockholm
  • Problem-solution matching: Cars → Congestion charges; Poor transport → Investment
  • Strong conclusion: Call to action with vivid language
Word count: 265 words. Notice the balance: roughly equal words for problems and solutions.

The introduction has 2 jobs: (1) State the problem dramatically, (2) Say what the essay will cover.

Write Your Conclusion

Summarise briefly and end with a strong call to action or warning. You're on your own!

Ideas for ending strongly:

  • Call to action: "Governments must act decisively..."
  • Warning: "Without urgent action, cities will become paralysed..."
  • Optimistic: "With proper investment, this problem can be solved..."
  • Conditional prediction: "Were these measures implemented, urban life would improve significantly."
