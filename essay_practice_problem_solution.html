What is a Problem/Solution Essay?

You are given a situation or issue and asked to identify the problems it causes and suggest practical solutions. Balance is key: you need one solid paragraph for problems and one for solutions.

Today's Question

In many cities around the world, traffic congestion has become a serious problem.

What are the main causes of this problem? What measures could be taken to solve it?

Key difference: This is NOT about your opinion. This essay is about identifying real problems and proposing realistic, specific solutions. Avoid vague solutions like "the government should do more."

Essay Structure

Introduction

Introduce the issue + state that this essay will examine causes and solutions

40-50 words
Body 1: The Problems/Causes

2 specific causes with explanation of their impact

90-110 words
Body 2: The Solutions

2 specific, realistic solutions with explanation of how they would work

90-110 words
Conclusion

Summarise: the problems are serious; the solutions are achievable

30-40 words

Model Essay: Band 8

Notice: the solutions are specific and explained in detail. Notice how the essay moves from problem to impact to solution to outcome.

INTROTraffic congestion in major urban centres has emerged as one of the most pressing infrastructure challenges of the modern era. This essay will examine the primary causes of this phenomenon and propose a number of practical measures to alleviate it.
PROBLEMSOne of the principal causes of urban congestion is the over-reliance on private vehicles. As car ownership rates continue to rise — particularly in rapidly developing economies — road networks designed for far fewer vehicles become overwhelmed. This is compounded by inadequate public transport infrastructure, which leaves commuters with little viable alternative to driving, thereby perpetuating a cycle of dependency on private cars.
SOLUTIONSA particularly effective solution would be the introduction of congestion pricing, whereby drivers are charged a variable fee to enter city centres during peak hours. London's congestion charge demonstrated that such schemes can reduce traffic volumes by up to 30%. In parallel, significant investment in reliable public transport — including expanded metro networks and bus rapid transit systems — would provide commuters with a genuine alternative, reducing the incentive to drive.
CONCLUSIONIn conclusion, while traffic congestion presents significant challenges for urban populations, targeted policy interventions combining financial disincentives for driving with improved public transport represent a viable path forward.

Cause language: "One of the principal causes of... is...", "This is compounded by..."

Solution language: "A particularly effective solution would be...", "In parallel, significant investment in..."

Specificity: A real example (London's congestion charge) and real data (30% reduction)

Outcome language: "...would provide commuters with a genuine alternative, reducing the incentive to drive"
