The Egg

Andy Weir (2009) — a man dies, meets God, and learns a huge secret about every person on Earth

Original version available: read this lesson at B2/C1 level →

Read the story in three parts

Andy Weir wrote this very short story in 2009 and put it online for free. Millions of people have read it and talked about it. This is the same idea, told here in simple English. Read each part, then stop and talk. The end is a big surprise — read slowly.

Part 1 — After the crash

You died. It happened in a car accident, on your way home. There was no pain. You left a family behind, and they were very sad.

Then you met someone. There was nothing around you — just empty space. But it did not feel cold or frightening.

"What happened? Where am I?" you asked.

"You died," the man said. He said it in a quiet, kind voice.

"There was a truck. It hit my car…"

"Yes," he said. "But do not worry about that now."

You looked at him. "Are you God?" you asked. "You must be God."

He smiled. "You understand quickly," he said.

Stop and talk:
  • How do you feel when the story says "you died"? Is it strange to be "you" in the story?
  • God is quiet and kind, not big and frightening. Why do you think the writer makes God like this?
  • What do you think will happen next?

Part 2 — Many lives

"What happens now?" you asked. "Heaven? Hell?"

"No," said God. "You will be born again. You will live a new life, as a new person — a new baby, in a new time. You will not remember this life, or any other life."

"Again? So I have done this before?"

"Yes. Many, many times. Each time, you were a different person."

You thought about this. "So maybe I lived a long time ago? I could have been someone in the past?"

"Yes, you were," said God. "But here is something you do not know yet. For me, time is not a line. Your lives do not happen in order."

Stop and talk:
  • God says your lives "do not happen in order." What do you think this means?
  • God does not tell you everything at once. Why do you think he lets you guess, step by step?

Part 3 — The big secret

"Every person who ever lived is you," said God. "Every person who will live is you too. You are every human being on Earth."

You could not speak. "I am… everyone?"

"Now you understand," said God. "Every time you hurt someone, you hurt yourself. Every kind thing you did, you did for yourself too."

"But why?" you asked. "Why all of this?"

"Because one day you will become like me," said God. "This whole universe is an egg. It is for you. You are growing. You must live every human life — all of them. When you have lived them all, and learned everything, you will be ready. Then you will be born."

You looked at God for a long time. Then you went on to your next life. And God went back to work.

The end — stop and talk:
  • "Every kind thing you did, you did for yourself too." If this were true, would you treat people differently? How?
  • The universe is "an egg" for one person to grow up in. Is this a nice idea, or a lonely one?
  • Did you guess the secret before the end? When did you start to understand?

Guessing with must, might and can't

In the story, the man does not know where he is. He has to guess. We use these words to show how sure we are about something:

must = I am sure it is true.   "You must be God."
can't = I am sure it is NOT true.   "This can't be real."
might / could = maybe — I am not sure.   "I might have lived before."

After all three, we use the base verb: must be, can't be, might be.

1. Choose must, might or can't

1. He knows everything about me. He be God. (I am sure)

2. I feel no pain at all. This be a normal place. (I am sure it is not)

3. Maybe I lived long ago. I have been a soldier. (not sure)

4. She is laughing and happy. She be sad. (I am sure she is not)

5. The lights are on, so someone be at home. (I am sure)

1. must   2. can't   3. might (or could)   4. can't   5. must

How the writer surprises you

This story is famous for its ending. Here are three simple things the writer does to make it work.

1. The slow secret (the "twist")

God does not tell you the truth all at once. The story gives it to you slowly, one step at a time. Then comes the big surprise: you are every person on Earth. A surprise like this at the end is called a twist.

2. The word "you"

The story does not say "a man died." It says "you died" and "you are everyone." This makes the story feel personal. It feels like it is about you, the reader.

3. A huge idea in easy words

The story is about life, death, and the whole universe — very big ideas. But the writer uses short, simple sentences. The small words make the big idea even more powerful.
