Dealing with Interruptions

During a presentation, people may interrupt with questions. Here are useful expressions to delay your answer politely:

Situation

✓ Someone interrupts
✓ You want to continue
✓ Someone disagrees
✓ You're confused by a question

What to Say

Thanks for that question. I was just about to cover this point.
Would you mind if I take questions at the end?
But I was planning to deal with this point later.
Could you clarify that for me?

Making Sure You Understand

These expressions help you check understanding during a presentation:

For checking: "Does that make sense?" • "Do you follow?" • "Is that clear?"

For disagreeing: "I see your point, but..." • "That's a good question. However..."

For responding: "Absolutely." • "Exactly." • "That's right."

For signalling more: "As I mentioned..." • "As I was saying..." • "Moving on to..."

Pronunciation: Intonation Matters

When you express doubt or ask a question, your intonation goes UP. When you make a statement, it goes DOWN. This makes you sound more confident and natural!

Example with rising intonation:

"Could you explain that?" (voice goes up at the end)

Example with falling intonation:

"This product is excellent." (voice goes down at the end)

Step 5: Handle Questions

When someone asks a question during your presentation, use these strategies:

If you need time to think: "That's a good question. Let me think about that for a moment."

If you don't understand: "Could you explain that a bit more?" or "I'm not sure what you mean."

If you want to continue: "I was just about to cover that point in my next slide."

If you want to agree: "That's an excellent point. I completely agree."

If you disagree politely: "I see your point, but I think there's another way to look at it."

Remember:

The best presenters are not perfect. They are prepared, stay calm when interrupted, and speak with confidence. You can do this!
