Robots and Neon: Is East Asia the Future?

Fast trains, robots and phone payments. But there are also old machines and quiet villages.

  • Do you use your phone for everything?

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. The fast train was on time.
train (n) — a long vehicle on rails. Example: “The fast train was on time.”
2. A robot works in the factory.
robot (n) — a machine that does work. Example: “A robot works in the factory.”
3. People pay with a phone.
phone (n) — a small device for calls and apps. Example: “People pay with a phone.”
4. Many shops in Japan take cash.
cash (n) — coins and notes. Example: “Many shops in Japan take cash.”
5. He lives in a quiet village.
village (n) — a very small town. Example: “He lives in a quiet village.”
6. The train is very fast.
fast (adj) — not slow. Example: “The train is very fast.”
Talk it through:
  • Which word is new for you? Say a sentence with it.
The stereotype: “Everyone in East Asia lives in a high-tech world.”

The story

Part 1 — High-tech

Japan opened its first fast train in 1964. The trains are almost always on time. China has the longest network of fast trains in the world. In South Korea, there are about 1,000 robots for every 10,000 factory workers. That is the most in the world. In China, many people pay with their phone for everything.

Quick check:

1. When did Japan open its first fast train?

2. How do many people in China pay?

1. In 1964.

2. With their phone.

Discuss:
  • Do you pay with your phone?

Part 2 — Old things too

Japan still uses a lot of cash. Many offices still use fax machines. In 2022, the digital minister said the government wanted to stop using floppy disks. Many vending machines take only coins and notes. A visitor can ride a very modern train and then eat in a restaurant that takes only cash.

Quick check:

1. Does Japan still use a lot of cash?

2. What did the digital minister want to stop using?

1. Yes.

2. Floppy disks.

Discuss:
  • Do you use cash?

Part 3 — Behind the neon

Not everyone lives in a big, modern city. Some villages are small and quiet, and many people there are old. Robots work in factories, not in homes. New technology also brings problems, for example long working hours and questions about privacy.

Quick check:

1. Where do most robots work?

2. Who lives in many villages?

1. In factories.

2. Many old people.

Discuss:
  • Would you like to live in a quiet village?

Grammar — past simple

Regular verbs: add -ed. “Japan opened its first fast train in 1964.”
Irregular verbs: build → built, use → used (regular), say → said, go → went.
Negative: did not (didn't) + base verb.

1. Choose the word

1. Japan its first fast train in 1964. (opened / opens)

2. China a very long train network. (built / builds)

3. The minister that floppy disks must stop. (said / says)

1. opened

2. built

3. said

2. Make your own

1. Yesterday I used my phone to ______.

2. Last year I went ______.

3. I didn't ______ yesterday.

1. ...pay for lunch. / ...call a friend.

2. ...to another city. / ...on a long train trip.

3. ...use cash. / ...watch TV.
