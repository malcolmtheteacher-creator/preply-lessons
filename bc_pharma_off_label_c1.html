Handling an Off-Label Question From a Physician

C1 Level · Pharmaceutical Sales · Compliant Communication

Pharma · Dewey 615.1

First Thoughts

You are a medical science liaison sitting across from a senior oncologist. She has just asked, half-casually, whether your drug — licensed for one indication — is being used ‘with any success’ for a different indication.

She is a busy, intelligent clinician who has given you her time. Promoting the drug off-label is not just poor practice; it is a regulatory breach that can end your career. But a clumsy refusal will close the door on a relationship that took eighteen months to build.

Take a moment with each question below. Share as much detail as you like — specific examples, what you would actually say, what tripped you up last time.

1. What is the real risk of answering an off-label question casually — to you, to your company, and to the patient?

2. Where is the line between informing a clinician and promoting a product? Who decides where that line sits?

3. Why do experienced MSLs sometimes say “the refusal is the relationship”? What do they mean by that?

4. How does a good clinician read your answer to this question? What are they actually testing?

Quick opener — pick whichever you prefer:

  • In twenty seconds, give me a refusal that protects compliance and keeps the conversation alive.
  • A compliance lead once said: “The best MSLs make the boundary feel like a professional courtesy, not a door slammed shut.” What do they do differently?

Key Vocabulary

Eight words and phrases you will actually use in this conversation. Click each card to reveal the meaning, then say the word aloud and try to use it in a sentence from your own experience.

off-labelA use of a drug that falls outside the indications for which it is officially licensed by the regulator.
the label / licenceThe regulator-approved document that defines how a drug may be prescribed and promoted.
an indicationA specific medical condition that a drug is approved to treat.
to promoteTo actively encourage the use of a product. Tightly regulated in pharma — promotion must match the label exactly.
an unsolicited medical enquiryA question from a clinician that they initiated, not you. Triggers specific response rules.
medical informationA separate, non-promotional function that answers clinical questions using company-approved scientific content.
complianceThe set of internal and external rules a company and its people must follow — especially under ABPI, FDA, EMA.
to route (a question)To formally pass an enquiry to the team or function best placed — and legally permitted — to answer it.

Key Language

Useful chunks of language for this conversation. Read each phrase aloud and think about when you would actually reach for it.

Catching the question — respectfully

  • “That’s a really important question, and it’s one I want to make sure you get a properly answered rather than a half answer from me.”
  • “I appreciate you raising that directly — let me be transparent about where I can and can’t help.”
  • “Before I go any further, can I just check — are you asking me in a promotional sense, or as an unsolicited clinical question?”

Naming the boundary without drama

  • “What you’re asking about is an off-label use, which I’m not able to discuss in a promotional context.”
  • “My remit is the licensed indication; anything outside that needs to go through our Medical Information team.”
  • “I want to be straight with you: if I answered this question here, I would be acting outside my licence to speak on behalf of the company.”

Keeping the door open

  • “What I can do — and what I’d love to do — is submit your question to Medical Information today, and you’ll have a written response, with references, within forty-eight hours.”
  • “If it’s helpful, I can also send you the published peer-reviewed literature on the area, without commentary from me.”
  • “This doesn’t need to slow us down — I just need to route it to the right place so we both stay on safe ground.”

Preserving the relationship

  • “I hope you take the fact that I’m being careful here as a sign of how seriously we take the relationship, not the opposite.”
  • “I’d rather have this slightly awkward moment now than put either of us in a difficult position later.”
  • “If anything, the fact that I can’t answer tells you something useful about how our team operates.”

The Scenario

The situation: You are a Medical Science Liaison for a specialty oncology drug, licensed for indication X. You have just finished a scheduled meeting with a senior oncologist at a teaching hospital. As you’re gathering your things, she says: “Out of interest — are any of your colleagues seeing this used for indication Y? I’ve got a patient I’m thinking about.”

Your task: Hold the compliance line firmly. Route the enquiry through the correct channel. Keep the relationship warm enough that she will still take your next meeting. End with her better informed about how to get her answer, not embarrassed about having asked.

Step 1 · Plan your response

Use the timer. Make notes — do not write full sentences. Think about what you will say first.

Step 2 · Deliver

When you are ready, role-play the conversation out loud. Be ready to handle the follow-up questions below.

Follow-up questions to handle:

  • Come on — off the record, what are you seeing in the field?
  • If I submit it formally, how do I know I’ll get a real answer and not corporate boilerplate?
  • Can you at least point me to a paper? That can’t be off-limits.
  • I have to decide on this patient this week — does your process understand that?
