Making a Complaint

You bought something, and it doesn't work. Now you want help. You don't need to be angry — you just need the right words, said with a smile.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

Ten days ago, you bought a new coffee machine. It was not cheap. For one week, it was perfect — nice hot coffee every morning.

Then this morning, it stopped. No lights, no water, no coffee. It doesn't work. So now you are back at the shop, with the machine in the box and your receipt in your hand.

You want a new machine, or your money back. Behind the desk is a shop assistant. Maybe they will say "call the company" or "it is not our job". Your plan is easy: stay calm, stay friendly, and don't go away.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Did you ever buy something that didn't work? What was it? What did you do?
  2. When something is wrong in a shop, do you say something, or do you stay quiet? Why?

Who's who

You
The Customer 📦

Calm and polite, but you don't give up. You bought a machine that doesn't work. You want a new one, or your money back.

The other person
The Shop Assistant 🧾

Not unkind — just tired. First they may say "call the company". But they can help you, if you are friendly and clear.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at how the customer stays polite — excuse me, please, I would like — but does not give up. It is OK to speak slowly, or to ask the other person to repeat.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the calm, polite words.

AssistantHello. Can I help you?
YouYes, hello. Excuse me, I have a problem with this coffee machine. I bought it here.
AssistantOh. What is the problem?
YouIt doesn't work. It was good for one week, and then it stopped. No lights, no coffee — nothing.
AssistantHmm. Do you have the receipt?
YouYes, here is my receipt. I bought it ten days ago.
AssistantFor a broken machine, you must call the company. Here is their phone number.
YouI know it is not your fault, but I bought it here, not from the company. It is broken, so I would like some help, please.
AssistantI see. Well… I can repair it for you.
YouThank you, but the machine is only ten days old. I would like a new one, please — or my money back.
AssistantOne moment… I am sorry. We don't have this machine now.
YouI understand. You have a newer machine there, on the shelf. Is it possible to have that one instead?
Assistant… Yes, OK. That is a good idea. I can do that for you.
YouThank you very much! That is very kind. You helped me a lot.

From memory — don't look up!

1. What is the problem with the coffee machine?

2. First, the assistant says to call the company. What does the customer say back?

3. In the end, what does the customer get? Why not the same machine?

1. It doesn't work. It was good for one week, then it stopped — no lights, no coffee.
2. The customer stays friendly. They say it is not the assistant's fault, but they bought the machine here, not from the company — so they ask the shop for help.
3. A newer machine from the shelf. The shop did not have the same machine any more (it was finished), so the customer asked for a different one instead.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases for any complaint. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it nicely. A complaint is not a fight. Speak calmly, with a friendly voice. You are not angry — you just need help. It is always OK to say excuse me, please, and thank you.

🚪 Starting politely

Phrase 1

You come to the desk with a broken machine. You want to start in a calm, friendly way. Which one is polite?

A. "This machine is rubbish!"
B. "Excuse me, I have a problem with this coffee machine."
C. "You sold me a bad machine."
B — "Excuse me, I have a problem with this coffee machine."
"Excuse me" is polite, and "I have a problem with…" is calm and friendly. A and C sound angry — and angry words make people want to say no.
Phrase 2

The person at the desk did not make the machine. Take them off the hook — tell them it is not their mistake. Complete it: "I know it is not your ___, but…"

"I know it is not your fault, but…"
Your fault means your mistake. This is a magic line: the person is now on your side, not against you. Cousins: "This is not your problem, I know, but…" and "You didn't make it, I know, but…"

🔍 Saying what is wrong

Phrase 3

Now say what is wrong. The machine turns on for nothing — it is broken. Put the words in order:

doesn'titwork
"It doesn't work."
Three small words for any broken thing: "The TV doesn't work", "The key doesn't work", "The phone doesn't work". You can also say "It is broken."
Phrase 4

The assistant asks how you know you bought it here. You show the little paper that says you paid. Complete it: "Here is my ___."

"Here is my receipt."
A receipt is the small paper (or email) that shows you paid, and when. It is your best friend in a complaint. Cousins: "I have the receipt here." / "I bought it ten days ago."

🤝 Asking for what you want

Phrase 5

The machine is broken, so you ask for a new one. Say it politely. Complete it: "I would ___ a new one, please."

"I would like a new one, please."
"I would like…" is the polite way to say "I want". Use it for everything: "I would like a refund", "I would like to speak to the manager".
Phrase 6

You also want to say you can take your money back. Which way is polite?

A. "Give me my money back now."
B. "Can I have my money back, please?"
C. "I want my money. Fast."
B — "Can I have my money back, please?"
"Can I…?" and "please" make it a friendly question, not an order. You can also say "Can I have a refund, please?" — a refund is your money back.
Phrase 7

The shop has no more of your machine, but there is a different one on the shelf. You ask if you can have that one. Complete it: "Is it ___ to have that one instead?"

"Is it possible to have that one instead?"
"Is it possible…?" is a very polite way to ask for something. "Instead" means "in the place of the first one". Cousins: "Is a new one possible?" / "Can I have that one instead?"

💛 Saying thank you

Phrase 8

The assistant helps you. You want to finish with a big, warm thank you. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you very much! That is very kind."
Add "very much" to make your thank you bigger, and "That is very kind" tells the person you saw their help. A warm end makes people happy to help you again next time.

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in shops, cafés, hotels — everywhere. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"I have a problem with ___." — Think of a thing you bought that went wrong. What was it?

"The ___ doesn't work." — Name a thing in your home that can break.

"I would like ___, please." — What do you want to ask for? (a new one, a refund, help…)

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole complaint out loud — start politely → say what is wrong → show the receipt → ask for what you want → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your complaint. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the customer 📦

This time your lamp is broken. The assistant speaks — you answer. Be polite, but don't give up.

AssistantHello. Can I help you?
AssistantOh dear. Do you have the receipt?
AssistantFor a broken lamp, you can call the company. Here is the number.
AssistantHmm… OK, I can repair it for you.
AssistantI am sorry, we don't have this lamp now.
Assistant… OK, yes. I can do that for you.

Round 2 — now YOU are the shop assistant 🧾

Change places. You work in the shop. Be friendly. Say "Hello", "I see", "I am sorry", "Of course". It is your shop — you can help.

CustomerHello. I have a problem with this clock. I bought it here last week, and it doesn't work.
CustomerHere is my receipt. It stopped after three days. It is broken.
CustomerI know it is not your fault, but I bought it here. I would like a new one, please.
CustomerThe clock is only one week old. Can I have my money back, or a new one?
CustomerThank you very much! That is very kind.
The Twist

🌩️ No receipt!

One more time — this is harder. Now you can't find your receipt! The assistant says: "I'm sorry — no receipt, no help." What do you say? Stay calm and friendly. Tell them when you bought it, and that it is broken. Maybe you paid by card — can they find it on their computer? Ask nicely for help. One rule: if you get angry or shout, you lose. Start the timer and try to get a good result with only your good manners.

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I started politely, with "excuse me" and "I have a problem with…".
  • I said "It doesn't work" or "It is broken" clearly.
  • I used "I would like…" to ask for what I want.
  • I stayed calm and friendly, not angry.
  • I said "thank you" warmly at the end.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

In 1773, people in America bought tea from Britain. But Britain put a new tax on the tea — extra money to pay. The people did not agree. They said: "This is not fair. We did not say yes to this."

They wrote letters. They asked, again and again. But no one in Britain listened. So one night, they went onto the ships in Boston and threw all the tea into the sea. It was a very big complaint!

Your complaint in a shop is much smaller — and much friendlier. But it is the same idea: say what is wrong, stay strong, and don't go away. The good news? You can win with a smile — and keep your coffee.

Two questions to talk about

  1. The people in Boston asked politely for a long time. When is it OK to stop being quiet and say loudly, "This is not fair"?
  2. In a shop, a smile helps you win. But sometimes people need to be loud to change big things. When is it good to be quiet and kind — and when do you need to be loud?
