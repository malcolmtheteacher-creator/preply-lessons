🛒 Bookshop vs Library

A2 • French speakers • False friends: librairie ≠ library

🔍 Let's notice what we see

Look at this sentence. What's the problem?

"I bought a book at the library."

(But libraries don't sell books — they lend them!)

Can you buy books at a library? Where do you borrow books? What place sells books?

⚠️ The False Friend:

The French word "librairie" LOOKS like English "library", but they mean DIFFERENT things!

In French: librairie = a shop where you BUY books

In English: library = a place where you BORROW books for free

In English: bookshop = a shop where you BUY books

🛠️ Understand the difference

📊 Two different places:

🇫🇷 French "librairie"

🛒 BOOKSHOP / STORE

You PAY and BUY books

"J'ai acheté un livre à la librairie" = I bought a book at the bookshop

🇬🇧 English "library"

📚 FREE LENDING SERVICE

You BORROW books for free

"I borrowed a book from the library" = I got it for free

🎯 Key Rule:

French librairie → English BOOKSHOP / STORE (where you BUY)

English library → French bibliothèque (where you BORROW)

When buying books: use BOOKSHOP. When borrowing: use LIBRARY.

3. "There's a great _____ near my house where I borrow books." (bookshop / library)
4. "The _____ has books on every topic you can imagine." (bookshop / library)

🚀 Now you try — no help!

Choose the correct place. Check your answers when you're done.

1. "I spent 30 euros at the _____ buying three books." (bookshop / library)
Answer: "I spent 30 euros at the bookshop."

→ You spend money at a bookshop.

2. "I got this detective novel from the _____ last week."
Answer: "I got this novel from the library."

→ "Got" means borrowed for free at the library.

3. "The _____ downtown offers a 20% student discount."
Answer: "The bookshop downtown offers a discount."

→ Only shops offer discounts, not libraries.

4. "I have to return my books to the _____ before they charge me a fine."
Answer: "I have to return them to the library."

→ Libraries charge fines for late returns.
