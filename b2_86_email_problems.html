Email Communication: Key Concepts

Understand the challenges of email communication across cultures.

1. Relationship Between Writer and Reader

The tone and formality of an email should match the relationship. A formal email to a boss differs greatly from a casual message to a friend. Different cultures have different expectations about what is appropriate.

2. General Tendencies vs. Stereotypes

We can observe general tendencies in how different cultures communicate. However, we must avoid being too simplistic or creating stereotypes. Every individual is different.

3. Put Things in Context

It is important to put things in context. Before misunderstanding an email, consider the writer"s background, culture, and situation. An email that seems rude might simply be culturally direct communication.

4. Respond Carefully

It is better to respond to an email in a considered way rather than immediately. Take time to think about the message and your response, especially in cross-cultural situations.

5. Training and Guidelines Matter

Companies with a multicultural workforce benefit from training their staff about cross-cultural communication. Guidelines should offer suggestions rather than strict rules.

Advice and Suggestions for Email Communication

Giving Advice

  • You should consider the relationship first.
  • It might be helpful to ask for clarification.
  • I would suggest taking time before responding.
  • It could be useful to check with your team.
  • We recommend providing clear guidelines.

Asking for Clarification

  • Could you explain what you mean by...?
  • I"m not sure I understand. Could you clarify...?
  • What did you intend by...?
  • Could you give me more details about...?

Responding Diplomatically

  • Thank you for your message. I understand your concern.
  • I appreciate your directness. However, I would like to explain...
  • Thank you for clarifying. I see the situation differently because...
  • I value our working relationship and would like to discuss this further.

Scenario A: Last-Minute Cancellation

You receive an email from a colleague named Karl. He says he cannot help with a presentation you organized three weeks ago. The email arrives two hours before the presentation. Although Karl says sorry, he does not explain why or offer any suggestions. You now have to do the presentation with no help.

Your task: How would you handle this situation? Would you respond immediately or take time? What would you say? How would you avoid damaging your professional relationship?

Scenario B: Enthusiastic Over-Planning

You are scheduled to travel to a conference overseas next week. This morning you received an email from a colleague named Jaime in that country. Jaime has organized everything for your visit—transport, hotels, meals, and entertainment. You can see Jaime has worked very hard and is excited. Ten minutes ago your boss told you that you must postpone the trip for a month.

Your task: How would you communicate the delay to Jaime? What would you say to show appreciation while explaining the situation? How would you suggest next steps?

Scenario C: Friendly but Overwhelming

You receive a very friendly email from a colleague named Sun from another branch of your company. Sun tells you all about his office and the people who work there. Sun asks you many questions about your office, how long you have worked there, and what the other people in your office are like. He also asks about the timetable for an important meeting next week.

Your task: What tone did Sun use and why? How should you respond? How would you answer his questions while maintaining professionalism? How might cultural differences affect how you understand this email?

Discussion Tips

Use the phrases you learned to discuss these scenarios. Consider:

  • What is the relationship between the people involved?
  • What cultural differences might affect the situation?
  • How would you explain the situation?
  • What is the best way to maintain the relationship?
