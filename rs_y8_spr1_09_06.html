Assessment: Why Do Rites Matter?

YEAR 8 · UNIT 3: RITES OF PASSAGE

What You'll Do

You will be assessed on your understanding of rites of passage across religions, Van Gennep's framework, and your ability to evaluate why human communities perform these ceremonies.

Assessment Task 1: Knowledge Retrieval Test

Question 1: Define "rite of passage" and give three examples from different religions.
Question 2: Explain Van Gennep's three stages. What happens at each stage, and why are all three necessary?
Question 3: What is the significance of water in Christian baptism? What does it symbolise?
Question 4: Explain what a sacred thread (janeu) represents in Hindu tradition and why young men wear it.
Question 5: What is the significance of reading the Torah in the Bar/Bat Mitzvah ceremony?
Question 6: Describe the Islamic marriage ceremony (Nikah). What is the role of the Mahr?

Assessment Task 2: Comparative Analysis Essay

Write an essay (400-500 words) comparing rites of passage for the same life transition across two different religions. Choose from:

  • Birth rites (Islamic Aqiqah vs. Christian Baptism)
  • Coming-of-age (Bar/Bat Mitzvah vs. Upanayana vs. Confirmation)
  • Marriage (Hindu Vivah vs. Islamic Nikah vs. Christian wedding)

Essay Structure

  1. Introduction: State which two religions you're comparing and what life transition they mark.
  2. First Religion's Rite: Describe the rite in detail. What are its symbolic elements? What does it teach the person/community?
  3. Second Religion's Rite: Describe the rite. Explain its symbolic elements and significance.
  4. Comparison: What are the similarities between the two rites? What are the key differences? Why might the religions approach this transition differently?
  5. Analysis: What does each rite reveal about the religion's values? For example, does it emphasise individual transformation, community recognition, spiritual commitment, or something else?
  6. Conclusion: Why do you think both religions feel the need to mark this transition with ritual?

Assessment Task 3: Evaluation Essay

Write an essay (500-600 words) evaluating the question: "Why do humans need rites of passage?"

Essay Structure

  1. Introduction: State your thesis: Do you think rites of passage are essential to human life, or are they optional cultural practices? What would happen without them?
  2. Psychological Perspective: Consider what rites of passage do for individuals. Do they help people process change? Make transitions less anxious? Create identity? Give examples from the rites you've studied.
  3. Social Perspective: Consider what rites of passage do for communities. How do they create shared meaning? Reinforce values? Maintain cultural continuity? Use specific examples.
  4. Challenges and Counterarguments: Some might argue rites of passage are outdated or unnecessary. How would you respond? Are there modern rites of passage in secular society?
  5. Your Position: Synthesise your thinking. Do you believe rites of passage are necessary? For whom? Under what circumstances?
  6. Conclusion: Reflect on what this unit has taught you about how humans create meaning around life's transitions.

Assessment Task 4: Creative Response

Choose one of the following:

Option A: Design Your Own Rite of Passage

Create a rite of passage for a modern secular life transition (e.g., graduation, becoming a teenager, moving to a new place, first day of work). Design it using Van Gennep's three stages. What would be the symbolism? What would the ritual include? Who would participate? Why would this rite be meaningful?

Option B: Comparative Infographic

Create a visual comparison of three rites of passage from this unit. Use symbols, colours, and text to show similarities and differences. Include Van Gennep's three stages for each rite.

Option C: Personal Reflection Essay

Reflect on a life transition you've experienced or witnessed (moving schools, puberty, a religious ceremony, family change). Which rite of passage from this unit does it most resemble? What elements did it share? What was missing? How did ritual (or lack of it) affect how you/others processed the change? (300-400 words)

Mark Scheme

Knowledge Retrieval (30 marks)

2-4 marks per answer. Award marks for accurate definitions, correct examples, clear explanations, and specific details from the lessons.

Comparative Analysis Essay (30 marks)

Criteria: Accurate descriptions of both rites (10 marks), thoughtful comparison identifying similarities and differences (10 marks), analysis of what each reveals about the religion's values (10 marks). Bonus marks for nuanced understanding and use of specific details.

Evaluation Essay (30 marks)

Criteria: Clear thesis statement (5 marks), psychological perspective with examples (10 marks), social perspective with examples (10 marks), engagement with counterarguments and modern rites (5 marks). Bonus marks for sophisticated thinking and synthesis.

Creative Response (10 marks)

Award marks for: understanding of Van Gennep's framework (if applicable), creativity and originality, clear communication, thoughtful reflection on why the rite matters.

Reflection Questions

Before submitting, ask yourself:
  • Have I accurately described the rites of passage I studied?
  • Do I understand why each religion performs these ceremonies?
  • Can I explain how the rites follow Van Gennep's three-stage pattern?
  • Have I thought deeply about the purpose of rites of passage in human life?
  • Have I used specific examples and details from the lessons?
  • Is my writing clear, organised, and well-reasoned?
  • Have I considered different perspectives and engaged with counterarguments?
