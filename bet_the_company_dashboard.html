Business English through real cases · C1

Bet the Company

Real companies, real decisions, endings still being argued about — twelve cases, from Ørsted to Boeing.

Every case here actually happened, and none of them closes cleanly: a utility that bet itself on wind just before the cost of money doubled; a phone giant that prototyped its own killer; a film company whose every annual decision was rational and whose sum was ruin; a toy maker rescued by subtraction; a video chain that fought back, briefly won, and was stopped by its own shareholders. Alongside them: a fraud with no order behind it, a champion its own regulator defended, ten days of world shipping run on paper, a $47bn story meeting its own prospectus, the hardest case in modern aviation, a founder who invented a fifth exit, and a company repriced for merely succeeding. These are the stories boards actually argue about — told properly, without the punchlines.

Each case is a 50-minute C1 lesson: the real vocabulary of the case, the story in three parts with the numbers kept honest, one executive grammar focus — hedging, deduction, causation, conditionals, concession — and timed speaking tasks in the four registers leaders actually use: the board, the market, the workforce and the press. The Going Deeper tab always argues at least two readings; the lesson never announces a moral.
