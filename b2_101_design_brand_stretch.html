Presenting a Bolder Design Concept

B2 Level · Design · Client Presentation

Design · Dewey 745.4

First Thoughts

You’re presenting three routes for the client’s brand refresh. Two of them are what the client expected. The third — route C — is the one your team truly believes in. It is a meaningful stretch from the current brand.

You have fifteen minutes on the agenda. How you set up route C in the first two of those minutes will decide whether the client sees it as brave or reckless.

Take a moment with each question below. Share as much detail as you like — specific examples, what you would actually say, what tripped you up last time.

1. When you present a bold idea, what are you really asking the client to trust — you, the data, or their own instincts?

2. Is it better to show the safe route first and build up to the bold one, or open with the bold one? Why?

3. What is the real risk for a client of picking the boldest design? And what is the risk of always picking the safest?

4. Have you ever been persuaded to say yes to something you were nervous about? What convinced you?

Quick opener — pick whichever you prefer:

  • Describe — in one sentence — the boldest design route you’d put in front of a client this year.
  • A senior designer once said: “Clients don’t buy the work — they buy how sure you are about the work.” Do you agree?

Key Language

Useful chunks of language for this conversation. Read each phrase aloud and think about when you would actually reach for it.

Opening — setting expectations

  • “We’re going to show you three routes today. Two that solve the brief in the way you might have expected, and one that we’d like you to really sit with.”
  • “Before we start, I want to promise you one thing: none of these routes is here to fill space — each one is a serious answer to the brief.”
  • “I’ll talk you through the thinking behind each route, then we’ll look at them, then we’ll come back to the thinking.”

Introducing route C without drama

  • “Route C is the one the team has been most excited about — and also the one that pushes you the most.”
  • “This is a deliberate stretch from where the brand sits today. Let me show you why we think that’s the right answer.”
  • “When we tested an early version of this internally, the reaction was: ‘It feels like where we should have been two years ago.’”

Handling the moment they hesitate

  • “I can see this is landing differently from the other two — that’s actually a good sign. Tell me what you’re reacting to.”
  • “Before you decide how you feel, let me show you how this lives across your real touchpoints — not just on a screen.”
  • “I’d rather you asked the hard questions now than live with a safer route you’re only half-proud of.”

Closing with a real choice

  • “We’re not going to push you into route C. We are going to give you the evidence to make a confident call.”
  • “What we’d suggest is taking 48 hours with all three routes, then coming back together for a proper decision.”
  • “Whichever route you pick, we’ll put the same care into making it the best version of itself.”

The Scenario

The situation: A conference room at the client’s office. You’re presenting the brand refresh to the marketing director, the brand lead, and the CEO. Fifteen minutes on the agenda. Three routes prepared; route C is a clear stretch — same brand, but much bolder colour, typography, and voice. The CEO is known for being cautious; the marketing director privately wants to go bold.

Your task: Present all three routes fairly, but give route C the setup it needs to be taken seriously. By the end, the room should understand that route C is a considered answer to the brief, not a dare.
