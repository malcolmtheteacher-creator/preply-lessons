Daydreaming: "If I Won the Lottery"

For dreams, wishes and things that are unlikely or impossible right now.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday daydreams:

  • "If I won the lottery, I'd buy a little house by the sea."
  • "If I had more free time, I'd finally learn the guitar."
  • "If I could live in any city, I'd choose Lisbon."

Before you check: what two ingredients appear in every sentence, and where does each one go?

Check the pattern CLICK
if + past simple (won, had, could) in the imagined condition, then would ('d) + base verb (buy, learn, choose) in the result. The past simple here doesn't mean the past — it signals "this is imagined or unlikely."
If + past simple, ... would ('d) + base verb

From real life to daydream

"I don't have a big garden, so I can't grow vegetables."
→
"If I had a big garden, I'd grow vegetables."

The daydream sentence doesn't say what's true now — it imagines something different.

Real Plan or Daydream? Choosing the Right One

Real life doesn't announce which pattern to use. You decide, based on how likely something really is.

Spot the pattern

  • "If I have time this weekend, I'll finally paint the fence." (a real plan)
  • "If I had unlimited time, I'd travel to a new country every month." (a daydream)
  • "If it's sunny tomorrow, we'll have a barbecue." (a real plan)

What tells you which pattern to use — is it about something that could really happen, or something imagined?

Check the pattern CLICK
A real, likely plan uses if + present simple, will + base ("If I have time, I'll paint..."). A daydream or unlikely idea uses if + past simple, would + base ("If I had unlimited time, I'd travel..."). Ask yourself: could this genuinely happen soon, or am I just imagining?
Real, likely: if + present simple, will + base
Unlikely, imagined: if + past simple, would + base
Signal words help: "this weekend," "later," "tomorrow" usually point to a real plan. "If I were rich," "if I could," "if I ran the world" usually point to a daydream.

Giving Advice: "If I Were You"

The classic pattern for advice — even though it's not really about you at all.

Spot the pattern

  • "If I were you, I'd talk to your manager about it."
  • "If I were you, I wouldn't wait any longer — just call them."
  • "If I were you, I'd ask for a refund."

Same question as before: what's the structure?

Check the pattern CLICK
If I were you + would ('d) / wouldn't + base verb. Same pattern as a daydream — it's not really about "me," it's advice dressed up as an imagined situation. In fast speech people sometimes say "if I was you," but "if I were you" is the classic set phrase for advice.
If I were you, I'd / I wouldn't + base verb
