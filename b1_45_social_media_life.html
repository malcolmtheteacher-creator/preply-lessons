Social Media Vocabulary

Algorithm A system that decides which content to show you based on your behavior
Influencer Someone with many followers who can influence people's opinions
Viral Content that spreads rapidly online, shared by millions
Content Creator Someone who makes and shares videos, photos, or text online
Follower A person who subscribes to see your social media updates
Trending Topics or hashtags that are popular at the moment
Filter Software that changes how you look in photos; also alters reality
Clickbait Sensational headlines designed to make you click, often misleading

Does Social Media Harm Our Mental Health?

Mental health researchers are increasingly concerned about social media's impact. Studies show a connection between heavy social media use and increased anxiety, depression, and low self-esteem, especially among young people.

Why? Several factors are at play. First, algorithms show us content designed to trigger emotions—outrage, envy, sadness—because emotional content gets more engagement. Second, we compare ourselves to carefully curated highlight reels of other people's lives, forgetting that nobody posts their worst moments. Third, social media creates a constant need for validation through likes and comments.

However, it's not all negative. Social media also helps us maintain friendships, find communities with shared interests, and access important information. The key is balance and awareness. Experts recommend limiting daily use, being critical of what you see, and taking regular breaks.

The Debate: Social Media Does More Harm Than Good

Prepare arguments for and against this statement. Use these phrases: "I believe...", "On the other hand...", "The evidence shows...", "Many people argue that..."

Arguments FOR (Harm)

• Increases anxiety, depression, and loneliness

• Creates unrealistic beauty and lifestyle standards

• Waste of time that could be spent on productive activities

• Cyberbullying and toxic behavior

• Reduces face-to-face social skills

Social media platforms are designed by engineers to be addictive. They use algorithms that exploit human psychology to keep us scrolling, often prioritizing sensational and negative content. This constant exposure to unrealistic standards and comparison with others leads to widespread mental health issues. Additionally, the time spent online could be invested in real relationships, exercise, and learning.

Arguments AGAINST (Benefits)

• Helps maintain relationships across distances

• Builds communities around shared interests

• Provides access to information and education

• Enables self-expression and creativity

• Creates opportunities for careers and entrepreneurship

Social media connects billions of people and has revolutionized how we communicate. It allows us to maintain friendships we might otherwise lose, find people with similar interests, and support important social movements. Many creators and small businesses depend on social media for their livelihoods. The issue isn't social media itself, but how we use it. Responsible use means it can be a powerful tool for connection and growth.

Digital Wellbeing

Reflect on Your Relationship with Technology

1. Do you think social media has a positive or negative effect on your life?

Be honest with yourself. There's no right answer—everyone's relationship with technology is different.

2. What's one thing you'd change about how you use social media?

Maybe it's spending less time, being more selective about what you follow, or using it only for certain purposes.

3. How do you think social media is different in other countries?

Social media use, preferences, and impacts vary greatly across cultures and countries.

Digital Wellbeing Tips:

• Set daily time limits

• Turn off notifications

• Unfollow accounts that make you feel bad

• Don't compare yourself to others online

• Take regular "digital detox" breaks

• Prioritize in-person connections
