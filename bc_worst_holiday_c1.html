What Can Go Wrong on Holiday?

Let's brainstorm problems that can happen when traveling.

Categories:

Journey

Travelling by plane, train, or car

Examples: delays, cancellations, lost luggage, jet-lag

Accommodation

Where you stay

Examples: dirty rooms, noisy neighbors, overbooking, pests

Destination

The place itself

Examples: crime, closed attractions, construction

Food & Drink

What you eat

Examples: food poisoning, unfamiliar cuisine, water safety

Weather

Climate and natural conditions

Examples: rain, extreme heat, storms, cold fronts

Think: Which of these is the worst holiday problem for you? Which is less serious?

How to Tell a Great Holiday Story

Learn the techniques that make bad holiday stories entertaining and memorable.

Vague Language (Being Fluent When Speaking From Notes)

Example from a real story:

"So, we had this kind of dormobile and erm, a tent, and we sort of spent all day virtually sitting in this soggy wet tent, and this not-so-soggy-wet dormobile, till we got to the point where mum said 'That's it. We're going home' because there was nothing to do."

Underline the Vague Language:

kind of, erm, sort of, virtually

Other Examples of Vague Language:

like, type of, you know, kind of thing, basically, or something

Dramatic Techniques to Make Stories More Interesting

1. Direct Speech (Quotes)

"What is that smell?!" makes the story come alive.

2. Repetition for Drama

"It was absolutely awful, I mean really really foul." (Emphasize with repetition)

3. Questions to the Listener

"Can you imagine?" — includes your listener in the story.

4. Exaggerated Language

stank to high heaven, sweltering, foul — make problems sound worse!

Example Story with All Techniques:

"We were sitting there, in this traffic jam, in the heat, I mean the firemen were driving up and down taking people water, because it was you know, really really sweltering. Can you imagine? So we were sitting in this traffic jam and suddenly there was this absolutely awful smell, I mean really really foul. 'What is that smell?!' It stank to high heaven."

Notice: Vague language (you know), repetition (really really), questions (Can you imagine?), direct speech (What is that smell?!), and exaggerated language (stank to high heaven).

Your Speaking Task: Tell Your Holiday Disaster Story

Prepare Your Anecdote:

Think of a real bad holiday experience you've had, or invent one! You can exaggerate and add white lies to make it more dramatic.

Remember: Make notes, but don't write word-for-word. You'll need to speak fluently with the vague language techniques you learned.

Planning Guide (Make Notes Only):

  • Where were you going?
  • How did the disaster start?
  • What was the worst moment? (Use direct speech!)
  • What happened in the end?
  • How did you feel?

Speaking Time:

Time for your story: 10:00
