Grammar: Narrative Tenses

To tell interesting stories, we use different past tenses to show what was happening and what happened.

Example Story Sentence:

"I was walking down Bell Street alone when I heard growling behind me."

Two Important Tenses for Stories

1. Past Continuous (was/were + -ing)

Use: To describe what was happening or the situation/background. It sets the scene.

Example: I was walking down Bell Street alone.

What it does: Gives context for the story, creates atmosphere

2. Past Simple (regular past tense)

Use: For the main actions or events that happened. These are the key moments in your story.

Example: I heard growling behind me.

What it does: Shows what actually happened in sequence

Timeline Visualization

In the sentence "I was walking down Bell Street when I heard growling," here's what's happening:

  • was walking (past continuous) - the background action
  • heard (past simple) - the event that interrupted

The past continuous provides the context, and the past simple shows what happened at a specific moment.

Making Stories Interesting with Adjectives & Adverbs

Use descriptive words to make your stories more engaging and vivid.

Word Type

Adjectives (describe nouns)
Adverbs (describe actions)

Examples from Stories

alone, scariest, stray, dark, loud
suddenly, slowly, quickly, carefully, loudly

Compare These Stories

Version 1 (boring): "I walked down the street and heard a dog."

Version 2 (interesting): "I was walking down Bell Street alone when I suddenly heard growling behind me. It was a large, scary dog with wild eyes."

Notice the difference:

  • More adjectives: alone, large, scary, wild
  • More adverbs: suddenly
  • Past continuous to set the scene
  • Past simple for the main action

Phrases to Show You Are Listening

When someone tells you a story, use these expressions to show interest:

  • "That's good!" - show encouragement
  • "How long ago?" - ask for details
  • "Wow! That's fantastic!" - express excitement
  • "Really?" - express surprise
  • "That's awful!" - show empathy
  • "Interesting!" - acknowledge what they said
  • "Were you? So was I." - share common experience
  • "What?!" - ask them to clarify
