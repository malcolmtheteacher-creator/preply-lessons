Every Day: "I work on Saturdays"

For routines — things you do every day, every week — and for facts.

Find the pattern

Three sentences about normal life:

  • "I work on Saturdays."
  • "He plays football on Sundays."
  • "They live in Madrid."

Look at the three verbs. One is a little different. Which one? Why? Say your idea, then check.

Check the pattern CLICK
"plays" has an -s.
With he, she, it → the verb takes -s.
With I, you, we, they → the verb stays simple, no change.
I / you / we / they + verb  →  I drive
he / she / it + verb + s  →  she drives
not: don't / doesn't + verb  →  I don't drive. She doesn't drive.
Words that go with this form: every day, every morning, always, usually, on Mondays.

Right Now: "It's raining!"

For things happening now, at this minute.

Find the pattern

Three sentences about this minute:

  • "Look! It's raining."
  • "Shh! The baby is sleeping."
  • "I can't talk now — I'm driving."

What two things do you see in every bold verb? Say your idea, then check.

Check the pattern CLICK
am / is / are + verb with -ing.
In speech we make it short: it's raining, I'm driving.
This form = now, at this minute. Often with Look!, Shh!, now.
I am + verb-ing  →  I'm reading
he / she / it is + verb-ing  →  she's reading
you / we / they are + verb-ing  →  they're reading
not:  I'm not reading.  She isn't reading.
Small spelling changes: write → writing (no e) · run → running (double letter) · sit → sitting

Which One?

Every day, or now? The little words in the sentence tell you.

Every-day words → simple form

every day · every morning · always · usually · never · on Mondays

Now words → am / is / are + -ing

now · right now · at the moment · today · Look! · Listen!

Every day:
"I walk to work."
→
Now:
"I'm walking to work."

Same person, same verb — but "every day" and "now" need different forms.
