What is Word Formation?

What Examiners Test

This part tests your knowledge of word families - how one base word can become nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs through different transformations:

  • Prefixes: un-, dis-, mis-, re-, over-, in-, im-
  • Suffixes: -tion, -ment, -ness, -ful, -less, -ly
  • Internal changes: long to length, strong to strength
  • Double changes: care to carelessness (prefix + suffix)

Common Mistakes

  • Spelling errors: "sucessful" instead of "successful"
  • Wrong part of speech: Using an adjective when you need a noun
  • Forgetting negatives: The sentence might need "unsuccessful" not "successful"
  • Missing double changes: Not adding BOTH prefix AND suffix when needed

The 4 Decisions for Word Formation

  1. Decision 1: What PART OF SPEECH do I need? (noun, verb, adjective, adverb)
  2. Decision 2: Do I need a PREFIX? (negative, repetition, degree)
  3. Decision 3: What SUFFIX creates the right form?
  4. Decision 4: Do I need a DOUBLE CHANGE? Check spelling!

Decision 1: Identify the Part of Speech

The Strategy

Before you transform the word, you MUST identify what type of word the gap needs. Look at the words AROUND the gap for clues.

Context Clues

If you see... You need a... Example
a/an/the + NOUN "the of the project" = completion
his/her/my + NOUN "his to learn" = ability
adjective + NOUN "great " = success
+ noun ADJECTIVE "a attempt" = successful
be/seem/look + ADJECTIVE "She seems " = successful
very/extremely + ADJECTIVE "very " = successful
+ verb / + adjective ADVERB " completed" = successfully
to + VERB "to in life" = succeed
modal + VERB "will " = succeed

Practice: Identify the Part of Speech

1. "Her to the problem was creative." SOLVE

2. "The test was difficult." SURPRISE

3. "They made a decision." COMPARE

4. "There is a for experienced staff." REQUIRE

5. "He was to help." ABLE

Key Insight

Identifying the part of speech FIRST narrows down your options dramatically. If you know you need a noun, you can immediately rule out adjective and adverb forms.

Decision 2: Know Your Prefixes

The Strategy

Prefixes go at the BEGINNING of words and usually change the MEANING (not the part of speech). The most important prefixes make words NEGATIVE.

Negative Prefixes (Most Common in CAE)

Watch the Context!

Always read the sentence carefully. A positive sentence needs a positive word, but a NEGATIVE sentence might need the opposite form!

un- not (unhappy, unable)
in- not (inactive, incomplete)
im- not before m/p (impossible)
il- not before l (illegal)
ir- not before r (irregular)
dis- opposite (disagree, disappear)
mis- wrongly (misunderstand)
non- not (non-existent)

Other Common Prefixes

re- again (redo, rewrite)
over- too much (overwork)
under- not enough (underpaid)
pre- before (prehistoric)
post- after (post-war)
en-/em- make (enable, empower)

Practice: Add the Correct Prefix

1. "His behavior was completely ___acceptable."

2. "It would be ___possible to finish by tomorrow."

3. "The parking in that area is ___legal."

4. "Her spelling is very ___regular."

5. "I think you ___understood what I said."

6. "The company decided to ___structure the department."

Prefix Rules for im-/il-/ir-

These are all forms of "in-" (meaning "not") that change based on the first letter of the root word:

  • im- before words starting with m or p: impossible, immature
  • il- before words starting with l: illegal, illogical
  • ir- before words starting with r: irregular, irresponsible
  • in- before other letters: incomplete, independent

Decision 3: Know Your Suffixes

The Strategy

Suffixes go at the END of words and usually change the PART OF SPEECH. Learn which suffixes create which word types.

Noun Suffixes

-tion / -sion
  • compete - competition
  • decide - decision
  • compare - comparison
-ment
  • require - requirement
  • achieve - achievement
  • develop - development
-ness
  • happy - happiness
  • aware - awareness
  • careless - carelessness
-ity
  • able - ability
  • possible - possibility
  • creative - creativity
-ance / -ence
  • appear - appearance
  • exist - existence
  • differ - difference
-er / -or / -ist
  • teach - teacher
  • act - actor
  • science - scientist

Adjective Suffixes

-ful (with)
  • success - successful
  • care - careful
  • hope - hopeful
-less (without)
  • care - careless
  • hope - hopeless
  • use - useless
-ous / -ious
  • danger - dangerous
  • ambition - ambitious
  • space - spacious
-ive
  • compare - comparative
  • create - creative
  • attract - attractive
-able / -ible
  • compare - comparable
  • rely - reliable
  • access - accessible
-al / -ical
  • tradition - traditional
  • economy - economical
  • practice - practical

Adverb Suffix

-ly (How something is done)

Most adverbs are formed by adding -ly to adjectives:

  • successful - successfully
  • apparent - apparently
  • comparative - comparatively

2. "She completed the task ___." SUCCESS

3. "There is a ___ for all applicants to have a degree." REQUIRE

4. "The two methods are not ___." COMPARE

5. "His ___ to speak three languages helped him get the job." ABLE

Decision 4: Watch for Double Changes

The Strategy

Some words need BOTH a prefix AND a suffix (or multiple suffixes). Read the sentence context carefully to catch these.

Common Double Change Patterns

Prefix + Suffix
CARE + -less + -ness = carelessness
SUCCESS + un- + -ful = unsuccessful
ABLE + dis- + -ity = disability

Internal Changes

Some words change internally (not just adding prefixes/suffixes):

Adjective to Noun
  • long - length
  • strong - strength
  • wide - width
  • deep - depth
  • high - height
Verb to Noun (Irregular)
  • choose - choice
  • prove - proof
  • believe - belief
  • grow - growth
  • lose - loss

Spelling Changes to Watch

Common Spelling Traps
successfull successful
happyness happiness
arguement argument
truely truly

Spelling Rules

  • -y to -i: happy = happiness (y changes to i before suffix)
  • -e drops: argue = argument (e often drops before suffix)
  • -ful has ONE L: successful, beautiful, careful
  • -ly after -ful: successfully (full word + ly)

Practice: Complete Double Changes

1. "His ___ caused the accident." CARE (negative noun)

2. "The attempt was ___." SUCCESS (negative adjective)

3. "The witness gave a detailed ___ of the suspect." DESCRIBE

4. "She spoke ___." COMPARE (adverb)

5. "The building was ___ at night." APPEAR (verb - gone)

The Importance of Sleep

Sleep is essential for both physical and mental health. Research has shown that people who get 1 SUFFICIENT sleep are more likely to suffer from various health problems. The 2 ABLE to concentrate and make good decisions depends heavily on getting enough rest.

Many people 3 SUCCESS manage their sleep schedules, often prioritizing work or entertainment over rest. This can lead to 4 CARE mistakes during the day, as tiredness affects our judgment.

The 5 APPEAR of dark circles under the eyes is a common sign of sleep deprivation. 6 COMPARE, people who sleep well tend to look healthier and more alert.

Gap Answer Transformation Process
1 insufficient SUFFICIENT + in- (negative prefix) = insufficient (adjective)
2 ability ABLE + -ity (noun suffix) = ability
3 unsuccessfully SUCCESS + un- + -ful + -ly = unsuccessfully (adverb)
4 careless CARE + -less (adjective suffix) = careless
5 appearance APPEAR + -ance (noun suffix) = appearance
6 Comparatively COMPARE + -ative + -ly = comparatively (adverb)
7 requirement REQUIRE + -ment (noun suffix) = requirement
8 incomparably COMPARE + in- + -able + -ly = incomparably (adverb meaning "extremely")

Analysis: The 4 Decisions in Action

  • Gap 3: Needed adverb (modifying "manage") + negative meaning (context shows failure) = unsuccessfully
  • Gap 6: Sentence-initial position + comparing two groups = Comparatively (adverb)
  • Gap 8: Modifying adjective "effective" + meaning "extremely" = incomparably

Exam Tip

Always re-read your completed sentence to check it makes sense grammatically AND in meaning. If something sounds wrong, reconsider your answer!
