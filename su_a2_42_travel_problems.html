Making Polite Complaints

Why Politeness Matters

When you have a travel problem, being polite helps you get a better result. Angry complaints often don't work. But polite, clear complaints get help and solutions. Today you'll learn 7 powerful phrases for every travel situation.

"Excuse me, I have a problem..."
When: Start any complaint politely
Examples:
"Excuse me, I have a problem with my booking."
"Excuse me, I have a problem with my room."
"I'm sorry, but my room isn't clean."
When: Report a problem politely
Examples:
"I'm sorry, but my room isn't clean."
"I'm sorry, but my food is cold."
"I'm sorry, but my bed is not comfortable."
"Could you help me, please?"
When: Ask for assistance politely
Examples:
"Could you help me, please? I'm lost."
"Could you help me, please? My flight is delayed."
"I'm afraid there's a mistake with my booking."
When: Report a booking or administrative error
Examples:
"I'm afraid there's a mistake with my reservation. I booked a double room, not a single."
"I'm afraid there's a mistake. The price is higher than I expected."
"Would it be possible to change my room?"
When: Make a polite request for something different
Examples:
"Would it be possible to change my room? It's too noisy."
"Would it be possible to move to a higher floor?"
"I'd like to speak to the manager, please."
When: Escalate if you need more serious help
Examples:
"I'd like to speak to the manager, please. This is serious."
"I'd like to speak to the manager, please. I'd like a solution."
"Thank you for your help."
When: Always end politely, even after a complaint
Examples:
"Thank you for your help. I appreciate it."
"Thank you for understanding. I'm grateful."

Cultural Awareness While Travelling

Learning from Mistakes

When you travel to another country, it's easy to make cultural mistakes. The good news: people understand you're a visitor! Learning to apologize respectfully shows you care about their culture.

Click each situation to see how to handle it respectfully.

Situation 1: You forget to take off your shoes when entering someone's home.
What do you say? How do you apologize?
"I'm so sorry, I didn't know. Where should I put my shoes?"
"Please forgive me. I'm not familiar with this custom. I apologize."
"Thank you for telling me. I'll remember next time."
Why it matters: In many Asian and Middle Eastern cultures, removing shoes shows respect in homes and temples.
Situation 2: You accidentally use your left hand to eat in a country where that's considered rude.
What do you say? How do you show respect?
"I'm so sorry. I didn't mean to be disrespectful. I'm left-handed, but I'll use my right hand if you prefer."
"Please forgive me. I didn't know that was offensive."
"Thank you for your patience as I learn your customs."
Why it matters: In many Middle Eastern, African, and Indian cultures, the left hand has traditionally been considered unclean for eating.
Situation 3: You tip in a restaurant in a country where tipping is not expected.
What happens? How should you explain?
"I'm from a country where we always tip. Your service was excellent. Please accept this."
"The meal was wonderful. I'd like to give you this as a thank you."
"I come from a different culture, but I wanted to show my appreciation."
Why it matters: In Japan, France, and many other countries, tipping is seen as insulting or unnecessary.
Situation 4: You speak too loudly in a quiet restaurant.
What do you do? How do you correct yourself?
"I'm so sorry. I didn't realize how loud I was being. I'll speak more quietly."
"Excuse me, I apologize for disturbing you. I'll keep my voice down."
"Thank you for your patience. I'm visiting from abroad and I'm still adjusting."
Why it matters: Different cultures have different ideas about appropriate volume. What's normal in one country might be rude in another.

Key Phrases for Cultural Mistakes

  • "I'm so sorry, I didn't know..."
  • "Please forgive me..."
  • "Could you tell me the right way to...?"
  • "I come from a different culture, but..."
  • "Thank you for your patience."

Tell Your Travel Story

Extended Speaking Practice

Now it's time to use everything you've learned! You'll tell a story about a travel problem. It can be real or imaginary.

Grammar Reminder

  • Past Simple for main events: "I arrived at the hotel..." "The flight was delayed..." "I lost my luggage..."
  • Past Continuous for background: "I was waiting for my flight when..." "I was checking in when..." "I was looking for my bag when..."
  • Mix them together: "I was waiting for my flight when I realized I missed my connection."
