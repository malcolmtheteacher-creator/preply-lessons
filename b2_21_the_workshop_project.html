Giving Instructions

Imperatives

When you give instructions, you use imperative sentences. The imperative is the base form of the verb with no subject (you).

Examples:
• Open the toolbox.
• Use the drill carefully.
• Fix the bolt with the wrench.
• Turn the screwdriver clockwise.
• Check the measurements before you cut.

Sequencing with First, Then, Next, Finally

Use these words to organize your instructions step by step:

Sequencing Structure:
First – the starting step
Then or Next – what comes after
Finally – the last step
Full Example:
"First, gather your tools and materials. Then, measure the wood carefully. Next, use the drill to make the holes. Finally, insert the bolts and tighten them with a wrench."

Practice: Put the Steps in Order

These steps are out of order. Can you arrange them correctly?

How to start a car:

a) Put the key in the ignition
b) Open the hood and check the oil
c) Test the engine by driving slowly
d) Release the parking brake

Bonus Challenge

Conditional Sentences: "If I had... I would..."

Use the second conditional to talk about what you would do if you had better tools or resources:

• "If I had a better drill, I would make more precise holes."
• "If I had more time, I would repair the entire workshop."
• "If I had a professional wrench, I would tighten the bolts faster."

Broader Discussion

Learning by doing vs. learning by reading: In this lesson, you learned instructions and then gave your own. How did doing it help you understand English better than just reading about it?
Tools and confidence: How does having the right tools change the way you approach a project? What's the parallel in learning English?
Helping others: If you had to teach someone else how to use a tool or complete your project, what would you say first? Why?
Real-world use: Where in your life do you use imperatives and instructions? (Work, hobbies, teaching family, sports, cooking...)
The conditional question: What's something you would do if you had more time, money, or resources? Use the second conditional to explain.

Key Takeaway

"Learning a language is like a workshop project: you need the right tools, clear steps, and practice. The more you *do* it, the better you become."
