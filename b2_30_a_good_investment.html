Reflect on These Questions

  • Have you invested money in something that resulted in losses? What did you learn?
  • What advice would you give to someone who's thinking about investing their entire savings?
  • Can a business profit while also doing good for society? Give examples.

Making Investment Statements

We often use conditional statements to talk about investments. These express possibilities, hypotheses, or general truths about money:

"If I had extra money, I'd invest in property."
"In times of crisis, investors convert their money to gold."
"My country is a great place to invest."
"Cars and designer clothes are terrible investments because they lose value immediately."

Investment Opportunities

The Scenario

You work for Whiterock, a private equity firm. You have €7 million to invest in lean European start-ups. Your portfolio is already heavy on technology start-ups, and you want to diversify into other sectors. Here are five proposals:

1. Trendygym

€3 million needed

What it is: A health club franchise offering yoga, pilates, and dance classes. Targeting young professionals in European cities.

Current status: Already operates 5 successful clubs in Germany and Italy. Wants to expand to 15 clubs across Europe.

Track record: Revenue growing 25% year-on-year. Profitable in all locations.

2. Walks with Friends

€1.5 million needed

What it is: A social media platform connecting people for group walking activities. Users find walking partners in their area and share health/fitness updates.

Current status: MVP (minimum viable product) exists. Funds needed for platform development and user acquisition.

Market potential: Health and wellness market is booming. Minimal competition so far.

3. Robodog

€6 million needed

What it is: High-tech robotic dog from Asia. Functions as virtual pet and carries advertising screens. Plans to distribute across Europe.

Current status: Product exists and is popular in Asia. Needs capital for European market entry and localization.

Concerns: Unproven in European market. Advertising model not yet validated. High tech product = rapid obsolescence risk.

4. Screen to Stage

€4 million needed

What it is: A musical theatre production adapting a famous cinema blockbuster. Top director and composer already attached. Developed screenplay completed.

Current status: In pre-production. Looking for funding to complete financing for London West End premiere.

Potential: Successful film-to-stage productions can run for years and generate substantial returns through tickets and merchandise.

5. Autocar

€5 million needed

What it is: A self-driving, eco-friendly vehicle. Based on an existing electric car platform. Has a basic working prototype.

Current status: Technology works, but needs further development for safety standards and market readiness.

Opportunity: Self-driving car market is hot. Governments are pushing for eco-friendly transport. But huge capital requirements and regulatory challenges.

Evaluate & Decide

Your Task

You have €7 million to invest. You can invest in any combination of the five proposals. No proposal is mandatory. You must decide which opportunities offer the best balance of profit potential, risk management, and alignment with your diversification strategy.

Evaluation Checklist

Questions to Discuss

  • Which proposal looks the safest? Which the riskiest?
  • Which would you invest in if you had €7 million? Why?
  • What information would you want to know more about before deciding?
  • If Robodog succeeds in Europe, what would a 10x return look like?
  • If Screen to Stage flops after opening, how would that affect Whiterock's reputation?

Decision Time

5:00

When the Timer Ends

Prepare to present your investment decisions. Which proposals did you choose? Why? What's your reasoning for each allocation?

Reflection & Further Discussion

Share Your Decisions

  • Which proposal did you choose to invest in?
  • How much money did you allocate to each?
  • What's your reasoning for each allocation?
  • If your partner made a different choice, whose strategy seems better?

Broader Questions

  • Would you ever invest in an investment bank? Why or why not?
  • Is your country a good place to invest? What sectors are strong?
  • What makes an investment risky? Give examples.
  • If you had a large lump sum of money, what would you invest in? Real estate? Stocks? Your own business?
  • Can profitable companies also be ethical? Give examples of businesses doing both.
