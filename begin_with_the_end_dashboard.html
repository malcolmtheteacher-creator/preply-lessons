Begin With the End

Know where you will land before you open your mouth. Three lessons on speaking like someone who is in control of the room.

Most speaking problems that sound like fillers, long answers and lost sentences have one cause: starting to talk before you know where the sentence is going. The cure is the oldest advice about communication — begin with the end in mind.

Each lesson tells a true story, teaches one tool you can use the same day, practises the language it needs, and ends with a lot of speaking. They build on each other, so take them in order.

Decide where you will land. Then say it first, give one reason, and stop.

Who it is for

Anyone who speaks English at work and already speaks it well — managers, specialists, presenters, people who sit in meetings where seniority is judged in seconds. If you talk too long, lose your sentence, fill silences, or give way too fast when challenged, this is the pathway. No grammar from scratch: the language here is the language of control.
