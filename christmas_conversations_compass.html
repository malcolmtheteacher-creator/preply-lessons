Why Most Holiday Conversations Feel... Meh

Harvard research shows people who ask more follow-up questions are rated as significantly more likable — yet most people don't ask ANY.

The Scene: You're at a Christmas dinner. Someone asks "So, what do you do?" You answer. They nod. Silence. Someone mentions the weather. More nodding. You check your phone.

Sound familiar? According to behavioral scientist Alison Wood Brooks from Harvard Business School, this isn't because you're bad at conversation — it's because most people are stuck in the WRONG QUADRANT of conversation.

The Problem: Most holiday conversations stay stuck in the INFORMATIONAL quadrant — "What do you do? Where do you live? How's work?" It's data exchange, not connection.

The Goal: Move conversations toward CONNECTION and SAVORING — where people actually enjoy talking to each other and feel genuinely liked.

The Key Insight: Likability increases dramatically when you move into the CONNECTION quadrant. And it only takes ONE good question to get there.

Navigation Tool #1: Go Deeper

These expressions help you move from Informational to Connection. They signal genuine curiosity and invite people to share more than just facts.

Click each expression to see how it works:

1. "Tell me more about that..."
→ Moves to CONNECTION Formality: Universal
Why it works: It's the simplest, most powerful follow-up. It shows you're interested WITHOUT interrogating. Harvard research found people who use this are rated significantly more likable.
Example:
"I just got back from Japan."
❌ "Nice." (conversation dies)
✅ "Oh! Tell me more about that — what made you want to go?"
NOW YOU TRY: Your partner says "I've been really into cooking lately." Respond using this expression.
2. "What's the story behind...?"
→ Moves to CONNECTION Formality: Neutral
Why it works: Everyone has stories. This question turns facts into narratives. It invites people to share their "origin story" rather than just data.
Example:
"I'm a graphic designer."
❌ "Oh, cool." (stuck in Informational)
✅ "What's the story behind that? How did you get into design?"
NOW YOU TRY: Someone at Christmas dinner says "We're thinking of moving to Canada." Use this expression to go deeper.
3. "What are you excited about lately?"
→ Moves to CONNECTION Formality: Warm/Informal
Why it works: This is a "capitalization" question — it asks someone to share positive emotions. When people relive excitement, they bond with whoever triggered that feeling (you!).
Example:
Instead of "How are you?" or "How's work?"
✅ "What are you excited about lately?" — Much better opener!
NOW YOU TRY: You're meeting your cousin's new partner for the first time. Use this question to start a real conversation.

Remember: "Boomer Asking" to Avoid

Don't immediately pivot back to yourself after they share. Stay with THEIR topic for at least 2-3 follow-ups before sharing your own experience.

❌ "Oh you went to Japan? I went to Thailand once..."

✅ "Oh you went to Japan? Tell me more — what surprised you most?"

Navigation Tool #2: Stay Warm Under Pressure

Christmas conversations can go wrong fast — Uncle's hot takes, awkward silences, someone bringing up politics. These expressions help you validate without agreeing and redirect without being rude.

Click each expression to see how it works:

4. "It makes sense that you feel that way..."
→ Keeps conversation in SAVORING Formality: Neutral/Diplomatic
Why it works: You can validate someone's FEELINGS without agreeing with their FACTS. This keeps them receptive instead of defensive. Starting with "I disagree" triggers defensiveness — this approach keeps the conversation warm.
Example:
Uncle: "Young people today don't want to work!"
❌ "That's not true at all." (triggers defensiveness)
✅ "It makes sense that you feel that way — the workplace has changed so much. What changes have you noticed?"
NOW YOU TRY: Someone says "AI is going to destroy society." Respond using validation first.
5. "Going back to what you said about..."
→ Moves back to CONNECTION Formality: Neutral
Why it works: This is a "callback" — referencing something from earlier shows you were actually listening. It's also perfect for redirecting away from dangerous topics back to something safer and more interesting.
Example:
The conversation has drifted to politics...
✅ "Going back to what you said about your trip to Portugal — did you try the pastéis de nata?"
NOW YOU TRY: Someone mentioned earlier they're learning guitar. The conversation has now turned awkward. Use this expression to redirect.

All 5 Navigation Expressions:

  • "Tell me more about that..." — Simple follow-up, maximum impact
  • "What's the story behind...?" — Turn facts into narratives
  • "What are you excited about lately?" — Capitalization (share positive emotions)
  • "It makes sense that you feel that way..." — Validate feelings, not facts
  • "Going back to what you said about..." — Callback + redirect

🧠 Quick Science: Reframe Anxiety as Excitement

Feeling nervous before a party? Your body can't tell the difference between anxiety and excitement — both have high heart rate, sweaty palms, etc.

Instead of telling yourself "calm down," say "I am excited." This reframes the arousal from threat to opportunity. Research shows this actually improves performance in social situations.
