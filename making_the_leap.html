The Language of Uncertainty

When we talk about big life decisions, we need language for:

  • Things that are almost certain to happen
  • Things that are likely but not guaranteed
  • Possibilities we can imagine
  • Complete uncertainty

Non-native speakers often use "maybe" and "probably" for everything. Native speakers have more precise tools - and they sound completely different.

Today's focus: Five native-speaker phrases for speculation - with the exact pronunciation patterns that make them sound natural, not textbook.

Native Phrases: Part A

Three phrases, from high certainty to possibility. Focus on the sound, not just the meaning.

Sound Check: Drilling

Time to lock in the pronunciation patterns. This is where the muscle memory builds.

Exercise 1: Mark the Stress

Click words that should be stressed. Then check your answers.

MARK THE STRESS
Making a career change is bound to be stressful.

MAKing a caREER change is BOUND to be STRESSful.

Note: "a", "is", "to", "be" are all weak - they almost disappear.

MARK THE STRESS
Your old colleagues may well think you're crazy.

Your OLD COLleagues MAY WELL THINK you're CRAzy.

Note: Both MAY and WELL are stressed - that's what makes this phrase powerful.

Exercise 2: Weak Forms

These words are almost invisible in natural speech. Listen and repeat.

Strong form → Weak form

"to" → /tə/ — "bound to happen" = "bound tə happen"
"a" → /ə/ — "such a risk" = "such ə risk"
"are" → /ə/ — "you are likely" = "you ə likely"
"the" → /ðə/ — "take the leap" = "take ðə leap"
"your" → /jə/ — "follow your passion" = "follow jə passion"
SHADOWING DRILL

Your teacher will say each sentence. Shadow immediately - don't wait for them to finish.

1. "It's bound to be difficult at first."
2. "You're likely to face some rejection."
3. "Following your heart may well be worth it."
4. "You could conceivably make it work."
5. "It remains to be seen whether you'll succeed."

Read this passage aloud. Pause at each | mark. Stress the CAPS.

What Makes the Difference

The phrases themselves are important - but it's the pronunciation that makes you sound native:

  • Stress the right syllables - this signals your certainty level
  • Weaken function words - "to", "a", "the", "are" become almost invisible
  • Chunk your speech - natural pauses between thought groups
