I Can, She Can

Saying what is possible — and what isn't.

Spot the pattern

Three things people say every day:

  • "I can cook — ask me about my pasta!"
  • "My best friend can sing really well."
  • "She can't drive, so she takes the bus."

Look at the bold words. Does can change with I, my friend, she? And what form is the verb after it? Say your idea first, then check.

Check the pattern CLICK
Can never changes. I can, my friend can, she can — always the same word, never "cans". And the verb after it stays in its base form: cook, sing, drive — never "to cook", never "cooks". The negative is can't (long form: cannot, one word).
I / you / he / she / we / they + can + verb
negative: can't (= cannot) + verb
The golden rule: can never changes. No -s, no "to", no extra words: she can cook — never "she cans cook", never "she can to cook".

Can You...?

Asking about other people — and answering fast.

Spot the pattern

Three little conversations:

  • "— Can you play the guitar? — Yes, I can!"
  • "— Can your dad cook? — No, he can't. But he can make tea!"
  • "— Can they understand the film? — Yes, they can, most of it."

Two questions for you: how is the question made? And in the short answers, which word comes back — the main verb, or can?

Check the pattern CLICK
The question just flips the order: Can + person + verb? No "do", no "does" — can makes the question by itself. And the short answer repeats can, not the main verb: "Yes, I can." — not "Yes, I play."
Can + I / you / he / she / we / they + verb ?
Yes, I can. / No, I can't.

Is It OK?

The same little word — a new job: asking and giving permission.

Spot the pattern

Three things you hear all the time:

  • "Can I open the window? It's hot in here."
  • "You can park here — it's free after six."
  • "Sorry, you can't take photos in the museum."

Nobody is talking about skills here. What does can mean in these sentences? Say your idea, then check.

Check your idea CLICK
Here can means "it's OK" and can't means "it's not OK — not allowed". And "Can I...?" is the polite way to ask first. Same word, same grammar — new meaning.
Can I + verb ? → asking if it's OK
You can + verb → it's OK
You can't + verb → it's not OK
