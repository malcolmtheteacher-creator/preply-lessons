🏨 Stay vs Rest

A2 • French speakers • False friends: rester ≠ rest

🔍 Let's notice what we see

Look at this sentence. What's the problem?

"I rested in Paris for three days."

(But they're talking about being in a city, not sleeping!)

Does this sentence tell you they relaxed? Or does it tell you where they were? What's wrong?

⚠️ The False Friend:

The French word "rester" LOOKS like English "rest", but they mean DIFFERENT things!

In French: rester = to stay, to remain (rester à la maison = stay at home)

In English: rest = to relax, to sleep, to recover from tiredness

In English: stay = to remain in a place

🛠️ Understand the difference

📊 Two different meanings:

🇫🇷 French "rester"

🏨 STAY / REMAIN

Be in a place, remain somewhere

"Je reste à la maison" = I stay at home

🇬🇧 English "rest"

😴 RELAX / SLEEP

Recover from tiredness, relax

"I need to rest" = I need to sleep

🎯 Key Rule:

French rester → English STAY (be in a place)

English rest → French se reposer (relax, sleep)

When talking about places: STAY. When talking about relaxing: REST.

🚀 Now you try — no help!

Choose the correct word. Check your answers when you're done.

1. "I _____ in Paris for two weeks last summer." (stayed / rested)
Answer: "I stayed in Paris."

→ Being in a city = stay.

2. "Please _____ for 15 minutes. You look exhausted."
Answer: "Please rest for 15 minutes."

→ Exhausted = need to relax = rest.

3. "They _____ in the hotel and never left the room."
Answer: "They stayed in the hotel."

→ In a place = stay.

4. "My doctor said I should _____ for a week."
Answer: "My doctor said I should rest."

→ Doctor's order = relax/sleep = rest.

✅ I can say 'stayed' instead of 'rested' when meaning remained somewhere
