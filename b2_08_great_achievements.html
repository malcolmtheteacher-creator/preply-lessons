Language Focus 1: Adding Emphasis

When we talk about great achievements, we often want to emphasize how important or surprising they are. Here are different ways to add emphasis:

Methods of Adding Emphasis

Intensifiers (very, absolutely, definitely, terribly): These words strengthen the adjective that follows them.
"Although I don't like telephones at all, I do use email all the time."
Comparative Emphasis (by far, far more): Compare things to show which is more important.
"Although this model is by far the most ecological, I still preferred the previous one."
Stress on Auxiliary Verbs (do/did/does): Add emphasis by using the auxiliary verb.
"I absolutely hated my last car. However, I did manage to sell it for a good price."
Starting Phrases (Without a doubt, How on earth): Emphasis at the beginning surprises the listener.
"Without a doubt our lives today are easier, but some say they are more dangerous."

Practice: Make These Sentences More Emphatic

Original: "I don't like smartphones, but I use them all the time."

"I absolutely don't like smartphones, but I do use them all the time."
OR
"I don't like smartphones at all, but I definitely use them constantly."

Original: "This technology is important, but it has problems."

"This technology is by far the most important, but it does have serious problems."
OR
"Without a doubt, this technology is crucial, yet it absolutely has significant drawbacks."

Vocabulary: Describing Technology

Key Adjectives for Technology

Innovative: New and original in design or function
The smartphone was an innovative device that changed communication forever.
Efficient: Working well without waste; producing results quickly
Modern solar panels are much more efficient than older models.
Beneficial: Helpful and providing advantage or improvement
The internet has been beneficial to education and business.
Convenient: Suitable; fitting in well with your plans
Online shopping is convenient because you can order from home.
Safe: Secure and without risk of harm
Modern aircraft are incredibly safe due to technology.
Productive: Successful in producing results; accomplishing much
Working with a computer makes people more productive than working by hand.
Progressive: Moving forward; supporting development and improvement
Progressive companies invest in research and new technology.
Comfortable: Physically or mentally at ease; allowing ease and relaxation
Air conditioning makes modern cars more comfortable during long journeys.

Verbs Related to Technology

Invent: To create something completely new
Alexander Graham Bell invented the telephone.
Develop: To improve or create gradually
Companies develop new features based on customer feedback.
Transform: To completely change the character of something
The internet transformed how we work and communicate.
Revolutionize: To completely change something in a dramatic way
Smartphones revolutionized personal communication.

Your Presentation: A Great Achievement

Now you'll prepare a short presentation on a 20th century technological achievement. Choose one of these technologies or pick your own:

📝 Planning Your Presentation

  • What is it? Briefly describe the technology and when it was invented
  • Life before: What was life like without this technology? Give examples
  • Life after: How is life better with this technology? What changed?
  • Impact: What is its most important contribution to society?
  • Conclusion: Why do you think it's a great achievement?
  • Language: Remember to use emphasis! Use adverbs and strong adjectives

Structure for Your Talk

Introduction: "I'm going to tell you about [technology]. This is definitely one of the greatest achievements of the 20th century."
Life Without: "Without [technology], life was far more difficult. People had to... and this was very..."
Life With: "The invention of [technology] absolutely transformed everything. It made... much more convenient and efficient."
Conclusion: "Without a doubt, [technology] has been incredibly beneficial to humanity. By far, it's one of the most important innovations."
