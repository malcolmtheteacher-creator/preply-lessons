The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The hiding prince

After losing the Battle of Worcester in 1651, the young Charles hid in an oak tree at Boscobel to escape capture, then fled to France. In 1660 he was invited back. The Restoration brought theatres reopening and celebrations.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did Charles hide in 1651?

2. What did the Restoration bring back?

1. In an oak tree at Boscobel.

2. The monarchy, theatres and celebrations.

Discuss:
  • Why would people welcome back a king?

Part 2 — Plague and fire

In 1665 the Great Plague killed about 100,000 Londoners. In September 1666 the Great Fire started in a bakery on Pudding Lane and burned for four days, destroying St Paul's and over 13,000 houses. Christopher Wren designed a new St Paul's and many churches.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did the Great Fire start?

2. Who designed the new St Paul's?

1. In a bakery on Pudding Lane.

2. Christopher Wren.

Discuss:
  • Can disasters lead to improvement?

Part 3 — Science and secrets

Charles supported science, granting a charter to the Royal Society in 1662. He had many mistresses and children, but no legitimate heir. He secretly promised Louis XIV to become Catholic, and he converted on his deathbed in 1685.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did Charles support?

2. What secret did he keep?

1. The Royal Society.

2. His promise to become Catholic.

Discuss:
  • Should rulers keep secrets?

Grammar — Used to & Would — Past Habits

To describe how life used to be — the habits and customs of a reign — English uses used to and would.

How it works:
• used to + verb for past habits and states: "People used to believe the king could heal."
• would + verb for repeated actions only (not states): "Every Christmas he would hold court."
• Negative: didn't use to.

1. used to or would?

Complete each sentence about Charles II. Use the word in brackets.

1. Londoners (go) to the reopened theatres.

2. Before the fire, houses (be) made of wood.

3. People (gather) in coffee houses.

1. used to go / would go

2. used to be

3. would gather / used to gather

2. Complete with the correct form

1. Charles (walk) his dogs in St James's Park.

2. Under Cromwell, Christmas (be) discouraged.

3. Scientists (meet) at the Royal Society.

1. used to walk / would walk

2. used to be

3. would meet

Going deeper

The Restoration was a period of fun and science after Puritan strictness. Disasters reshaped London, and the rebuilt city became more brick and stone.

Worth noticing: King Charles spaniels are named after him.
Learning from the past — for today: Rebuild better. After the fire, London was rebuilt in stone. After a setback, don't just restore — improve.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Was Charles a good king?
  • How can a disaster make things better?
  • What would you rebuild better?
