At the Train Station

You need to catch a train today. Buy your ticket, find the right platform — and stay calm and friendly if something goes wrong.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is a busy morning at a big train station. There are a lot of people. You need to go to a town called Durham. A friend is waiting for you there.

First, you need three things: a ticket, the right platform, and the right time. That sounds easy. You go to the ticket desk and you wait in the queue.

But there is a small problem coming. Your train is cancelled — it will not go today. Do not worry! You can stay calm, ask for help, and still get to Durham. The person at the desk is kind and wants to help you.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like trains? Do you take the train, the bus, or the car? Why?
  2. When a train or bus is late, how do you feel? Do you stay calm, or do you get angry?

Who's who

You
The Traveller 🎒

You want to go to Durham today. You are polite and friendly. When there is a problem, you stay calm and ask for help.

The other person
The Ticket Clerk 🎫

They work at the ticket desk. They know the trains, the platforms and the prices — and they help nice, polite people the most.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the polite words: please, thank you, can I. And watch how the traveller stays calm when the train is cancelled. That is the important part.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the polite words, and for the calm voice when the news is bad.

ClerkGood morning! Where do you want to go today?
YouGood morning. Can I have a ticket to Durham, please?
ClerkA single or a return?
YouA single, please. How much is it, and which platform?
ClerkForty-two pounds. It goes from platform four, at 10:15. You have twenty minutes.
YouThank you. Here is my card. Is the train on time?
ClerkLet me see… Oh, I am so sorry. The train is cancelled. There is a problem on the line.
YouOh no. What can I do now? Is there another train to Durham?
ClerkYes, there is. Take the 10:40 to York. Then change trains, and the next train goes to Durham.
YouSo I change at York? And is my ticket OK for this?
ClerkYes, your ticket is fine. You do not pay more money.
YouGood. But the train is late now — can I get my money back?
ClerkYes, you can. Fill in this form for a refund. Here you are.
YouThank you very much for your help. You are very kind.

From memory — don't look up!

1. What is the problem? Why is the train cancelled?

2. The first train is cancelled. How can the traveller still get to Durham today?

3. The train is late now. What can the traveller get, and how?

1. The train is cancelled because there is a problem on the line.
2. The traveller takes a different train (the 10:40) to York, then changes trains. The next train goes to Durham. The ticket is fine — no extra money.
3. The traveller can get some money back (a refund). They fill in a form to get it.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use at any station. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Stay calm and be nice. When something goes wrong, take a breath. A calm, polite voice helps a lot. The word please and a smile make the other person want to help you. Short sentences are perfect.

🎫 Buying a ticket

Phrase 1

You are at the desk. You want to buy a ticket to Durham. Complete it: "Can I have a ___ to Durham, please?"

"Can I have a ticket to Durham, please?"
"Can I have…, please?" is an easy, polite way to buy things: "Can I have a coffee, please?", "Can I have the bill, please?"
Phrase 2

You want one ticket for going, not coming back (that is a "single"). Which sentence is polite?

A. "Give me a single."
B. "A single to Durham, please."
C. "I want a single ticket now."
B — "A single to Durham, please."
"Please" makes it polite. A "single" is a one-way ticket; a "return" is there and back. A and C sound like an order, not a nice request.

🚉 Finding the platform

Phrase 3

You do not know where your train goes from. You want to ask. Complete it: "Which ___ is it, please?"

"Which platform is it, please?"
The "platform" is the place where you get on the train. You can also ask: "Which platform for Durham?"
Phrase 4

You want to know if the train will leave at the right time. Put the words in order:

isthe trainon time?
"Is the train on time?"
"On time" means "not late". This is a very useful question at stations and airports: "Is the bus on time?", "Is the flight on time?"

🌧️ When there is a problem

Phrase 5

The clerk says your train is cancelled. You are not happy, but you stay calm. Which sentence is calm and polite?

A. "This is terrible!"
B. "What can I do now?"
C. "This is your fault."
B — "What can I do now?"
This asks the clerk to help you find a way. It is calm and kind. A and C are angry — and the clerk did not break the train, so it is not fair to be angry with them.
Phrase 6

You still want to get to Durham today. You ask about a different train. Complete it: "Is there ___ train to Durham?"

"Is there another train to Durham?"
"Another" means "one more" or "a different one". This is great when your first plan is gone: "Is there another bus?", "Is there another way?"

💛 Sorting it out and saying thank you

Phrase 7

The clerk gives you a plan: take one train, then change. You want to check you understand. Put the words in order:

soI changeat York?
"So I change at York?"
Saying the plan back in your own words checks it is right, before you make a mistake. "Change" means get off one train and get on another. You can also say: "So I take the 10:40, then change?"
Phrase 8

The clerk helped you a lot. You want to say a big, warm thank you. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you very much for your help. You are very kind."
"Thank you for your help" tells the person you noticed what they did. Add "very much" to make it bigger and warmer — and a smile helps too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work at stations, bus stops, shops — everywhere. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"Can I have ___, please?" — What do you buy often? (a coffee, a bus ticket, some bread…)

"Is the ___ on time?" — Think of something you wait for. What is it?

"Is there another ___?" — When your first plan is gone, what do you ask for? (train, bus, shop…)

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — buy the ticket → ask the platform → hear the bad news → stay calm → find another train → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your station. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the traveller 🎒

The clerk speaks — you answer. Be polite: use please, thank you, and can I. And stay calm when the news is bad!

ClerkGood morning! Where do you want to go today?
ClerkA single or a return?
ClerkForty-two pounds. You can pay by card here.
ClerkPlatform four, at 10:15. You have twenty minutes.
ClerkOh… I am so sorry. The train is cancelled. There is a problem on the line.
ClerkYes — take the 10:40 to York, then change trains for Durham.
ClerkYour ticket is fine, no extra money. Anything else?

Round 2 — now YOU are the ticket clerk 🎫

Change places. You work at the desk. Be friendly and helpful. Say "Good morning", "Of course", "I am sorry", "Here you are".

TravellerGood morning. Can I have a ticket to Durham, please?
TravellerA single, please. How much is it?
TravellerThank you. Is the train on time?
TravellerOh no. Is there another train to Durham?
TravellerThank you. Can I get my money back?
The Twist

🚃 The train is full

One more time — no help this round. You get on the 10:40 to York, but the train is very full. There are no free seats. You do not want to stand for one hour. What do you say? Ask a person by the window, politely: "Excuse me, is this seat free?" Or ask a train worker for help. Stay calm and friendly. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I asked for my ticket politely, with "please".
  • I asked which platform, and if the train was on time.
  • I stayed calm when the train was cancelled.
  • I asked about another train and about my money back.
  • I said "thank you" warmly at the end.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

In 1914, there was a big war. Many young soldiers took trains to go and fight. Everyone said, "It will be a short war. You will be home for Christmas."

But the war was not short. At Christmas, the soldiers were cold and far from home. Then something amazing happened. On Christmas Day, the guns stopped. Soldiers from both sides came out and said hello. They shared food. They even played football together.

A cancelled train is a small problem. You wait, you talk to people, and you find another way. In 1914, for one day, enemies waited together too — and they found that they liked each other.

Two questions to talk about

  1. Sometimes a problem — a late train, a long wait — makes you talk to a new person. Has this happened to you? What happened?
  2. When something goes wrong, some people get angry and some stay calm. The soldiers chose to be friendly. Is it better to be angry or calm? Why?
