Fluency First

On this test, never stopping matters more than being perfect. A speaker who keeps going with a small mistake scores higher than a speaker who freezes to search for the “right” word. Your main job in every speaking task is simple: keep the sound coming.
Three habits that build fluency:
1Speak in short chunks. Instead of building one long, perfect sentence in your head, say a short piece as soon as it's ready — then add the next piece.
2Link your ideas. Join chunks with simple connectors — and, but, because, so, then — so your speech flows as one continuous stream, not separate sentences with gaps between them.
3Avoid long silent pauses. A short pause to breathe is fine. A long silent pause while you search for a word is what costs you points — fill it with sound instead.
Your buy-time phrases — use these the instant you feel a gap coming, so you never go silent:
  • “Well…”
  • “That's a good question…”
  • “Let me think…”
  • “What I mean is…”
Warm up (60 seconds)

Talk about your morning today — what you did, in order — without stopping until the timer ends. If you feel stuck, use one of the buy-time phrases above and keep going.

Read each sentence aloud clearly and at a natural, steady pace — not too fast, not word-by-word. Focus on sounding like a real conversation, not a robot reading a list.

Fluency lap (45 seconds)

Read all six sentences aloud, one after another, without stopping between them. If you trip on a word, keep going — don't go back and restart.

00:45
Common trip points in these sentences:
  • Could you turn — the words link together smoothly: “could-you-turn,” not four separate words.
  • bakery / hospital — stress the first syllable: BAK-er-y, HOS-pi-tal.
  • She's been working — say the contraction as one sound, “she'z,” not “she is.”
  • -ing / -ed endings — don't drop them: working, planning need the final sound.
  • if the weather — the small words (if, the) are said quickly and lightly; don't stress them.

Describe the Photo

Use the frame from earlier lessons: general → details → a guess. Start with what the picture shows overall, add specific details, then guess something about the situation or the people.

First, write it (2 minutes)

Now say it out loud (90 seconds)

Look at the photo for a moment, then speak for the full 90 seconds without stopping.

A few pigeons are walking on the ground in front of the bench. It feels warm, peaceful and full of affection — they have probably been together for many years.”

Interactive Listening

A short phone call. Your teacher reads the other person's lines aloud; for each one, choose the reply that is polite, clear and moves the conversation forward. Then sum up the whole call in your own words.
The situation: you are calling a dental clinic to change the time of an appointment.
Receptionist: “Hello, this is Bright Smile Dental. How may I assist you today?”
Your best reply:
Receptionist: “No problem at all. What day were you originally booked for?”

Now sum it up (60 seconds)

In your own words, say what the phone call was about, what you asked for, and how it ended. Full sentences, no stopping.

Keep Improving

Fluency is a habit, not a talent — it builds with practice. Use this check after any speaking practice, including everything in this lesson.

Speaking self-check

  • I kept talking for the full time, without long silent pauses.
  • When I got stuck, I used a buy-time phrase instead of going silent.
  • I linked my ideas with words like and, but, because, so.
  • I gave my opinion early, then supported it with reasons and an example.
  • My pace was steady — not rushed, not word-by-word.

I can…

  • I can keep speaking for 60–90 seconds without stopping, even if I make small mistakes.
  • I can use a buy-time phrase the moment I feel a pause coming, instead of freezing.
  • I can describe a photo using general → details → a guess.
  • I can answer a topic question with my opinion, two reasons and an example.
  • I can hold a short, polite phone-style conversation and choose replies that move it forward.
Fluency wins more points than perfection. Keep the sound coming, use your buy-time phrases, and never freeze into silence.
