# Think Inside the Box — isometric diorama images

One picture per tool, used as a memory palace: the student walks through rooms instead of reading a diagram.
Saved at the repo root as `tib_<tool>_diorama.jpg`. The lesson hides the figure automatically if the file is missing,
so a lesson never shows a broken image while a picture is still being made.

## House rules
- **Always ask for the labels you want.** Never write "no text" or "without words" in a prompt — that clause has
  gutted images before. Name the exact words that should appear.
- Isometric cutaway building, miniature-model feel, soft warm light, muted teal and cream (matches the lesson CSS).
- One small human figure, repeated in each room, so the eye follows a route.
- 16:9, and readable when it is 700px wide on a laptop.
- The room that carries the difficult idea gets the most detail. Everything else stays plain.

## OODA — `tib_ooda_diorama.jpg`

> A charming isometric diorama of a small cutaway building with four connected rooms arranged in a circle, in the
> style of a detailed miniature model, soft warm lighting, muted teal and cream palette, clean vector-like 3D
> render, 16:9.
>
> Room 1, labelled OBSERVE: a room of windows and telescopes, a tiny figure looking out at weather and traffic outside.
> Room 2, labelled ORIENT: a map room, walls covered with charts and mirrors, a large table holding a model of this
> same building; four doors lead out of this room, two standing open with warm light behind them and two closed, and
> a small sign above them reads WHAT IS POSSIBLE NOW.
> Room 3, labelled DECIDE: a room with a single lever and three paths painted on the floor, the figure choosing one.
> Room 4, labelled ACT: an open doorway onto a street, the figure stepping out.
>
> A glowing arrow-path runs from room 1 to room 2 to room 3 to room 4 and loops back to room 1, showing the cycle.
> Include the four room labels clearly in a clean sans-serif, and a small title at the bottom reading "The Loop".

## How the picture is used in the lesson
Before the loop is explained: describe the building — *in the second room there are…*, *behind the open door you can
see…* (prepositions, present simple). After: cover the page and retell the four rooms from memory.

## Reusing the pattern
Same prompt shape, one room per stage, the hard idea in the most detailed room:
- SWOT — a house with four rooms, two lit from inside and two with windows onto the street.
- Eisenhower — a sorting office with four chutes, one of them a furnace.
- Five Whys — a staircase going down into a cellar, each step a question on the riser.
