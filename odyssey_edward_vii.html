The words for today's story

1. He was heir for nearly sixty years.

2. He had to wait a long time.

3. He was skilled at diplomacy.

5. He was called "the Peacemaker".

6. He was involved in several scandals.

7. His coronation was delayed.
a coronation (n) — the crowning ceremony.
8. He had appendicitis.
appendicitis (n) — inflammation of the appendix.
9. The Edwardian era followed.

Grammar — Past Perfect & Sequencing

History is a chain of causes. To show that one thing happened before another in the past, English uses the past perfect.

How it works:
• Past simple tells the main line: "The Danes attacked at Christmas."
• Past perfect (had + past participle) reaches further back: "By then, Alfred had already escaped into the marshes."
• Signal words: by the time, already, before, after, when, until.

1. Past simple or past perfect?

Complete each sentence about Edward VII. Use the word in brackets.

1. Before he became king, he (wait) almost sixty years.

2. His mother (keep) him from serious work.

3. By 1902 he (have) an operation.

1. had waited

2. had kept

3. had had

2. Put the earlier event in the past perfect

1. Before the Entente, he (visit) Paris.

2. When he died, Britain and France (become) friends.

3. He (enjoy) travel since his youth.

1. had visited

2. had become

3. had enjoyed

Going deeper

Edward VII's charm helped Britain's diplomacy. The Edwardian era is remembered as a golden afternoon before World War I.

Worth noticing: his reign begins the dynasty later renamed Windsor in 1917, which is why he appears here.
Learning from the past — for today: Relationships matter in diplomacy and business. Charm, visits and respect can open doors that arguments can't.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Did charm make a difference?
  • Was it good to wait?
  • Who could you build a relationship with?
