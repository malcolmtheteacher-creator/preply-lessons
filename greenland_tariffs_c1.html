Why Does the US Want Greenland?

Strategic Location

Greenland sits between North America and Europe, making it crucial for Arctic monitoring and missile defense systems. The US already has a military base there (Pituffik Space Base).

Critical Minerals

Greenland has massive deposits of rare earth elements needed for electric vehicles, wind turbines, and military equipment. Currently, China controls 90% of global rare earth processing.

Climate Change

As Arctic ice melts, new shipping routes and resources become accessible. Whoever controls Greenland controls access to these new opportunities.

Language Focus: Part A

These expressions will help you discuss economic pressure, alliances, and international disputes with precision. Click each card to reveal the details.

to impose tariffs (on) Click to reveal
Meaning To officially require a tax on imported goods, typically as an economic or political pressure tactic
Form impose + tariffs + on + country/goods | Often in passive: "Tariffs were imposed on..."
Register Formal, news/political language. Common collocations: impose tariffs, lift tariffs, raise tariffs, retaliatory tariffs

In Context

"Trump said the US will impose tariffs on eight European nations starting February 1."

"The EU warned it would respond if tariffs were imposed on member states."

Now You Try

Complete this sentence about a hypothetical situation: "If my government tried to impose tariffs on _________________, I think..."

to stand in solidarity with Click to reveal
Meaning To publicly support someone or a group, especially during difficult times or when they face opposition
Form stand in solidarity with + person/group/country | Also: "show solidarity," "express solidarity"
Register Formal to neutral. Often used in political speeches, union contexts, and international diplomacy. Implies moral support, not just practical help.

In Context

"The EU stands in solidarity with Denmark and the people of Greenland."

"Protesters gathered in Copenhagen to stand in solidarity with Greenlanders."

Now You Try

Think of a time when a group of people needed support. Who should have stood in solidarity with them, and why?

an absolute necessity Click to reveal
Meaning Something that is completely essential and cannot be compromised on; non-negotiable
Form X is an absolute necessity (for Y) | Often: "an absolute necessity," "absolutely necessary," "a matter of necessity"
Register Formal and emphatic. Common in political rhetoric to justify controversial actions. Can sound exaggerated in casual speech.

In Context

"Trump called US ownership of Greenland 'an absolute necessity' for national security."

"Climate action is an absolute necessity, not a luxury we can delay."

Now You Try

What do you consider an absolute necessity for a healthy democracy? Explain your reasoning.

Language Focus: Part B

Two more expressions for discussing conflict, criticism, and consequences.

to hit back at Click to reveal
Meaning To respond forcefully to criticism, an attack, or negative action; to retaliate verbally or through action
Form hit back at + person/criticism/accusation | Also: "hit back with [response]," "hit back by [doing something]"
Register Neutral to informal. Very common in news headlines. Implies a strong, defensive response. Not physical violence in political contexts.

In Context

"European leaders hit back at Trump's tariff threats, calling them 'unacceptable.'"

"The company hit back at critics by releasing the full report."

Now You Try

Describe a situation where someone needed to hit back at unfair criticism. How did they do it?

at stake Click to reveal
Meaning At risk; in a position to be lost or gained depending on what happens; in jeopardy
Form X is at stake | Also: "what's at stake," "with [something] at stake," "there's a lot at stake"
Register Neutral, works in formal and informal contexts. Creates urgency. Common collocations: lives at stake, jobs at stake, future at stake, everything at stake

In Context

"Trump wrote that 'World Peace is at stake!' in his social media post."

"With the NATO alliance at stake, European leaders called for unity."

"Danish protesters said: 'There's a lot at stake - our identity, our culture, our future.'"

Now You Try

From the Greenlandic perspective, what do you think is really at stake in this situation?

Quick Reference: All 5 Expressions

1 impose tariffs on - to require taxes on imports as economic pressure
2 stand in solidarity with - to publicly support someone facing opposition
3 an absolute necessity - something completely essential, non-negotiable
4 hit back at - to respond forcefully to criticism or attack
5 at stake - at risk, in jeopardy
