Recall: Key Terms Vocabulary

Click each term to reveal its definition.

Complementarian
A view that men and women have equal worth but different, divinely ordained roles.
Egalitarian
A view that gender should not determine roles; all should have equal opportunity and authority.
Sacrament
A sacred ritual act believed to carry divine grace (e.g., Christian marriage).
Nikah
Islamic marriage contract.
Mahr
A gift from groom to bride in Islam, symbolising commitment.
Kiddushin
Jewish marriage ceremony in which the groom gives something of value to the bride.
Talaq
Islamic divorce.
Iddah
Waiting period after Islamic divorce, typically 3 months, for reflection and pregnancy determination.
Get
Jewish divorce document.
Celibacy
Choosing not to marry or have sexual relations.
Humanae Vitae
1968 papal encyclical reaffirming Catholic opposition to artificial contraception.
Qawwamun
Arabic term meaning "protectors and providers"; describes men's role in Qur'an 4:34.
Ketubah
Jewish marriage contract documenting the groom's obligations.
Pronatalist
A tradition or view that values having many children (from the Jewish mitzvah "be fruitful and multiply").
Annulment
A declaration (in Catholic and civil law) that a marriage was never valid.

Review: Key Questions

  1. Explain the difference between complementarian and egalitarian views of gender roles in religion.
  2. Compare Christian, Islamic, Jewish, and humanist teachings on marriage.
  3. Why do religious traditions generally discourage or prohibit divorce? Name at least three reasons.
  4. Describe the range of Christian views on same-sex relationships, from most conservative to most progressive.
  5. How do Catholic, Islamic, Jewish, and humanist perspectives on contraception differ?

Model answers:

  1. Complementarian: Equal but different roles (men lead, women support). Egalitarian: Gender shouldn't determine roles; equality in authority and opportunity.
  2. Christian: Covenant/sacrament. Islamic: Contract (nikah) with mahr. Jewish: Covenant with ketubah. Humanist: Personal commitment based on love.
  3. Marriage is seen as sacred/covenant; procreation; reconciliation valued. Divorce causes family breakdown and suffering; commitment is sacred.
  4. Conservative: Condemned in scripture. Moderate: Pastoral care but traditional teaching. Progressive: Blessed unions. Quaker: Fully affirmed since 1963.
  5. Catholic: Forbidden (Humanae Vitae). Islamic: Generally permitted. Jewish: Accepted, especially for health. Humanist: Personal right; improves women's lives.
