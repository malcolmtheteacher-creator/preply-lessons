2

Phrases for the meeting

Four kinds of thing you'll need to say. Read them aloud once.

Telling your partner

  • According to her reference, …
  • It says here that …
  • Apparently, …
  • The interview notes mention that …
  • There's nothing in my file about …

Asking for more

  • Is there anything in your file about …?
  • What does it say about …?
  • Does anything worry you about …?
  • Sorry, did you say …?

Weighing it up

  • That's a real plus.
  • That's a bit of a red flag.
  • On paper, she looks strong, but …
  • I'd be slightly worried that …
  • His strengths outweigh the drawbacks.

Agreeing and giving way

  • Fair point.
  • I take your point, but …
  • OK, you've convinced me.
  • I could live with that.
  • I'm still not sure about …

Listen to how it sounds

ASo, about Sam. It says here that he trained six new staff in his last job. BThat's a real plus. Is there anything in your file about his timekeeping? ANothing at all. Why, does something worry you? BApparently, he was late for two of his last three shifts. That's a bit of a red flag. AFair point. But I'd say his experience outweighs that. BI take your point, but I'm still not sure about him.

Which kind of phrase is it?

These are new phrases. Say which group each one belongs to.

Make it sound professional

Say each one again, the way you would in a real meeting. Then compare.

Key words

candidate (noun) — a person who has applied for a job. Example: "We interviewed three candidates on Friday."
shortlist (verb) — choose a small group from everyone who applied. Example: "We shortlisted four people out of sixty."
reference (noun) — a letter from a former employer saying what someone was like at work. Example: "Her reference said she was never late."
reliable (adjective) — you can trust them to do what they say, every time. Example: "We need someone reliable for the early shifts."
drawback (noun) — a disadvantage or problem. Example: "The only drawback is that he can't start until March."
red flag (noun) — a warning sign that something could go wrong. Example: "Speaking badly about a former boss is a red flag for me."
outweigh (verb) — be more important than something else. Example: "Her experience outweighs her nerves in the interview."
concede (verb) — admit that the other person is right, often after arguing. Example: "In the end, I had to concede that she was the better choice."
