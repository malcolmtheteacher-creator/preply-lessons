Marie Antoinette

A young queen who lost everything.

Today: tell the story of Marie Antoinette, a young queen of France. Grammar: "if" sentences with would.

Key words — match them first

Before we read, let's learn the important words. Match each word (1–8) to its meaning (A–H). Say your answers out loud — "I think 1 is D" — then check.

Words

  1. queen
  2. king
  3. palace
  4. rich
  5. to spend (money)
  6. poor
  7. revolution
  8. to blame

Meanings

  • A. the most important man in a country, long ago
  • B. to use money to buy things
  • C. having very little money
  • D. the most important woman in a country, long ago
  • E. a very big, beautiful house for a king or queen
  • F. to say that someone did something bad; to say it is their fault
  • G. having a lot of money
  • H. when the people change the government, often with anger

1 = D (queen) · 2 = A (king) · 3 = E (palace) · 4 = G (rich)

5 = B (to spend) · 6 = C (poor) · 7 = H (revolution) · 8 = F (to blame)

Now talk — out loud

Before we read:
  • Which words are about money? Which word is about anger and change?
  • Use three of the words to guess: what do you think happens in this story?

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A young queen

Marie Antoinette was born in Austria in 1755. When she was only 14 years old, she married a French prince. She was very young, and she went to live in a new country.

Later, the prince became King Louis XVI. So Marie Antoinette became the Queen of France. She lived in a huge, beautiful palace called Versailles.

Marie Antoinette loved expensive clothes. She loved big parties. And she loved spending money. She was rich, and her life looked wonderful.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where was Marie Antoinette born, and how old was she when she married?

2. What did she love?

1. She was born in Austria. She was only 14 years old when she married a French prince.

2. She loved expensive clothes, big parties, and spending money.

Talk about it:
  • Marie Antoinette married at 14 and moved to a new country. Was this a good life or a difficult life? Why?

Part 2 — The people are angry

The French people did not like Marie Antoinette. She was from another country, and they thought she did not care about them. They watched her spend a lot of money, and they were angry.

There is one famous story. People say she said "Let them eat cake" when she heard the poor people had no bread. But this is probably not true. She never really said it. It is only a story.

At this time, France was poor. Many people were hungry. They had no money and no bread. And they blamed the queen for their problems.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did the French people not like the queen?

2. Did Marie Antoinette really say "Let them eat cake"?

1. She was from another country, they thought she did not care about them, and she spent a lot of money while the people were poor and hungry.

2. No. This is probably not true — she never really said it. It is only a story.

Talk about it:
  • People still tell the "cake" story today, but it is not true. Why do people like to tell stories like this?

Part 3 — The end

In 1789, the French Revolution began. The people were tired and angry. They wanted to change their country.

The people removed the king and the queen from power. Marie Antoinette was not a queen any more. She lost her palace, her money and her beautiful life.

In 1793, Marie Antoinette was killed. The young queen from Austria lost everything in the end. Today, many people still know her name and her story.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened in 1789?

2. What happened to Marie Antoinette in the end?

1. The French Revolution began. The people wanted to change their country.

2. The people removed the king and queen from power. In 1793, Marie Antoinette was killed. She lost everything.

Talk about it:
  • Marie Antoinette had a wonderful life, and then she lost everything. What is the most surprising part of her story for you?

Grammar: "if" sentences with would

We use these sentences to imagine things that are not real now.

How it works: If + past simple, … would + verb.
Example: "If I were a queen, I would live in a big palace."
Example: "If I had a lot of money, I would buy a new house."
We use this to talk about an imaginary situation — something that is not true right now.

1. Complete the sentences

1. If you were a king or queen, what you (do)?

2. If I (have) a lot of money, I would buy a big house.

3. If she (be) here now, she (be) happy.

1. would … do ("what would you do?")

2. had

3. were (or: was) / would be

2. Finish the sentence — your own ideas

Complete these sentences in your own way. Then read them out loud.

1. If I had a lot of money, I would…

2. If I lived in a big palace, I would…

3. If I were a king or queen for one day, I would…

1. "If I had a lot of money, I would travel the world."

2. "If I lived in a big palace, I would have a big party every day."

3. "If I were a king for one day, I would help poor people."

Your ideas can be different — that is fine! Just use "if" + past, and "would" + verb.

Task A · Tell the story (3 minutes)

Tell the story of Marie Antoinette in your own words. Use these steps to help you: born in Austria → married a prince at 14 → became Queen of France → lived in a big palace → loved spending money → the people were poor and angry → the revolution → she lost everything.

Try to use some key words (queen, king, palace, rich, poor, revolution, to blame). You can also use one "if" sentence: "If she had cared about the people, they would have liked her."

Task B · Is it fair? (3 minutes)

The people blamed the queen for everything. Is it fair to blame one person for big problems? Why or why not? Tell me what you think.

Task C · A lot of money (3 minutes)

If you had a lot of money, what would you do with it? Talk for as long as you can. Use "I would…" many times.

Not sure what to talk about? Choose one:

  • "I would buy a big house with a garden and a swimming pool."
  • "I would give money to my family and to a charity I like."
  • "I would travel the world and see Paris, like Marie Antoinette's palace."
  • "I would stop working and learn something new, like music or cooking."
  • "I would not spend it all — I would save some for the future."

To finish

  1. Marie Antoinette was very young when she became queen. Was her life easy or hard? Why?
  2. The people were poor and hungry. Do you think they were right to be angry with the queen?
  3. From memory: how do we make an "if" sentence with would? Give me one example.
