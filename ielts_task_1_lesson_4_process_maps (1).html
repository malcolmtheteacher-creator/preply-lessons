Why These Need Different Skills

Critical Difference: Process diagrams and maps have NO NUMBERS. You cannot use vocabulary from charts. Instead you need: PASSIVE VOICE, SEQUENCING LANGUAGE, and LOCATION PHRASES.
Data Charts (Line/Bar/Pie) Process Diagrams and Maps
Numbers and percentages NO numbers - describe steps or changes
Active voice: "Sales increased" Passive voice: "The beans are dried"
Comparison language Sequencing (First, Then, Finally)
Trend vocabulary Location vocabulary (north, adjacent to)
Good News: Process and map questions are less common (about 1 in 4 tests), and many students find them easier because there is no data to select from - you just describe what you see in order.

Step 5Oil separated

Why Passive Voice?

In processes, we do not know (or care) WHO does the action. We focus on WHAT happens.

WRONG: Active Voice

"Workers harvest the olives. Then they wash them. After that, a machine crushes them."

Problem: Focuses on WHO (workers, machine) when we should focus on WHAT happens to the olives.

CORRECT: Passive Voice

"The olives are harvested and then washed. Following this, they are crushed into a paste."

Passive Voice Formula: be + past participle
  • Present passive: is/are + past participle - "The olives are washed"
  • Use present tense for processes that are ALWAYS true

Step 1: Write the Overview

State how many stages and the start/end points. NO detailed description.

0 words

Model Overview:

"Overall, the diagram illustrates an eight-stage process for producing recycled paper, beginning with collection from recycling bins and ending with the cutting of dried sheets."

Step 2: Write the Body Paragraphs

Use passive voice + sequencing words. Group related stages.

0 words

Body Paragraph 1 (Collection and Preparation):

At the first stage, used paper is collected from recycling bins. Following this, it is sorted by type to separate different grades. The sorted paper then is shredded into small pieces before being mixed with water to create pulp.

Body Paragraph 2 (Processing and Finishing):

Once the pulp is formed, any ink is removed through a cleaning process. At this point, the pulp may be bleached if white paper is required. In the final stages, the cleaned pulp is rolled into thin sheets, which are then dried and cut to size.

Maps: Describing Change Over Time

Map Questions Usually Show:
  • The SAME place at TWO different times (e.g., 1990 vs 2020)
  • OR a proposed development plan (present vs future)

Tense for Maps:
  • Past maps (1990 vs 2020): Use PAST PASSIVE - "was built", "were removed"
  • Future plans (now vs proposed): Use FUTURE - "will be built", "is going to be removed"

Map Practice: Complete Answer

Using the Greenfield Village maps, write a complete answer.

Step 1: Write Overview

0 words

"Overall, Greenfield Village was transformed from a small rural settlement into a developed suburban area, with the northern natural areas being replaced by residential and commercial facilities."

Step 2: Write Body Paragraphs

Organize by: what was removed/replaced, then what was added.

0 words

Body Paragraph 1 (Removals and Major Changes):

The most significant changes occurred in the northern part of the village. The forest in the northwest was cleared and replaced by a car park. Similarly, the farmland in the northeast was developed into a large housing estate containing 150 homes, compared to the original 12 houses.

Body Paragraph 2 (New Facilities):

In the centre of the village, a shopping center was constructed, with a school built to the east of it. The river in the south remained unchanged, although a marina was added alongside it.

Key Takeaways

Process Diagrams - Remember:
  • Use PASSIVE VOICE throughout (is/are + past participle)
  • Use SEQUENCING words (First, Then, Following this, Finally)
  • Overview states: number of stages + start/end points
  • Group related stages into paragraphs
Maps - Remember:
  • Use PAST PASSIVE for historical maps (was built, were removed)
  • Use LOCATION phrases (in the north, adjacent to, opposite)
  • Overview states: main transformation
  • Organize by: removals/replacements, then additions
Common Mistakes to Avoid:
  • Using active voice: "Workers build" instead of "is built"
  • Forgetting location phrases in maps
  • Using chart vocabulary (increased, decreased) - there are no numbers!
  • Wrong tense: present for past maps, or past for future plans
