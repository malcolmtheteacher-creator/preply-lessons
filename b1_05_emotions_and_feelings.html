Language for Expressing Annoyance

Phrases to Express Annoyance

These are different ways to say you're annoyed or angry. Some are stronger than others:

Expressing Annoyance (Weak to Strong)

Mild: "It's a bit annoying..." / "That bothers me..."
Medium: I'm fed up with... / It gets on my nerves...
Strong: It drives me crazy! / It makes my blood boil! / I can't stand...
Very Strong: I've had enough of... / I'm absolutely furious!

I'm fed up with...
When to use: You're tired and annoyed about something that keeps happening

Example: "I'm fed up with him being late all the time!"
It drives me crazy!
When to use: Something really annoys you and makes you feel frustrated

Example: "When people interrupt me, it drives me crazy!"
It gets on my nerves
When to use: Something bothers you, especially if it happens repeatedly

Example: "Loud music at night really gets on my nerves."
It makes my blood boil
When to use: Something makes you very angry (idiomatic - very strong)

Example: "When people are rude, it makes my blood boil!"
I can't stand...
When to use: You absolutely hate something or someone

Example: "I can't stand waiting in long queues."
I've had enough of...
When to use: You've tolerated something for too long and can't take it anymore

Example: "I've had enough of these constant interruptions!"

Present Continuous for Expressing Annoyance

We use the present continuous (be + -ing) to talk about annoying habits - things someone does repeatedly that bother you:

How It Works

Subject + be + always/constantly + verb-ing + object

Key Point: "Always" or "constantly" with the present continuous shows that this is an annoying habit:

She's always taking my biscuits.
He's constantly interrupting me.
They're always leaving the kitchen messy.

More Examples by Person

Present Continuous Examples

I am: "I'm always forgetting where I put my keys."
You are: "You're constantly checking your phone during conversations!"
He/She/It is: "She's always complaining about everything."
We are: "We're always fighting about the same things."
They are: "They're constantly making noise at night."

Practice: Identify the Structure

Which sentences use present continuous to express annoyance?

1. "She's always talking loudly on the phone."

✓ Yes! "is always talking" = present continuous + always = annoying habit

2. "He's in the office right now."

✗ No. This is just present continuous describing location, not an annoying habit.

3. "They're constantly arriving late to meetings."

✓ Yes! "are constantly arriving" = present continuous + constantly = annoying habit

Share Your Annoying Situations

Think about annoying situations and prepare to discuss them:

Annoying Situation 1: Noisy Neighbors

You: "I'm really fed up with my neighbors. They're always playing loud music late at night!"
Friend: "Oh no! That must be terrible. What time does it usually happen?"
You: "Mostly after 11 pm. It drives me crazy because I need to wake up early for work."
Friend: "That's really annoying. Have you tried talking to them about it?"
  • Talk politely to your neighbors
  • Leave a polite note
  • Contact the building manager
  • Use earplugs or white noise
  • Check local noise regulations

Annoying Situation 2: Work Problems

You: "I can't stand the situation at work anymore. My colleague is constantly interrupting me during meetings!"
Friend: "That sounds frustrating. How often does it happen?"
You: "Every single meeting! It makes my blood boil because nobody takes me seriously."
Friend: "Have you mentioned it to them or to your manager?"
  • Speak privately with your colleague
  • Talk to your manager
  • Be more assertive in meetings
  • Suggest a meeting agenda
  • Use phrases like "Please let me finish"

Annoying Situation 3: Social Situations

You: "I'm fed up with my friend always being late. We've arranged to meet at 7 pm, but she never arrives before 7:45 pm!"
Friend: "Oh, that's annoying! How long has this been going on?"
You: "For years! She doesn't respect my time."
Friend: "Maybe you should talk to her about it. She might not realize it bothers you."
  • Have an honest conversation about punctuality
  • Agree on realistic meeting times
  • Suggest waiting at a café instead
  • Set a firm deadline and leave if they're late
  • Consider if the friendship is worth the frustration

It makes my blood boil! something makes you very angry (idiomatic)
I can't stand... you absolutely hate something or someone
I've had enough of... you've tolerated something for too long
