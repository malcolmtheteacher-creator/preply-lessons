Key words

elaborate (verb) — add more detail to what you have just said. Example: "She gave a short answer and then went on to elaborate with an example."
hesitation (noun) — a pause before you speak or continue. Example: "A short hesitation while you think about an idea is completely natural."
hedge (verb) — soften a statement so that it is not too strong or too certain. Example: "Good speakers hedge with phrases like 'to some extent'."
speculate (verb) — say what you think might happen or might be true, without being sure. Example: "The examiner asked him to speculate about cities in fifty years' time."
generalise (verb) — say something about a whole group, not one person. Example: "In Part 3 you need to generalise about people, not talk about yourself."
anecdote (noun) — a short personal story about something that happened. Example: "He began his long turn with an anecdote about a delayed train."
filler (noun) — a small word or sound used while you think. Example: "'Well' and 'I suppose' are more natural than a silent filler like 'erm'."
idiomatic (adjective) — sounding natural, the way fluent speakers really say things. Example: "'It's not my cup of tea' is an idiomatic way to say you do not like something."
