Assessment: Does God Exist?

YEAR 9 · UNIT 13: DOES GOD EXIST? · LESSON 6

What You'll Learn

Synthesize arguments for and against God's existence, and develop your own reasoned position on this fundamental philosophical question.

Define theism, atheism, and agnosticism.

Answer: Theism: belief that God exists. Atheism: belief that God does not exist. Agnosticism: belief that God's existence is unknown or unknowable.

What is the Design Argument?

Answer: The argument that the universe's complexity proves an intelligent designer (God) created it.

What is the Cosmological Argument?

Answer: The argument that the universe must have had a cause, and that cause is God.

What is religious experience as evidence for God?

Answer: The argument that people's direct encounters with God or the divine are evidence for God's existence.

State the Problem of Evil.

Answer: If God is all-powerful and all-good, why does evil and suffering exist?

How does evolution challenge the Design Argument?

Answer: Evolution shows that complexity in living things arose through natural selection, not requiring an intelligent designer.

What is the burden of proof argument?

Answer: The claim that extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence, so theists must provide strong proof of God's existence.

What is the humanist case against God?

Answer: Humanists argue that meaning, morality, and purpose come from human reason and relationships, not from God.

What did Freud argue about belief in God?

Answer: Freud saw belief in God as a psychological projection — a comforting illusion created to cope with an uncaring universe.

What is William James's contribution to understanding religious experience?

Answer: James identified four characteristics of genuine religious experiences: ineffability, noetic quality, transience, and passivity.

Unit Review: Does God Exist?

Lesson 1: Theism, atheism, and agnosticism are three different positions on God's existence. Different traditions (Christianity, Islam, Humanism) understand God differently.

Lesson 2: The Design Argument claims the universe's complexity proves an intelligent designer. Darwin's evolution theory, the Problem of Evil, and Hume's criticisms challenge it.

Lesson 3: The Cosmological Argument claims the universe must have had a cause (God). Critics question whether causation applies to the universe, or why God isn't similarly challenged.

Lesson 4: Religious experience — mystical, numinous, conversion experiences — is cited as evidence for God. William James provided criteria for evaluating them. Psychology offers naturalistic explanations.

Lesson 5: The strongest arguments against God include the Problem of Evil, scientific explanations for belief (Freud, Marx), and the burden of proof. Humanists argue for meaning without God.

The Big Question: What are the strongest arguments for and against God's existence? You must now decide for yourself which arguments you find most convincing.

Extended Response: Does God Exist?

Words: 0/300

Guidance

Your essay should include:

  • Introduction: State your position clearly (theist, atheist, or agnostic, and why)
  • Arguments: Discuss 2-3 specific arguments or pieces of evidence (from any side)
  • Strength: Explain why you found certain arguments convincing
  • Objections: Acknowledge the strongest challenge to your position
  • Conclusion: Restate your position and explain what further evidence, if any, might change your mind

Reflection: Evaluating the Arguments

The arguments for God's existence (Design, Cosmological, Religious Experience) remain philosophically interesting but contested. The arguments against God (Problem of Evil, burden of proof, scientific alternatives) are formidable but not universally accepted.

The question 'Does God exist?' remains one of the greatest questions in human thought. Brilliant minds disagree. Your task is to engage carefully with the best arguments on both sides and develop your own reasoned position.

Remember: This is not just an intellectual exercise. For many people, whether or not God exists shapes how they understand meaning, morality, relationships, and their place in the universe. Take the question seriously, but also with intellectual humility — recognizing that thoughtful, intelligent people disagree.
