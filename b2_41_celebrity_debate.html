Linking Words in Speaking

When we speak quickly, we link the end of one word with the start of the next. This helps us sound more natural and fluent.

Rule: Consonant + Vowel

When the final sound of one word is a consonant and the next word starts with a vowel sound, we link them together:

Think about it

Sounds like: "Think-a-bout-it" (all one smooth sound)

Examples of Linking

Try linking these phrases:

1. "Look at the job" - The final 'k' in 'look' links to the 'a' in 'at'

2. "Ask about it" - The 'k' in 'ask' links to 'a' in 'about'

3. "Decide on it" - The 'e' sound in 'decide' links to 'o' in 'on'

4. "Run away" - The 'n' in 'run' links to the 'a' in 'away'

A Funny Limerick to Practice

Limericks are short, funny poems. Listen to the rhythm and linking:

"There once was an actor called Perry
Who lived in a house by the ferry
He'd spend all his days
In acting and plays
And never was serious or very!"

Linking in the Limerick:

Notice how words flow together: "ac-tor-called", "house-by-the", "spend-all"

This smooth linking helps the poem sound fun and quick!

Practice Speaking

Read the limerick aloud and try to link the words smoothly. Don't pause between words—let them flow together naturally!

The Debate Question

Do you agree or disagree?

"Movie stars get paid too much for doing too little. They don't deserve to be paid the money they are."

Phrases to Justify Your Opinion

Expressing Agreement

"I completely agree..."
"That's a good point..."
"I'd say that's true..."
"You're right about..."

Expressing Disagreement

"I disagree with that..."
"I see your point, but..."
"Actually, I think..."
"That's not entirely true because..."

Arguments For and Against

If You AGREE (movie stars earn too much):

  • They work fewer hours than teachers, nurses, and engineers
  • Many important jobs pay much less
  • A few people earn millions while others struggle
  • They earn money from acting in films, not creating real value

If You DISAGREE (movie stars deserve their pay):

  • Entertainment makes billions in profits for studios
  • Stars work hard with long hours on set and for promotion
  • Only a few talented people can be "movie stars"—it's rare
  • Fans pay to see them; the money comes from entertainment value

Key Takeaways

In this lesson, you've learned to:

  • Justify your opinion with clear reasons
  • Challenge others' opinions respectfully
  • Link words smoothly when speaking
  • Use debate phrases to express agreement and disagreement

Remember for Future Discussions:

Good debaters don't just say "I disagree." They explain why and support their ideas with reasons. Listen to others, link your words smoothly, and speak with confidence!
