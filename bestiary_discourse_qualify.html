The QUALIFY Toolkit

Certainty is a trap. The most sophisticated speakers know when to be definitive and when to leave room to manoeuvre. These 5 expressions give you a spectrum — from "it's possible" to "here's how I see it unfolding."

Emu → Weasel → Lionfish → Wolverine → Walrus

Your QUALIFY Chain
Five tools for calibrating exactly how certain you are.
The Certainty Spectrum

These five move from open possibility to confident forecast: possible → hypothetical → probable → not ruled out → predicted. You choose the right level of commitment for the situation.

When to Use Each One
Emu — "It's entirely possible that..." The widest net. You're introducing a possibility without attaching probability. Use when you want to raise a scenario that others haven't considered, without overcommitting. Perfect for brainstorming, early-stage strategy, or when the data is incomplete.
Weasel — "Were that to happen..." The conditional. You're exploring a hypothetical consequence — if X, then Y. Use when scenario-planning, stress-testing proposals, or walking stakeholders through downstream effects. The subjunctive "were" signals sophistication.
Lionfish — "In all likelihood..." Confident but not absolute. You're expressing high probability while leaving a margin. Use when presenting forecasts, summarising risk assessments, or giving your professional read on a situation. Carries authority without hubris.
Wolverine — "I wouldn't rule out..." The strategic hedge. You're keeping an option alive that others want to close. Use when the group is converging too quickly, when you sense an overlooked risk, or when you want to preserve optionality for a future pivot.
Walrus — "The way I see it playing out is..." The forecast. You're committing to a narrative — this is how I think events will unfold. Use when you have enough data and experience to project forward confidently. It invites others to challenge your reading, which sharpens the analysis.
