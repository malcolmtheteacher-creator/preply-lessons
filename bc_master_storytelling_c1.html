Four Classic Storytelling Structures

Click each card to reveal what each structure involves.

MONOMYTH The "hero's journey" — a protagonist faces a challenge, overcomes it, and returns transformed. Ideal for showing the benefits of taking risks or change.
IN MEDIAS RES Begin in the middle of the action — at the most dramatic moment — then fill in the backstory. Grabs attention immediately and creates instant tension.
FALSE START Begin with an expected story, then disrupt it — revealing a twist or contradiction. Powerful for challenging assumptions or disrupting your audience's expectations.
SPARKLINES Contrast the world as it is with the world as it could be. Inspired by Martin Luther King's "I Have a Dream." Ideal for motivating teams or influencing company culture.

When can you use a story?

1. What's the best way to start a presentation when you're using a story?

Drop the audience straight into the story — start with a vivid moment or image before any context. Avoid starting with "Today I'm going to talk about..." or agenda slides.

2. Why is it powerful to return to the story at the end of the presentation?

It creates a sense of resolution and circularity. The audience feels a satisfying "arrival" — the story frames everything in between. It also makes the key message more memorable.

3. Can stories be used in all types of presentations?

Not always — data-heavy or technical presentations may need a lighter touch. However, even a brief anecdote or a single human example can make dry content more engaging and relatable.
