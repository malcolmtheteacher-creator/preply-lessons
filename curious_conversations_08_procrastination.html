⏰ Why We Procrastinate (And How to Stop)

Curious Conversations #8 | Advanced English Speaking Practice

🤔 Quick Question:

Right now, is there something you should be doing instead of this? A project you're avoiding? An email you haven't sent? A task that's been on your list for weeks?

95% of people admit to procrastinating regularly. 20% identify as "chronic procrastinators."

📊 The Procrastination Paradox

Dr. Piers Steel at the University of Calgary studied over 24,000 people across multiple countries and discovered something fascinating: procrastination has increased by 300% since 1978. We have more productivity tools than ever before—apps, reminders, time management systems—yet we procrastinate more than any previous generation.

Take Rachel, a freelance graphic designer from Toronto. She describes her typical morning: "I open my laptop at 9 AM to work on a client project due tomorrow. By 9:45, I've organized my desktop files, made a second coffee, checked three news sites, and reorganized my project folders—everything except the actual work. The deadline hasn't changed. The work hasn't gotten harder. But I've built an elaborate ritual of avoidance."

The average person wastes 218 minutes per day procrastinating—that's 55 full days per year.

🧠 Why Your Brain Chooses Now Over Later

Dr. Timothy Pychyl, a leading procrastination researcher at Carleton University, explains that procrastination isn't about poor time management—it's about mood management. When we face a task that makes us feel anxious, bored, or inadequate, our brain offers us an immediate escape: "Do it later. Do something easier now."

The problem? Our brain privileges present feelings over future consequences. The anxiety of starting that difficult task is felt NOW. The regret of not doing it is felt LATER. And later always loses.

Marcus, a PhD student from Amsterdam, perfectly captures this: "I have three months to write my thesis chapter. On day one, three months feels infinite. On day 89, I'm in absolute panic. But somehow, on day one, I convinced myself that cleaning my entire apartment was more urgent than writing a single paragraph."

⚡ The Hidden Cost

A study by Fuschia Sirois at the University of Sheffield found that chronic procrastinators have significantly higher levels of stress and lower wellbeing. They don't just waste time—they experience constant low-level anxiety about what they're not doing.

And here's the cruel irony: the task we're avoiding rarely takes as long as we fear. Research shows people overestimate task duration by 250% on average. That email you've been avoiding for a week? Probably takes 10 minutes. That report? Two focused hours, not two days.

People who procrastinate chronically earn $15,000 less per year on average and report 4x higher stress levels.

🎯 What Actually Works

Dr. Steel's research found the most effective strategy isn't willpower—it's shrinking the task. Instead of "write the report," it's "write one sentence." Instead of "start exercising," it's "put on running shoes." The goal is to make starting so small it feels ridiculous NOT to do it.

Elena, a software engineer from Barcelona, discovered this by accident: "I told myself I'd code for just two minutes. Just open the file. Once I was in the file, momentum took over. The starting was the entire problem."

💬 Sophisticated Expressions: Part 1

Click each expression to learn how to use it naturally. These are C1-C2 level expressions.

1. "fall into the trap of"
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: To be caught or deceived by a common mistake or pattern, especially one you didn't realize you were making. "Fall into the trap" suggests you walked into something harmful without noticing it.
Example: "Rachel fell into the trap of perfectionism—she convinced herself she needed the 'perfect' time to start her project, which of course never arrived. The trap was thinking she needed ideal conditions."
NOW YOU TRY: Describe a trap you've fallen into with procrastination. What pattern keeps catching you? How do you rationalize putting things off?
2. "relinquish control to"
Formality: Formal
Meaning: To give up your power or authority over something, allowing something else to take charge. "Relinquish" is a sophisticated verb meaning to voluntarily give up or surrender something.
Example: "When Marcus checked social media 'just for five minutes,' he relinquished control to the algorithm. He was no longer deciding how to spend his time—the endless feed was deciding for him."
NOW YOU TRY: When do you relinquish control to distractions or habits? What takes over when you should be working? How does it feel when you realize you've given up control?
3. "exacerbate the problem"
Formality: Formal
Meaning: To make a bad situation worse or more severe. "Exacerbate" is a precise, formal verb often used in academic or professional contexts to describe worsening conditions.
Example: "The cruel irony of procrastination is that avoiding the task actually exacerbates the problem. The report doesn't get easier with time—the deadline gets closer, the stress increases, and now you have less time to do the same amount of work."
NOW YOU TRY: Give an example of how procrastination has exacerbated a problem in your life. How did waiting make things worse? What additional problems did the delay create?

💬 Sophisticated Expressions: Part 2

Two more powerful expressions. Click to reveal.

4. "a slippery slope"
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: A situation where one small action leads to increasingly worse consequences, like sliding down a slope where it becomes harder and harder to stop. Once you start sliding, momentum takes over.
Example: "Putting off that task 'until tomorrow' is a slippery slope. Tomorrow becomes next week, next week becomes next month, and suddenly you're facing a mountain of neglected work that feels impossible to climb."
NOW YOU TRY: Describe a slippery slope you've experienced with procrastination. How did one small delay lead to bigger and bigger problems? At what point did you realize you were sliding?
5. "succumb to the temptation"
Formality: Neutral-Formal
Meaning: To give in to something attractive or desirable that you know you should resist. "Succumb" suggests a battle where you ultimately lose—you tried to resist but the temptation won.
Example: "Elena knew she should start her presentation, but she succumbed to the temptation of organizing her files instead. It felt productive, but it was just elaborate avoidance—busy work that let her feel like she was working without actually working."
NOW YOU TRY: What temptations do you succumb to when procrastinating? Netflix? Social media? Cleaning? How do you justify these distractions to yourself in the moment?

🗣️ Your Turn to Speak

Instructions: Select 2-3 topics that interest you. Discuss each one for 3-5 minutes, using the expressions you just learned.

📋 Your Selected Topics:

    ⏰ Your Procrastination Origin Story
    When did procrastination become a problem for you? Was there a specific moment or project? How has your relationship with procrastination evolved? Are you getting better or worse at it over time?
    🎭 The Performance of Productivity
    What "productive procrastination" do you engage in? Organizing? Cleaning? Making lists? How do you convince yourself that these activities are important? When do you admit you're just avoiding the real work?
    🧠 Your Procrastination Triggers
    What types of tasks make you procrastinate most? Boring ones? Difficult ones? Open-ended creative work? Important high-stakes projects? What is it specifically about these tasks that makes you want to avoid them?
    💪 What's Actually Worked
    Have you found any strategies that genuinely help you procrastinate less? Deadlines? Accountability partners? Breaking tasks down? What worked, what failed, and why? Be honest about what you've actually tried versus what you've thought about trying.
    🎯 The One You're Avoiding Right Now
    What are you procrastinating on RIGHT NOW? Not in general—what specific task is haunting you? Why haven't you started? What would need to happen for you to finally begin? What's the worst that could happen if you keep avoiding it?
    🔮 Life Without Procrastination
    Imagine you woke up tomorrow and procrastination was no longer part of your life. What would change? What would you accomplish? How would your stress levels differ? Would there be any downsides to never procrastinating?
