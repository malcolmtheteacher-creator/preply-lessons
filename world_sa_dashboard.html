South and Southeast Asia: Stereotype vs Reality

8 lessons on South and Southeast Asian cultures and differences — each one opens with a stereotype and finds out what is really true.

Curry, head wobbles, arranged marriage, Bollywood, the Land of Smiles: India, Thailand and their neighbours attract plenty of stereotypes, and two huge regions are often treated as one. Start with the Background lesson, then work through the topics. Each lesson asks three questions — what does the stereotype say, what is the reality, and why does the idea exist? — and finishes with practical tips for working, travelling and making friends across cultures.

📖 Background1

Read this first: who lives in South and Southeast Asia, and how the many cultures connect.

BackgroundSouth and Southeast Asia: Two Regions, Hundreds of Cultures
Background
Around 2.6 billion people live in these two regions, speaking hundreds of languages and following many religions. Spices, trade and monsoon rains connect them — but no single picture fits.
The stereotype: “South and Southeast Asia is just one exotic place of spices, temples and crowds.”

🧭 Stereotype vs Reality7

Culture, cultural differences and the stories we tell about each other.

1Is It All Curry? Food from India to Indonesia
Food & daily life
Cooking from India to Vietnam to the Philippines is far more varied than “curry”, and often balances sweet, sour, salty and spicy rather than only heat.
The stereotype: “South and Southeast Asian food is all curry and chilli — so hot that it hurts.”
Cleft Sentences — What … is, It is … thatOpen →
2The Head Wobble and Other Gestures
Gestures & greetings
The head wobble is a flexible signal, not a mystery, and greetings such as namaste and the Thai wai follow simple rules about respect that visitors can learn quickly.
The stereotype: “In India, people wobble their heads and nobody knows what it means.”
Modals of Advice — Should, Ought To, Had BetterOpen →
3Arranged Marriage: Forced or Family-Guided?
Family & marriage
Arranged marriage is common in South Asia, but it is different from forced marriage, which is illegal. Many couples meet, talk and decide together, and the practice is changing.
The stereotype: “In South Asia, every marriage is forced — brides and grooms never even meet.”
Reported SpeechOpen →
4Bollywood: Song, Dance and So Much More
Film & music
Bollywood is one industry among many: India makes films in dozens of languages, in every style from musical to realistic drama, and some of them have won Oscars.
The stereotype: “Indian cinema is just Bollywood — three hours of singing and dancing, and all the films are the same.”
Participle ClausesOpen →
5Land of Smiles: Is Every Smile the Same?
Smiles & emotions
Smiles in Thailand are a flexible language of manners, calm and consideration. They often replace words, and they do not always mean that everything is fine.
The stereotype: “Thai people smile all the time because they are always happy.”
Gerunds & InfinitivesOpen →
6Temple Manners: Shoes Off, Feet Down
Religion & etiquette
Temples and shrines are places where people pray, make offerings and meet every day. The rules about shoes, clothes and feet follow a few simple ideas about respect.
The stereotype: “Temples in Asia are just tourist sights with strange rules.”
Obligation & Permission — Must, Have To, Be Allowed ToOpen →
7Chaos or Order? Life in Busy Asian Cities
Cities & daily life
Many cities in South and Southeast Asia face real problems such as traffic, heat and pollution, but they also run on clever, informal systems, and some are among the most orderly in the world.
The stereotype: “Asian cities are chaotic, dirty and crowded — nothing works.”
