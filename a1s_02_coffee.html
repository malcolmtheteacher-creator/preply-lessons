Coffee with Tom 📖

Read it slowly. Say the words out loud.

"Come, Mia! Let's have a coffee," says Tom.

They go to a small café. It is warm. It smells of coffee and fresh bread.

A woman is at the counter. "Hello! What can I get you?" she says. Her name is Rosa.

"Can I have a coffee, please?" says Mia.

"Of course. And for you?" Rosa asks Tom.

"A tea for me, please. And a sandwich," says Tom.

"How much is it?" asks Mia.

"Four pounds, please," says Rosa.

Mia pays. "Thank you!" she says. She is happy — she ordered in English! ☕

Notice: Mia orders with three little words — Can I have… please? Learn them next.

The pattern: a / an 🧩

Before one thing, we use a little word: a or an.

a coffee  ·  a tea  ·  a sandwich
We use a before most words.
an apple  ·  an orange  ·  an egg
We use an before a, e, i, o, u sounds.

To order, put it together like Mia:

Can I have a coffee, please?
That's all you need today. The story is the heart — this little pattern just makes it work.
