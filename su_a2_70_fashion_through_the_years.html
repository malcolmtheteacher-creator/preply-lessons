How Things Used To Be

Understanding "used to"

We use "used to" to talk about past habits or situations that are no longer true today.

Affirmative: "She used to wear long skirts."
(She wore long skirts in the past, but she doesn"t now)
Negative: "He didn"t use to wear jeans."
(He didn"t wear jeans in the past, but he might now)
Questions: "Did people use to dress more formally?"
(Were people more formal in the past?)

How Things Have Changed

Understanding Present Perfect for Change

We use the present perfect to describe changes from the past to now. The change started in the past and continues to affect the present.

With "become": "Fashion has become more casual."
(It was formal before, and it"s casual now)
With "change": "Clothing styles have changed a lot in 30 years."
(Styles were different; now they"re different)
With "always": "She has always worn bright colors."
(From the past until now, she wears bright colors)

Practice: Describe Changes

Use these sentence starters to describe how fashion has changed:

Fashion has become...
People have started to...
Clothing has changed because...
Young people have begun...

Example: "Fashion has become more casual. People have started to wear jeans everywhere, even to the office."
