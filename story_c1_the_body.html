Six words for astonishment

Say what you think each word means from the sentence, then reveal.

1. The eye is so intricate that even a short description leaves out most of what it does.
intricate (adj) — made of many small, connected parts, so finely detailed that it is hard to describe. Example: "An intricate network of tiny blood vessels feeds the retina."
2. The numbers are staggering: your bone marrow makes about two million red blood cells every second.
staggering (adj) — so large or surprising that it is almost too much to take in. Example: "The cost of the project was staggering."
3. Your gut bacteria are indispensable: without them you could not digest your food properly.
indispensable (adj) — so necessary that you cannot manage without it. Example: "A good map is indispensable on a long hike."
4. A red blood cell is minuscule: about forty of them would fit across one grain of salt.
minuscule (adj) — extremely small. Example: "The dust was made of minuscule particles."
5. If you lose part of your liver, it can regenerate: it grows back to nearly its full size.
regenerate (v) — to grow again after being damaged or lost. Example: "Some lizards can regenerate a lost tail."
6. It is improbable that any one of us exists, and yet here we are.
improbable (adj) — not likely to happen or be true. Example: "It seemed improbable that she would win, but she did."

Six facts: guess first

Say your guess out loud. Then reveal, and react.

?
How many times does your heart beat in one day?
About 100,000 times a day. Over a long life that is nearly three billion beats, and it has never had a holiday.
?
The brain is about 2% of your body weight. What share of your energy does it use?
About 20%. Kilo for kilo, it uses about ten times more energy than the rest of you.
?
How many new red blood cells does your body make every second?
About two million. By the time you finish saying this sentence, it has made another ten million or so.
?
For every human cell in your body, how many bacterial cells are living in and on you?
About one. For years people said ten. Newer estimates say you are roughly half human cells, half bacteria, and you need them.
?
Stretch out the DNA from one single cell. How long would it be?
About two metres. It is folded into a space far too small to see, and you have it in almost every cell.
?
Your brain has a night-time cleaning system. When did scientists first describe it?
Only in 2012 and 2013. It works mostly while you sleep. The most important organ we have kept a basic secret until very recently.

Make numbers real

A number on its own is easy to forget. Good writers make you see it. Three moves:

1. Shrink it to something you can picture: "About forty of them would fit across a grain of salt."
2. Put it in the present: "In the time it takes to read this sentence, your body has made another ten million."
3. Compare it: "You have about as many bacterial cells as human cells."
