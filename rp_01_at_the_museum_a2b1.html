At the Museum

It is a wet day. You are at a big museum. You want two tickets and a place on the tour — so you ask nicely.

The Scene 🎬

It is a rainy Saturday. You are in a new city, and you want to visit the museum. There is a big Ancient Egypt show — with real mummies! You have wanted to see this for a long time.

You come in out of the rain and stand at the ticket desk. You need two tickets, and you want a place on the guided tour — the tour that takes you to the mummy room. But it is a wet day, so many people are here today.

Behind the desk is the ticket clerk. They are friendly and know everything about the museum. If the tour is full, that is OK. You can ask politely, and often there is another way in.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like museums? What do you like to see — old things, art, or animals?
  2. When a place is very busy and full, do you ask for help, or do you go home? Why?

Who's who

You
The Visitor 🧳

Wet from the rain, but happy and polite. You want two tickets and a place on the tour to the mummy room.

The other person
The Ticket Clerk 🎟️

Kind and helpful. They know when every tour is full — and how to help you, if you ask nicely.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the polite words: please, thank you, can I, we would like. These small words help a lot.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the polite words.

ClerkGood afternoon! It is very wet today. How can I help you?
YouHello. Can I have two tickets for the Egypt show, please? One adult and one student.
ClerkYes, of course. That is eighteen pounds. Do you have a student card?
YouYes, here it is. Can I pay by card, please?
ClerkNo problem. Here are your two tickets.
YouThank you. We would like to join the two o'clock tour. Is it still free?
ClerkOh, I am sorry. The two o'clock tour is full. A lot of people came today, because of the rain.
YouOh, that's a shame. We came a long way for this. Is there a later tour?
ClerkYes! There is a tour at four o'clock. It is smaller and very good. It is free with your ticket.
YouThat's great! Can we join the four o'clock tour, please?
ClerkOf course. Two people for four o'clock. Done.
YouThank you. One more question — what time do you close?
ClerkWe close at six. The last time to enter the mummy room is half past five. Go now — it is quiet.
YouThank you so much. You are very kind!

From memory — don't look up!

1. The visitor buys two tickets. Who are they for?

2. What is the problem with the two o'clock tour?

3. How does the visitor still get on a tour? What does the clerk give them?

1. One ticket for an adult, and one ticket for a student.
2. The two o'clock tour is full — a lot of people came because of the rain.
3. The clerk gives them a place on the later tour, at four o'clock. It is smaller, very good, and free with their ticket.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use at any museum, cinema, or ticket desk. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it with a smile. A friendly voice and the words please and thank you do a lot of work. Speak slowly and clearly. You do not need long sentences — short and polite is perfect.

🎟️ Buying tickets

Phrase 1

You are at the desk. You want two tickets for the show. Complete it: "Can I have two ___ for the Egypt show, please?"

"Can I have two tickets for the Egypt show, please?"
"Can I have…, please?" is an easy, polite way to ask for anything: "Can I have a coffee, please?", "Can I have the menu, please?"
Phrase 2

You want to pay with your bank card. Which sentence is polite?

A. "Card."
B. "I pay card now."
C. "Can I pay by card, please?"
C — "Can I pay by card, please?"
"Can I pay by card?" is the normal, polite question. You can also ask "Can I pay by cash?" A and B are too short and sound like an order.

🕑 The guided tour

Phrase 3

You want to ask if the tour has no more space. Put the words in order:

the tourIsfull?
"Is the tour full?"
"Is … full?" is a quick way to ask if there is no more space: "Is the bus full?", "Is the class full?"
Phrase 4

You want to go on the tour with the group. Complete it: "We would like to ___ the two o'clock tour, please."

"We would like to join the two o'clock tour, please."
To join a tour means to go with the group. "We would like to…" is the polite way to say "we want to". Also: "We would like to join the class."

🌧️ When it is full

Phrase 5

The clerk says the tour is full. You are sad, but you stay calm and polite. Which answer is best?

A. "That is bad. Why is it full?"
B. "Oh, that's a shame."
C. "No! I want it now!"
B — "Oh, that's a shame."
"That's a shame" shows you are a little sad, but you are still calm and kind. A and C sound angry — and people help you more when you stay friendly.
Phrase 6

The two o'clock tour is full. You want to ask about a tour at another time. Complete it: "Is there a ___ tour?"

"Is there a later tour?"
A later tour is a tour at a time after this one. "Is there a…?" asks if something exists: "Is there a bus at six?", "Is there a smaller room?"

💛 Saying thank you

Phrase 7

The clerk helps you a lot. Say a warm thank you out loud first. Then look. (There is more than one good answer.)

"Thank you. That's very kind."
"That's very kind" tells the person you saw their help. It is warm, not cold. A warm thank you makes people happy to help you again.
Phrase 8

At the end, you want to say a big thank you. Complete it: "Thank you so ___!"

"Thank you so much!"
Add "so much" or "very much" to make your thank you bigger and warmer. A smile helps too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work at museums, cinemas, shops — everywhere. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"Can I have ___, please?" — What do you want to ask for the next time you go out? (a ticket, a coffee, the menu…)

"We would like to join ___." — Think of a group thing you can join (a tour, a class, a game).

"Is there a later ___?" — Ask about something at a later time (a tour, a bus, a train).

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — buy the tickets → ask about the tour → it is full → ask for a later tour → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your museum. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the visitor 🧳

The clerk speaks — you answer. Be polite: use please, thank you, and we would like.

ClerkGood afternoon! Very wet today, isn't it? How can I help you?
ClerkOf course — that is eighteen pounds. How would you like to pay?
ClerkNo problem. Here are your tickets.
ClerkOh, I am sorry. The two o'clock tour is full today.
ClerkYes — there is a good one at four o'clock, free with your ticket.

Round 2 — now YOU are the ticket clerk 🎟️

Change places. You work at the museum. Be friendly and helpful. Say "Of course", "No problem", "I am sorry".

VisitorHello. Can I have two tickets for the Egypt show, please?
VisitorHere you are. We would like to join the two o'clock tour. Is it full?
VisitorOh, that's a shame. Is there a later tour?
VisitorThank you so much. What time do you close?
The Twist

🌙 Only one ticket left

One more time — this round is harder. It is the last day of the Egypt show. You ask for two tickets, but the clerk says: "I am sorry. We have only one ticket left." You are with a friend, and you both really want to go. What do you say? Stay polite and friendly. Ask nicely — maybe you can share, maybe there is a ticket for tomorrow, maybe a later time. Try to leave happy. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I asked for my tickets clearly.
  • I used "please" and "we would like" to ask for things.
  • I stayed calm and polite when the tour was full.
  • I asked about a later tour.
  • I said "thank you" warmly at the end.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

In 1922, a man called Howard Carter found something amazing. He made a small hole in an old door, held up a light, and saw gold — the tomb of a young king called Tutankhamun. It was closed for 3,000 years.

Soon, lots of people wanted to see it. They waited in long lines, just to look at old Egyptian things behind glass.

Today, you stand at a ticket desk and ask about the mummy room. You are in a very old line — people have waited to see wonders for a very long time.

Two questions to talk about

  1. People wait a long time and pay money to see very old things. Why do we like to do this?
  2. Tutankhamun's gold was made only for the king. Now anyone can see it. Is that a good thing, or should some things stay closed?
