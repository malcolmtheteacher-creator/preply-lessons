At the Museum

The Scene 🎬

It is a rainy Saturday. You are in a new city, and you want to visit the museum. There is a big Ancient Egypt show — with real mummies! You have wanted to see this for a long time.

You come in out of the rain and stand at the ticket desk. You need two tickets, and you want a place on the guided tour — the tour that takes you to the mummy room. But it is a wet day, so many people are here today.

Behind the desk is the ticket clerk. They are friendly and know everything about the museum. If the tour is full, that is OK. You can ask politely, and often there is another way in.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like museums? What do you like to see — old things, art, or animals?
  2. When a place is very busy and full, do you ask for help, or do you go home? Why?

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each, then change. Find the phrases from your toolkit. Listen for the polite words.

ClerkGood afternoon! It is very wet today. How can I help you?
YouHello. Can I have two tickets for the Egypt show, please? One adult and one student.
ClerkYes, of course. That is eighteen pounds. Do you have a student card?
YouYes, here it is. Can I pay by card, please?
ClerkNo problem. Here are your two tickets.
YouThank you. We would like to join the two o'clock tour. Is it still free?
ClerkOh, I am sorry. The two o'clock tour is full. A lot of people came today, because of the rain.
YouOh, that's a shame. We came a long way for this. Is there a later tour?
ClerkYes! There is a tour at four o'clock. It is smaller and very good. It is free with your ticket.
YouThat's great! Can we join the four o'clock tour, please?
ClerkOf course. Two people for four o'clock. Done.
YouThank you. One more question — what time do you close?
ClerkWe close at six. The last time to enter the mummy room is half past five. Go now — it is quiet.
YouThank you so much. You are very kind!

From memory — don't look up!

The Echo 🕰️

In 1922, a man called Howard Carter found something amazing. He made a small hole in an old door, held up a light, and saw gold — the tomb of a young king called Tutankhamun. It was closed for 3,000 years.

Soon, lots of people wanted to see it. They waited in long lines, just to look at old Egyptian things behind glass.

Today, you stand at a ticket desk and ask about the mummy room. You are in a very old line — people have waited to see wonders for a very long time.

Two questions to talk about

  1. People wait a long time and pay money to see very old things. Why do we like to do this?
  2. Tutankhamun's gold was made only for the king. Now anyone can see it. Is that a good thing, or should some things stay closed?
