The rules

A question appears on screen. The answerer secretly decides: truth or lie? They answer the question — and the detective has to figure out which it is.

The detective gets 2 follow-up questions to probe the story. Look for hesitation, inconsistency, too much detail, or not enough detail. Then make your verdict: truth or lie?

How it works

Rounds 1–3: The student answers, the teacher detects.
Rounds 4–6: Roles swap. Teacher answers, student detects.
Final Round: The hardest question. Highest stakes.

Score: 1 point if you fool the detective. 1 point if you catch the lie.

Lying language (for the answerer)

"Well, it was something like..." "I think it was probably..." "To be honest..." "I remember because..."

Probing language (for the detective)

"Can you be more specific about...?" "That's interesting — why did you...?" "Wait, you said earlier that..." "How did that make you feel?"

Reflection

What gives a liar away? Was it hesitation, too much detail, not enough detail, or something in the voice? What patterns did you notice?
Were you a better liar or a better detective? Which was harder — making up a convincing story, or spotting the fake one?
Think about real life. When do people lie in everyday situations? White lies, social lies, professional lies. Is it always wrong? When is it acceptable?
