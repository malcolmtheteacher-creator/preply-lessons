Ordering at a Café

A hot drink, something sweet, and one quiet hour with your book — until the wrong plate lands on your table.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is a quiet Saturday morning. You have one free hour and a good book. You go to your favourite café on the corner. It smells of coffee and warm cake.

You want a hot drink and something sweet. You know what you want — but then you see the cakes on the counter, and you want to change your mind! That is OK. You can order, change your mind, and say it all in simple, polite English.

Behind the counter is a friendly server. They are busy, but they are happy to help. Later, there is one small problem: they bring you the wrong food. But that is fine — you can fix it, politely.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. What do you like to drink in a café — coffee, tea, or something else? Do you always order the same thing?
  2. A café brings you the wrong cake, but it looks nice. Do you say something, or do you just eat it? Why?

Who's who

You
The Customer ☕

You want a hot drink and a cake. You are friendly and polite. You change your mind once — and, when the food is wrong, you ask for help.

The other person
The Server 🫖

Kind and busy. They take your order, change it when you ask, and fix the mix-up — happily, because you are nice about it.

Next: learn the phrases you need in the next tab. Then see them in a real conversation. Look at the polite words: please, thank you, can I, sorry. It is OK to speak slowly. You can also ask the server to say it again.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each, then change. Find the phrases from your toolkit. Listen for the polite words.

ServerGood morning! What can I get you?
YouGood morning. Can I have a coffee, please?
ServerOf course. Would you like anything to eat?
YouYes. I would like a croissant, too, please.
ServerSure. Is it to eat in or takeaway?
YouTo eat in, please. Oh — sorry, can I change my order? Can I have the cherry scone instead of the croissant?
ServerNo problem! One coffee and one cherry scone. Please take a seat. That's four pounds.
ServerHere you are — one tea and a piece of carrot cake.
YouExcuse me, I think this is not my order. I ordered a coffee and the cherry scone.
ServerOh, I am so sorry! You are right. Let me change it. One minute, please.
YouThank you. It's OK, no problem.
ServerHere is your coffee and your cherry scone. Sorry for the wait!
YouThank you very much! How much is it?
ServerIt's four pounds. And the scone is free today — sorry for the mix-up!

The Echo 🕰️

Agatha Christie wrote crime stories. She sold about two billion books — more than almost anyone. A lot of her stories happen in quiet, polite places: a café, a tea room, a nice hotel.

She liked these calm places because they feel safe. People smile, they drink tea, they talk about small things. Nobody thinks about danger. That is why they are perfect for a story.

So the next time your coffee order goes a little wrong, think of Agatha Christie. She knew that the most interesting things can happen at the quietest, most polite table in the room.
