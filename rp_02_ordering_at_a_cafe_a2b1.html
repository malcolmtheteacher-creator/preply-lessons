Ordering at a Café

A hot drink, something sweet, and one quiet hour with your book — until the wrong plate lands on your table.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is a quiet Saturday morning. You have one free hour and a good book. You go to your favourite café on the corner. It smells of coffee and warm cake.

You want a hot drink and something sweet. You know what you want — but then you see the cakes on the counter, and you want to change your mind! That is OK. You can order, change your mind, and say it all in simple, polite English.

Behind the counter is a friendly server. They are busy, but they are happy to help. Later, there is one small problem: they bring you the wrong food. But that is fine — you can fix it, politely.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. What do you like to drink in a café — coffee, tea, or something else? Do you always order the same thing?
  2. A café brings you the wrong cake, but it looks nice. Do you say something, or do you just eat it? Why?

Who's who

You
The Customer ☕

You want a hot drink and a cake. You are friendly and polite. You change your mind once — and, when the food is wrong, you ask for help.

The other person
The Server 🫖

Kind and busy. They take your order, change it when you ask, and fix the mix-up — happily, because you are nice about it.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the polite words: please, thank you, can I, sorry. It is OK to speak slowly. You can also ask the server to say it again.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the polite words.

ServerGood morning! What can I get you?
YouGood morning. Can I have a coffee, please?
ServerOf course. Would you like anything to eat?
YouYes. I would like a croissant, too, please.
ServerSure. Is it to eat in or takeaway?
YouTo eat in, please. Oh — sorry, can I change my order? Can I have the cherry scone instead of the croissant?
ServerNo problem! One coffee and one cherry scone. Please take a seat. That's four pounds.
ServerHere you are — one tea and a piece of carrot cake.
YouExcuse me, I think this is not my order. I ordered a coffee and the cherry scone.
ServerOh, I am so sorry! You are right. Let me change it. One minute, please.
YouThank you. It's OK, no problem.
ServerHere is your coffee and your cherry scone. Sorry for the wait!
YouThank you very much! How much is it?
ServerIt's four pounds. And the scone is free today — sorry for the mix-up!

From memory — don't look up!

1. At the end, what does the customer have? (There are two things.)

2. The customer changes the order. What do they NOT want any more, and what do they want instead?

3. The server brings the wrong food first. What two wrong things do they bring?

1. A coffee and a cherry scone.
2. The customer does not want the croissant any more. They want the cherry scone instead.
3. A tea and a piece of carrot cake (not the coffee and the scone).

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use in any café. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it with a smile. A friendly voice and the words please and thank you do a lot. You do not need long sentences — short and polite is perfect. If you do not hear the price, it is OK to ask, "Sorry, how much?"

☕ Ordering

Phrase 1

You are at the counter. You want a coffee. Ask in a polite way. Complete it: "Can I ___ a coffee, please?"

"Can I have a coffee, please?"
"Can I have…, please?" is the easy, polite way to order anything: "Can I have a tea, please?", "Can I have a glass of water, please?"
Phrase 2

You want a cake as well as your drink. Complete it: "I would ___ a cake, too, please."

"I would like a cake, too, please."
"I would like…" is a polite way to say "I want". The word "too" means "also" — you want the cake as well as the drink.

🔄 Changing your mind

Phrase 3

You ordered, but now you want something different. First, ask politely. Complete it: "Sorry, can I ___ my order?"

"Sorry, can I change my order?"
Start with "Sorry" — it is friendly. "Can I change my order?" tells the server you want something different. Then say what you want next.
Phrase 4

You want the scone in place of the croissant. Put the words in order:

can I havethe sconeinstead?
"Can I have the scone instead?"
"Instead" means "in place of the other thing". Use it when you swap: "Can I have tea instead?", "Can I have the small one instead?"

🍽️ A mix-up

Phrase 5

The server brings the wrong food. You want to tell them, in a soft way. Complete it: "Excuse me, I think this is not my ___."

"Excuse me, I think this is not my order."
"Excuse me" is polite. "I think this is not my order" is soft and kind — you are not angry, you just want the right food. Then say what you ordered.
Phrase 6

You need to tell the server about the mix-up. Which one is polite and kind?

A. "This is wrong. You made a mistake."
B. "Sorry, I think this is not my order. I ordered a coffee."
C. "No. I don't want this."
B — "Sorry, I think this is not my order. I ordered a coffee."
B is soft and clear: you say sorry, you say the problem, and you say what you ordered. A and C sound angry. When you are kind, the server is happy to help you fast.

💳 Paying & thank you

Phrase 7

You are ready to pay. You want to know the price. Complete it: "How ___ is it, please?"

"How much is it, please?"
"How much is it?" asks for the price. You can also ask, "Can I pay, please?" and "Do you take card?"
Phrase 8

The server helped you and fixed the mix-up. You want to say a warm, big thank you. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you very much!" / "Thank you so much!"
Add "very much" or "so much" to make your thank you bigger and warmer. A smile helps too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in cafés, shops — everywhere. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"Can I have ___, please?" — What do you want to order next time? (a coffee, a cake, a juice…)

"Can I have ___ instead?" — You change your mind. What do you want in place of it?

"Excuse me, I think this is not my ___." — Finish this polite sentence for a mix-up.

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — order → change your mind → the mix-up → pay → thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your café. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Role-play timer: 3:00

Round 1 — you are the customer ☕

The server speaks — you answer. Be polite: use please, thank you, and can I.

ServerGood morning! What can I get you?
ServerOf course. Would you like anything to eat?
ServerSure. Is it to eat in or takeaway?
ServerNo problem! Please take a seat, and I'll bring it over.
ServerHere you are — one tea and a piece of chocolate cake.
ServerOh, I'm so sorry! Let me change it right away.

Round 2 — now YOU are the server 🫖

Change places. You work at the café. Be friendly and helpful. Say "Good morning", "Of course", "No problem", "I'm sorry".

CustomerGood morning. Can I have a tea, please?
CustomerYes, I would like a piece of cake, too, please.
CustomerSorry, can I change my order? Can I have a coffee instead of the tea?
CustomerExcuse me, I think this is not my order. I ordered a coffee.
The Twist

🍰 No more scones

One more time — no help this round. Today the café is very busy. You want a cherry scone, but the server says: "I'm sorry, there are no more scones today." What do you say? Stay polite and friendly. Ask for something else — maybe a different cake, or a biscuit. Try to leave happy. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I ordered a drink and something to eat clearly.
  • I used "please" and "can I have" to ask for things.
  • I changed my mind politely, with "Sorry, can I…".
  • I told them the mix-up in a calm, kind way.
  • I said "thank you" warmly at the end.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

Agatha Christie wrote crime stories. She sold about two billion books — more than almost anyone. A lot of her stories happen in quiet, polite places: a café, a tea room, a nice hotel.

She liked these calm places because they feel safe. People smile, they drink tea, they talk about small things. Nobody thinks about danger. That is why they are perfect for a story.

So the next time your coffee order goes a little wrong, think of Agatha Christie. She knew that the most interesting things can happen at the quietest, most polite table in the room.
