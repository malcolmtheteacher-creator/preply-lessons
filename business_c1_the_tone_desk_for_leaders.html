Key words

candid (adjective) — honest and open, even when the truth is unwelcome. Example: "The CEO gave a candid account of what went wrong."
euphemism (noun) — a gentler word used in place of one that sounds harsh or unpleasant. Example: "'Restructuring' is often a euphemism for job cuts."
measured (adjective) — careful and controlled, not extreme or emotional. Example: "She gave a measured response to the criticism."
accountable (adjective) — expected to explain your decisions and accept their results. Example: "Leaders must be held accountable for their mistakes."
reassure (verb) — make somebody feel less worried. Example: "He tried to reassure staff that their jobs were safe."
convey (verb) — communicate a feeling, idea or message. Example: "The email failed to convey any real regret."
tone-deaf (adjective) — not noticing how your words or actions will make other people feel. Example: "Announcing bonuses on the day of the layoffs was tone-deaf."
stakeholder (noun) — a person or group with an interest in how an organisation performs. Example: "We need to keep every stakeholder informed."
hedge (verb) — avoid making a clear statement or commitment. Example: "When she was asked about job cuts, the director hedged."
spin (noun) — a way of presenting information that makes it look better than it really is. Example: "Nobody believed the press release: it was pure spin."
