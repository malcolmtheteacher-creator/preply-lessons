Spot the difference

A: "Hundreds of staff walked out at noon and stood outside the building, refusing to return until managers agreed to talk."

B: "Staff signed a letter asking managers to talk, and agreed to wait two weeks for a reply."

SPEAK Which action is stronger? What does each one risk? How might managers react differently?
A is stronger because it stops work immediately and puts pressure on managers, but it risks lost pay and sharper conflict. B is safer and builds goodwill, but it is easy to ignore. Managers may feel forced by A and respected by B.

    SPEAK Explain your top choice and your bottom choice. Use: I'd argue that… / What matters most is… / That said, …

    Researchers who study movements often stress: broad, varied support (not just one group), discipline and peaceful methods, a clear, achievable demand, staying power, and pressure that actually affects the people in charge. Media attention helps but rarely works alone. Did your order match? Where did you disagree, and why?

    Two sides of the same story

    Read both, then tell me which argument is stronger and why.

    Supporters argue…

    Ordinary people have few other ways to be heard. Disruption forces leaders to pay attention, and many rights we now take for granted began as unpopular protests.

    Critics counter…

    Disruption hurts people who had nothing to do with the problem. Decisions should be made through votes and negotiation, and anger can harden opinions instead of changing them.
