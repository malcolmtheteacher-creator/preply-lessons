Why Boardrooms Kill Stories

The higher the stakes, the more people retreat to bullet points.

Today: We're learning the Leadership Arc — a four-part narrative structure for boardroom presentations that moves people from understanding to action.

Most boardroom presentations follow the same pattern: here's the data, here's the analysis, here are the recommendations. It's logical. It's comprehensive. And it puts half the room on their phones by slide three.

The paradox: the higher the stakes, the more people default to information-dumping. As if enough data will compel action. It won't. Data informs decisions. Stories drive them.

Today we're building a structure that works inside the constraints of a boardroom — time-limited, sceptical audience, high stakes — while doing what only narrative can do: creating momentum toward a decision.

The Leadership Arc

Four moves that turn a presentation into a story that moves people.

Where We Were

Shared ground. What everyone in the room already knows and agrees on.

What Changed

The disruption. The data that shifted everything. The thing that means we can't stay here.

Where We're Going

The vision. Paint it vividly. Use BAB — make them see the future before you explain the bridge.

Your Role In It

The ask. What you need from this room, these people, right now. Make it specific and actionable.

Why this works

Notice what the Leadership Arc does: it's essentially SCR from Lesson 1, with a crucial addition — the fourth beat, "Your Role In It," which turns the story into a call to action. Without it, you've told a good story. With it, you've created momentum.

In the wild

Where We Were: "Two years ago, we owned 34% of the mid-market. We were the safe choice."

What Changed: "Then three SaaS competitors entered with pricing models we can't match on our current infrastructure. In 12 months, our market share dropped to 26%."

Where We're Going: "But here's what they can't replicate: our relationships with 3,000 enterprise clients and 15 years of implementation data. If we rebuild the platform, we don't just compete — we become the only player who can offer personalised enterprise solutions at mid-market speed."

Your Role In It: "I need three things from this room today: approval for the £2M platform rebuild, a six-month runway before we review metrics, and your commitment to the narrative that this is an investment, not a cost."

The "Your Role In It" principle: Never end a boardroom story on the vision. End on the ask. The audience should leave the room knowing exactly what they need to do — and feeling like doing it is the natural next step in the story you just told them.

Build a Boardroom Story

Map a real presentation onto the Leadership Arc.

Let's analyse one together

Here's the opening of a CFO's board presentation. What's working? What's missing? Which part of the Leadership Arc is she in?

"Good morning. I want to start by acknowledging something we all felt at last quarter's review: we missed our revenue target, and the mood in that room was grim. Since then, two things have happened that changed my thinking completely. First, our customer lifetime value increased 23% — meaning fewer customers are worth more. Second, our three biggest competitors all reported declining retention this quarter. We're losing the race on acquisition. But we're winning the war on loyalty. And that changes our entire strategy."

"Where We Were" is handled in one sentence (last quarter's grim review — shared ground). "What Changed" occupies the heart — two data points reframing the picture (Lesson 5's reframing in action). She's beginning "Where We're Going" with the loyalty insight. What's missing: "Your Role In It" — the specific ask. This is where most presentations fail. The story builds beautifully, then dies with "any questions?"

Your turn — map a real presentation

Think of a presentation you need to give — or one you've given recently that could have been stronger. Map it onto the four beats:

Where We Were

The shared ground. What does everyone already know and agree on? (Keep it to 1-2 sentences.)

What Changed

The disruption. What data, event, or insight means we can't keep doing what we were doing?

Where We're Going

The vision. Paint it. Use showing techniques (Lesson 4) and make the data breathe (Lesson 6).

Your Role In It

The ask. What do you need from this specific room? Make it concrete.

Deliver It

The full presentation

Don't read your notes. Glance at the structure if you need to, but the delivery should be spoken, not recited.

Final Review

1. Name the four beats of the Leadership Arc

2. Why is "Your Role In It" the most important beat?

3. How does the Leadership Arc relate to SCR from Lesson 1?

1. Where We Were → What Changed → Where We're Going → Your Role In It.

2. Because without it, you've told a story but haven't created action. The ask turns understanding into momentum. It gives the audience a role in the story — which is what makes them commit.

3. It's SCR with a crucial addition. "Where We Were" = Situation. "What Changed" = Complication. "Where We're Going" = Resolution. "Your Role In It" extends SCR by turning the resolution into a call to action — moving from "here's what we should do" to "here's what I need you to do."
