Real Stories from the Court

Jana Novotna - Wimbledon 1993
Leading 4-1 in the final set against Steffi Graf, serving for the championship, Jana suddenly couldn't hit the ball. She double-faulted, made simple errors, and lost 6 games in a row. At the trophy ceremony, she cried on the Duchess of Kent's shoulder. The tennis world watched one of the most painful collapses in history unfold in real time.
Novak Djokovic - The Transformation
Before 2011, Djokovic was talented but couldn't beat Nadal or Federer when it mattered. He hired a mental coach, changed his diet, and started meditation. That year, he went 43-0 to start the season. He later said the change was "80% mental, 20% physical." Today, he's considered the greatest mental competitor in tennis history.
Rafael Nadal - The Rituals
Watch Nadal before any serve: he touches his face, adjusts his shorts, bounces the ball exactly the same number of times. These aren't superstitions - they're anchors. They keep his mind from wandering to pressure, past mistakes, or future consequences. Every ritual brings him back to the present moment.
Think about your own tennis: When do you play your best? When do you play your worst? What's different in your head?

Expressions for the Mental Game

be in the zone

Click to reveal
Meaning
A mental state of complete focus where everything feels effortless. Time seems to slow down, you react perfectly without thinking, and you feel unstoppable. Athletes call it "flow state."
Form
be + in the zone
Often used with: completely, totally, really
Past: "I was in the zone" | Present: "When I'm in the zone..."
"I won the second set 6-0. I was completely in the zone - every shot went exactly where I wanted."
Register
Informal but widely used. Common in sports commentary and everyday conversation about performance.

NOW YOU TRY:

Describe a time when you were completely in the zone during a match or practice. What did it feel like? What triggered it?

choke under pressure

Click to reveal
Meaning
To perform badly because of nervousness or stress in an important moment. Your skills disappear when they matter most. The opposite of clutch performance.
Form
choke + under pressure / at the crucial moment / when it matters
Can also be used as noun: "a choke" | Person: "a choker"
Be careful: calling someone "a choker" is quite harsh!
"He was serving for the match at 5-4, but he completely choked under pressure - double fault, double fault, forehand into the net."
Register
Informal. Very common in sports. Can sound harsh when applied to a specific person, so often softened: "I kind of choked" rather than "You choked."

NOW YOU TRY:

Have you ever choked under pressure in a match? What happened? Or have you seen a professional player choke in an important match?

raise your game

Click to reveal
Meaning
To play better than your usual level, especially when facing a strong opponent or important situation. To elevate your performance when it matters.
Form
raise + my/your/his/her/their + game
Often followed by: against [opponent], in [situation], to [do something]
Similar: step up, elevate your game
"She always raises her game against top players. The bigger the match, the better she plays."
Register
Neutral to slightly informal. Used in sports and business contexts ("We need to raise our game to compete with them").

NOW YOU TRY:

When do you raise your game? Against certain opponents? In certain situations? What helps you play above your normal level?

More Mental Game Expressions

have a mental block

Click to reveal
Meaning
A psychological barrier that prevents you from doing something you can normally do. Often related to a specific shot, opponent, or situation. Your mind gets in the way of your ability.
Form
have + a mental block + about/with/against [something]
Also: develop a mental block, get over a mental block
Variations: psychological block, mental barrier
"I have a complete mental block about my second serve. In practice it's fine, but in matches I just can't trust it."
Register
Neutral. Used in sports psychology and everyday conversation. Acknowledging a mental block is often the first step to overcoming it.

NOW YOU TRY:

Do you have any mental blocks in your tennis? A shot you can't trust? An opponent you can't beat? A situation that always goes wrong?

be on a roll

Click to reveal
Meaning
To be experiencing a series of successes; to have momentum on your side. Everything is going well and you feel like you can't lose. Often feels like positive energy building.
Form
be + on a roll
Often modified: really on a roll, on a bit of a roll
Related: ride the momentum, keep the momentum going
"I won four games in a row and I was really on a roll. I felt like nothing could stop me - until he called a medical timeout and killed my momentum."
Register
Informal but very common. Used for sports, gambling, work, and any situation involving a winning streak or series of successes.

NOW YOU TRY:

What do you do when you're on a roll in a match? Do you try to finish quickly? Do you stay patient? What can break your momentum?
