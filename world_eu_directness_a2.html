Blunt Dutch, Polite British?

The Dutch say exactly what they think. The British often say less. Why?

Talk first:
  • Do you say what you think?

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. My boss is very direct.
direct (adj) — saying things clearly and honestly. Example: “My boss is very direct.”
2. Please be polite to the guests.
polite (adj) — kind, with good manners. Example: “Please be polite to the guests.”
3. She gave me good feedback.
feedback (n) — comments on how well you did something. Example: “She gave me good feedback.”
4. Be honest with me.
honest (adj) — telling the truth. Example: “Be honest with me.”
5. His hint was easy to miss.
hint (n) — a small sign about what you really mean. Example: “His hint was easy to miss.”
6. The meeting starts at nine.
meeting (n) — a time when people talk about work. Example: “The meeting starts at nine.”
Talk it through:
  • Which word is new for you? Say a sentence with it.
The stereotype: “The Dutch are rude and the British never say what they mean.”

The story

Part 1 — The Dutch

Many Dutch people say things clearly. A Dutch colleague can say, “I don't agree. This plan has three problems.” For many Dutch people this is not rude. It shows respect. They treat you as an adult.

Quick check:

1. Is being direct rude for many Dutch people?

2. What can a Dutch colleague say?

1. No, it shows respect.

2. “I don't agree. This plan has three problems.”

Discuss:
  • Do you like direct people?

Part 2 — The British

British English often makes criticism soft. “That's quite interesting” can mean “I don't like it”. “With the greatest respect” can mean “I think you are wrong”. A Dutch listener can miss the hint.

Quick check:

1. What can “That's quite interesting” mean?

2. Who can miss the hint?

1. I don't like it.

2. A Dutch listener.

Discuss:
  • Is it better to be soft or direct?

Part 3 — Two styles

Both styles are polite in their own way. Problems start when people judge the other style with their own rules. In international teams, agree how you give feedback.

Quick check:

1. Is one style ruder?

2. What can an international team do?

1. No, both are polite in their own way.

2. Agree how to give feedback.

Discuss:
  • How can you give feedback politely?

Grammar — polite requests with could

Could you + verb? is polite: “Could you explain the plan?”
Could I + verb? asks for permission: “Could I ask a question?”
Add please: “Could you help me, please?”

1. Choose the word

1. you repeat that, please? (Could / Do)

2. I ask a question? (Could / Are)

3. Could you me the report, please? (send / sending)

1. Could

2. Could

3. send

2. Make your own

1. Could you ______, please?

2. Could I ______?

3. Could you tell me ______?

1. ...explain it again? / ...speak more slowly?

2. ...ask a question? / ...open the window?

3. ...the time? / ...where the station is?
