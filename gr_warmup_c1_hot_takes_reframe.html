Hot Takes: The Reframe

Same take. Three lenses. Three different answers.

The reframe move

Most hot takes feel right because they sit comfortably in one frame. Move them to a different frame and the answer often flips. The skill of educated argument is being able to see the same claim from at least three angles before picking your view.

Ethical lens

"Is it right? Who is helped, who is harmed, what duties are at stake?"

Economic lens

"Does it work? Who pays, who benefits, what are the second-order effects?"

Cultural lens

"What does it mean for the kind of society we become? What signals does it send? What does it teach the next generation?"

How to play

Each take has three lens-questions. Speak each for 45 seconds. Then a final 60-second synthesis: which lens did you find most convincing — and which one changed your mind?

Ethical lens
Do we have a duty to teach children where the world they've inherited came from — including the parts that produced their freedoms and the parts that hurt people? Is "happiness" enough?
45 seconds.
0:45
Economic lens
Do happier workers earn more? Do well-being skills boost productivity? Or are we trading away knowledge that makes whole industries possible — engineering, medicine, law — for soft skills?
45 seconds.
0:45
Cultural lens
A nation that doesn't teach its history forgets it. What kind of society does that produce in 50 years? And whose definition of "happiness" gets taught — yours, or someone else's?

Ethical lens
Cars cause pollution, deaths and emissions. But they also give freedom, especially to disabled people, parents and rural communities. Where does moral responsibility actually sit — with the driver, or the city that depends on cars?
45 seconds.
0:45
Economic lens
Mass car-ownership built whole industries — manufacturing, fuel, insurance, repair. If we shrink that, what replaces those jobs? Are public transport and cycling enough at scale?
45 seconds.
0:45
Cultural lens
A car is not just transport — it's status, privacy, freedom, music in the rain, road trips. What part of culture dies when cars become unfashionable, and is that loss real?
45 seconds.
0:45

Synthesis

If you had to revise the take, what would it become? "Owning a car is morally questionable when…" Speak the revision.

Take 5
"Assisted dying should be available to anyone of sound mind, at any age."
📰 News💭 Opinion⬆ Case FOR⬇ Case AGAINST🤖 Build lesson
Ethical lens
Whose life is it? Where does autonomy end and protection begin? What do we owe people in terrible pain — and what do we owe those who might one day feel pressured to leave so as not to be a burden?
45 seconds.
0:45
Economic lens
Healthcare systems already ration care. If assisted dying becomes a normal option, does it quietly start to feel like the cheaper one? Are there real economic pressures that could distort an "individual" choice?
45 seconds.
0:45
Cultural lens
In countries that have introduced assisted dying, attitudes to disability, ageing and depression have shifted in ways the original laws didn't predict. What kind of society do we become — and is that change reversible?
45 seconds.
0:45

Synthesis

This take is the heaviest. Did any lens make you change your starting position? Where exactly is the line you wouldn't cross — and why?

Real-world use

Pick a debate that's happening in the news this week. Apply all three lenses to it in your head. Which one is the news report using? Which one is it ignoring?
