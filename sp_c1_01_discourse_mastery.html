The Edge: What Separates Good from Commanding

At C1/C2 level, your grammar is solid. Your vocabulary is extensive. So why does speaking still sometimes feel like work?

The Real Challenge

The difference between "fluent" and "commanding" isn't about knowing more words — it's about processing under pressure while maintaining elegance.

Consider two speakers answering the same difficult question in a high-stakes meeting:

Question: "What's your view on the proposed restructuring?"

Speaker A: "Um... I think... well, the restructuring is... I mean, there are good points and bad points... It's complicated."

Speaker B: "That's a nuanced question, and I think it warrants a careful response. On balance, I'd say the proposal has merit, though I do have some reservations about the timeline. Let me explain..."

Same thinking time. Same level of uncertainty. Completely different impression.

Today's Focus

You'll master 5 sophisticated discourse strategies that let you think clearly while speaking elegantly — even when you're uncertain, challenged, or under pressure.

This isn't about memorising phrases. It's about developing automatic processing that frees your brain to focus on what you're saying, not how you're saying it.

Your Toolkit: 5 Discourse Strategies

"That's a nuanced question..." Click to expand
Function
Validates complexity while buying 3-4 seconds
Register
Professional / Academic — signals sophistication
When to Use
Complex questions, controversial topics, when you need time
In a strategy meeting:

"That's a nuanced question, and I think it deserves a considered response. My initial view is..."

Variations: "That's a layered question..." / "There's a lot to unpack there..." / "That touches on several things..."

🎤 Try It Now

Your manager asks: "Do you think we should expand into the Asian market?" Use this strategy to begin your response.

"On balance, I'd say..." Click to expand
Function
Signals you've weighed options; introduces measured opinion
Register
Professional — projects analytical thinking
When to Use
When asked for opinions, evaluations, recommendations
Evaluating a proposal:

"On balance, I'd say the benefits outweigh the risks, though we'd need to address the compliance concerns."

Variations: "All things considered..." / "Taking everything into account..." / "Weighing it all up..."

🎤 Try It Now

Someone asks: "Was the project a success?" Give a balanced assessment using this strategy.

"It's conceivable that..." / "One could argue..." Click to expand
Function
Introduces speculation or alternative views with distance
Register
Formal / Academic — creates intellectual distance
When to Use
Hypotheticals, devil's advocate, uncertain predictions
Discussing future scenarios:

"It's conceivable that AI will fundamentally reshape the industry, though the timeline remains uncertain."

Spectrum of certainty: "It's possible..." → "It's conceivable..." → "It's plausible..." → "It's likely..."

🎤 Try It Now

Speculate: "What might happen to your industry in 10 years?" Use graduated certainty.

"Let me frame this another way..." Click to expand
Function
Redirects or clarifies; recovers from unclear explanations
Register
Professional — shows self-awareness and control
When to Use
Mid-explanation pivots, when you've lost the thread, clarifying
Realising you're not being clear:

"Actually, let me frame this another way. The core issue is really about resource allocation, not strategy."

Variations: "Put differently..." / "To put it more concretely..." / "Let me approach this from a different angle..."

🎤 Try It Now

Explain something complex about your work. Halfway through, pivot using this strategy to clarify.

"I'd want to caveat that by saying..." Click to expand
Function
Adds nuance or limitations to a statement
Register
Professional / Consultative — shows thoroughness
When to Use
After making claims, when precision matters, managing expectations
After making a recommendation:

"I think we should proceed, though I'd want to caveat that by saying the data is preliminary and we should review again in Q2."

Variations: "That said..." / "With the caveat that..." / "Bearing in mind that..."

The Expertise Paradox

Some argue that deep expertise makes people worse at explaining things to non-experts. What's your view? Have you experienced this?

Target: 3-4 mins | Use: "On balance..." "That said..."
2

Remote Work Trade-offs

What have we genuinely gained and lost with the shift to remote/hybrid work? Go beyond the obvious productivity debate.

Target: 4-5 mins | Use: "It's conceivable..." "I'd caveat..."
3

Information Overload

We have more information than ever, but are we making better decisions? What's the relationship between information and wisdom?

Target: 4-5 mins | Use: "That's nuanced..." "Let me frame..."
4

The Generalist vs Specialist

In your field, is it better to be a deep specialist or a broad generalist? How might this change in the future?

Target: 3-4 mins | Use: "On balance..." "It's conceivable..."
5

Measuring What Matters

"Not everything that counts can be counted." How do we measure success in areas that resist quantification?

Target: 4-5 mins | Use: "That's layered..." "Put differently..."
6

The Feedback Culture

We're told constant feedback is essential for growth. But can there be too much feedback? When does it become counterproductive?

Target: 3-4 mins | Use: "I'd caveat..." "One could argue..."
Coaching Note

Deep Dive: Extended Speaking Challenge

Choose Your Challenge

Boardroom Briefing

You've been asked to brief senior leadership on a significant change in your industry. Explain what's happening, why it matters, what the implications are, and what you'd recommend — all while acknowledging uncertainty.

Target strategies: Nuanced framing, balanced assessment, caveating, speculation

The Difficult Question

Someone challenges you: "Your approach seems outdated. Why haven't you adapted to newer methods?" Respond thoughtfully — defend your position while acknowledging valid criticism and showing openness to change.

Target strategies: Reframing, balanced response, caveating, speculation about future

The Big Picture

A younger colleague asks: "What do you wish you'd known at the start of your career? What advice would you give?" Reflect meaningfully while acknowledging that your experience may not generalise.

Target strategies: Personal framing, caveating, speculation, balanced assessment
