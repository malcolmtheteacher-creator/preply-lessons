Key language in three steps

Three small groups of words. Guess the meaning first, then click to check.

① Finding a tenant

furnished / unfurnishedFurnished = comes with furniture. Unfurnished = empty, the tenant brings their own.
available from…The date the tenant can move in. "Available from 1st March."
a referenceA letter or contact from a previous landlord or employer saying the person is reliable.
SPEAK Describe a property you would advertise in 20 seconds. Use furnished and available from.

② Setting the terms

a tenancy agreementThe written contract: rent, length of stay, rules for both sides.
notice periodHow long before leaving (or asking someone to leave) you must tell the other person. "One month's notice."
bills included / excludedWhether electricity, water and internet are inside the rent, or paid separately by the tenant.
SPEAK Explain to a new tenant: how much the rent is, when it's due, and how much notice they need to give.

③ When there's a problem

wear and tearNormal ageing from everyday use (faded paint, a worn carpet) — not damage. You can't usually charge for it.
to carry out repairsTo fix something broken (or arrange for someone to fix it).
to fall behind with the rentTo not pay on time, so that you owe more and more.
SPEAK Give me an example of wear and tear, and an example of real damage.

Same message, different tone

A good landlord is clear and polite. Say each blunt sentence in a more professional way, then check.

too bluntprofessionaltoo soft

1. "Pay the rent on time."

Model: "I'd be grateful if the rent could be paid by the 1st of each month."

2. "No parties."

Model: "I'd appreciate it if you could keep the noise down after eleven at night."

3. "That's your problem, not mine."

Model: "I'm afraid that's the tenant's responsibility under the agreement, but I can recommend someone who could help."

4. "You owe me money."

Model: "I noticed this month's rent hasn't come through yet. Is everything all right? Could you let me know when I can expect it?"

Your rule

SPEAKLook at the four models. What do they have in common? Say the pattern in one sentence.

They use softening phrases (I'd be grateful if… / I'd appreciate it if… / I'm afraid… / Is everything all right?), they give a reason or a next step, and they still say clearly what is needed.

Fix the email

This message to a tenant has four mistakes. Rewrite it in the box, then compare.

Dear Sam,

I am writing for tell you that the plumber will come in Monday morning. Please to be at home between nine and twelve. If you will not be at home, you must to tell me before Friday.

Best wishes,
Your landlord

I am writing to tell you that the plumber will come on Monday morning. Please be at home between nine and twelve. If you will not be at home, you must tell me before Friday.

Four fixes: to + verb for purpose · on + day · no to after please · no to after must.

SPEAKWhy is "you must tell me" a bit strong? Say a softer version of that last sentence.

e.g. "Could you please let me know before Friday?" / "It would be great if you could let me know by Friday."

From earlier

SPEAKWhat are three ways to talk about the future in English? Give an example of each.

will ("I'll call you tomorrow") · going to ("I'm going to move next year") · present continuous for plans ("I'm meeting the tenant at five"). Also: present simple for timetables.

SPEAKWhat's the difference between "I used to live there" and "I'm used to living there"?

Used to + verb = a past habit or state that has stopped. Be used to + -ing = it feels normal and familiar now.

SPEAKHow do you make a polite request using "could" and "would"? Give two examples.

"Could you send me a photo?" · "Would you mind letting me know by Friday?" · "I was wondering if you could…"
