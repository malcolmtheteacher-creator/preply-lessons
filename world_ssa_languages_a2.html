Thousands of Languages: Multilingual by Default

Africa has about 2,000 languages. Many people speak three or more.

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. She can speak four languages.
speak (v) — to say words with your voice. Example: “She can speak four languages.”
2. At home, they speak Yoruba.
home (n) — the place where you live. Example: “At home, they speak Yoruba.”
3. At school, they learn in English.
school (n) — a place where children learn. Example: “At school, they learn in English.”
4. At the market, they speak Swahili.
market (n) — a place where people buy and sell things. Example: “At the market, they speak Swahili.”
5. Young people mix English and Swahili.
mix (v) — to put different things together. Example: “Young people mix English and Swahili.”
6. Children learn three languages.
learn (v) — to get new knowledge or skills. Example: “Children learn three languages.”

  • Which word is new for you? Say a sentence with it.
The stereotype: “Africans speak ‘African’.”

The story

Part 1 — Many languages

There is no language called “African”. Africa has about 2,000 languages. Nigeria alone has more than 500. Some languages, like Swahili and Hausa, have many millions of speakers. Some languages, like Zulu and Xhosa, have click sounds. Some languages, like Yoruba, use high and low voices to change the meaning.

Quick check:

1. How many languages does Africa have?

2. Which languages have click sounds?

1. About 2,000.

2. Zulu and Xhosa.

Discuss:
  • Which sounds are difficult in your language?

Part 2 — Home, market and school

Many people speak a different language in each place. At home, they speak their mother tongue. At the market, they speak a language like Swahili or Hausa. At school and at work, they often speak English, French or Portuguese. South Africa has twelve official languages.

Quick check:

1. Which language do people often speak at home?

2. How many official languages does South Africa have?

1. Their mother tongue.

2. Twelve.

Discuss:
  • Which language do you use at work?

Part 3 — Mixing languages

In Nairobi, young people mix Swahili and English. This is called Sheng. In Nigeria, millions of people speak Pidgin English. It is not bad language. It is a clever way to speak and to make jokes. Many people want to learn in their home language at school, too.

Quick check:

1. What is Sheng?

2. Is mixing languages bad?

1. A mix of Swahili and English.

2. No, it is clever and creative.

Discuss:
  • Do you mix languages with your friends?

Grammar — Which, What and How many questions

How many + plural noun: “How many languages do you speak?”
Which for a choice: “Which language do you use at home?”
What for a question about a thing: “What is Sheng?”

1. Choose the word

1. languages does Nigeria have? (How many / Which)

2. language do you speak at home? (Which / How many)

3. is Sheng? (What / How many)
