The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. Scotland agreed to a huge ransom.
a ransom (n) — money paid to free a prisoner.
2. During his minority, guardians ruled.
a minority (n) — the time when a king is a child.
3. He fled to France in 1334.
to flee (v) — to run away from danger.
4. Scottish nobles became hostages.
a hostage (n) — a person held to guarantee a promise.
5. The ransom was a heavy burden.
a burden (n) — something difficult to carry.
6. He negotiated with Edward III.
to negotiate (v) — to discuss to reach agreement.
7. He died childless.
childless (adj) — without children.
8. He was wounded in the battle.
wounded (adj) — injured.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — A child king

David, Bruce's only surviving son, was married at four to Joan, sister of Edward III of England, in 1328. He became king at five in 1329. After English-backed invasions, he was sent to safety in France in 1334, and he used to live at Château Gaillard in Normandy. He returned in 1341.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How old was David when he became king?

2. Where did he live in the 1330s?

1. Five.

2. In France, at Château Gaillard.

Discuss:
  • What is it like to grow up as a king in exile?

Part 2 — Neville's Cross

In 1346, to help his French allies, David invaded England. At Neville's Cross near Durham his army was defeated. He was wounded — tradition says by arrows in the face — and captured. For eleven years he was a prisoner in England.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did he invade in 1346?

2. What happened to him?

1. To help France, Scotland's ally.

2. He was wounded, captured and held for eleven years.

Discuss:
  • Was the invasion brave or foolish?

Part 3 — The ransom

In 1357 the Treaty of Berwick freed him for a ransom of 100,000 merks, paid in instalments. Payments were heavy and often late. David even discussed accepting an English heir in exchange for cancelling the ransom; the Scottish parliament refused. He died in 1371 without children, and the Bruce line ended.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How much was the ransom?

2. What did parliament refuse?

1. 100,000 merks.

2. David's idea of an English heir in exchange for cancelling the ransom.

Discuss:
  • Was David wrong to negotiate an English heir?

Grammar — Used to & Would — Past Habits

To describe how life used to be — the habits and customs of a reign — English uses used to and would.

How it works:
• used to + verb for past habits and states: "People used to believe the king could heal."
• would + verb for repeated actions only (not states): "Every Christmas he would hold court."
• Negative: didn't use to.

1. used to or would?

Complete each sentence about David II. Use the word in brackets.

1. As a boy, David (live) in France. (used to)

2. In prison, he (receive) visitors. (would)

3. Scotland (pay) the ransom in instalments every year. (would)

1. used to live

2. would receive

3. would pay

2. Complete with the correct form

1. He (be) a prisoner — later he was free. (used to)

2. He (not trust) the English nobles. (didn't use to)

3. Collectors (travel) around raising money. (would)

1. used to be

2. didn't use to trust

3. would travel

Going deeper

David's reign was once dismissed as weak, but historians now see him as a tough negotiator who rebuilt royal power after captivity.

Worth noticing: A merk was a Scottish unit of money worth two-thirds of a pound.
Learning from the past — for today: Debts shape futures. Scotland paid for one bad afternoon for decades. Before a risky decision, ask: if this fails, how long will I be paying for it?
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Should Scotland have paid?
  • Was David a failure?
  • What "ransom" have you paid for a mistake?
