The Challenge: Speaking Confidently About the Uncertain

Advanced speakers face a particular problem: the more you know about something, the more you recognise what you don't know.

The Paradox

Beginners say "I think" and "maybe" constantly. Experts say it even more — but they say it strategically to signal intellectual rigour, not weakness.

Compare these two speakers discussing industry trends:

Question: "What do you think will happen to your industry in the next 5 years?"

Speaker A: "Maybe AI will change things? I'm not sure. It could go either way."

Speaker B: "That's a fascinating question. Were AI adoption to accelerate as I suspect it will, we'd see significant disruption. In all likelihood, we'll see consolidation within 24 months. I wouldn't rule out some companies pivoting entirely, though."

Both are expressing uncertainty, but one sounds evasive, the other sounds thoughtful. The difference? Precision in how you frame the uncertainty.

Today's Focus

You'll master 5 sophisticated strategies for speculation that let you discuss futures, hypotheticals, and probabilities with precision — and with credibility.

This matters because at C1/C2 level, you're expected to discuss complex ideas where absolute certainty doesn't exist. Mastering speculative language is how you navigate that space without sounding wishy-washy.

Your Toolkit: 5 Speculation Strategies

Click each strategy to explore its use. Practice each one before moving on.

"It's entirely possible that..." Click to expand
Function
Acknowledges a scenario while staying neutral
Certainty Level
Medium-low (25-40% probability)
When to Use
Scenarios that could happen but aren't your prediction
In a business context:

"It's entirely possible that competitors could undercut us on price, though that's not the scenario I'm betting on."

Spectrum of possibility: "It's possible..." → "It's entirely possible..." → "It's conceivable..." → "It's plausible..."

🎤 Try It Now

Discuss your industry: "It's entirely possible that [scenario]. However, what I think is more likely is..."

"Were that to happen..." Click to expand
Function
Introduces hypotheticals with formal distance
Certainty Level
Conditional / hypothetical (explores "if-then")
When to Use
Exploring consequences, scenarios, contingencies
Discussing potential changes:

"Were regulations to tighten, we'd need to completely restructure our compliance operations."

Note: This is formal conditional language — particularly strong in professional settings. It signals sophisticated thinking.

🎤 Try It Now

Hypothetical: "Were you to start your career over, what would you do differently?"

"In all likelihood..." Click to expand
Function
Expresses confident prediction while acknowledging uncertainty
Certainty Level
Medium-high (60-75% probability)
When to Use
Your main prediction, what you think will happen
Predicting trends:

"In all likelihood, we'll see interest rates rise another 50 basis points by Q2."

Variations: "In all probability..." / "Most likely..." / "By and large..." / "Presumably..."

🎤 Try It Now

Make a prediction about trends you notice: "In all likelihood, the next 5 years will see..."

"I wouldn't rule out..." Click to expand
Function
Hedges against dismissing less likely scenarios
Certainty Level
Low-medium (15-35% probability)
When to Use
Outlier scenarios, tail risks, possibilities you're open to
Risk assessment:

"We're targeting steady growth, but I wouldn't rule out an aggressive acquisition if the right opportunity emerged."

Variations: "I wouldn't dismiss..." / "I wouldn't exclude..." / "I wouldn't overlook..."

🎤 Try It Now

Discuss possibilities: "My prediction is X, but I wouldn't rule out Y if circumstances changed."

"The way I see it playing out is..." Click to expand
Function
Frames prediction as your interpretation while staying measured
Certainty Level
Personal perspective (70%+ confidence in your view)
When to Use
Predictions based on your expertise, scenario planning
Drawing on experience:

"The way I see it playing out is that we'll initially see resistance from stakeholders, then adoption will accelerate once early wins are visible."

Variations: "The way I imagine it..." / "The trajectory I foresee..." / "My best read on how this unfolds..."

Predict a future scenario in detail: "The way I see it playing out is..."

Coaching Note

Listen for natural integration of speculative language. Is the speaker precise about degrees of certainty, or does everything sound equally probable? Does the language reflect genuine thinking, or sound rehearsed?

Deep Dive: Extended Speaking Challenge

Now speak for 5-7 minutes on one of these challenging prompts. The goal is to sustain complex speculation while grounding it in evidence and logic.

5:00

Choose Your Challenge

The Prediction

You're asked to brief leadership on what you think will happen in your market or sector over the next 3-5 years. Build your case: what trends are you seeing? What's your baseline prediction? What wild cards might change everything?

Target strategies: "In all likelihood..." "I wouldn't rule out..." "Were..." "The way I see it..."

The Counterfactual

A colleague proposes: "The biggest decision we made was choosing to enter the market when we did. But what if we'd waited 5 years?" Explore this scenario: what would be different? Better? Worse? Why? What does this tell us about timing and luck?

Target strategies: "Were that to have..." "It's entirely possible..." "In all likelihood..."

The Advice Question

A junior colleague asks: "What advice would you give someone starting out in this field today? How do you think their path will be different from yours was?" Speculate thoughtfully about changes and constants.

Target strategies: "The way I see it..." "In all likelihood..." "I wouldn't rule out..."

Self-Monitoring

Notice: Do you stick to one certainty level or do you vary it appropriately? Are you using speculative language to mask weak thinking, or to add precision? Can you shift smoothly between "possible," "likely," and "certain"?
