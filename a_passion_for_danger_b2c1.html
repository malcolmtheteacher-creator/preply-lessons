Multi-Word Verbs

Instructions: Match each multi-word verb with its definition. Click a verb, then click its matching definition.

Multi-Word Verbs

1 check out
2 deal with
3 get into
4 get on for
5 get to
6 go into
7 go on
8 move on to
9 point out
10 set up

Definitions

a arrange for an activity or event to take place
b become interested or start being involved in an activity
c change the topic of conversation
d continue doing something
e explain
f get nearer (a time or number)
g go to see
h (of time, money, or effort) be used when producing or doing
i reach, arrive at
j take action so as to solve a problem or achieve something

✓ Answer Key

1. check out→g. go to see
2. deal with→j. take action to solve a problem
3. get into→b. become interested/involved in
4. get on for→f. get nearer (time/number)
5. get to→i. reach, arrive at
6. go into→h. be used when producing/doing
7. go on→d. continue doing something
8. move on to→c. change the topic
9. point out→e. explain
10. set up→a. arrange for an event

Using the Multi-Word Verbs

Instructions: Discuss these questions with your partner. Try to use the multi-word verbs naturally in your answers. Click each card for follow-up prompts.

If you had more free time, which sports or activities would you like to get into? Why?

Follow-up: What's stopping you from getting into it now? What would you need to start?

What problems have you had to deal with recently at work / during your studies / at home?

Follow-up: How did you deal with it? Was it difficult? Would you do anything differently?

Which countries would you like to check out? Why?

Follow-up: What specific places would you want to check out there? Have you checked out any new places recently?

Do you think enough public money goes into sports and sport education in your country? Why / why not?

Follow-up: What other areas should more money go into? How much time goes into your hobbies?

Do you know any English expressions you can use to politely move on to another topic during a conversation?

Follow-up: Examples: "Anyway...", "Speaking of which...", "That reminds me...", "By the way..."

Do you plan to go on learning English next year?

Follow-up: What specific goals do you have? How will you go on improving your English?

The Debate: Adventurers in Trouble

"There are many people who are getting fed up with adventurers who get themselves into trouble and then have to be rescued. That can mean complicated, time-consuming missions involving the coast guard and even the navy, which can put others' lives at risk and end up costing governments – and therefore tax-payers – millions of pounds."

Discussion questions:

  • Do you agree with this viewpoint? Why or why not?
  • Should adventurers have to pay for their own rescue?
  • Should extreme sports be more regulated?
  • Where do you draw the line between personal freedom and public responsibility?
  • Can you think of any famous rescue missions that cost a lot of money?
