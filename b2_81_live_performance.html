Use these phrases to structure your proposal effectively.

• The purpose of this project is to…

• This proposal has been written to demonstrate…

Making Suggestions

• One possibility would be to…

• Another idea would be to…

• It would be useful to…

Finishing Your Proposal

• On the whole…

• All in all…

• In conclusion, it appears that…

Linking Words and Phrases

These words help you join sentences and ideas smoothly in your proposal.

Showing Contrast

1. "In spite of being old, the museum still attracts visitors."

Linking phrase: In spite of

Use this to show contrast between two ideas.

2. "Even though the number of visitors has fallen, the museum has expansion plans."

Linking phrase: Even though

Similar to "although," this introduces an unexpected contrast.

3. "Despite opening a new wing, visitor numbers failed to increase."

Linking phrase: Despite

Use "despite" before a noun or noun phrase.

Adding Related Ideas

4. "As well as thinking it was cold and dull, people thought it was boring."

Linking phrase: As well as

Use this to add multiple related ideas or criticisms.

Showing Opposition

5. "Whereas some people think it is the best museum, others think it is the worst."

Linking phrase: Whereas

Use this to compare different viewpoints or groups.

Using Linking Words in Your Proposal

Linking Phrase Function Example in Proposal
Although Show contrast "Although a café is expensive, it increases visitor satisfaction."
On the other hand Present alternative "The museum is popular. On the other hand, funding is limited."
For this reason Show cause/effect "Parking is important. For this reason, we recommend a garage."

What Makes a Museum Popular?

Think about what features make museums attractive to visitors.

Features That Attract Visitors

Put these reasons in order from most to least important:

  • Modern space with good facilities for families and lots of places to sit and rest
  • A good café or restaurant and an interesting shop to buy souvenirs
  • Clear information on exhibits in a number of languages
  • Regular guided tours of the exhibits
  • Interactive exhibits that allow hands-on learning

Discussion Questions

Why are these features important?

Think about how each feature improves the visitor experience and affects museum popularity.

Which features would you prioritize?

Consider which would have the greatest impact on making your cultural centre successful.

Developing a Cultural Centre Concept

When planning a cultural centre, consider:

  • Cultural education: What types of art and culture will you showcase?
  • Performance space: Will you include dance, theatre, music, or other performances?
  • Exhibitions: What kinds of exhibits will attract your community?
  • Community features: Cafés, shops, spaces for socializing
  • Accessibility: How will you ensure people of all ages and abilities can enjoy it?

Planning Your Proposal

The Scenario

Your local council has invited proposals for ideas on how to adapt an empty warehouse in your city into a cultural centre for the community.

Step 1: Brainstorm with Your Group

Think about and discuss:

  • What types of cultural activities does your community need?
  • Would you include dance, theatre, music, or exhibitions?
  • What other features would make the space popular?
  • How would you make it accessible and welcoming?

Step 2: Plan Your Proposal Structure

Create a paragraph plan for your proposal. Include:

Paragraph Purpose What to Include
1. Introduction State the purpose What the proposal is about and why the cultural centre is needed
2. Background Provide context Information about the warehouse and the community's needs
3. Recommendations Suggest features Specific ideas for the cultural centre and why they're important
4. Conclusion Summarize impact How the centre will benefit the community

Step 3: Discuss and Compare Plans

With your group or partner, discuss:

  • Is there enough background information?
  • Are the recommendations clear and practical?
  • Is there a clear outcome or benefit?
  • What's the next step after approval?

Step 4: Use Formal Language

Remember to use:

  • Proposal phrases: "The purpose of this project is…" "One possibility would be…"
  • Linking words: Despite, Although, In spite of, Furthermore
  • Professional tone: Formal, organized, impersonal style
  • Appropriate vocabulary: "facilities," "recommendations," "enhance," "sustainable"

Success Criteria

Your proposal is successful if it includes:

  • ✓ Relevant and appropriate recommendations for the centre
  • ✓ Clear organization with appropriate headings for each section
  • ✓ Appropriately impersonal and formal style
  • ✓ Good range of vocabulary and structures
  • ✓ Variety of linking phrases to connect ideas

Tips for Success

Make recommendations specific

Instead of "add a café," say "add a café serving local food to generate revenue and encourage visitors to stay longer."

Address potential concerns

Use "although" and "despite" to acknowledge limitations: "Although renovation costs are high, the long-term community benefit justifies the investment."

Think about your audience

Remember you're writing to a council, so focus on community benefit, feasibility, and impact.
