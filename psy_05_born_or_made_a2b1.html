The two Jims: true or made up?

In 1979, two identical twins met for the first time. They were 39 years old. Different families took them home when they were babies. People wrote about the things they had in common. Some things below are real and some are made up for this game. Which are real?

1. Both men had a dog called Toy.
Real, according to the news.
2. Both men played the violin.
Made up for this game.
3. Both men were called Jim.
Real. Their two families did not know about each other.
4. Both men married a woman called Linda first.
Real, according to the news. Later, both married a woman called Betty.
5. They met at school when they were ten.
Made up. They did not meet until they were 39.
6. Both men named their son James Alan.
Real, according to the news.

The evidence, in three steps

Before each part, make a prediction out loud. Then read, and check yourself.

Part 1 — The twins

Before you read: Two identical twins grow up in different families. They meet after 39 years. Do you think they are very similar, a little similar, or not similar?

In 1979, two men in the USA met for the first time. Their names were Jim Lewis and Jim Springer. They were identical twins. When they were four weeks old, two different families took them home. Neither of them knew about the other for 39 years.

When the brothers met, people were surprised. News reports said that both men liked the same things. They both worked in similar jobs, and each man had a dog with the same name. Of course, some of these stories were just luck.

twins (n) — two babies born together.
Which word means 'exactly the same'? (i________)
identical (adj) — Example: "They are identical twins. They look exactly the same."

Part 2 — The big study

Before you read: Scientists tested 100 pairs of twins who grew up apart. Which twins do you think were more alike: identical twins or non-identical twins?

A scientist at the University of Minnesota, Thomas Bouchard, studied more than 100 pairs of twins who grew up apart. They did many tests with them: tests of intelligence, personality and interests.

The identical twins were more similar than non-identical twins. Identical twins have exactly the same genes. Non-identical twins have about half the same genes, like normal brothers and sisters. So the scientists thought genes were important for many things, including intelligence and personality.

New words in this part — say what you think each one means, then reveal.

Your genes are the information in your body that comes from your parents.
genes (n) — small parts in your body's cells that decide things like the colour of your eyes.
Your personality is the kind of person you are: shy, friendly, funny…
personality (n) — the way you think, feel and behave.
Your call:
  • Are you surprised that twins who grew up apart are similar? Why, or why not?

Part 3 — Not only genes

Before you read: Can genes decide everything about a person? What else changes us?

But the story is more complicated. Some twins grew up in similar families, and some twins shared the same life before birth. Also, genes do not decide everything. A gene for being tall will not make you tall if you do not eat enough food.

Today most scientists say: it is not 'nature or nurture'. It is both. Genes make some things easier or harder, and the environment changes what happens. Height is mostly about genes. Language is about environment: you speak the language of the people around you.

New words in this part — say what you think each one means, then reveal.

Environment means the world around you: your family, your school and your friends.
What does environment mean here? (a) the weather only   (b) everything around you while you grow up   (c) your house only
environment (n) — answer (b). Everything around you that can change you.
Which word means 'to grow up in a family or place'? (b_____)
brought up / raised — Example: "She was brought up by her grandparents."
Your call:
  • What in your life is mostly 'nature', and what is mostly 'nurture'?

Comparing things

Use these phrases to say how two people are the same or different.

Same:
• the same as — Jim's job was the same as his brother's.
• similar to — Their personalities are similar to each other.
• as + adjective + as — He is as tall as his twin.
Different:
• different from — Non-identical twins can be different from each other.
• comparative + than — Identical twins are more similar than other brothers.
• not as + adjective + as — She is not as shy as her sister.

Nature or nurture?

Five things about a person. Choose where each one comes from, then say why in one sentence. Check an idea last. Your idea can be different.

1. The colour of your eyes.
Idea. Nature. Genes decide this.
2. Speaking English.
Idea. Nurture. You learn it from people around you.
3. Being good at music.
Idea. Both. Some people find it easier, but practice is very important.
4. Being shy.
Idea. Both. Some babies are born shy, but family and friends change it.
5. Your favourite food.
Idea. Mostly nurture: you eat what you grew up with.
