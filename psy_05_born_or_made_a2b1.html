Born or Made?

Twins who grew up apart are very similar. Why? Genes or life?

← All the Psychology Questions

Today: you'll explain how twins help scientists understand people, and compare things with 'the same as', 'different from', 'similar to' and 'more than'.
Warm up — have a go, and don't worry about mistakes. Say whatever comes to mind.
  • Who in your family are you most like? In what ways?
  • Do you think you are more like your parents because of your genes, or because of how you grew up?
  • Do you know any twins? Are they the same or different?

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, answer the quick check from memory first, then take the discussion question.

Part 1 — The twins

In 1979, two men in the USA met for the first time. Their names were Jim Lewis and Jim Springer. They were identical twins. When they were four weeks old, two different families took them home. Neither of them knew about the other for 39 years.

When the brothers met, people were surprised. News reports said that both men liked the same things. They both worked in similar jobs, and each man had a dog with the same name. Of course, some of these stories were just luck.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How old were the twins when they were separated?

2. When did they meet?

1. Four weeks old.

2. After 39 years, in 1979.

Discuss:
  • Imagine you meet your twin after 39 years. What do you want to ask?

Part 2 — The big study

A scientist at the University of Minnesota, Thomas Bouchard, studied more than 100 pairs of twins who grew up apart. They did many tests with them: tests of intelligence, personality and interests.

The identical twins were more similar than non-identical twins. Identical twins have exactly the same genes. Non-identical twins have about half the same genes, like normal brothers and sisters. So the scientists thought genes were important for many things, including intelligence and personality.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did the scientists test?

2. Which twins were more similar?

1. Intelligence, personality and interests.

2. Identical twins.

Discuss:
  • Are you surprised that twins who grew up apart are similar?

Part 3 — Not only genes

But the story is more complicated. Some twins grew up in similar families, and some twins shared the same life before birth. Also, genes do not decide everything. A gene for being tall will not make you tall if you do not eat enough food.

Today most scientists say: it is not 'nature or nurture'. It is both. Genes make some things easier or harder, and the environment changes what happens. Height is mostly about genes. Language is about environment: you speak the language of the people around you.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What do most scientists say today?

2. Give one example of genes and one of environment.

1. It is both nature and nurture.

2. Height is mostly genes; language comes from the environment.

Discuss:
  • What in your life is mostly 'nature' and what is mostly 'nurture'?

Grammar — comparing things

Use these phrases to say how two people or things are the same or different.

Same:
• the same as — Jim's job was the same as his brother's.
• similar to — Their personalities are similar to each other.
• as + adjective + as — He is as tall as his twin.
Different:
• different from — Non-identical twins can be different from each other.
• comparative + than — Identical twins are more similar than other brothers.
• not as + adjective + as — She is not as shy as her sister.

Nature or nurture?

Five things. Say whether they come from genes (nature), from life (nurture) or both. Then reveal an idea.

1. The colour of your eyes.
Idea. Nature. Genes decide this.
2. Speaking English.
Idea. Nurture. You learn it from people around you.
3. Being good at music.
Idea. Both. Some people find it easier, but practice is very important.
4. Being shy.
Idea. Both. Some babies are born shy, but family and friends change it.
5. Your favourite food.
Idea. Mostly nurture: you eat what you grew up with.
