Open the model answer

Write your own essay first, then open this. There is no single right answer: your position and ideas can be different and still score well.

Model answer 300 words

Children need to learn how to behave as responsible members of society, but people disagree about who should teach them. Some believe this is mainly the role of parents, while others think schools are better placed to do it. In my view, parents should lay the foundations, but schools play an essential part in developing these values further.

Those who see this as the parents’ job argue that values are learnt most deeply at home. Children spend their earliest and most formative years with their families, and they learn by copying what they see. A child whose parents greet their neighbours, share household chores and treat others with respect is likely to see these behaviours as normal. Furthermore, parents can adapt their guidance to their own child’s character and deal with problems immediately, which a teacher with thirty pupils cannot always do.

On the other hand, there are strong reasons to give schools this responsibility. At school, children must cooperate with people from different backgrounds, follow shared rules and resolve conflicts without their parents’ help, which is much closer to life in the wider community. Schools can also teach civic knowledge, such as how laws are made and why voting matters, which many parents are not equipped to explain. In addition, not every child grows up in a supportive home, and school may be the only place where some children learn fairness and responsibility. For these reasons, I believe schools are not a substitute for parents but a vital second influence.

In conclusion, both parents and schools help children become good members of society. While families provide the first and most lasting example, schools give children the chance to practise these values in a community and to learn how society works. The best results come when the two work together.

Why this answer works

  • Both views get a full paragraph: each one has its own reasons and an example, written fairly (“Those who see this as… argue that…”).
  • The writer’s own opinion is clear throughout: stated briefly in the introduction, explained at the end of body paragraph 2, and confirmed in the conclusion.
  • The opinion is not just “one side wins”: a balanced view (parents first, school as a second influence) is fine, as long as it is clear.
  • Specific examples instead of general claims: greeting neighbours, sharing chores, a teacher with thirty pupils, how laws are made.
