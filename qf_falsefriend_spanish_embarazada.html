😳 Embarrassed ≠ Embarazada

A2 • Spanish speakers • The most confusing false friend!

🔍 Let's notice what we see

Look at this sentence. What's the problem?

"I was very embarrassed at the party."

(But they're saying something VERY different than they mean!)

What emotion do they REALLY want to describe? What's going wrong here?

⚠️ The BIGGEST False Friend:

The Spanish word "embarazada" looks exactly like English "embarrassed", but they mean COMPLETELY DIFFERENT things!

English: embarrassed = avergonzado/a (feeling shy/awkward because you made a mistake)

Spanish: embarazada = pregnant (expecting a baby — de parto = giving birth)

🚨 This is the #1 mistake Spanish speakers make in English!

🛠️ Understand the difference

😳 EMBARRASSED

😳🙈
Meaning: Feel shy, awkward, or ashamed when others notice a mistake you made
Example: "I tripped in front of everyone — I was so embarrassed!"

🤰 PREGNANT (embarazada)

🤰👶
Meaning: Expecting a baby; carrying a child in your belly
Example: "She announced she was pregnant and will have a baby in June."
💡 The Key Difference:

Embarrassed = about your feelings (an emotion)

Pregnant = about your body (a physical condition)

🔗 Related: Ashamed vs Embarrassed

Embarrassed 😳
Lighter feeling. You made a small social mistake or did something awkward in front of others.
Ashamed 😞
Stronger feeling. You did something morally wrong or against your values. Deeper guilt.

🚨 Other Spanish false friends

Watch out for these too!

asistir → means → attend (NOT assist)

Example: "I attended the meeting" (asistí a la reunión)

éxito → means → success (NOT exit)

Example: "Great success!" (¡Mucho éxito!)
