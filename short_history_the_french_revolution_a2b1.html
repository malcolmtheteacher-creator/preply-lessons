The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A king, and a country with no money

In the 1780s, France had a king. His name was Louis XVI. The king had all the power. There was no vote and no parliament with real power. The king did what he wanted.

The rich people — the nobles and the church — had a lot of land. But they paid almost no tax. So the poor people paid nearly all the tax. This was not fair, and people were angry.

The country had no money because the king spent too much on wars. Then the harvests were bad, so there was not enough food. The price of bread went up and up. Bread was the main food of the poor, so many families were hungry. People were poor, they were hungry, and they were angry with the king.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who paid most of the tax in France, and who paid almost nothing?

2. Why were so many people hungry?

1. The poor people paid most of the tax. The rich nobles and the church paid almost nothing.

2. The harvests were bad, so there was not enough food. The price of bread went up, and the poor could not buy enough.

Talk about it:
  • The rich paid no tax, and the poor paid a lot. Why do you think this went on for so many years?

Part 2 — The people take the Bastille

In the summer of 1789, the people of Paris were very worried. They thought the king wanted to send soldiers to stop them. So they went to look for guns to protect themselves.

In the east of Paris there was an old fortress called the Bastille. For a long time it was a prison. The king could put people in it with no trial — just because he said so. So for the people, the Bastille was a symbol of the king's power. There was gunpowder inside, so a big crowd went there on 14 July 1789.

The crowd asked the soldiers to give them the gunpowder. The soldiers said no. Then there was fighting, and some people died on both sides. After some hours, the soldiers gave up. The crowd went in. The king's great fortress had fallen to the people. This was very important, so today 14 July is still the national day of France.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did the crowd go to the Bastille?

2. Why was 14 July 1789 so important?

1. The people were afraid of the king's soldiers, so they looked for guns. There was gunpowder inside the Bastille, so they went there.

2. The people took the king's great fortress. It showed the people could beat the king's power. Today it is the national day of France.

Talk about it:
  • The Bastille was almost empty, but taking it was very important. Why can a symbol be so powerful?

Part 3 — The end of the king

After the Bastille, things changed fast. The leaders wrote a paper called the Declaration of the Rights of Man. It said that all men are born free and equal. This was a big new idea.

But the king did not really want the change. In 1791, he tried to leave the country in secret. The people caught him and brought him back. Now many people did not trust the king at all.

In 1792, France said it did not want a king any more. France became a republic — a country with no king. The next year, in 1793, the king was put on trial and then killed. A thousand years of kings in France ended. In the years after, there was a lot of fear and killing. But the big ideas of the revolution stayed: that people are free, that people are equal, and that power comes from the people, not from a king.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did the Declaration of the Rights of Man say?

2. What happened to the king in the end?

1. It said that all men are born free and equal. It was a big new idea.

2. He tried to leave the country but the people caught him. France became a republic, and in 1793 the king was put on trial and killed.

Talk about it:
  • The revolution started with a good idea — everyone is free and equal — but there was also a lot of killing. How can both things be true?

Grammar: past simple + because / so

This is a story from the past, so we use the past simple. And to say why things happened, we use two small but powerful words: because and so.

Past simple = a finished action in the past. Many verbs add -ed: "The price of bread rose." "The crowd walked to the Bastille." Some verbs are irregular: go → went, take → took, be → was / were, have → had.
because gives the reason. It answers "Why?": "The people were angry because they were hungry." (reason = they were hungry)
so gives the result. It answers "What happened next?": "The people were hungry, so they were angry." (result = they were angry)
Feel the difference:
• reason + because + reason → "They went to the Bastille because there was gunpowder inside."
• cause + so + result → "There was gunpowder inside, so they went to the Bastille."
