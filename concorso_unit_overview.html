🧩 SEN Provision — consistent across both lessons

Student 1 — Dyslexia (DSA)

  • Colour-coded materials, min 14pt font throughout
  • Image + bullet-point jigsaw card (L1) — reduced text load
  • Word bank on desk for both lessons
  • Debate phrase card remains on desk in L2 (permanent scaffold)
  • No cold-calling in either lesson
  • Open floor contribution in debate — self-selected timing
  • Exit ticket can be typed rather than handwritten
  • Teacher reads aloud during text presentation (L1)

Student 2 — Disruptive Behaviour

  • Strategic seating near teacher in both lessons
  • Structured role in every activity — never unguided
  • L1: pre-assigned as jigsaw expert who "goes first" in home group
  • L2: pre-assigned as FOR opening speaker — high status, clear task
  • Movement built into both lessons (jigsaw phase transitions, debate setup)
  • Short chunked instructions throughout
  • Immediate positive reinforcement after structured contributions

❓ Key panel questions — unit-level answers

🔄
Why did you use two different methodologies?
Methodology should serve the objective. Lesson 1 aimed to build content knowledge — PPP+CLIL was appropriate because students needed new concepts before they could use them. Lesson 2 aimed to develop communicative competence — CLT was the right choice because the debate IS the learning. Selecting different approaches for different objectives shows pedagogical awareness, not inconsistency.
📚
How do the two lessons connect?
Lesson 1 is the foundation for Lesson 2. The five jigsaw expert cards become the evidence base for the debate — students walk into Lesson 2 already armed with arguments. The unit follows Bloom's Taxonomy: Lesson 1 operates at the Remember/Understand levels; Lesson 2 operates at Analyse and Evaluate. Together they form a complete learning arc from knowledge to critical judgment.
🌍
How does this unit reflect the CEFR action-oriented approach?
In Lesson 1, the jigsaw creates a genuine information gap — students are social agents who must communicate to complete a real task. In Lesson 2, the debate is a real act of persuasion — students are social agents trying to convince the room. Neither lesson treats language as an end in itself; in both, students use language to accomplish a meaningful communicative purpose.
📖
How does this unit link to the Indicazioni Nazionali?
The unit develops plurilingual competence, intercultural awareness, and critical thinking — all central to the Indicazioni Nazionali for Liceo linguistico. The British Empire topic creates a natural CLIL connection with history, which the curriculum explicitly supports. It develops two competenze chiave europee: communication in foreign languages, and social and civic competence — students must engage critically with contested historical narratives.
