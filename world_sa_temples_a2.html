Temple Manners: Shoes Off, Feet Down

Temples are living places with simple rules. What are they?

Today: you will learn how to behave at a temple, and use must and mustn't.
Talk first:
  • Have you visited a temple or church abroad?
  • What do you wear there?
  • Why do places have rules?

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. The temple is quiet and calm.
temple (n) — a building for prayer. Example: “The temple is quiet and calm.”
2. Take off your shoes at the door.
shoes (n) — things you wear on your feet. Example: “Take off your shoes at the door.”
3. A monk walked past.
monk (n) — a man who lives in a religious group. Example: “A monk walked past.”
4. People pray every day.
pray (v) — to talk to a god. Example: “People pray every day.”
5. Please be quiet inside.
quiet (adj) — not noisy. Example: “Please be quiet inside.”
6. Cover your shoulders and knees.
cover (v) — to put clothes over your body. Example: “Cover your shoulders and knees.”
Talk it through:
  • Which word is new for you? Say a sentence with it.
The stereotype: “Temples in Asia are tourist places with strange rules.”

The story

Part 1 — A living place

Temples are not only for tourists. People go there every day. They bring flowers, fruit and incense. They pray. In Bali, families put small flower offerings on the ground every morning. Angkor Wat in Cambodia is the biggest religious building in the world. People still pray there.

Quick check:

1. Who goes to temples every day?

2. Where is Angkor Wat?

1. People who pray.

2. In Cambodia.

Discuss:
  • Is there a place like this in your country?

Part 2 — Simple rules

Visitors must take off their shoes at the door. They must cover their shoulders and knees. They must not point their feet at a Buddha statue. They must not touch monks. Visitors can usually take photos outside, but not always inside.

Quick check:

1. What must visitors take off?

2. What must visitors cover?

1. Their shoes.

2. Their shoulders and knees.

Discuss:
  • Which rule is easy for you?

Part 3 — The ideas

The ground outside is dirty, and the floor inside is clean and holy. The head is the highest part of the body, and the feet are the lowest. Good clothes show respect. These rules are easy to understand when you know the idea behind them.

Quick check:

1. Why do people take off their shoes?

2. Which part of the body is the lowest?

1. The floor inside is clean and holy.

2. The feet.

Discuss:
  • What rules does your culture have for a holy place?

Grammar — must and mustn't

must = it is necessary: “You must take off your shoes.”
mustn't = it is not allowed: “You mustn't point your feet at a statue.”
Use the base verb after them: must go, mustn't touch.
