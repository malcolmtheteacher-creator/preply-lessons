Playing a Different Game

When a rival is bigger, richer and better established, the smartest move is often to stop playing on their ground.

A problem every challenger has

You run a small company. A giant already owns the market: more money, more customers, more everything. If you copy the giant, you will lose. So what can you do?

Their groundBig budgets · famous brand · every shop and screen · years of habits
A new groundA place, a set of rules or a moment they have not prepared for

Three companies, three surprising moves

Read each short case. Before you reveal, predict what the challenger changed.

🎬 A film-rental chain had thousands of stores. A newcomer began posting DVDs to people’s homes in 1998, and later moved to streaming in 2007.

SPEAK: What did the newcomer change?

It changed the arena: customers no longer had to go to a shop at all. Stores, the incumbent’s biggest asset, became a weakness.

🏠 A start-up offered travellers somewhere to stay in 2008. It owns no hotels, yet it lists millions of places to sleep.

SPEAK: What did it change?

It changed the rules of what a “hotel business” is: it sells access to other people’s rooms, so it never has to build or maintain buildings.

✈️ A budget airline often chose smaller, cheaper airports and sold tickets at very low prices, while others fought for the biggest hubs.

SPEAK: What did it change?

It changed the arena again — and the rules of pricing. Skipping the expensive hubs lowered its costs, so it could undercut rivals on price.

Three ways to change the game

🗺️Change the arenaCompete somewhere the leader is weak: a different place, channel or customer group.
📏Change the rulesOffer something that makes the leader’s strengths less important: new pricing, new product, new model.
⏱️Change the timingMove before the leader is ready — or wait until they have made a costly mistake.

Which move is it?

Say your answer, then reveal. There may be more than one good answer — explain your reasoning.

A small bank refuses to open branches and offers everything in an app.

Arena (no branches, a different channel) and rules (a different cost structure). The big banks’ buildings stop being an advantage.

A new phone-maker launches one month before the leader’s annual event, when journalists are hungry for news.

Timing. It borrows attention at a moment the leader cannot control.

A local software firm gives its product away free and charges only for support.

Rules. The leader’s expensive licences become harder to defend.

-ever clauses at work

You met these in the story. In business they help you make promises, policies and plans that hold in every situation.

WordMeaningIn business
whoeverany person who…“Whoever joins us will get a full induction.”
whateveranything that… / no matter what“Whatever the market does, our prices will stay the same.”
whereverin any place…“Wherever we open, we hire local staff.”
wheneverat any time…“Call me whenever a client has a problem.”
however + adjective / adverbno matter how…“However tough the talks become, we stay polite.”

What do all these clauses have in common?

SPEAK: Give your own rule in one sentence before you reveal.

They remove limits. “It doesn’t matter who / what / where / when / how” — and the main clause stays true in every case. The -ever clause usually comes first and is followed by a comma.

Rewrite it

Say a new sentence with an -ever clause that keeps the meaning, then check.

a) It doesn’t matter who the customer is; they all get the same guarantee.

“Whoever the customer is, they get the same guarantee.”

b) It doesn’t matter how fast the rival grows; we will keep our quality high.

“However fast the rival grows, we will keep our quality high.”

From the story

Answer from memory, then check. These come from the story you read before.

1. Complete: “It does not matter where he hides…” using wherever.

“Wherever he hides, the general will search for him.”

2. What is the difference between prey and predator? Use your own words.

Prey is hunted by another creature; a predator is the hunter. In business, an incumbent can feel like a predator and a challenger like prey — until the challenger changes the game.

3. What does outwit mean, and how is it different from outmanoeuvre?

Outwit = defeat someone by being cleverer. Outmanoeuvre is very close but is used more for planning and positioning, especially in business and politics.
