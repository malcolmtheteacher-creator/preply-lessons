Religious Responses to Prejudice and Discrimination

Key Terms

  • Prejudice: Pre-judging someone based on their group (race, religion, gender, sexuality)
  • Discrimination: Treating someone unfairly because of prejudice — it's prejudice in action
  • Racism: Belief that some races are superior/inferior; systemic discrimination based on race
  • Sexism: Discrimination based on gender

Christianity: All Equal in Christ

Key teaching: "There is neither Jew nor Gentile, slave nor free, male nor female — you are all one in Christ Jesus" (Galatians 3:28)

This was revolutionary. In a hierarchical world, Paul proclaimed radical equality.

Martin Luther King Jr. (1929–1968): American Christian minister who led the Civil Rights Movement. His dream: a world where people are judged not by race but by character.

Desmond Tutu: South African archbishop who fought apartheid. He taught "ubuntu" — "I am because we are." Our humanity is shared.

Islam: Equality in Faith

Muhammad's Last Sermon (632 CE): "No Arab has superiority over a non-Arab... except by piety and good deeds."

Islam explicitly rejected tribalism and racial hierarchy. Before God, all are equal.

Malcolm X: American activist imprisoned for crime, then converted to Islam. At Hajj (pilgrimage), he saw Muslims of all races praying together as equals. This transformed his view of race relations.

Sikhism: Rejection of Caste

Guru Nanak (1469–1539): Rejected the Hindu caste system that said some people were "pure" and others "untouchable."

In Sikhism, all are equal before Waheguru. Social status means nothing spiritually.

Langar (again): Rich and poor, high and low, eat together. This daily practice embodied equality.

The Uncomfortable Truth: Religion Has Also Been Used to Discriminate

We must be honest: religions have also been used to justify discrimination:

  • Christian churches supported slavery using Bible verses out of context
  • The caste system was originally justified using Hindu texts (though many Hindus reject this today)
  • Antisemitism has deep Christian roots
  • All religions have been used to oppress women

But the lesson is: religious teachings can be misused. The cure is not to abandon the teachings, but to reclaim their true meaning.

Statement to Evaluate

"Religion is part of the problem of discrimination, not the solution."

Do you agree or disagree? Why?

Think about:

  • Has religion been used to justify discrimination? Give examples.
  • Have religions also led people to fight discrimination? Give examples.
  • Is the problem religion itself, or how people misuse it?
  • Can secular worldviews also discriminate?
  • Is one approach (religious or secular) better at fighting discrimination?

Quick-Fire Recall

1. What does Galatians 3:28 teach?
All are equal in Christ — no distinction based on Jew/Gentile, slave/free, male/female
2. Who was Martin Luther King Jr. and what was his dream?
Christian minister who led the Civil Rights Movement. Dream: a world where people are judged by character, not race
3. What did Muhammad's Last Sermon say about equality?
"No Arab has superiority over a non-Arab... except by piety and good deeds"
4. What did Guru Nanak do about the caste system?
Rejected it. Taught that all people are equal before Waheguru, regardless of caste or social status
5. Name one way religion has been misused to justify discrimination
Slavery (Christianity), caste system (Hinduism), antisemitism, oppression of women (all religions)
