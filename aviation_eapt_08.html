What is an ATIS?

The ATIS is a recorded weather and airfield message, repeated all day and updated when conditions change. It is the weather observer’s work in the controller’s voice — exactly the step between where you are now and where you want to be.

It always follows the same order. Learn the order and every ATIS becomes easy to follow:

#PartExample
1Airport, information letter, timeLakeside information Delta, time zero eight five zero
2Runway in useRunway in use two seven
3WindTwo five zero degrees, one eight knots, gusting two eight
4Visibility and weatherFour thousand metres in light rain
5CloudBroken at one thousand five hundred feet
6Temperature, dew point, QNHNiner, seven, one zero zero four
7WarningsWind shear reported on final
8Instruction to the pilotAdvise on initial contact you have information Delta
The letter (Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta…) changes every time the message changes. If a pilot says “information Charlie” and the current one is Delta, the controller knows the pilot has old weather.

Read it

Read the message once, slowly. Then answer. You may look back at the message.

From the code to the voice

You already read METARs. On the radio, nobody says the code — they say it in full, with numbers one digit at a time. This is the step from reading a report to speaking it.
METAR: LKSD 100850Z 25018G28KT 4000 -RA BKN015 09/07 Q1004
In the codeSaid aloud
25018G28KTWind two five zero degrees, one eight knots, gusting two eight knots
4000Visibility four thousand metres
-RALight rain
BKN015Cloud broken, one thousand five hundred feet
09/07Temperature niner, dew point seven
Q1004QNH one zero zero four
Say the digits one by one (except round thousands and hundreds): 250 = two five zero; 1004 = one zero zero four. Special sounds: 3 = tree, 5 = fife, 9 = niner. Always give the unit: degrees, knots, metres, feet.

Pilots ask, you answer

Pilots and controllers ask short questions and want short answers. Give the fact first, with its unit, then stop. If you do not know, say so clearly — never guess about weather.
A controller asks: “What is the current visibility?” (Report: 1,500 metres in fog.) Best answer?
A pilot asks for the wind but your sensor is being checked. Best answer?
The wind just changed from 250 to 310 degrees. What do you tell the controller?

Your turn

A controller calls: “Weather, tower. Give me the current weather.” Report: wind 200° 8 kt, visibility 10 km or more, few clouds at 3,000 ft, temperature 18, dew point 11, QNH 1021. Say it all.
01:00
Tower, weather. Wind two zero zero degrees, eight knots. Visibility ten kilometres or more. Cloud few at three thousand feet. Temperature one eight, dew point one one. QNH one zero two one.
Now the weather has a problem: a thunderstorm is three miles west of the airport and moving east. Warn the controller. What do you say, in order: what, where, what next?
01:00
Tower, weather. Thunderstorm three miles west of the airport, moving east. Expect it over the field in about ten minutes. Wind may gust and visibility will reduce in heavy rain. I will update you.

Round 1 — memory check (90 seconds)

Look at the message for 20 seconds, then tell a pilot who has just called in the five things they most need: runway, wind, visibility, cloud, and the warning.

01:30
Runway in use two seven. Wind two five zero degrees, one eight knots, gusting two eight. Visibility four thousand metres in light rain. Cloud broken at one thousand five hundred feet. Wind shear is reported on final for runway two seven.

Round 2 — write the next ATIS

At 0920 the weather changes: wind 280° 22 kt gusting 35 kt, visibility 2,000 m in moderate rain, broken cloud at 800 ft, temperature 8, dew point 8, QNH 1001. The wind shear warning is cancelled. Write the whole new message on paper, starting with the correct information letter, then read it aloud in 60 seconds.

01:00
Lakeside information Echo. Time zero niner two zero. Runway in use two seven. Surface wind two eight zero degrees, two two knots, gusting three fife knots. Visibility two thousand metres in moderate rain. Cloud broken at eight hundred feet. Temperature eight, dew point eight. QNH one zero zero one. Advise on initial contact you have information Echo.
