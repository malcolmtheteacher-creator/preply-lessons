Language Focus 1: Verbs with Prepositions

In appraisals, you often use verb + preposition collocations to talk about your performance and goals. Match each phrase with the correct preposition:

Click "Show Answer" to check:

An area I"d really like to focus ____ is my time management
Answer: on (focus on = concentrate on)
I think I"m good ____ communicating with clients
Answer: at (be good at = have a skill/talent)
Last year, I succeeded ____ landing three major contracts
Answer: in (succeed in = achieve something)
I"m getting better ____ public speaking
Answer: at (improve in a skill)
I still find it difficult ____ delegate tasks to my team
Answer: to (find it difficult to = have a challenge with)
I"ve improved my presentation skills but I can still improve ____ my sales approach
Answer: on (improve on = make better in a specific area)

Language Focus 2: Sentence Stress in Appraisals

When presenting yourself in an appraisal, word stress and sentence stress make your message stronger and more confident. Here are key phrases you"ll use:

STRESS on "area" and "focus"
An "AREA I"d really LIKE to FOCUS on is...
STRESS on "really" and "difficult"
I find it REALLY "DIFFICULT to...
STRESS on "As" and "aware"
AS you"re aWARE, ...
STRESS on "succeeded"
I SUCceeded in...
STRESS on "specific"
On one SPEcific ocCAsion...
STRESS on "improved"
I think I"ve imPROVED in the way I...

Why does stress matter?

Correct stress helps you sound natural, confident, and professional. It also helps your listener understand what you think is most important about what you"re saying.

Practice: Read each phrase above out loud, stressing the words in CAPITALS. Record yourself if possible and listen back.

Role Play: An Appraisal Meeting

You will role-play a performance review meeting. Read the situation and two role cards carefully, then have the conversation.

Context: It"s the end of the year. An employee (Junior Sales Manager) is meeting their Line Manager for an appraisal. They will discuss performance, achievements, challenges, salary, and career development.

Role Card A: You are the Junior Sales Manager

Your Role
Background: This is your first management position. You have the right attitude and the right skills.
Problem: Last year, you lost a contract with an old customer (a café chain that had been with the company for 10 years). You blame your predecessor for letting the relationship weaken. You think it"s not entirely your fault.
Success: You succeeded in winning a major supermarket account — this is significant for the company.
Strengths: Customers find you polite and approachable. You have good customer skills.
Weakness: Your colleagues think you could be more assertive. You"re sometimes too agreeable.
Your requests:
  • A 4% salary increase
  • A negotiation course (to improve assertiveness)
  • Fast-track promotion to regional manager within 2 years

Role Card B: You are the Line Manager

Your Role
Overall view: It"s been a mixed year. Some positives, some negatives.
Big positive: The supermarket deal is excellent. It will bring significant new revenue.
Negative: Losing the café chain customer is a concern. Even if they had a weak relationship, the employee should have tried harder to rebuild it.
Observation: The employee is popular with customers — very friendly and reliable. However, they"re sometimes too soft in negotiations. They need to be tougher.
What you"ll offer:
  • Salary increase: Approximately 1% (not 4%)
  • Could offer other benefits instead of more salary
  • Training: Yes, negotiation skills training is needed
  • Promotion: Not yet — they need more experience first

Useful Language During the Role Play

To present your achievements:

"I think I"m good at..." / "Last year, I succeeded in..." / "I"m getting better at..."

To discuss areas for development:

"An area I"d really like to focus on..." / "I still find it difficult to..." / "I"ve improved my [...] but I can still improve on..."

To give examples:

"On one specific occasion..." / "For example..." / "As you"re aware,..."

The Conversation

Start the conversation. The manager should open the meeting and discuss both the positive and negative aspects of the year. The employee should defend their position on the lost contract, highlight the supermarket win, and make their requests. Try to reach an agreement.

Time: Give yourselves 8–10 minutes for this conversation.
