The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. Henry wanted an annulment of his first marriage.
an annulment (n) — an official statement that a marriage was never valid.
2. He desperately wanted a male heir.
a male heir (n) — a son to inherit.
3. England chose to break with Rome.
to break with (v) — to end a relationship.
4. He became Supreme Head of the Church.
Supreme Head (n) — the highest leader.
5. Every monastery was closed.
a monastery (n) — a religious house of monks.
6. The monasteries were dissolved.
to dissolve (v) — to close officially.
7. Two of his wives were beheaded.
to behead (v) — to cut off the head.
8. He built up the royal navy.
a navy (n) — a country's warships.
9. His later rule was tyrannical.
tyrannical (adj) — cruel and unfair.
10. Children learn a rhyme: "divorced, beheaded, died…"
a rhyme (n) — a short poem with matching sounds.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The golden prince

Henry VIII became king in 1509 at seventeen. Young, athletic and educated, he married Catherine of Aragon, his brother's widow. They had a daughter, Mary, but no surviving son.

Henry became convinced the marriage was cursed and wanted an annulment so he could marry Anne Boleyn.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Whom did Henry first marry?

2. Why did he want an annulment?

1. Catherine of Aragon.

2. They had no surviving son, and he wanted to marry Anne Boleyn.

Discuss:
  • Why was a male heir so important to him?

Part 2 — The break with Rome

The Pope refused. Henry broke with Rome, and in 1534 the Act of Supremacy made him Supreme Head of the Church of England. Those who refused to accept this, like Thomas More, were executed. From 1536 the monasteries were dissolved, and their wealth went to the crown.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did the Act of Supremacy do?

2. What happened to the monasteries?

1. Made Henry Supreme Head of the Church of England.

2. They were dissolved and their wealth taken.

Discuss:
  • Was this about religion, love or money?

Part 3 — Six wives

Henry married six times: Catherine of Aragon (annulled), Anne Boleyn (beheaded), Jane Seymour (died after giving birth to Edward), Anne of Cleves (annulled), Catherine Howard (beheaded) and Catherine Parr (survived him). He died in 1547, leaving three children who would all reign.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Which wife gave him a son?

2. Which wife survived him?

1. Jane Seymour.

2. Catherine Parr.

Discuss:
  • How should we judge Henry today?

Grammar — The Passive Voice

Historians often care more about what was done than who did it — or they simply do not know who did it. That is the job of the passive.

How it works:
• Form: be + past participle: "The king was crowned at Westminster."
• Add by only when the doer matters: "The castle was built by Norman masons."
• Reporting passives hedge a legend: "He is said to have…", "It was believed that…"

1. Make it passive

Complete each sentence about Henry VIII. Use the word in brackets.

1. The marriage (annul) in 1533.

2. Henry (make) Supreme Head in 1534.

3. The monasteries (dissolve).

1. was annulled

2. was made

3. were dissolved

2. Choose the right passive form

1. Thomas More (execute) in 1535.

2. Two queens (behead).

3. A stronger royal navy (build) during his reign.

1. was executed

2. were beheaded

3. was built

Going deeper

Henry's need for a son led to one of the biggest changes in English history. The Church of England, the dissolution, and a stronger navy all came from his reign.

Worth noticing: the rhyme "divorced, beheaded, died; divorced, beheaded, survived" is a memory aid, though technically his marriages were annulled, not divorced.
Learning from the past — for today: Personal decisions can have public consequences. Leaders' private choices affect many people. Think about who else your decisions touch.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Was Henry a monster?
  • What changed most because of him?
  • Which personal decision of yours affected others?
