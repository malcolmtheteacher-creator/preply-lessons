Expressing Regrets: Wish / If Only

Use "wish" with past perfect for direct regrets, and "if only" to emphasise hypothetical alternatives. These structures let you discuss what could have been different.

Structure: Wish + Past Perfect

I wish + subject + had/hadn't + past participle

I wish we had carried out a thorough risk assessment.

I wish we had got the logistics right.

I wish we hadn't invited quite so many speakers.

Structure: If Only + Past Perfect

If only + subject + had/hadn't + past participle

If only the budget hadn't been so low.

If only we had used a time management tool, the project management wouldn't have been so inefficient.

If only the venues we chose had been more appropriate.

Expressing Empathy in Professional Contexts

When a colleague expresses a regret or challenge, respond with genuine empathy. Click each phrase to see when and how to use it.

Empathy Responses

UNDERSTANDING DIFFICULTY
That must have been difficult to deal with.
Acknowledges a serious challenge without being dismissive. Formal and professional.
SHARED EMOTION
You must have been frustrated by that.
Names the likely emotion. Shows you understand their perspective. Validates their feelings.
ACKNOWLEDGING DIFFICULTY
That can't have been easy to do.
Recognises the effort required despite challenges. Shows respect for their attempt.
ASKING ABOUT COPING
How did you cope?
Moves toward solutions while showing interest. Invites them to reflect on their resilience.
GENERAL ENQUIRY
What happened next?
Encourages them to continue the narrative. Shows active listening.
CASUAL BUT SYMPATHETIC
Oh no! How annoying.
Spontaneous and informal. Works well in collaborative or less formal team meetings.
EXPRESSING FRUSTRATION
What a pain!
Colloquial. Bonds with the person by sharing the frustration. Lighthearted but empathetic.

Match Regrets to Empathy Responses

For each regret, choose the most appropriate empathy response.

Colleague says: "If only the budget hadn't been so low. We had to cut so many things."

You respond: "You must have been frustrated by that." or "That must have been difficult to deal with."

Colleague says: "I wish we hadn't invited that many speakers. The schedule became chaos."

You respond: "What a pain! How did you manage the timetable?"

Colleague says: "If only we had carried out a proper risk assessment. We had three emergencies."

You respond: "That can't have been easy to do. What happened next?"

Last Year's Problem Events

Here are challenges from previous international events. Read each scenario and think about how these problems could be avoided this year.

Budget Crisis

What happened: The budget was too low to deliver what the client expected.

Impact: Key activities had to be cut. Vendors complained about payment delays.

What we might say: "I wish we had had a larger budget from the start." "If only we'd negotiated with vendors earlier..."

Logistics Breakdown

What happened: Poor coordination between the venue, catering, and technical teams.

Impact: Sessions started late. Equipment wasn't ready. Attendees were confused.

What we might say: "I wish we had got the logistics right." "If only we'd used a proper project management system..."

Time Management Issues

What happened: No centralised time management tool. Different teams worked from different schedules and deadlines.

Impact: Inefficient project management. Missed deadlines for promotional materials. Last-minute surprises.

What we might say: "If only we had used a time management tool. The project management wouldn't have been so inefficient."

Risk Assessment Failure

What happened: No thorough risk assessment before the event. Potential problems weren't identified.

Impact: Multiple unexpected crises. Poor contingency planning. Reactive rather than proactive decision-making.

What we might say: "I wish we had carried out a thorough risk assessment." "That wasn't easy to deal with."

Speaker Overload

What happened: Too many speakers were invited, leading to an overcrowded schedule.

Impact: No time for proper discussions. Speakers felt rushed. Attendee engagement dropped.

What we might say: "I wish we hadn't invited quite so many speakers." "What a pain! How did you manage the timing?"

Venue Selection Problems

What happened: The venues chosen weren't suitable for the event type or attendee needs.

Impact: Acoustics problems. Insufficient meeting spaces. Catering facilities inadequate.

What we might say: "If only the venues we chose had been more appropriate." "That must have been frustrating for the attendees."

Role-play Meeting: Event Debrief

You are meeting as a team to plan this year's international event. Your role is to discuss what went wrong last year, express regrets and empathy, and agree on action plans to avoid repeating those mistakes.

Your Roles

Role 1: Event Director

You organised last year's event. You need to explain the major problems, take responsibility, and express your regrets about what went wrong. You want changes for this year. Prepare notes about:

  • What major problems were there?
  • Why did these things go wrong?
  • What are your regrets?
  • What changes do you want this year?
Role 2 & 3: External Consultants (x2)

This is your first meeting with this client. You're here to:

  • Listen carefully to what went wrong last year
  • Show genuine empathy for the challenges they faced
  • Ask follow-up questions to fully understand the problems
  • Propose practical actions to ensure these problems don't happen again

Useful Language for the Meeting

I wish we'd had a larger budget. Direct regret about past situation
If only the budget hadn't been so low. Emphasising what could have been different
You must have been frustrated by that. Showing empathy for their experience
That can't have been easy to do. Acknowledging the difficulty they faced
How did you cope? Asking how they handled the challenge
What happened next? Encouraging them to continue narrative

What Happens During the Discussion

The Consultant asks:

"So, tell us what the major problems were. Why do you think things went wrong?"

The Director explains:

Describes problems and expresses regrets using "I wish" or "If only" structures.

The Consultant responds:

Shows empathy: "That must have been difficult..." or "You must have been frustrated..."

Consultant then proposes solutions:

"What if we...?" "Perhaps we could..." "One approach might be..."

Proposed Actions to Discuss

Solutions to Consider
BUDGET ISSUE
Manage budgets closely
Break down costs into clear categories. Build contingency. Negotiate with vendors upfront. Regular budget reviews.
LOGISTICS
Research all venues thoroughly
Site visits. Check technical infrastructure. Confirm catering capacity. Test equipment in advance.
SPEAKERS
Invite fewer, higher-impact speakers
Quality over quantity. Allow time for discussions. Balance keynotes with panel sessions. Confirm speakers early.
PROJECT MANAGEMENT
Set clear objectives and timelines
Use project management tools. Assign clear responsibilities. Weekly check-ins. Document all decisions.

Lesson Learned & Action Plan

After discussing last year's problems, summarise what you've decided to do differently for this year's event.

Write your agreed action plan:

Key Changes This Year

Budget Management

Create detailed cost breakdowns. Build in contingency. Hold vendors to written agreements. Conduct budget reviews every two weeks.

Logistics Coordination

Conduct venue site visits. Check all technical infrastructure. Confirm catering details. Schedule equipment tests before the event.

Time Management

Implement a centralised project management tool. Assign clear ownership of each task. Hold weekly coordination meetings. Document every decision.

Risk Assessment

Create a risk register at the start. Identify potential problems early. Plan contingency measures. Have backup vendors and speakers ready.

Speaker Selection

Confirm fewer, higher-quality speakers. Allow time for meaningful discussions. Plan panel sessions instead of back-to-back lectures. Confirm speakers by hard deadline.

Venue Selection

Visit multiple venues in person. Check acoustics and lighting. Test catering facilities. Ensure adequate breakout spaces. Get everything in writing.
