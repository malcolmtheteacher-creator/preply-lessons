Check your idea CLICK
"But" joins two ideas that don't match — Sam wants to come, but he can't. It's a small surprise or contrast.
"So" joins a reason to its result — Sam is tired, so he wants an early night. These tiny words are everywhere in everyday talk: they turn short, choppy sentences into one clear idea.

Adding & Surprising: and / but

"And" adds a matching idea. "But" shows a surprise — the opposite of what you expected.

Spot the pattern — adding ideas

Three everyday sentences:

  • "I live in Madrid, and I work in a school."
  • "She likes cats, and she likes dogs."
  • "We had pizza, and we watched a film."

Before you check: do the two ideas in each sentence agree, or disagree?

Check the pattern CLICK
They agree — both ideas fit together with no surprise. And simply adds one more true thing.

Spot the pattern — a surprise

Three more sentences:

  • "I like coffee, but I don't drink it at night."
  • "He wanted to go out, but it was raining."
  • "We booked a table, but the restaurant was closed."

What's different about these ones?

Check the pattern CLICK
The two ideas don't match — there's a small surprise or problem. But shows that contrast.
idea 1 + and + idea 2 (the ideas agree)
idea 1 + but + idea 2 (the ideas don't agree — a surprise)

Why? Because!

"Because" gives a reason. It answers the question "Why?"

Spot the pattern

Three everyday reasons:

  • "I stayed home because I was tired."
  • "She took an umbrella because it was raining."
  • "He passed the exam because he studied hard."

Before you check: does the reason come before "because" or after it?

Check the pattern CLICK
The reason comes after "because". First you say what happened, then "because" + the reason. "Because" always sits right in front of the reason.
[what happened] + because + [the reason]
"Because" doesn't show surprise, and it doesn't add matching facts — it only ever answers "Why?"

So What Happened? because vs. so

"So" gives a result. It's "because" in the opposite order.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday results:

  • "I was tired, so I stayed home."
  • "It was raining, so she took an umbrella."
  • "He studied hard, so he passed the exam."

Compare these with the "because" sentences on the last tab. What changed — the words, or the order?

Check the pattern CLICK
The order flipped. With so, the reason comes first and the result comes after. With because, it's the opposite: the result comes first and the reason comes after.
[the reason] + so + [the result]
[the result] + because + [the reason]
Same meaning, opposite order: "I stayed home because I was tired." = "I was tired, so I stayed home."
