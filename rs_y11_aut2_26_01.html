The Significance of Hajj

Hajj is the fifth pillar of Islam and one of the most important spiritual journeys a Muslim can undertake. Qur'an 3:97 states: "In it are clear signs: the Station of Abraham. Whoever enters it shall be safe."

For Muslims, Hajj represents:

  • Spiritual cleansing — a journey of submission to Allah
  • Equality — all pilgrims wear the same simple white garments regardless of wealth or status
  • Community — Muslims from around the world gather in unity
  • Obedience — following the example set by Prophet Muhammad and Prophet Abraham

The hajj demonstrates Islam's emphasis on both personal devotion and collective identity.

Who Must Go?

Hajj is compulsory for all able Muslims at least once in their lifetime, if they are:

  • Adult (reached puberty)
  • Physically healthy
  • Mentally capable
  • Financially able to afford the journey
  • Have dependents provided for during absence

Qur'an 3:97: "Pilgrimage to the House is a duty owed to Allah by those who can afford the journey."

When Does Hajj Occur?

Hajj takes place in the Dhu al-Hijjah (12th month of the Islamic lunar calendar), specifically from the 8th to 13th of the month.

This timing commemorates the events of Prophet Abraham's life and the revelation of Islam's oneness.

  • The 8th: Pilgrims begin to gather (Yawm al-Tarwiyah — "Day of Watering")
  • The 9th: The main day of Hajj (Arafat)
  • 10th-13th: Remaining rituals and celebrations

The Islamic calendar is lunar, so Hajj dates change each year on the Gregorian calendar.

Preparation for Hajj

Before embarking on Hajj, pilgrims undergo months of spiritual and physical preparation. This includes:

Spiritual Preparation

  • Increased prayer and Qur'anic study
  • Seeking forgiveness for past sins (Tawbah)
  • Setting intention (Niyyah) to perform Hajj for Allah alone
  • Studying the rituals and their meanings

Physical Preparation

  • Medical check-ups and vaccinations (as required)
  • Physical fitness training for walking long distances
  • Arranging travel and accommodation (often through tour operators)
  • Obtaining passport and visa

Financial Preparation

  • Saving funds for travel, accommodation, and food
  • Often taking 2-3 weeks from work/studies
  • Hajj costs average £3,000–£10,000 depending on accommodation standards

The Journey to Makkah

Hajj attracts approximately 2–3 million pilgrims annually from across the globe. The journey is both challenging and transformative:

  • Many travel by air to Saudi Arabia, then by coach or train to Makkah
  • Accommodation is found in Makkah, Arafat, and Mina (sacred valley near Makkah)
  • The roads become extremely crowded, requiring careful coordination
  • Pilgrim safety is paramount — the Saudi government deploys hundreds of thousands of staff and volunteers

In 2019, the Saudi government used AI technology and drone surveillance to manage crowd flow and prevent dangerous stampedes.

Key Terms

  • Hajj: The pilgrimage to Makkah, the fifth pillar of Islam
  • Ka'bah: The holy cubic structure at the heart of the Grand Mosque in Makkah
  • Makkah: The holiest city in Islam, birthplace of Prophet Muhammad
  • Dhu al-Hijjah: The 12th month of the Islamic calendar when Hajj takes place
  • Niyyah: Intention, essential for all Islamic acts of worship
  • Tawbah: Repentance and seeking forgiveness from Allah
