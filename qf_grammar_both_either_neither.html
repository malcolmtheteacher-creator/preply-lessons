🤝 Quick Fix: Both / Either / Neither

Talking about two things — positive, choice, or negative

What's wrong here?

I don't like neither of them.

I don't like neither — Double negative!

NEITHER already contains the negative idea. You cannot add another negative.

The Rule

BOTH = the two things (positive)

Example: "Both sisters are tall."

EITHER = one or the other (choice OR negative)

Examples: "I don't like either." / "You can take either bus."

NEITHER = not one and not the other (negative — NEVER with don't/not)

Example: "I like neither of them."

Key: NEITHER already has the negative meaning. Never add "don't" or "not" with neither!

Correct versions:

  • I don't like either of them. (negative verb + either)
  • I like neither of them. (positive verb + neither)

Verb Agreement (Important!):

  • Both of my parents is → Both of my parents are (plural verb)
  • Neither of them have → Neither of them has (singular verb, formal)
