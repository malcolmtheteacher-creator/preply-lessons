Language Focus: Debate Phrases & Pronunciation

Debate Phrases for Justifying & Challenging Opinions

Use these phrases to make your opinion stronger and to challenge what others say:

"What do you mean easy?"
Challenge someone's assumption by questioning their wording
"Come on. Surely you don't really think that."
Express doubt about someone else's opinion
"Yeah, but look at it this way."
Introduce your alternative perspective
"Think about it."
Ask someone to consider your point more carefully
"Look. Take [X] for example."
Introduce a specific example to support your argument
"Face facts."
Tell someone to accept the reality of a situation

Pronunciation Focus: Linking Words

When a word ends with a consonant sound and the next word begins with a vowel sound, we "link" them together. Say them smoothly as one phrase, not separately.

Linking Examples:
"Think about it" /ˈθɪŋk əˈbaʊt ɪt/
The "k" at the end of "think" links to the "a" sound in "about"
"Yeah, but look at it this way" Link: "look" + "at" and "it" + "this"
Say smoothly: /ˈlʊk ət ɪt ðɪs weɪ/
"In reality, I bet they don't end up with much" Link: "bet" + "they", "don't" + "end", "up" + "with"
Say each linked phrase smoothly without stopping between words
"Anyone could put up with that" Link: "put" + "up", multiple consonant-vowel links
Keep the rhythm smooth and connected

Debate Task

The Topic: "Movie stars get paid too much for doing too little"

Do you agree or disagree? Prepare arguments for BOTH sides, then choose your position. Use the debate phrases you learned.

Arguments FOR the Statement (Agreement)

Movie stars earn millions while teachers and doctors earn much less. What justifies such high pay?

Phrase to use: "Think about it..."
Movie stars work for just a few hours of filming per day (often under 12 hours)
Phrase to use: "Look. Take... for example."
A teacher shapes minds for 30+ years; a movie star entertains for 2 hours
Phrase to use: "Face facts."
The money comes from entertainment, not essential work for society
Phrase to use: "Yeah, but look at it this way."
Salaries reflect entertainment value, not the importance of the job

Arguments AGAINST the Statement (Disagreement)

Movie stars earn their pay through hard work, risk, and providing value to audiences worldwide.

Phrase to use: "What do you mean 'too little'?"
Actors train for years and work extremely hard to perfect their craft
Phrase to use: "Come on. Surely you don't really think that."
Their work generates billions in revenue for studios and creates thousands of jobs
Phrase to use: "Think about it."
A popular film can entertain millions; its global reach justifies high pay
Phrase to use: "Face facts."
People choose to pay for entertainment; the market decides their salary

Your Position

Do you agree or disagree with the statement?

Write or speak your position here. Include at least two reasons, and use at least two debate phrases from the list above.

Discussion Questions

Discuss these questions with your teacher. Try to use the debate phrases and justify your opinions:

1. Global vs. Local Earnings

Movie stars in Hollywood earn much more than actors in smaller countries. Why do you think this is? Is it fair?

2. Reality TV & Social Media

Some reality TV stars and social media influencers earn huge amounts of money for doing very little. Do you think they deserve such high pay? Why or why not?

3. Different Types of Fame

Should a musician, actor, and athlete all earn the same amount? What factors should influence how much a celebrity earns?

4. Personal Values & Career

If you could become famous, would you choose to be a movie star, a scientist, a teacher, or something else? Justify your choice.

5. Public Opinion & Celebrity

Do you think the public has the right to criticize how much celebrities earn? Why or why not?

Remember: When you answer, always justify your opinion with reasons and examples. Use the debate phrases to challenge and support ideas respectfully.
