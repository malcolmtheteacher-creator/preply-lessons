Group Harmony or Individual Voices?

Some people say East Asians always follow the group. Is it true?

Today: you will learn about groups and people in East Asia, and use but and although.
Talk first:
  • Do you like to work in a group?
  • Is it easy to say “I disagree”?
  • Do you like to be different?

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. He works in a group of five.
group (n) — a number of people together. Example: “He works in a group of five.”
2. The team likes harmony.
harmony (n) — living and working together in a calm way. Example: “The team likes harmony.”
3. We all agree.
agree (v) — to have the same idea. Example: “We all agree.”
4. She has an individual style.
individual (adj) — one person, not a group. Example: “She has an individual style.”
5. A proverb says, “The nail that sticks out gets hammered down.”
proverb (n) — a short old saying. Example: “A proverb says, “The nail that sticks out gets hammered down.””
6. It is his duty to help his family.
duty (n) — something you must do. Example: “It is his duty to help his family.”
Talk it through:
  • Which word is new for you? Say a sentence with it.
The stereotype: “Everyone in East Asia just follows the group.”

The story

Part 1 — The nail

A Japanese proverb says, “The nail that sticks out gets hammered down.” It means that people who are very different feel pressure to be like others. Researchers compare countries with a scale. The USA is very high for “individualism”, and China and Korea are low. But the scores are only averages. They do not describe every person.

Quick check:

1. What does the proverb mean?

2. Do the scores describe every person?

1. People who are different feel pressure to be like others.

2. No, they are only averages.

Discuss:
  • Is there a proverb like this in your language?

Part 2 — Harmony

In Japan, people like harmony. They do not like open conflict. Teams often talk before the meeting and agree together. In Korea, “nunchi” means you can feel what other people think. In China, trusted friends and family help you get things done. Groups give support, but they also give duties.

Quick check:

1. What do people in Japan like?

2. What does a group give you?

1. Harmony.

2. Support and duties.

Discuss:
  • What do groups give you?

Part 3 — Individuals

People are not all the same. There are artists, chefs, musicians and business people in East Asia with very personal styles. Young people today often choose their own way more than their grandparents did. Although groups are important, individuals stand out too.

Quick check:

1. Who has a very personal style?

2. What do young people choose more than their grandparents?

1. Artists, chefs, musicians and business people.

2. Their own way.

Discuss:
  • Do you follow your own way?

Grammar — but and although

but goes in the middle: “Groups are important, but individuals stand out.”
although goes at the start: “Although groups are important, individuals stand out.”
Use a comma after the first part.

1. Choose the word

1. Groups are important, people can still disagree. (but / although)

2. harmony is important, people have their own ideas. (But / Although)

3. The scores are low, not every person is the same. (but / although)

2. Although

3. but

2. Make your own

1. Although I like groups, ______.

2. I am quiet, but ______.

3. Although it is hard to say “no”, ______.

1. ...I need time alone. / ...I like my own ideas.

2. ...I have many ideas. / ...I like talking to friends.

3. ...I try. / ...it is important.
