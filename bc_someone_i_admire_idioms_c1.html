Why people are admired — key vocabulary

People are admired for different qualities. Here are some key words that describe them. Fill in the blanks, then check your answers.

Word bank: fight / distracted / trials / mission / modest / welfare

Complete these descriptions of admirable qualities:

1. They face dangers and trials without fear.

2. They are more concerned with the welfare of others than their own.

3. They have strong beliefs and a sense of mission.

4. They had to fight hard for what they achieved.

5. Despite enormous success, they remain modest.

6. They stayed focused and were never distracted from their goals.

trials = difficult experiences or tests of character
welfare = the health, happiness, and wellbeing of a person or group
mission = a strong sense of purpose or calling
fight = to struggle hard, often against opposition
modest = not boastful; understating one's own achievements
distracted = having your attention pulled away from your goal

Think about this:

Which of these qualities is most important to you when you admire someone? Why?

Which of these qualities does the person you admire have? Can you give a specific example?

Idiomatic expressions for personality

These idioms are commonly used to describe remarkable people. Click each card to reveal the meaning, then use them to describe the people you talked about in Tab 1.

a live wire A lively, energetic, and entertaining person — full of energy and enthusiasm that's infectious to everyone around them.
a dark horse Someone with hidden talents or surprising abilities that others don't know about — they keep their skills private.
had the makings of Showed early signs of becoming something great — even from a young age, the potential was visible.
in a class of his/her own Incomparably superior — so outstanding that no one else can really be compared to them in their field.
down-to-earth Practical, unpretentious, and genuinely approachable — doesn't put on airs despite success or status.

Try using the idioms in context:

1. "Jenny is such a vivacious and energetic person — she keeps us all entertained." → Use an idiom:

"Jenny is such a live wire — she keeps us all entertained with her jokes and stories."

2. "I had no idea he used to be a well-known sportsperson — he's very private about it." → Use an idiom:

"I had no idea he used to be a well-known sportsperson — he can be a dark horse at times!"

Plan a Feature Article

You're going to write a feature article (approximately 300 words) about a famous person you admire. First, brainstorm your ideas.

📝 Gather your information — make notes on:

• Biographical details (year/place of birth, family background, education, career path)

• Appearance and first impression

• Personality traits — use the idioms and vocabulary from today

• Key achievements — what makes this person remarkable?

• Why you personally admire them — what have they shown you about what's possible?

Structure your article:

Introduction: Hook the reader — who is this person and why should we care?

Main paragraph 1: Background and journey — how did they get here?

Main paragraph 2: Achievements and qualities — what makes them exceptional?

Conclusion: Personal reflection — why you admire them and what they represent.
