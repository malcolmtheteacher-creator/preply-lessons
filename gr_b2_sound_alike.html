Why does English sound so different from how it's written?

When English speakers talk naturally, words blend together. Sounds disappear, merge, and change. "Did you eat yet?" becomes "Jeet yet?" "I don't know" becomes "I dunno."

This game trains your ear and your mouth to handle connected speech — the way English actually sounds at normal speed.

3 rounds

Round 1 — Pairs: Two phrases that sound almost identical in fast speech. One person says one — the other has to identify which.
Round 2 — Say & Guess: One person says a sentence at NATURAL speed. The other writes/says what they heard. Then compare.
Round 3 — Decode: Sentences written as they SOUND. Figure out the real English.

Warm-up

Say "What do you want to do?" as fast as you naturally can. Now say it again. And again, even faster. What happened to the words? Which sounds disappeared?

Reflection

Which sounds were hardest to distinguish? Was it vowel sounds, consonant clusters, or the way words joined together?
Do you think you should try to speak with connected speech? Or is it only important for listening? What are the risks of using it too much?
What advice would you give to someone learning English about understanding native speakers? What's the most important thing you learned today?
