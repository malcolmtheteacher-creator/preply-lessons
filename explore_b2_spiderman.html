Spider-Man: The Whole Story

B2 Level · Speaking & Discussion

The most famous teenager in the world

Almost everyone on Earth recognises the red-and-blue mask. What makes Spider-Man unusual among superheroes is who is underneath it: not a god, not a billionaire — an ordinary, anxious teenager with money worries and homework. Today we tell his whole story, and then put the films on trial.

Before we start — no pressure, just what comes to mind:

What do you already know about Spider-Man's story? Who is he under the mask?

Have you seen any of the films? Which ones, and what do you remember?

Spider-Man is popular almost everywhere in the world. Why do you think that is?

an origin story The tale of how a hero got their powers and became who they are.
radioactive Giving off dangerous energy — here, the spider whose bite changes Peter.
responsibility A duty to deal with something, and to answer for what you do.
guilt The heavy feeling that you did something wrong, or failed to act.
an alter ego A second, hidden identity — Peter Parker by day, Spider-Man in secret.
a mentor An older, wiser person who guides you — for Peter, his Uncle Ben.
a reboot Starting a story again from the beginning, with new actors and a new take.
relatable Easy to identify with, because it feels close to your own life.

The Story

Here it is in five short beats. Read each one, then you'll retell the whole thing in your own words.

1An ordinary kid

Peter Parker is a shy, clever teenager in Queens, New York. His parents are gone, so he lives with his kind Aunt May and Uncle Ben. He is good at science and often picked on at school — about as far from a superhero as you can get.

2The bite

On a school trip, Peter is bitten by a radioactive spider. Soon he can climb walls, sense danger, and lift far more than any human should. At first, he uses these powers for himself — to make money and show off.

3The mistake

One night, Peter lets a thief run past him. Stopping him wasn't his problem, he thinks. Days later, that same thief kills Uncle Ben. Peter's selfishness has cost him the person he loved most.

4The lesson

Crushed by guilt, Peter finally understands the idea that gives the whole story its heart: with great power comes great responsibility. He decides to use his gifts to protect other people, whatever it costs him.

5Not just Peter

Years later, the mask is passed on. Miles Morales, a teenager from Brooklyn, is bitten too and becomes Spider-Man in his own world. The message is clear: anyone could be under the mask — maybe even you.

The Films

Spider-Man has been played on screen more than almost any character alive. Here are the four big versions of the last twenty years.

2002–2007

The Raimi trilogy

Tobey Maguire in the first big Spider-Man blockbusters. Warm, colourful and emotional — the second film especially is often named one of the best superhero films ever.

2012–2014

The Amazing Spider-Man

Andrew Garfield in a darker, more romantic reboot. Only two films — admired by many fans, but it never quite took off the way the studio hoped.

2016–present

The MCU era

Tom Holland's Spider-Man joins the Avengers as a nervous high-schooler. Homecoming, Far From Home and the record-breaking No Way Home, which pulled the earlier actors back together.

2018–present

Spider-Verse (animated)

Miles Morales leads a wildly inventive animated world of many Spider-people. Into the Spider-Verse won the Oscar; its sequel pushed the animation even further.

Language for comparing — reach for these

  • I'd put X above Y because…
  • What sets it apart is… / What lets it down is…
  • It hasn't aged as well as… / It holds up better than…
  • For me, nothing beats… / It's in a different league.

Rank them

Put these four versions in your personal order, best to worst — then talk me through your ranking, using the comparison language above. There is no right answer; the reasons are what matter.

Big Questions

Choose two or three of these and really dig in. Take a side, give reasons, change your mind if you want to.

“With great power comes great responsibility.” Is that true in ordinary life — for a boss, a parent, a government — and not just for superheroes?

Peter let the thief go because it “wasn't his problem.” When is something genuinely your responsibility, and when is it fair to walk past?

Why do you think the mask matters so much — the idea that anyone at all could be underneath it?

Hollywood has restarted Spider-Man three times in twenty years. Why keep retelling the same origin? Does it still work, or is it too much?

Miles Morales made Spider-Man a different kind of kid. Why does it matter who gets to be the hero in a story?

The one-minute case

Time to persuade me. Start the clock and make the strongest possible case for your favourite Spider-Man — a film, an actor, or a version of the character. Keep going for the full time.
