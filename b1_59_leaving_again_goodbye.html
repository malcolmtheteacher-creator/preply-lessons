Leaving Again: The Goodbye

B1+ Level · Reported Speech, Promises & Saying Farewell

The Last Day

The last day at home is a strange one. The bag is half-packed. Mum is too cheerful. Dad is too quiet. You keep looking at small things — a cup, a chair, the garden — as if you're trying to memorise them.

Read & Notice

"On the morning of my flight, my mum kept asking if I had packed everything. My dad said he would drive me to the airport himself. My sister told me she was going to come and visit next year — she promised. My grandma just held my hand and said, 'Don't be a stranger.' I told her I wouldn't. I'm not sure I meant it. I wanted to mean it."

1. Find all the things people said. Who said what?

Mum: asked if I had packed everything. Dad: said he would drive me. Sister: said she was going to visit next year, and promised. Grandma: said "Don't be a stranger." Speaker: said he/she wouldn't be a stranger.

2. The writer says, "I'm not sure I meant it." Why might they say that?

It's a very honest line. Promises at goodbyes are often made with love but are difficult to keep. Life gets busy. The writer is being truthful with themselves.

3. What's the difference between "Dad said he would drive me" and "Dad said, 'I'll drive you'"?

The first is reported speech (we tell someone what was said). The second is direct speech (the exact words, in quotation marks). In reported speech, will becomes would.

She Said…

When we tell another person what someone said, we usually shift the tense one step back into the past. This is called reported speech (or indirect speech).

The Tense Shift

One step back in time

Direct (the actual words) Reported (what we tell someone)
"I am tired."She said she was tired.
"I love you."She said she loved me.
"I 'm coming back."She said she was coming back.
"I saw her yesterday."She said she had seen her the day before.
"I 'll call you."She said she would call me.
"I can come."She said she could come.
"I must go."She said she had to go.
Tip: Pronouns and time words also change. "I'll see you tomorrow" → He said he would see me the next day.

Said vs. Told

Two very common verbs

say — no person directly after it.  She said (that) she was tired.

tell — you tell someone.  She told me (that) she was tired.

× She told that she was tired.    × She said me that she was tired.

Report Them

Turn the direct speech into reported speech. Imagine you're now back home, telling a friend what people said during your visit.

1. Mum: "I miss you so much." → Mum said .

Mum said (that) she missed me so much.

2. My brother: "I'll visit you next year." → My brother told me .

My brother told me (that) he would visit me the following year.

3. Grandma: "I made your favourite cake." → Grandma said .

Grandma said (that) she had made my favourite cake.

4. Dad: "Don't forget to call when you land." → Dad told me .

Dad told me not to forget to call when I landed. (Reported commands use told someone (not) to + infinitive.)

5. My old teacher: "You haven't changed at all!" → My old teacher said .

My old teacher said (that) I hadn't changed at all.

Promises & Plans

Goodbyes are full of future tenses. We make promises, share plans, predict the future. Different futures, different feelings.

Which Future?

will — for promises and on-the-spot decisions

"I'll write to you every week, I promise."

"Don't worry — I'll come back soon."

going to — for intentions and plans

"I'm going to save up for a longer visit next time."

"We're going to video-call every Sunday."

Present continuous — for fixed arrangements

"I'm flying home in December — it's booked."

"My cousin is coming to stay with me in March."

Choose the Best Future

1. (At the door, holding her tight) "Don't cry, Mum. I back soon!"

'll come / 'll be — spontaneous promise, made in the moment.

2. "I in March — I've already booked the flight."

'm coming back / 'm flying home — present continuous for a fixed arrangement.

3. "This year I learn to cook Mum's recipes properly."

'm going to — an intention/plan you've decided on.

4. (Phone rings, you check it) "It's my brother. I answer it."

'll — on-the-spot decision.

Useful Phrases for Promises

"I promise I'll…"
"I swear I won't leave it so long next time."
"I give you my word — I'll call every week."
"I'll do my best to…"
"You have my word."
"This time will be different."

Mini Dialogue — At the Airport

Mum: "Have you got your passport? Your tickets? Phone charger?"

You: "Yes, Mum. Three times. I've got everything."

Mum: "Don't forget to text me when you land. Promise?"

You: "I promise. The second I land."

Mum: "And don't leave it so long next time. Two years is too much."

You: "I know. I won't. I'll be back before you know it."

Mum: "Right. Off you go. Don't make me cry in public."

You: "Too late, Mum. Take care of yourself, okay?"

Settling Back

Then you're on the plane. Then in a taxi. Then in your own bed in your own city — the one you call home, the other home. The first few days are strange. Everything is in the wrong place.

Read & React

"For three days after I got back, I felt half-asleep. Everything in my flat looked different — smaller. The fridge was empty, the plants were dying. My friends asked me how it had been and I didn't know how to answer. How do you explain a week of feeling like two different people at once? I said it had been lovely. That was true. I just didn't say everything."

1. Why does the writer say "I didn't say everything"?

Some experiences are too complicated to share in a quick chat. The writer felt the trip was beautiful and difficult at the same time, but only shared the easy part.

2. "Two different people at once" — what do you think this means?

The person you are now, in your new life — and the person you used to be, who still lives at home in everyone's memory. Both versions are real, but they don't always fit together.

Speaking Task — A Letter You Won't Send

Imagine you're back in your own life, three days after the trip. You want to write one short message to someone you said goodbye to — but you're not going to send it. You just need to say it.

Speak for 2–3 minutes. Try to include:
  • Who the letter is to
  • One thing you remember most from the visit
  • One thing you wanted to say but didn't (use "I wish I had said…")
  • One real promise for next time
  • How you're feeling now you're back
Letter time: 3:00

Discussion

Final conversation. Take your time. Long answers.

Conversation Questions

1. Are you better at hellos or goodbyes?

Why? Are you the one who cries, the one who jokes, or the one who escapes quickly?

2. Have you ever made a promise at a goodbye that you didn't keep?

What was it? Why didn't you keep it? Do you feel bad about it now?

3. Is it easier to leave when you know exactly when you'll be back — or when the date is open?

Which kind of goodbye is harder for you?

4. After a trip home, do you usually feel happier or sadder than before you went?

What does that tell you?

5. Some people say, "You can never really go home again." What do you think they mean?

Do you agree?

6. If you could leave one thing behind every time you go home — one feeling, one habit, one memory — what would it be?

And what would you take with you?
