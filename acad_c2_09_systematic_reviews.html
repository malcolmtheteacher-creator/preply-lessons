Why Systematic Reviews Provoke Scrutiny

Systematic reviews synthesize evidence across studies. But synthesis choices—what studies you include, how you weight them, what you conclude from heterogeneity—are places where bias creeps in. Reviewers will challenge you on the logic of every boundary.

Move 1: The PICOS Statement

1 Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome, Study Design +
PICOS is your protocol's foundation. Every inclusion/exclusion criterion flows from these five elements. You must be able to recite and justify each component. This is what makes your review reproducible.
PICOS Example

P: Adults aged 18+ with Type 2 diabetes | I: Metformin monotherapy | C: Placebo or standard care | O: HbA1c reduction | S: Randomized controlled trials only

Move 2: Defending Your Search Strategy

2 Justifying databases, keywords, language limits, date ranges +
You'll be asked: Why those databases? Why that date range? Why exclude non-English? Every choice is a risk for missing studies. Your job is to show that your choices minimize bias while remaining feasible.
Defensible Answer

"We searched Medline, Embase, and Cochrane because these capture 95% of RCTs in this domain. We limited to 2000-2024 because earlier trials used different diagnostic criteria. We included only English-language publications because our team cannot reliably extract data from other languages; this is a limitation we acknowledge."

Move 3: Inclusion/Exclusion Decisions

3 When to exclude and when to include borderline studies +
Most of your difficult decisions will be here. A study is quasi-experimental instead of randomized. A population overlaps with your inclusion criteria but isn't quite the same. You need pre-set decision rules. "If X, then we exclude" must be written BEFORE screening studies.
Good Practice

"We will exclude studies with <20 subjects because studies below this threshold tend to have wider confidence intervals and are often underpowered. We will exclude studies using X methodology because [specific methodological concern]. Studies that violate these criteria will be discussed in a sensitivity analysis."

Move 4: Explaining Heterogeneity

4 Statistical vs. clinical heterogeneity +
Heterogeneity (I-squared) tells you studies vary. But WHY? Is it clinical (different populations, interventions) or statistical noise? You must have a plan to explore both. High heterogeneity doesn't mean "don't pool"—it means "pool cautiously and investigate."
Addressing Heterogeneity

"If I-squared >75%, we will conduct subgroup analyses by age, gender, and baseline severity. If heterogeneity cannot be explained, we will report results using random-effects models and discuss clinical implications of the variation."

Move 5: Communicating Certainty of Evidence

5 GRADE methodology: rating quality of evidence +
GRADE (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluation) rates evidence as high, moderate, low, or very low. You'll be asked how you're assessing risk of bias, indirectness, imprecision, inconsistency, and publication bias. Know the framework.
GRADE Language

"We will downgrade for risk of bias if >25% of participants are in studies with unclear allocation concealment. We will downgrade for imprecision if the 95% CI crosses the minimal clinically important difference."

Move 6: The Meta-Analysis Decision

6 When to pool data, when to report narratively +
You don't HAVE to do meta-analysis. Systematic reviews can be narrative syntheses. If you choose meta-analysis, you must justify why pooling is appropriate. If you choose NOT to pool despite similar studies, defend that choice too.
The Decision

"We will conduct meta-analysis if >=3 studies with comparable populations and interventions are available. If studies are clinically too heterogeneous to pool, we will present a narrative synthesis organized by population subgroup."

Scaffold: Key Points

  • "Our PICOS is narrow because [rationale]. This increases specificity but may limit generalizability."
  • "We chose this date range because [methodological reason]."
  • "We will exclude [category] because [specific criterion]."
  • "If we find heterogeneity, we will [specific plan]."
  • "We're registering this protocol with [registry] to ensure transparency."
