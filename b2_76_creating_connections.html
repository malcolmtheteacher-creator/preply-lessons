Three Circles of Presence

The key to creating connections is understanding and working in the Second Circle. Here are the three approaches:

First Circle: Inward Focused

What it is: You are inwardly focused and introspective. You absorb external energy but send out little of your own.

Effect on others: People may perceive you as withdrawn, distracted, or uninterested.

When it happens: Stress, anxiety, fatigue, or lack of confidence can push you into the First Circle.

Energy flow: Low outbound → receiving only ← Low connection

Second Circle: Connection (The Sweet Spot)

What it is: The circle of genuine connection. Energy is reciprocal and balanced. It is a give-and-take exchange. This is the essence of true presence.

Effect on others: People feel heard, valued, and respected. Trust develops naturally.

Signs you are in the Second Circle:

  • You are fully listening and engaged
  • Your body language is open and relaxed
  • You make appropriate eye contact
  • You respond authentically to what others say
  • People want to work with and trust you

Third Circle: Outward Focused

What it is: You are outward facing and push energy onto others but absorb very little of theirs.

Effect on others: People may feel talked at, dominated, or dismissed. They may perceive you as arrogant or self-centered.

When it happens: Overconfidence, impatience, or the desire to control the conversation can push you into the Third Circle.

Energy flow: High outbound → not receiving ← Limited connection

Reflection Question

Which circle do you naturally gravitate toward? Think about your last important conversation or meeting. Were you primarily in the First, Second, or Third Circle?

Micro-Behaviours That Create Connections

Small actions have a big impact. Here are the key behaviours that strengthen relationships:

1. Active Listening

What it is: Fully focusing on what the other person is saying, without planning your response.

  • Put away your phone and distractions
  • Maintain appropriate eye contact
  • Nod and use small verbal cues ("I see," "That makes sense")
  • Ask clarifying questions
  • Reflect back what you heard

2. Authentic Presence

What it is: Being genuinely yourself, not putting on a false persona.

  • Show genuine interest in the other person
  • Be honest about your feelings and thoughts
  • Admit when you don't know something
  • Share appropriate personal details when relevant

3. Emotional Awareness

What it is: Recognizing emotions in yourself and others, and responding appropriately.

  • Notice emotional cues in the other person (tone, body language, facial expressions)
  • Acknowledge their emotions: "You seem concerned about this"
  • Manage your own emotions so they don't dominate the interaction
  • Show empathy: "That must have been difficult for you"

4. Remembering Details

What it is: Showing that what someone has told you matters by recalling and referring to it later.

  • Remember people's names and pronounce them correctly
  • Ask follow-up questions about things they've mentioned before
  • Reference previous conversations: "You mentioned last month that..."
  • Send a quick message about something they cared about

5. Small Acts of Consideration

What it is: Doing thoughtful things that show you care about the other person's wellbeing.

  • Offer help without being asked
  • Acknowledge important dates (birthdays, work anniversaries)
  • Give sincere compliments
  • Share relevant information or resources

6. Respectful Communication

What it is: Speaking to and about others with dignity and respect.

  • Use people's preferred names and titles
  • Don't interrupt
  • Avoid gossip or criticism
  • Disagree respectfully, focusing on ideas not people

Analyzing Connections: Speaking Task

You have 5 minutes to analyze and discuss how to create better connections with someone you know professionally. Choose a specific relationship and think about how to strengthen it.

Time remaining: 5:00

Structure Your Analysis:

1. Describe the Person

What do you know about this person's characteristics, values, and needs? What matters to them?

2. Identify Their Circle of Presence

Which circle of presence do you think is their preferred one (First, Second, or Third)? What signs make you think this?

3. Plan Your Approach

What approach could you take to create or strengthen a connection with this person? Think about:

  • Which micro-behaviours would be most effective?
  • How can you meet them in the Second Circle?
  • What are their interests and values?
  • How can you show genuine interest?

4. Expected Outcomes

What positive changes might happen in your relationship if you implement these strategies?

Discussion Questions

1. Meaningful Connections

What makes a connection with someone feel meaningful or genuine to you? What are the signs?

2. Trust and Relationships

How important is trust in building professional relationships? How long does it typically take to build trust?

3. Emotional Intelligence

4. Authenticity vs. Professional Image

How do you balance being authentic with maintaining a professional image at work?

5. Difficult Personalities

Is it possible to create a genuine connection with someone you find difficult? How would you approach it?

6. Diversity and Connection
