Conditionals for Hypothetical Negotiation Outcomes

Advanced negotiators use conditional structures to explore potential scenarios and their consequences. Complete these exercises to master this essential language.

Using Second and Third Conditionals

Express hypothetical situations and their possible outcomes.

If you were to sponsor us, it would expose your brand to a new demographic.

This expresses a hypothetical outcome if the company agrees to sponsor.

If we accepted your offer, there would be a number of conditions attached.

Shows what would result if the other party agrees to terms.

Were you to increase your investment, we could offer additional visibility.

Inverted conditional structure (more formal).

Advanced Linking Devices for Negotiation

Sophisticated negotiators use precise linking words to build logical arguments and manage counterarguments effectively. Master these transitions to sound more persuasive.

Practical Examples: Replace the Underlined Words

Available linking devices:

furthermore • moreover • additionally • in addition • as a result • consequently • therefore • so • however • nevertheless • still • although • even though • whereas • in contrast • on the other hand

ADDITION: Sponsoring our event would raise your company's profile. And it would help you reach a new demographic.

Furthermore / In addition / Moreover / Additionally it would help you reach a new demographic.

CONTRAST: We have to stand our ground on this. But we do think it might be possible to find a compromise.

However / Nevertheless / Still / Although we do think it might be possible to find a compromise.

RESULT: We don't want to delay negotiations. So it would be sensible to get the ball rolling straightaway.

As a result / Consequently / Therefore it would be sensible to get the ball rolling straightaway.

COMPARISON: Your target demographic is young professionals. On the other hand, our festival attracts families and older audiences.

Language for Achieving Consensus

These expressions help you navigate toward agreement by acknowledging shared interests, working out practical details, and confirming understanding. Click to see how each phrase functions.

Identifying Areas of Agreement

ACKNOWLEDGING COMMON GROUND
There's a lot of common ground between us.
Establishes a foundation for continued negotiation by highlighting shared interests
IDENTIFYING PRIORITIES
It's clear we both want a long-term relationship.
Brings focus to shared objectives that can anchor the agreement
MOVING FORWARD
Let's focus on areas where we can make progress.
Redirects attention away from deadlock toward productive conversation

Working Out the Details

PROBLEM-SOLVING
How can we make this work for both of us?
Signals openness to creative solutions and collaborative thinking
PROPOSING ACTION
Let's think about how we can structure this arrangement.
Moves the conversation from principle to practical implementation
SETTING CONDITIONS
We could make this happen if you were willing to...
Links agreement to specific conditions that would make the deal acceptable

Confirming Agreement

POSITIVE AGREEMENT
Yes, we could live with that.
Indicates acceptance without full enthusiasm, showing flexibility
CONDITIONAL ACCEPTANCE
That works for us, provided that...
Accepts a proposal while attaching conditions necessary for acceptance
SUMMARIZING AGREEMENT
So, to summarize our position: we agree on the following...
Clarifies what has been agreed upon and prevents misunderstandings
NEXT STEPS
Shall we draw up the terms in writing?
Moves negotiation toward formal agreement and documentation

Sponsorship Negotiation Task

You are going to role-play a negotiation between a festival organizer and a sponsoring company. Prepare your position, then conduct the negotiation, aiming for a mutually beneficial agreement.

Role A: Festival Director

Your Position

What you want: A major sponsorship from a drinks company to fund your annual film festival.

Financial target: At least $75,000, but you'd consider $60,000 with additional perks.

Key concerns: Your attendees (film lovers, aged 18-50, educated professionals) don't want excessive advertising. Maintain festival credibility.

What you can offer: Brand visibility through publicity, merchandise, venue signage, social media, and association with a prestigious cultural event.

Your bottom line: You must have sponsorship funding. Without it, the festival cannot proceed. Be willing to compromise on branding placement if the financial offer is good.

Role B: Drinks Company Representative

Your Position

What you want: Maximum brand exposure to reach affluent, educated professionals in the 25-50 age range.

Budget available: $50,000 is your opening offer. You could stretch to $70,000 if the exposure is exceptional.

Key requirements: Prominent branding, sampling opportunities, social media presence, and documented ROI. You need to see short-term returns on investment.

What you want to negotiate: Product placement, branded merchandise, exclusive beverage rights at the venue.

Your bottom line: You will only commit to a multi-year partnership if the first year demonstrates strong brand awareness among your target demographic.

Preparation: What Do You Want?

Before the negotiation, write down your priorities, what you're willing to compromise on, and your bottom line.

Useful Negotiation Language

Specific sponsorship terms:

  • Branding on all promotional materials and publicity
  • Logo placement on venue signage, banners, and digital displays
  • Exclusive beverage rights (only your brand served)
  • Product sampling opportunities for attendees
  • Featured presence in social media posts and campaigns
  • Mention in press releases and media coverage
  • VIP hospitality area at the festival
  • Annual partnership with commitment to future years
