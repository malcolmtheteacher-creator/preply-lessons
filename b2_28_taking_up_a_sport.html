Taking Up a Sport

B2 Lesson — Persuasion & Hobbies

Let's Start

Think about sports and hobbies you know. Here are some questions to explore:

What is your favourite sport, and why do you enjoy it?

Take a moment to think. What sport or activity do you love? Why does it make you happy? What keeps you coming back?

Which sports are the most popular in your country?

Think about what you see on TV, in parks, or in schools. Which sports do most people know or play?

Do you prefer to watch sports or play them? Why?

Are you someone who enjoys the action and competition of playing? Or do you prefer watching others perform? What draws you to one or the other?

If you could start learning a new sport, which one would it be, and why?

Dream a little. What sport have you always wanted to try? What appeals to you about it?

Persuasive Language

When you want to convince someone to try a new sport or hobby, certain language patterns work best. Read these phrases and guess which category they fit.

Four Categories of Persuasion

  • A. Encouraging Action: Words that push someone to do something
  • B. Appealing to Logic: Reasons and evidence
  • C. Generally Persuasive Phrases: Friendly, convincing language
  • D. Expressing Benefits: What someone will gain
"Why not give it a try?"

Answer: A (Encouraging Action) — It invites action in a casual, friendly way.

"You'd be really good at it."

Answer: C (Generally Persuasive Phrases) — It appeals to confidence and potential.

"It's proven to improve both your physical and mental health."

Answer: B (Appealing to Logic) — It uses research and evidence.

"You'll make so many friends and have fun."

Answer: D (Expressing Benefits) — It focuses on positive outcomes.

"I really think you should try it."

Answer: A (Encouraging Action) — It's a direct, personal recommendation.

"You'll feel stronger and more confident over time."

Answer: D (Expressing Benefits) — It highlights personal gains.

Using Persuasion Language

Notice how the best persuasive speakers combine these strategies. A strong pitch uses logic, shows benefits, and encourages action — all with genuine confidence.

Your Hobby Pitch

Now you«ll prepare a short presentation persuading your partner to take up one of your favourite hobbies or sports.

What to Include

  • How long you have been doing it
  • Why you started
  • What equipment and skills you need
  • The benefits (physical, mental, social)
  • Why your partner should try it

Sample Framework

"I've been [hobby] for [time period]. I started because... The main things you need are... What I love most is that it... You should try it because it will help you..."

Tips for Your Pitch

  • Start with your own genuine enthusiasm — that«s contagious
  • Use specific examples from your own experience
  • Address any concerns your partner might have
  • Mix persuasion strategies: logic + benefits + encouragement
  • End with a clear call to action: "Why not try it?"

Ready?

Take 5 minutes to prepare. Think about your hobby and how to present it persuasively. Your partner will listen, ask questions, and give you feedback.
