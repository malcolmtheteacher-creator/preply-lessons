Why do misunderstandings happen?

In today's lesson, we'll explore the many ways people misunderstand each other. We'll learn how to tell stories about these experiences using special verb forms that help explain what happened and when.

What Leads to Misunderstandings?

Misunderstandings happen for many reasons. Read these situations and see how each one could create a problem:

Not expressing yourself clearly
When you don't explain your ideas well, people might form the wrong idea.
Not listening carefully
When you don't pay close attention, you miss important details.
Misinterpreting what someone means
Understanding something in a wrong way, even if you heard it correctly.
Cultural differences
Different cultures have different rules about what is polite, rude, or normal.
Linguistic differences
Words or expressions that mean something different in another language.
Lack of knowledge
When you don't understand something because you haven't learned enough about it.
Information through a third party
Hearing news from someone else, not from the original source — it often gets mixed up.

Example:

"If you don't express yourself clearly, people might form the wrong idea of what you are trying to say."

Think about these situations. Which cause applies to each one?

Past Perfect & Past Perfect Continuous

When we tell stories about misunderstandings, we often need to explain what happened before another event. We use past perfect (had + 3rd form) and past perfect continuous (had been + -ing).

Real Examples from Stories About Misunderstandings

Past Perfect (completed action)
"Anyway, turned out I'd got my syllables mixed up and I wasn't saying "Bless you"…"
What happened: "I had got my syllables mixed up" (before they realized it). The past perfect shows this happened before they realized the mistake.
Past Perfect Continuous (duration)
"I think I'd been going around saying 'toilet bowl' for a number of years before somebody managed to correct me."
What happened: "I had been going around saying..." emphasizes HOW LONG this went on (years!). The focus is on the duration, not just the fact that it happened.
Past Perfect (completed action)
"After I discovered what a mistake I'd been making, it registered..."
What happened: "I had been making" (this is also past perfect continuous, showing the ongoing mistake). After discovering it, they understood.
Past Perfect (completed action)
"I'd realised from her accent she was Scottish but it was only then I realised what she'd been saying was 'Is that in your way?'"
What happened: Two past perfects here! "I had realised" + "she had been saying" — both show actions that finished before the moment of understanding.
Past Perfect (completed action)
"Seems obvious now, but I just hadn't heard that."
What happened: "I had not heard" — a simple past perfect showing the lack of knowledge before this moment.

The Pattern

  • Had + 3rd form = a completed action that happened before another past action
  • Had been + -ing = a completed action with emphasis on how long it lasted or how often

Plan Your Own Anecdote

Your Task

Think of a misunderstanding — either your own experience or someone else's. It can be linguistic (about words or language) or any other kind. Write some notes about it.

What to Include

🎯
What happened
The misunderstanding and how it was discovered
⏰
When
When did this happen? How long had it been going on?
🔍
Why it happened
What caused the misunderstanding?
💬
The reaction
How did people feel when they realized?

Grammar Focus

Try to use:

  • At least 2 examples of past perfect ("I'd", "he'd", "she'd", "they'd")
  • At least 1 example of past perfect continuous ("I'd been", "he'd been", etc.)

Preparation Time

2:00

When you're ready:

Tell your story to your partner. Your partner should ask follow-up questions about the misunderstanding. Then swap — listen to their story and ask questions.
