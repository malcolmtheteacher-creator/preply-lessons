English has two kinds of past verbs. Most verbs just add -ed (watch → watched) — we call these regular. But some very common verbs change completely (go → went) — we call these irregular. You have to learn those ones by heart, but there aren't many of the really common ones.
Today: regular verbs with -ed, seven common irregular verbs (go, see, eat, have, make, get, come), and questions and negatives with did — so you can tell someone about yesterday.

Regular Verbs: Add -ed

Most verbs are easy — just add -ed for the past.

Spot the pattern

Three things people said this morning:

  • "I watched a film last night."
  • "We walked to the shop."
  • "She cleaned her room this morning."

Before you check: what do all three bold verbs have in common?

Check the pattern CLICK
Every verb just adds -ed to the end: watch → watched, walk → walked, clean → cleaned. The verb stays the same for I, you, he, she, we, they — no extra "s" like in the present.
verb + -ed
Small spelling note: if the verb already ends in -e, just add -d. For example: dance → danced, arrive → arrived.

The Wild Ones: Irregular Verbs

A few very common verbs don't follow the -ed rule. They change completely.

Spot the pattern

  • "I went to the beach."
  • "She saw a good film."
  • "We ate breakfast at 8 o'clock."

Check the pattern CLICK
No -ed here! These verbs change into a completely new word: go → went, see → saw, eat → ate. Seven of the most useful ones to learn now: go → went, see → saw, eat → ate, have → had, make → made, get → got, come → came.

Questions & No: "Did you...?"

To ask a question or say "no" in the past, we need the little word did.

Spot the pattern

Watch what happens to the same idea three ways:

"I washed the car."
→
"I didn't wash the car."
"I washed the car."
→
"Did you wash the car?"

Look carefully: in the negative and the question, is the verb "washed" or "wash"? Say your idea before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
It's "wash" — the base verb, with no -ed! Once "did" appears, it carries the past for the whole sentence, so the main verb goes back to normal. This is true for irregular verbs too: not "did you went", but "did you go?"
Positive: subject + verb-ed / irregular past
Negative: subject + did not (didn't) + base verb
Question: Did + subject + base verb ... ?
Common mistake to avoid: never put two past forms together, like "did went" or "didn't went". Once "did" is there, the main verb stays in its base form.
