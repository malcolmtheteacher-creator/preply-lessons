Is a wrong explanation better than no explanation at all?

Your laptop suddenly stops working. One friend shrugs: "It's just bad luck." Another says, "Let's guess why. Maybe the battery? Maybe the update?" Meet Thales

Read one part at a time. After each part, close your eyes and answer the question before you check.

I

A city of ships and ideas

Thales lived from about 624 to 546 BC in Miletus, a rich Greek trading city on the coast of what is now Turkey. Ships arrived from Egypt and Babylon, bringing goods and new ideas. None of Thales' writings survive, so most of what we know comes from later writers, especially Aristotle. The Greeks counted him among the Seven Wise Men, and today he is often called the first philosopher.

Why do we know about Thales mainly through Aristotle?

Because none of Thales' own writings survive. Later writers, especially Aristotle, reported his ideas.
II

Everything is water

The Greeks already had rich stories about gods who made the world. Thales asked a different question: not who made the world, but what it is made of. His answer was water. It sounds strange, but think about it. Water can be ice, liquid or steam, and every living thing needs it. Perhaps, he thought, one simple thing lies behind all the variety we see. We don't know his exact reasons; even Aristotle could only guess.

What different question did Thales ask about the world?

Not who made the world, but what it is made of. He looked for a natural answer, and his answer was water.
III

Eyes on the sky

Thales is said to have predicted an eclipse of the sun in 585 BC, by watching nature carefully instead of blaming angry gods. Plato tells another old story. One night Thales was walking along, looking up at the stars, and he fell into a well. A servant girl laughed at him: he wanted to understand the heavens, but he couldn't see what was at his own feet. People have told that joke about thinkers ever since.

What happened to Thales while he was looking at the stars, according to the story?

He fell into a well, and a servant girl laughed that he could study the sky but not see what was at his feet.
Talk: Thales' answer, water, was wrong. But his way of asking was new. In science or in life, which matters more: the right answer, or the right kind of question?

Key words

Each word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means. Then reveal and compare.

1. Without any written records, we can only speculate about why the ancient city was abandoned.
speculate (v) — to form ideas or guesses about something when you don't have all the facts. Example: "Everyone is speculating about why the manager suddenly left."
2. If you observe the night sky for a whole year, you'll notice that the stars slowly change position.
observe (v) — to watch something carefully in order to learn about it. Example: "The scientists observed the birds for six months before writing their report."
3. Water, salt and iron are all different substances, and each one behaves in its own way.
substance (n) — a particular kind of material with its own qualities. Example: "Honey is a thick, sticky substance made by bees."
4. Lightning was once a terrifying mystery, but today we can explain the phenomenon with electricity.
phenomenon (n) — something that happens or exists that people can see or experience, especially something unusual (plural: phenomena). Example: "Rainbows are a beautiful natural phenomenon."
5. Nobody could predict the result of the match; it was a complete surprise.
predict (v) — to say what will happen in the future, based on knowledge or experience. Example: "The weather app predicts heavy rain tomorrow afternoon."
6. Darwin's theory tried to explain how living things slowly change over millions of years.
theory (n) — an idea or set of ideas that tries to explain why something happens. Example: "Her theory is that people are kinder when they aren't in a hurry."
7. Standing under the night sky, she was filled with wonder at how huge the universe is.
wonder (n) — a feeling of surprise and admiration when you see something beautiful, strange or new. Example: "The little boy stared at the falling snow with wonder."

The idea: Looking for natural explanations

1

2

GuessSuggest one natural cause: water.
4
CheckTest the guess against what you see, and improve it.

See it in action

Here is the idea in action. Imagine a village where the river floods every spring. The old story says: 'The river god is angry.' A thinker like Thales says: 'Perhaps the floods come when the snow on the mountains melts.' Then she watches. Are the floods bigger after a snowy winter? If yes, the guess gets stronger. If not, she tries a new one. Her answer might be wrong, but now it can be tested and improved.

Thought experiment: The mysterious lights

Strange lights appear in the sky above your town every night for a week. Nobody can explain them. One expert says, "My theory is that it's a reflection from the new factory. I might be wrong, but we can test it." Another expert says, "We don't know yet. It's better to say nothing until we have all the facts."

Connect

Compare

Modern science and Thales: Thales guessed that everything is water. Modern scientists say everything is made of atoms, and atoms of even smaller particles. In what ways is a modern scientist doing the same thing as Thales, and in what ways something different?
Both look for a natural explanation and try to find a few simple things behind the variety of the world. The difference is that modern scientists test their ideas with careful experiments and measurements, and they change a theory when the evidence goes against it.
