An English lesson on special events and narrative skills

Tell About One

Choose one special event you've attended and think about these questions:

  • Where and when did it take place?
  • How many guests were there? Who were they?
  • How were guests dressed?
  • Was this typical of that kind of event in your country?
  • What was the atmosphere like?
  • What do you remember most vividly?

Cultural Context

Different cultures celebrate special occasions in different ways. The dress code, the timing, the food, the activities — everything can vary.

Think about how special occasions are celebrated in your country. What makes an event "special" there? What rituals or customs are typical? How is it different from what you've seen in other cultures?

Past Simple vs Past Continuous in Storytelling

When we tell stories about past events, we use two tenses together:

  • Past Continuous sets the scene — what was happening in the background
  • Past Simple tells the main events — what happened

Example

"The sun was shining but it wasn't too hot. Guests were arriving in expensive cars. The bride's father was greeting everyone at the entrance. Suddenly, the first violin note rang out and everyone fell silent."

Notice: "was shining", "was arriving", "was greeting" (continuous) create atmosphere. "rang out", "fell silent" (simple past) show the action.

Why This Matters

These tenses work together to create vivid storytelling. The past continuous gives us the "camera" perspective — we're watching the scene unfold. The past simple moves the story forward.

How to Write About a Special Event

A well-written letter about a special occasion has three clear parts:

1. Introduction

Purpose: Set up your letter and explain why you're writing.

Include:

  • What event you're writing about
  • When it happened
  • Why you're sharing this story

Example opening: "I wanted to tell you about the most amazing graduation ceremony I attended last month. It was unforgettable."

2. Body Paragraphs

Purpose: Develop your story with vivid detail and personal response.

Include:

  • Events in chronological order
  • Descriptions of the setting, people, atmosphere
  • Your personal feelings and reactions
  • What went well and what was challenging

Remember: Use past continuous to set scenes, past simple for main events.

3. Conclusion

Purpose: Wrap up your story with reflection and a sense of closure.

Include:

  • Your overall opinion of the event
  • What it meant to you
  • What's next (are you looking forward to similar events?)

Example closing: "Looking back, it was a day I'll never forget. It made me realise how much family means to me."

Your Turn: Plan and Describe a Special Event

Choose one of these events and plan your description:

  • An opening night at the theatre
  • A graduation ceremony
  • An important birthday party
  • A concert
  • An awards ceremony

Planning Framework

Think about these points before you start writing:

  • Where and when? Location, date, time of day
  • Who was there? Friends, family, celebrities, strangers?
  • What happened? The sequence of events
  • What was the atmosphere? Exciting, emotional, formal, casual?
  • What went well? Highlights and memorable moments
  • What was challenging? Did anything go wrong? How did people react?
  • How did you feel? Your emotional journey through the event

Speaking Tips

  • Use past continuous to paint the scene: "It was raining..."
  • Use past simple for main events: "Then everyone stood up and..."
  • Include adjectives to make it vivid: "The beautiful bride", "the excited crowd"
  • Don't rush. Pause between ideas.
  • It's okay to say "um" or "let me think" — that's natural in English.
  • Be specific with details — they make the story memorable.

Reflection & Discussion

How often do you attend special occasions?

Think about the frequency and what role they play in your life.

What do you like most about them?

The celebration? The company? The formality? The traditions?

What do you find challenging about them?

The cost? The emotional intensity? Having to dress up or behave formally? Meeting people you don't know?

What special occasions are celebrated regularly in your country?

National holidays? Religious festivals? Seasonal celebrations? How are they unique to your culture?
