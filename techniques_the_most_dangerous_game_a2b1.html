The Most Dangerous Game

A famous hunter falls into the sea, swims to a strange island, and meets a man who hunts a very different animal.

Today: you'll read a famous adventure story about a hunter who becomes the hunted — and you'll practise the past continuous with the past simple, so you can say what was happening when something else happened.
Warm up — before we read. Talk about these out loud. There are no wrong answers.
Discuss:
  • Do you like adventure stories or films? Which one is your favourite, and why?
  • Imagine you are alone on a strange island. What is the first thing you do?
  • Is it ever right to hunt animals? Why, or why not?

Now — the words for today's story

Ten words. Do the four short activities, and say your answer out loud before you reveal it.

A · What do they mean here? Look at the sentence

1. The island was covered in thick green trees and plants. It was hot, wet and dark in the jungle, and you could not see far.
a jungle (n) — a hot, wet forest where trees and plants grow very close together. "Tigers live in the jungle."
2. He ran and ran until the trees stopped. In front of him there was only air, and far below, the sea hit the rocks. He was standing at the edge of a cliff.
a cliff (n) — a very high wall of rock, usually next to the sea. "Be careful near the edge of the cliff."
3. He dug a hole and covered it with branches. If a dog walked there, it would fall in. It was a good trap.
a trap (n) — something you make to catch a person or an animal. Verb: to trap. "The mouse is in the trap."

B · Which word means…?

Three words: prey · to chase · to escape. Say the right word out loud, then reveal.

1. Which word means an animal (or a person) that another animal hunts?
prey (n, no plural) — "The lion watched its prey." The hunter is the predator.
2. Which verb means to run after someone to catch them?
to chase (v) — chase, chased, chased. "The dog chased the cat across the garden."
3. Which verb means to get away from a dangerous place or person?
to escape (v) — "Two prisoners escaped last night." Noun: an escape.

C · Choose the meaning — a / b / c

1. Rainsford was not afraid of tigers or buffalo. He was a very brave man. Brave means…
  (a) not afraid of danger   (b) very tired   (c) very rich
(a) — brave (adj) = ready to do dangerous things, not afraid. Opposite: afraid, scared.
2. The general looked everywhere, but he could not see Rainsford. Rainsford was hiding in a tree. Hiding means…
  (a) running very fast   (b) sleeping   (c) staying where nobody can see you
(c) — to hide (v) = to go or stay where people cannot find you. hide – hid – hidden.

D · Word families

1. The man shot animals for sport. The verb is to hunt. What is the word for the person who does it?
to hunt (v) → person: a hunter; noun for the activity: hunting. "My grandfather was a hunter."
2. "Don't go into the jungle — it is full of danger." What is the adjective? And what is the opposite of that adjective?
danger (n) → adjective dangerous. Opposite: safe (noun: safety). "Is it safe to swim here?"

Now use them — out loud

Talk before we read:
  • Use jungle, chase and escape in one short story of three sentences.
  • Which of today's words are about the hunter? Which are about the person who runs? Sort them out loud.

The story, in three parts

This is Richard Connell's story from 1924, in simple English. Some parts are dark, but nothing is described in detail. After each part, answer the questions from memory, then discuss.

Part 1 — The island

Rainsford was a famous hunter. One night he was standing alone on a yacht near the Caribbean. The other men were asleep. Suddenly he heard three shots in the dark. They came from an island that sailors called Ship-Trap Island, because ships often hit its rocks and sank.

Rainsford leaned over the rail to look. His pipe hit a rope and fell, and when he reached for it, he fell into the sea. The yacht sailed away. Nobody heard him shout.

He swam towards the shots. At dawn he was tired, but he reached the rocks. In the jungle he found footprints and an empty bullet. Then he saw a big house with towers and a heavy iron door.

A tall, elegant man opened the door. "I am General Zaroff," he said. "You are Rainsford, the famous hunter! I read your book. Please, come in."

At dinner the general said, "I am bored. Tigers and buffalo are too easy now. So I found a new animal to hunt. It is clever, and it can feel fear."

"You mean… a man," said Rainsford.

"Yes. Lights on the coast bring ships onto the rocks. I take the sailors who survive. I give them food and a knife, and a three-hour start in the jungle. If they stay alive for three days, they are free. But nobody has won."

"That is murder!" said Rainsford. "I will never hunt a man."

The general smiled. "Then you will be the prey."

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did Rainsford get to the island?

2. What are the rules of the general's game?

1. He fell from a yacht into the sea at night and swam to the island, towards the sound of shots.

2. The man gets food, a knife and a three-hour start. If he is alive after three days, he is free.

Discuss:
  • Zaroff is polite, clever and rich. Why does that make him more frightening, not less?

Part 2 — Three days

Rainsford ran into the jungle with his knife. At first he ran without thinking. Then he stopped, and the old hunter in his head woke up. He went in a circle, crossed his own steps and made a false path.

At midnight he climbed a tree. Soon the general came and looked at the ground. Then he looked up, straight at Rainsford, and smiled. Then he walked away. Rainsford understood. Zaroff could catch him, but he wanted to play for longer.

The next day Rainsford made a trap from a dead tree. It hit the general's shoulder, but it did not kill him. Zaroff laughed and went home to rest.

On the second night Rainsford dug a hole and covered it with branches. One of Zaroff's dogs fell in and died. On the third day the other dogs were chasing him again. Rainsford tied his knife to a young tree and bent it across the path. It killed one of the general's men, but the dogs did not stop.

Then the trees ended. Rainsford was standing at the edge of a cliff. Far below, the sea hit the rocks. He could hear the dogs behind him, very near. There was no way to escape.

Rainsford took a breath and jumped.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did Zaroff smile when he looked up at the tree?

2. What did Rainsford do at the cliff?

1. He saw Rainsford, but he did not catch him. He wanted the game to continue.

2. He jumped into the sea.

Discuss:
  • Rainsford was a hunter before. Now he is the prey. What do you think he feels?

Part 3 — The bedroom

Before you read this part, say what you think happens. Is Rainsford alive? Then read and check.

Zaroff stood at the top of the cliff and looked at the empty sea. He was a little sad. Rainsford was the best opponent he ever had. "The sea has taken him," he thought.

That evening he ate a good dinner and drank some wine. He read a book for an hour. Then he went to his bedroom and turned on the light.

A man was standing behind the curtain.

"Rainsford!" said the general.

Rainsford was not dead. He swam round the island and climbed up the wall of the house while the general and the dogs were searching the shore. The jump from the cliff was a surprise. It was the one thing Zaroff did not expect.

The general smiled slowly. "I congratulate you. You have won the game."

"I am still an animal that is being hunted," said Rainsford quietly.

Zaroff bowed. "Then one of us will feed the dogs tonight, and the other will sleep in this very good bed. En garde, Rainsford."

The story does not show the fight. It ends with one sentence: Rainsford slept in the general's bed, and he never slept in a better one.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did Rainsford survive?

2. What happens at the end? How do we know?

1. He swam round the island and climbed the wall of the house while Zaroff was searching the shore.

2. The writer does not show the fight. But Rainsford slept in Zaroff's bed, so we understand that Rainsford won.

Discuss:
  • Why does the writer not describe the fight? Is it better or worse for the reader?

Grammar — was doing & did

To tell a story well, you need two past tenses. One shows the background — what was happening. The other shows the event — what happened, and interrupted it.

1 · The past continuous: was / were + verb-ing.
It describes an action in progress at a moment in the past.
• I was cooking dinner.   • They were watching TV.
• Negative: He wasn't listening.   Question: Were you sleeping?
2 · Past continuous + past simple.
• A long action was happening → a short event interrupted it. Use when before the short event:
  I was walking home when it started to rain.
• Two long actions at the same time. Use while:
  She was studying while her brother was playing music.
• A list of finished actions in order: past simple only. He opened the door, looked outside and left.

1. Past continuous — complete the verb

1. Rainsford (stand) alone on the yacht.

2. The dogs (bark) behind him.

3. The general (not / sleep) in his bedroom — he was reading.

4. Zaroff (look) for Rainsford on the shore?

1. was standing   2. were barking   3. wasn't sleeping   4. Was … looking

Zaroff is one person, so: was. The dogs are many, so: were.

2. Which tense? Past continuous or past simple

Write the correct form of each verb. Ask yourself: is it a long action in progress, or a short event?

1. Rainsford (swim) towards the island when the sun (come) up.

2. When he (reach) the shore, he was very tired.

3. While Zaroff (read) a book, Rainsford (hide) behind the curtain.

4. The dogs (run) through the jungle when one of them (fall) into the hole.

1. was swimming · came   2. reached   3. was reading · was hiding   4. were running · fell

In number 2, "reached" is a short event, not a background. In number 3, both actions happen at the same time, so both are continuous.

3. Tell the night — complete the paragraph

Put in the verb in the correct tense, or the missing word (when / while). Then read the whole paragraph aloud.

It (be) midnight and Rainsford (sit) in a tree. Zaroff (walk) below him he suddenly (look) up. He (smile) and then he (go) away. the general (search) the shore with his dogs, Rainsford (climb) the wall of the house.

was · was sitting · was walking · when · looked · smiled · went · While · was searching · climbed

"looked", "smiled" and "went" are short events in order. "was walking" and "was searching" are the background.

Going deeper

Richard Connell published this story in 1924, a few years after the First World War. Many men had come home from the war. Some of them were used to violence, and some people worried about what that did to them. The story asks a hard question: what happens to a hunter when he becomes the prey?

Why does it still work? The idea is simple and strong: the hunter who does not care about animals' fear must feel it himself. Zaroff is elegant and polite, with a beautiful house, good food and fine manners — but he does terrible things. Connell shows that a nice surface does not mean a good person.

The last sentence. Rainsford sleeps in Zaroff's bed. Did he change his mind about hunting? Or did he just become a better hunter? Connell does not say. The reader has to decide.
Talk it through:
  • Rainsford says hunted animals have nothing to feel — "the world is made up of two classes, the hunters and the huntees." After the island, is he the same man?
  • Zaroff is polite and civilised. Can a person be both polite and cruel? Do you know an example from films or books?
  • The story does not show the fight at the end. What do you imagine happened?
  • Do you think Rainsford was right to fight Zaroff? What else could he do?
