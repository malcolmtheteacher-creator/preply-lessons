Lesson 1: Who Was Abraham?

Abraham's Story

🔒 Try to answer from memory first!

Key Points: Abraham was called by God to leave his home and go to a promised land. He trusted God even when asked to sacrifice his son Isaac. All three Abrahamic faiths see Abraham as the founding figure. For Jews, he's the father of the nation. For Christians, he's a model of faith and covenant. For Muslims, he (Ibrahim) is a great prophet and father of Ishmael.

Lesson 2: Judaism

Core Beliefs and Practices

🔒 Try to answer from memory first!

Key Points: Monotheism—God is one. The Torah is the most sacred text. Jews see themselves as descendants of Abraham through Isaac, and believe they are in covenant with God. Sabbath (weekly day of rest), keeping kosher (dietary laws), celebrating festivals like Passover and Hanukkah, daily prayers and blessings. The Talmud (rabbinic interpretation) guides how Jews live and understand the Torah today.

Lesson 3: Christianity

Core Belief, Scripture, and Practices

🔒 Try to answer from memory first!

Key Points: Jesus Christ is the Son of God and Saviour of humanity. The Trinity—one God in three persons (Father, Son, Holy Spirit)—is central to Christian theology. The Bible, including the Old Testament (Hebrew scriptures) and the New Testament (Gospels, epistles, Revelation). Worship, prayer, the sacraments of Baptism and Communion (Eucharist), celebrating Christmas (Jesus's birth) and Easter (resurrection). Jesus died for humanity's sins and rose from the dead, offering salvation to all who believe.

Lesson 4: Islam

Core Belief, Scripture, and Practices

🔒 Try to answer from memory first!

Key Points: Tawhid—the absolute oneness of Allah (God). The Quran is the word of God revealed to the Prophet Muhammad. The Five Pillars: Shahada (declaration of faith), Salah (five daily prayers), Zakat (almsgiving), Sawm (fasting during Ramadan), and Hajj (pilgrimage to Mecca). Ibrahim (Abraham) is greatly honoured as a great prophet and patriarch. Muslims see themselves as spiritual descendants of Abraham through Ishmael.

Lesson 5: Abraham's Legacy Today

Continuity and Difference

🔒 Try to answer from memory first!

Key Points: Abraham connects the three faiths through covenant, faith, and monotheism. Each faith interprets Abraham's story differently and holds him as a founding figure. Jews trace their identity back to Abraham through Isaac and the covenant at Sinai. Christians see Abraham as a model of faith and a foreshadowing of Christ. Muslims honour Ibrahim as a great prophet and see themselves as his spiritual descendants. Despite differences in theology and practice, all three traditions uphold Abraham as a hero of faith and moral leadership.

Encounter: Key Sources and Ideas

Here are key extracts and ideas you'll need for your assessment tasks.

Connections Across the Faiths

Abraham (Abram): All three faiths trace their spiritual heritage back to Abraham. He is often called the "Father of Faith" because he trusted God absolutely, even when asked to sacrifice his son.

  • Jews: Abraham is the founder of the Jewish nation. God's covenant with Abraham established the Jewish people and their special relationship with God.
  • Christians: Abraham is a model of faith. Paul in the New Testament calls Abraham the "father of all those who believe" (Romans 4:11).
  • Muslims: Ibrahim (Abraham) is honoured as one of the greatest prophets. The Quran mentions him many times, and he is seen as a bridge between the prophets.

The Test of Faith: In Genesis 22, God asks Abraham to sacrifice his son Isaac. This is seen as the ultimate test of faith:

  • Jews interpret this as a test Abraham passes, proving his devotion. God intervenes and provides a ram instead.
  • Christians see it as foreshadowing Christ's sacrifice.
  • Muslims (Quran 37:99–109) recount the story with Ishmael as the son (though Abraham obeys, God provides a ram).

Key Quotes and Texts

Task 2: Interpreting the Sacrifice Story

Genesis 22 and Quran 37 both recount the story of Abraham being asked to sacrifice his son. Compare and contrast how the two texts tell this story. What does each tradition emphasise?

  • What is the same in both accounts?
  • What is different (e.g., the name of the son)?
  • Why might these differences matter to each faith?

Task 3: Modern Relevance

Discuss: How might Abraham's example of faith and obedience speak to people of the three faiths today? What challenges do believers face in living out the values Abraham represents?

Review: Key Learning and Next Steps

Consolidate your understanding with this summary.

Abraham's Role in the Three Faiths: Summary

Judaism: Abraham is the founder of the Jewish nation. God's covenant with Abraham established the special relationship between God and the Jewish people. Jews are called "children of Abraham" through descent from Isaac.

Christianity: Abraham is a model of faith and obedience. Paul teaches that Abraham's faith—not works—justified him before God. All believers are called "children of Abraham" through faith in Christ.

Islam: Ibrahim is honoured as one of the greatest prophets and patriarchs. Muslims see themselves as Abraham's spiritual descendants through Ishmael. The Hajj pilgrimage commemorates Abraham's obedience and willingness to sacrifice.

Key Themes Across All Three Faiths

  • Faith and Trust: All three faiths emphasise Abraham's absolute trust in God, even when challenged.
  • Covenant: God's covenant with Abraham is central to Jewish theology and foundational to Christian and Islamic understanding.
  • Chosen People: Each faith claims spiritual descent from Abraham, though they understand this differently.
  • Obedience and Sacrifice: Abraham's willingness to sacrifice Isaac/Ishmael is interpreted as the ultimate act of devotion across all three traditions.
