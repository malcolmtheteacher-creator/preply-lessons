Lesson 1: Who Was Abraham?

Abraham's Story: Abraham was called by God to leave his home and go to a promised land. He trusted God even when asked to sacrifice his son Isaac. All three Abrahamic faiths see Abraham as the founding figure. For Jews, he's the father of the nation. For Christians, he's a model of faith and covenant. For Muslims, he (Ibrahim) is a great prophet and father of Ishmael.
🔒

Lesson 2: Judaism

Core Beliefs: Monotheism—God is one. The Torah is the most sacred text. Jews see themselves as descendants of Abraham through Isaac, and believe they are in covenant with God. Key Practices: Sabbath (weekly day of rest), keeping kosher (dietary laws), celebrating festivals like Passover and Hanukkah, daily prayers and blessings. Authority: The Talmud (rabbinic interpretation) guides how Jews live and understand the Torah today.
🔒

Lesson 3: Christianity

Core Belief: Jesus Christ is the Son of God and Saviour of humanity. The Trinity—one God in three persons (Father, Son, Holy Spirit)—is central to Christian theology. Scripture: The Bible, including the Old Testament (Hebrew scriptures) and the New Testament (Gospels, epistles, Revelation). Key Practices: Worship, prayer, the sacraments of Baptism and Communion (Eucharist), celebrating Christmas (Jesus's birth) and Easter (resurrection). Salvation: Christians believe Jesus died for humanity's sins and rose from the dead, offering salvation to all who believe.
🔒

Lesson 4: Islam

Core Belief: Tawhid—belief in one God, Allah, who is absolute and indivisible. The Prophet: Muhammad is the final and greatest prophet, who received God's revelation (the Qur'an). Scripture: The Qur'an is God's literal word, revealed to Muhammad. The Hadith records Muhammad's actions and teachings. Five Pillars: (1) Shahada (declaration of faith), (2) Salah (five daily prayers), (3) Zakat (alms-giving), (4) Sawm (fasting during Ramadan), (5) Hajj (pilgrimage to Mecca). View of Jesus: Jesus (Isa) is an honoured prophet and born of the virgin Mary, but not the Son of God.
🔒

Lesson 5: What Unites and Divides?

Uniting Factors: All three share Abraham as founder, believe in one God, practice prayer and devotion, follow revealed scripture, emphasize ethical living, and trace their origins to the ancient Middle East. Key Differences: Christianity teaches the Trinity and sees Jesus as divine; Islam strictly rejects any partner to God and sees Muhammad as the final prophet; Judaism doesn't accept Jesus as the Messiah and doesn't believe God became human. Practices: Judaism and Islam have detailed dietary laws; Islam has the Five Pillars; Christianity emphasizes faith in Jesus and the sacraments.
🔒

Task 2: Extended Writing — Explain

2 The Question

"Explain how the three Abrahamic faiths trace their origins to Abraham and how this shared heritage shapes their beliefs today."

Aim for 250–350 words. Use the scaffold and starters below to help you structure your answer.

Writing Scaffold

Paragraph 1: Introduction — Set the scene
"The three Abrahamic faiths—Judaism, Christianity, and Islam—share a common root in the life and covenant of Abraham. Although they developed differently over time, Abraham's faith and relationship with God remain central to all three traditions. This means..."
Paragraph 2: Abraham's Role in Each Faith
"For Jews, Abraham is the founding patriarch because... For Christians, Abraham represents... For Muslims, Abraham (Ibrahim) is important because..."
Paragraph 3: Shared Heritage & Beliefs
"All three faiths share a belief in... They also both value... This shared heritage means that all three traditions emphasize..."
Paragraph 4: How This Shapes Modern Beliefs
"Abraham's covenant with God shapes Jewish beliefs today by... It influences Christian theology by... For Muslims, Abraham's example teaches..."
Paragraph 5: Conclusion
"In conclusion, Abraham's shared heritage unites the three Abrahamic faiths because... This means that even though they differ in some beliefs, they remain connected by..."

Your Answer

Helpful Reminders:

  • Answer the question asked—explain both the connection to Abraham AND how this shapes beliefs today
  • Use linking words like "because," "this means," "as a result"
  • Give specific examples: Torah, prayer, prophets, God's covenant
  • Use subject vocabulary confidently: monotheism, covenant, prophet, revelation, faith
  • Check your spelling, punctuation, and grammar

Task 3: Evaluation Essay

3 The Question

"'The Abrahamic faiths have more in common than what divides them.' How far do you agree?"

Aim for 300–400 words. Present a balanced argument with evidence from at least two traditions, then give your reasoned judgment.

Planning Frame

Arguments FOR ("More in Common")
Arguments AGAINST ("More Divided")

Sentence Starters for Balance

To introduce the "for" side: "The Abrahamic faiths do share significant similarities. For example, all three believe in... Additionally, they are united by..."
To introduce the "against" side: "However, significant differences also divide the three faiths. Christianity teaches..., whereas Islam believes..., and Judaism emphasizes..."
To introduce your conclusion: "On balance, I believe... This is because... The evidence suggests..."

Your Answer

Evaluation Checklist:

  • ✓ I've presented arguments on BOTH sides (not just one)
  • ✓ I've used specific evidence from at least two traditions
  • ✓ I've used contrasting language ("However," "On the other hand," "Nevertheless")
  • ✓ I've reached a clear, reasoned conclusion
  • ✓ I've used subject vocabulary accurately
  • ✓ My spelling, punctuation, and grammar are correct
