The Cop and the Anthem

A story by O. Henry about a homeless man who tries to go to prison for the winter — and always fails.

Today: tell the story of Soapy, a homeless man in New York who wants to spend the cold winter in a warm prison — and talk about plans that go wrong. Grammar: be going to, want to, and the past simple.
Warm up — before we read. Talk about these with your teacher:
  • Winter can be very cold. Where can people with no home go to stay warm?
  • Have you ever tried very hard to do something, but it did not work?

Meanings

  • B. a long seat, often outside in a park
  • C. to start living in a very different way
  • D. having no home to live in

  • F. when the police take someone away for a crime
  • G. a place where people are kept as a punishment
  • H. to do something that is not allowed by the law

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A plan for the winter

Soapy sat on a bench in a park in New York. It was autumn, and the nights were getting cold. A dead leaf fell into his lap. Soapy knew what this meant: winter was coming, and winter was hard for a homeless man.

Every year, Soapy made the same plan. He did not want to sleep outside in the cold. He wanted a warm room and hot food for three months. So he wanted to go to prison for the winter. In prison, a man had a bed, a roof and food. It was warm.

But to go to prison, you must break the law. So Soapy made a simple plan. He was going to do something small and bad. Then a police officer was going to arrest him. It was easy, he thought. He only needed to get caught.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What does Soapy want for the winter?

2. What is Soapy's plan? Where does he want to go?

1. He wants a warm place, food and a bed for three months. He does not want to sleep outside in the cold.

2. He wants to break the law so a police officer will arrest him. Then he can go to prison, where it is warm.

Talk about it:
  • Soapy wants to go to prison, not ask people for help. Why do you think he chooses prison?

Part 2 — Every plan goes wrong

First, Soapy went to a nice restaurant. He wanted to eat a big dinner and then say, "I have no money." Then the police were going to come. But at the door, a waiter looked at his old shoes and dirty clothes. The waiter pushed him out into the street. No dinner. No police.

Next, Soapy picked up a stone and broke a shop window. Then he stood next to it and waited. He was going to say, "I did it!" A police officer ran to the window. But the officer did not think Soapy did it. He ran after a different man. Soapy was angry.

Again and again, Soapy tried. He ate food in a small café and said he had no money — but the waiters only pushed him outside. He shouted on a busy street corner — but a police officer thought he was just a happy student and walked away. Every plan went wrong. The city did not care about Soapy at all. The warm prison was still far away.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Name two things Soapy tried to do to get arrested.

2. Did his plans work? What happened?

1. Any two: he tried to eat in a nice restaurant with no money; he broke a shop window; he said he had no money in a café; he shouted on a busy corner.

2. No, they did not work. The waiter pushed him out, the police officer ran after a different man, and another officer thought he was just a student. Every plan went wrong.

Talk about it:
  • It is funny that Soapy cannot get arrested. But it is also sad. What does it show about how the city sees a poor man?

Part 3 — The music, and the surprise

Soapy was cold and tired. He walked back to the park. Then he stopped. Music was coming from an old church. It was a beautiful song, and it made him think of his old life — his mother, his friends, and good days, long ago.

Something changed in Soapy's heart. He did not want to live on the street any more. He made a new plan. Tomorrow, he was going to find a job. He was going to work hard and be a good man again. For the first time in years, he felt happy and full of hope.

But as he stood there, a police officer touched his arm. "What are you doing here?" the officer asked. "Nothing," said Soapy. "Then come with me," said the officer. The next day, a judge sent Soapy to prison for three months. Soapy got the thing he wanted all winter — but only now, when he did not want it any more.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What does Soapy hear at the church? How does he feel after?

2. What is the surprise at the end of the story?

1. He hears beautiful music from the church. It makes him remember his old life. Now he wants to change and find a job.

2. Just when he wants to be a good man, a police officer arrests him. He goes to prison for three months — the thing he wanted before, but not now.

Talk about it:
  • Soapy gets what he wanted, but at the wrong time. Do you think this is bad luck, or something else?

Grammar: going to / want to + past simple

This story is about plans. Soapy wanted things and he was going to do things — but they went wrong. Here are two easy ways to talk about plans in the past.

was/were going to + verb = a plan for the future, seen from the past. Often the plan did not happen: "Soapy was going to eat and not pay." (but the waiter pushed him out)
wanted to + verb = you had this wish in the past: "He wanted to go to prison." "He wanted to stay warm."
Remember the past simple for what really happened: regular verbs add -ed (want → wanted, walk → walked), and many common verbs are irregular (go → went, break → broke, hear → heard).

Going deeper

A good short story stays in your mind after you finish it. Before you talk, think about this.

Why we still read this story. O. Henry loved a surprise at the end. Here, Soapy tries all winter to go to prison — and he fails every time. Then, at the exact moment he wants to change and be good, the police finally take him. It is funny, but it is also sad. The story asks big questions about poor people, luck, and how a city treats a man who has nothing.

Think and talk

Talk about these questions:
  • The surprise ending. Soapy is arrested at the worst moment — when he wants to be good. How does this ending make you feel? Is it funny, sad, or both?
  • Bad luck or fate? Soapy gets what he wanted, but too late. Do you think this is just bad luck, or something bigger?
  • The music. Why do you think the music from the church changes Soapy? Can music or a place change how you feel?
  • The city. The city does not care about Soapy — until he wants to be good. What does this say about how we treat poor people? Is it the same today?
  • What would you do? If you were the judge and you knew Soapy's whole story, would you send him to prison? Why, or why not?

Speak

Do all three tasks out loud. Use the timer to keep talking.

Useful language: use was going to for a plan ("he was going to break a window"), wanted to for a wish ("he wanted to go to prison"), and the past simple for what happened ("he sat", "he broke", "he heard"). To give an opinion: "I think…", "In my opinion…", "For me, the reason is…".

Task C · A plan of your own (3 minutes)

Tell a story about a plan that went wrong — yours or someone else's. Use was going to for the plan, wanted to for the wish, and the past simple for what happened.

Not sure what to talk about? Choose one:

  • A day when you had a plan, but it did not work.
  • A time when you wanted something, and then you got it at the wrong time.
  • A film or story where someone's plan went wrong.
  • A time when music, or a place, made you feel very different.
  • A plan you have now — something you are going to do soon.
  • A time you wanted to change something in your life. What did you do?
