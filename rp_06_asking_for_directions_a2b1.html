Asking for Directions

You are in a new city. It is beautiful — but you are lost. Your phone is dead. Time to stop a friendly person and find your way.

The Scene 🎬

You are in a new city. It is your first day here. The streets are small and pretty, and there is coffee everywhere. You love it. But there is one problem: you are lost.

You wanted to see the old cathedral. Your phone had a map, but then the map stopped — "no signal" — and then your phone died. Now you don't know the way. Every street looks the same.

A person is walking near you. They live here. They know every street. This is your chance: stop them, say hello, and ask for help. It is easy when you are polite.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like new cities? Is it fun to get lost, or not fun for you?
  2. When you are lost, do you ask a person, or do you look at your phone? Why?

Who's who

You
The Traveller 🧭

New in the city. Your phone is dead. You are friendly, and you are happy to ask people for help.

The other person
The Local 🏘️

They live here. They know every street. Kind and friendly — they are happy to help you find the way.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. It is OK to speak slowly. It is OK to say "sorry, again please?" People are happy to help you.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the polite words.

YouExcuse me, sorry to bother you. Can you help me, please? I am lost.
LocalOf course! No problem. Where do you want to go?
YouI want to go to the old cathedral. Where is it, please?
LocalAh, the cathedral! It's easy. Go straight on, then take the second street on the left, and it's next to the big park.
YouSorry, that was a little fast. Can you say that again, please?
LocalYes, of course! Go straight on. Then take the second street on the left. The cathedral is next to the park.
YouCan you speak more slowly, please? My English is not very good.
LocalNo problem. Go… straight… on. Then… the second left. The cathedral is by the park.
YouSo, straight on, then the second left, and it's by the park. Is that right?
LocalYes, that's right! The second left — not the first one.
YouThank you. Is it near? How do I get there — can I walk?
LocalIt's very near. Five minutes. Yes, you can walk!
YouThank you so much! You helped me a lot.
LocalYou're welcome! Have a nice day.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use when you are lost. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

It's OK to ask. Everyone gets lost. A smile, and the words excuse me and please, do a lot. You can speak slowly. You can ask again. People are happy to help a friendly person.

🚶 Stopping someone

Phrase 1

You want to stop a person on the street. You need help. Complete it: "Excuse me, can you ___ me, please?"

"Excuse me, can you help me, please?"
"Excuse me" is the polite way to start. "Can you help me?" is short and easy, and it works everywhere — in the street, in a shop, at the station.
Phrase 2

You want to stop a person nicely. Which one is polite?

A. "Hey! Where is the cathedral?"
B. "Excuse me, sorry to bother you."
C. "Tell me the way. Now."
B — "Excuse me, sorry to bother you."
"Sorry to bother you" is a soft, kind way to start. A and C sound like an order, not a question — and people help you more when you are polite.

🗺️ Asking the way

Phrase 3

You don't know where you are. Tell the person. Complete it: "I am ___. I can't find the cathedral."

"I am lost."
"I am lost" tells the person everything in two words: you don't know the way, and you need help. It is not a bad thing to say — everyone is lost sometimes!
Phrase 4

You know the place, but you want to ask the way to it. Put the words in order:

gethow do Ithere?
"How do I get there?"
"How do I get there?" asks for the way to a place. You can also say the place: "How do I get to the station?", "How do I get to the park?"

🔁 When it is too fast

Phrase 5

The directions were too fast. You did not understand. Which one is polite?

A. "What? Again."
B. "Sorry, can you say that again, please?"
B — "Sorry, can you say that again, please?"
A sounds a little angry. B is soft and friendly. "Say that again, please?" asks the person to repeat, so you can understand. It is always OK to ask.
Phrase 6

It is still too fast. You want the person to slow down. Complete it: "Can you speak more ___, please?"

"Can you speak more slowly, please?"
"More slowly" asks for a slower voice. This is a great phrase for any time — a shop, a phone call, a class. Most people are happy to slow down.

💛 Checking and saying thank you

Phrase 7

You want to check. You say the way back to the person, and you ask them. Complete it: "So, left and then right. ___ that right?"

"Is that right?"
Say the way back in your own words, then ask "Is that right?" The person can say yes, or fix a small mistake — before you walk the wrong way!
Phrase 8

The person helped you a lot. You want to say a big thank you. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you so much! You helped me a lot."
Add "so much" to make your thank you bigger and warmer. "You helped me a lot" tells the person you are happy. A smile helps too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in any city, any country. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"How do I get to ___?" — Where do you want to go in a new city? (the station, the beach, a café…)

"Can you speak more slowly, please?" — When is this a good phrase for you? Write when you would use it.

"Thank you so much! You helped me ___." — Finish your own warm thank you.

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole thing out loud — stop the person → say you are lost → ask the way → ask again slowly → check it → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now you are lost in the city. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the traveller 🧭

The local speaks — you answer. Be polite: use excuse me, please, and thank you.

LocalYou look a little lost! Can I help you?
LocalThe old cathedral? Easy — go straight on, then the second left, and it's by the park.
LocalOf course! Straight on… then the second left… the cathedral is next to the park.
LocalYes, that's right! It's only five minutes from here.
LocalYes, you can walk. It's very near. Have a nice day!

Round 2 — now YOU are the local 🏘️

Change places. It is your city now. Be kind and friendly. Give the way slowly, one step at a time. Say "Of course", "No problem", "It's easy".

TravellerExcuse me, sorry to bother you. I am lost. Where is the train station, please?
TravellerSorry, can you say that again, please? It was a little fast.
TravellerSo, straight on, then turn left. Is that right?
TravellerThank you so much! You helped me a lot.
The Twist

🌙 It is starting to rain

One more time — no help this round. You are lost, and now it is raining! You want to get to the cathedral fast, but it is far — twenty minutes on foot. What do you say? Ask the person: is there a bus? Is there a taxi? Can you wait somewhere dry? Stay polite and friendly. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I stopped the person politely, with "excuse me".
  • I said "I am lost" and where I wanted to go.
  • I asked them to say it again, or to speak more slowly.
  • I said the way back and asked "Is that right?"
  • I said "thank you" warmly at the end.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

About 750 years ago, a young man from Venice named Marco Polo left home. He went east — a very, very long way. He had no phone, no map, no GPS. He had only the stars in the sky and the help of kind strangers.

He walked over deserts and big mountains. He saw cities that no one in his country knew. Every day, he had to stop people, ask the way, and understand the answer — just like you did today.

Your cathedral is five minutes away. His journey was many years and thousands of miles. Same idea — ask, listen, understand — but a much bigger trip!

Two questions to talk about

  1. Today we have a phone that always knows where we are. Is this good, or do we lose something when we never ask people the way?
  2. Marco Polo had to trust strangers, again and again. Is it easy for you to trust a stranger's help? When is it not a good idea?
