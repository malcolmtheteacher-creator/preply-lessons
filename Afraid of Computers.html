Speaking Lesson: True Crime & The Art of Persuasion

Duration: 50 minutes | Level: B2-C1 | Focus: Discussion & Debate

Warm-up (5 minutes)

Quick Questions:

  • Have you ever watched or listened to true crime content? What was it about?
  • When was the last time someone persuaded you to change your mind? What convinced you?

Key Vocabulary (5 minutes)

Crime & Justice:

  • Rehabilitation - helping offenders reintegrate
  • Recidivism - reoffending after punishment
  • Deterrent - something that discourages
  • Mitigating circumstances - factors that reduce blame
  • Premeditated - planned in advance

Persuasion:

  • Manipulative - controlling deceptively
  • Rhetoric - persuasive speaking or writing
  • Coercion - forcing through threats
  • To sway opinion - to change views
  • Appeal to emotion - using feelings to persuade

Part 1: True Crime Phenomenon (15 minutes)

Discussion Questions:

  1. Why do you think true crime documentaries and podcasts are so popular? What psychological need do they fulfill?
  2. Is there a difference between being interested in true crime and being morbidly fascinated by violence? Where is the line?
  3. Should the families of victims have a say in whether their cases become entertainment? Why or why not?
  4. Can true crime content actually help solve cases or prevent crimes, or does it just sensationalize tragedy?

Debate Topic:

"Justice systems should focus on rehabilitation rather than punishment."

Consider: Does punishment deter crime? Can all offenders be rehabilitated? What about victims need for justice?

Part 2: The Art of Persuasion (15 minutes)

Discussion Questions:

  1. What is the difference between persuasion and manipulation? Can you give examples of each?
  2. Think about advertising, politics, or social media. What persuasion techniques do you notice most often?
  3. Are you more easily persuaded by logical arguments or emotional appeals? Why?
  4. Should there be limits on persuasive techniques in advertising or political campaigns? What would those limits be?

Role-Play Activity:

You are a lawyer defending someone accused of a crime. Choose ONE scenario:

  • Scenario A: A teenager who stole food because their family was struggling financially
  • Scenario B: A person who hacked a company website to expose unethical practices
  • Scenario C: Someone who drove recklessly and caused an accident while rushing to the hospital

Task: Persuade the court (your teacher) that your client deserves leniency. Use persuasive techniques!

Connecting the Topics (7 minutes)

Think Deeper:

  • How do true crime shows persuade us to see suspects as guilty or innocent?
  • What role does media persuasion play in public opinion about criminal cases?
  • Have you ever changed your opinion about a criminal case after hearing more information? What persuaded you?
  • Should juries be protected from media coverage of trials? Why or why not?

Wrap-up & Reflection (3 minutes)

  • What was the most interesting point discussed today?
  • Did any of the discussions change your perspective? How?
  • Which new vocabulary words will you try to use this week?

Useful Phrases for Discussion

Expressing Opinion:

  • In my view...
  • I strongly believe that...
  • From my perspective...
  • I would argue that...

Disagreeing Politely:

  • I see your point, but...
  • That is an interesting take, however...
  • I am not entirely convinced that...
  • I would have to disagree because...

Adding to Discussion:

  • Building on that idea...
  • To add to what you said...
  • That raises another question...
  • On a related note...

Asking for Clarification:

  • Could you elaborate on that?
  • What exactly do you mean by that?
  • I am curious about your view on this...
  • How would you respond to that?

Remember: There are no right or wrong answers - the goal is to express your ideas clearly and engage thoughtfully with different perspectives!
