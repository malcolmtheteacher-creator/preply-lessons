Clarification Language

In academic and professional contexts, we need to ask for and give clarification. Click each category:

🔍 Signalling Lack of Understanding
"I'm not quite sure I follow..."
"I didn't quite catch that..."
"Could you run that by me again?"
"I'm a bit confused about..."
❓ Asking for Clarification
"Could you elaborate on that?"
"What exactly do you mean by...?"
"Could you give me an example?"
"Are you saying that...?"
💡 Explaining / Clarifying
"What I mean is..."
"To put it another way..."
"In other words..."
"Let me explain that differently..."
✅ Checking Understanding
"Does that make sense?"
"Is that clearer now?"
"Do you see what I mean?"
"Have I answered your question?"

Academic Collocations

These collocations are common in health research discussions. Click to see examples:

controversial topic
"Mind-body medicine has always been a controversial topic in mainstream science."
argue strongly
"Some researchers argue strongly that stress directly impacts immune function."
solid science
"There is now solid science supporting the mind-body connection."
incontrovertible evidence
"We need incontrovertible evidence before changing medical practice."
clearly shown
"Studies have clearly shown a link between meditation and reduced cortisol."
measured objectively
"The effects of mindfulness can now be measured objectively using brain scans."
detrimental effects
"Chronic stress has detrimental effects on physical health."
increasing openness
"There's an increasing openness in medicine to complementary approaches."
