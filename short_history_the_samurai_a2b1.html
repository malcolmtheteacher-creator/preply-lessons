The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The warriors of Japan

The samurai were the famous warriors of old Japan. They lived hundreds of years ago, from about the 12th century to the 19th century.

Every samurai served a powerful lord, called a daimyo. The lords served the most powerful man of all: a military ruler called the shogun. There was also an emperor, but for a long time he had no real power. The shogun was the true ruler of Japan.

The samurai lived by a special code of rules. This code was called bushido, which means "the way of the warrior". The rules said that a samurai must be brave, loyal and full of honour. He had to serve his lord — even if it meant dying for him. The famous weapon of the samurai was the katana, a long, curved sword.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who had the real power in old Japan — the emperor or the shogun?

2. What was bushido? Name two of its rules.

1. The shogun (the military ruler) had the real power. The emperor had no real power for a long time.

2. Bushido was the samurai's code of rules ("the way of the warrior"). For example: be brave, be loyal, have honour, serve your lord.

Talk about it:
  • The samurai had to be loyal to their lord, even to die for him. Is loyalty always a good thing? Why or why not?

Part 2 — War and peace

For a long time, Japan had many wars. The most famous time of war was the 1500s, when many lords fought each other to be the strongest. This was a dangerous and exciting time for the samurai.

Then, in the 1600s, one family — the Tokugawa — won and became the new shoguns. They brought peace to Japan for more than 200 years. This was strange for the samurai: they were warriors, but now there were no wars! Many of them became teachers, officials and writers instead of fighters.

The code of honour was very strong. If a samurai lost his lord, he became a ronin — a warrior with no master. A ronin had no place and no work, so people often felt sorry for him, but also feared him. The lonely ronin became one of the most famous pictures in Japanese stories.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened to the samurai when peace came in the 1600s?

2. What was a ronin?

1. There were no more wars, so many samurai became teachers, officials and writers instead of fighters.

2. A ronin was a samurai with no master (because his lord had died or lost power). He had no place and no work.

Talk about it:
  • The samurai were warriors, but in peacetime they did office jobs. Is it hard to change your life when the world changes? Why?

Part 3 — The end of the samurai

In the 1800s, ships from other countries came to Japan. Japan saw that the world had changed and become more modern. The country wanted to change too.

In 1868, there was a big change called the Meiji Restoration. The shogun lost his power, and the young emperor became important again. The new government wanted a modern army with normal soldiers. So it decided to end the samurai class. The samurai lost their special money and their special rights. They could not even carry their swords any more.

But the samurai did not really disappear. Many of them became soldiers, teachers and businessmen in the new, modern Japan. And their ideas — honour, loyalty and discipline — are still famous today, all around the world, in films and books.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What big change happened in 1868?

2. Did the samurai completely disappear? What happened to their ideas?

1. The Meiji Restoration: the shogun lost power, the emperor became important again, and the government ended the samurai class.

2. No. Many samurai became soldiers, teachers and businessmen. Their ideas — honour, loyalty and discipline — are still famous today in films and books.

Talk about it:
  • The samurai are gone, but people still love their story. Why do you think old warriors like these are so popular today?

Grammar: relative clauses (who / which / that)

We use relative clauses to give more information about a person or a thing in one sentence.

who = for people: "a warrior who served a lord."
which = for things: "a sword which was very sharp."
that = for people or things: "the lord that he served," "the code that they followed."
This lets us join two short sentences into one. The samurai was a warrior. He served a lord. → The samurai was a warrior who served a lord.

1. Choose who, which or that

1. A samurai was a warrior served a lord.

2. The katana was a sword was long and curved.

3. The shogun was the ruler had the real power.

4. Bushido was the code the samurai followed.

1. who (or that) — a person

2. which (or that) — a thing

3. who (or that) — a person

4. which / that — a thing

2. Join the two sentences

Make one sentence using who or which. Then read it out loud.

1. A ronin was a samurai. He had no master. →

2. The katana was a weapon. It was very famous. →

3. The daimyo were lords. They controlled the land. →

1. A ronin was a samurai who had no master.

2. The katana was a weapon which (or that) was very famous.

3. The daimyo were lords who controlled the land.
