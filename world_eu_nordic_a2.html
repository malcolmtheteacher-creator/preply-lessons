Cold Nordics, Happy Nordics?

Nordic people are quiet and reserved. But Finland is often the happiest country. How?

Talk first:
  • Are you quiet or talkative?

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. Finns are often quiet at bus stops.
quiet (adj) — not talking much. Example: “Finns are often quiet at bus stops.”
2. She is happy with her life.
happy (adj) — feeling good and glad. Example: “She is happy with her life.”
3. There is a lot of trust in the Nordic countries.
trust (n) — a belief that people are honest. Example: “There is a lot of trust in the Nordic countries.”
4. He is shy with strangers.
shy (adj) — not comfortable with other people. Example: “He is shy with strangers.”
5. She is modest about her success.
modest (adj) — not saying you are better than others. Example: “She is modest about her success.”
6. Equality is important in Sweden.
equality (n) — the same rights for everyone. Example: “Equality is important in Sweden.”
Talk it through:
  • Which word is new for you? Say a sentence with it.
The stereotype: “Nordic people are cold and unfriendly. And they are always happy.”

The story

Part 1 — Quiet people

People joke that Finns stand far apart at bus stops and do not like small talk. A Finnish cartoonist made a popular series about a shy man. Many Finns laughed because it felt true. But quiet is not the same as cold. Many Finns say, when they talk, they mean it.

Quick check:

1. Where do Finns stand at bus stops, in the joke?

2. Is quiet the same as cold?

1. Far apart.

2. No.

Discuss:
  • Are you comfortable with silence?

Part 2 — The happiest country

Since 2018, Finland has been number one in the World Happiness Report every year, up to at least 2025. Denmark, Iceland and Sweden are often near the top too. The report asks people to rate their lives from 0 to 10. It measures life satisfaction, not smiles.

Quick check:

1. Which country is number one?

2. What does the report measure?

1. Finland.

2. Life satisfaction, not smiles.

Discuss:
  • What does a happy life mean to you?

Part 3 — Trust and modesty

Nordic countries have a lot of trust. In a 2013 test, people “lost” wallets in many cities. In Helsinki, 11 of 12 wallets came back. Modesty is also important. The “Law of Jante” says, “Do not think you are better than us.” Some people say it brings equality. Others say it stops ambition.

Quick check:

1. How many wallets came back in Helsinki?

2. What does the Law of Jante say?

1. 11 of 12.

2. Do not think you are better than us.

Discuss:
  • Is modesty good or bad?

Grammar — but and although

but goes in the middle: “Finns are quiet, but they are very honest.”
although goes at the start: “Although Finns are quiet, they are very honest.”
Use a comma after the first part.

1. Choose the word

1. Finns are quiet, they are very honest. (but / although)

2. Finns are quiet, they trust each other. (But / Although)

3. Nordic people are modest, they are happy. (but / although)

1. but

2. Although

3. but

2. Make your own

1. Although I am shy, ______.

2. I am quiet, but ______.

3. Although it is cold in Finland, ______.

1. ...I like parties. / ...I talk to my friends.

2. ...I am friendly. / ...I have many ideas.

3. ...people are happy. / ...they like the sauna.
