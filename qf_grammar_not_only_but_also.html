Not only...but also

Mastering correlative conjunctions with subject-verb inversion

What's the problem?

When "not only" starts a sentence, it's a fronted negative. This triggers subject-verb inversion—the auxiliary comes before the subject.

❌ Incorrect

"Not only she sings but also she dances."

Why it's wrong: The subject she comes directly after "not only," but we need the auxiliary verb first. This sounds unnatural in English.
✓ Correct

"Not only does she sing, but she also dances."

Why it works: The auxiliary does inverts to the front, creating the correct pattern: "Not only does she..." This is natural, emphatic English.
Remember: If "not only" appears in the middle of a sentence (not at the start), no inversion is needed. Example: "She not only sings but also dances."

Another example:

❌ Incorrect

"Not only he was late but also he forgot the keys."

Why it's wrong: Using the main verb "was" directly. We need the auxiliary was to invert ahead of the subject.
✓ Correct

"Not only was he late, but he also forgot the keys."

Why it works: The auxiliary was inverts to the front. The pattern becomes: "Not only was he..."

The pattern

When "not only" starts the sentence:
NOT ONLY AUX SUBJECT BASE VERB → but (also) subject verb
Example transformation:
Original (wrong): "Not only she sings but also she dances."
Step 1 (front): "Not only" moves to the front
Step 2 (invert): Add the auxiliary and invert: does + she
Result (correct): "Not only does she sing, but she also dances."
Choosing the auxiliary: Match the tense of the original sentence.
  • Present simple → does / do
  • Past simple → did
  • Present continuous → is / are
  • Past continuous → was / were
  • Perfect → has / have / had
