The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. America had thirteen colonies.
a colony (n) — a land ruled by another country.
2. "No taxation without representation."
taxation (n) — the system of taxes.
3. The colonies chose to declare independence.
to declare (v) — to state officially.
4. They won independence.
independence (n) — freedom from control.
5. Some think he had porphyria.
porphyria (n) — a blood disorder.
6. He suffered from mental illness.
mental illness (n) — an illness affecting the mind.
7. His son ruled during the Regency.
a regency (n) — rule by a regent.
8. He was nicknamed Farmer George.
Farmer George (n) — a nickname for his love of agriculture.
9. Britain fought Napoleon.
Napoleon (n) — the French emperor.
10. Napoleon was defeated at Waterloo.
Waterloo (n) — a battle in 1815.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The young king

George III became king in 1760 at twenty-two, the first Hanoverian born in Britain. He loved farming and science, earning the nickname "Farmer George".

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was new about George III?

2. Why "Farmer George"?

1. He was the first Hanoverian born in Britain.

2. He loved agriculture.

Discuss:
  • Why might a British-born king be more popular?

Part 2 — America

Britain taxed the American colonies, which protested that they had no representation in Parliament. In 1776 the colonies declared independence. Britain was defeated, and in 1783 recognised the United States.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did colonists protest?

2. When did Britain recognise the USA?

1. Taxation without representation.

2. 1783.

Discuss:
  • Could Britain have kept America?

Part 3 — Illness and Napoleon

George suffered episodes of serious mental illness from 1788. The cause is debated — porphyria was once popular, but many historians now suggest bipolar disorder. From 1811 his son ruled as Prince Regent. Britain fought Napoleon, winning at Trafalgar (1805) and Waterloo (1815). George died in 1820.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who ruled from 1811?

2. Name a victory against Napoleon.

1. His son, the Prince Regent.

2. Trafalgar or Waterloo.

Discuss:
  • How should historians describe a ruler's illness?

Grammar — Third Conditional — What If?

History invites the great question: what if? The third conditional imagines a different past and its different result.

How it works:
• Form: If + past perfect, would have + past participle.
• "If the wind had changed a day earlier, William would have landed first."
• Use might have / could have for less certainty.

1. Complete the conditional

Complete each sentence about George III. Use the word in brackets.

1. If Britain (give) Americans representation, they (might / stay).

2. If George (not / become) ill, there (be) no Regency.

3. If Napoleon (win) at Waterloo, Europe (be) different.

1. had given … might have stayed

2. had not become … would have been

3. had won … would have been

2. Mixed practice — what if?

1. If the colonists (not / protest), the tax (stay).

2. If doctors (understand) his illness, they (treat) him better.

3. If George (die) young, his son (rule) earlier.

1. had not protested … would have stayed

2. had understood … would have treated

3. had died … would have ruled

Going deeper

George III is remembered as "the king who lost America", but historians now see him as conscientious and unfairly caricatured. His illness and its cruel treatment have helped change attitudes to mental health.

Worth noticing: the musical Hamilton made him famous again with the song "You'll Be Back".
Learning from the past — for today: Listen before it's too late. The colonists asked for representation. Ignoring complaints can turn into a crisis.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Was George a tyrant?
  • How has attitude to mental illness changed?
  • When should complaints be listened to?
