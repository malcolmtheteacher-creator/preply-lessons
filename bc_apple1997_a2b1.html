The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then answer the questions before you read the next part.

Part 1 — Too many products

Steve Jobs started Apple with a friend in 1976.

But in 1985, after a big fight with the board, Jobs left his own company.

In the 1990s, Apple had big problems. Most people bought computers with Microsoft Windows, not Apple computers.

Apple tried to answer by making more and more products. There were many computers with confusing numbers, and also printers, cameras and a small handheld computer.

Customers did not understand the difference between all the computers. Shop workers did not understand either.

Apple's software was also old. It tried to make new software, but the project failed.

In late 1996 Apple bought NeXT, Jobs's new company, for about 400 million dollars. The deal gave Apple new software. And it brought Jobs back.

In 1997 Apple had a loss of about one billion dollars. Jobs later said the company was about ninety days from running out of money.

True or false? Click your answer.

1. Steve Jobs left Apple in 1985.

2. In the 1990s, Apple made only a few simple products.

3. Apple bought NeXT because it needed new software.

Talk about it
  • Why is it a problem when a company makes too many products?
  • Apple bought a company and got its old boss back. Is it a good idea to bring back an old leader? Why?

Part 2 — Help from a rival, and four boxes

In August 1997, Jobs spoke at a big Apple event. He had surprising news.

Microsoft, Apple's biggest rival, was going to invest 150 million dollars in Apple. It also promised to make its Office software for Apple computers for five years.

When Bill Gates, Microsoft's boss, appeared on the big screen, some people in the audience booed.

But the news was important. It told customers that Apple was not going to die.

Then Jobs cut most of the products. He drew a simple picture with four boxes.

One computer for home and one for professionals. One desktop and one laptop for each. That was all.

In 1998 Apple showed the iMac. It was a colourful computer that was easy to connect to the internet.

People loved the design. It sold very well, and in 1998 Apple made a profit again.

True or false? Click your answer.

1. Microsoft gave money to Apple in 1997.

2. Everybody in the audience was happy to see Bill Gates.

3. After the cuts, Apple planned to make four main types of computer.

Talk about it
  • Would you accept help from your biggest rival? What are the dangers?
  • Jobs made the plan very simple: four boxes. Why is a simple plan powerful?

Part 3 — From four boxes to everything

After the iMac, Apple made the iPod in 2001, the iPhone in 2007 and the iPad in 2010.

Steve Jobs died in 2011. Tim Cook became the new boss.

Since then, Apple has become one of the most valuable companies in the world. It has been worth more than three trillion dollars.

Many people tell this story with one simple lesson: focus. Do a few things very well.

But the story is not so simple. Microsoft's help was very important. And Jobs was the founder, so people accepted big cuts from him.

Today Apple has many products again: phones, computers, watches, headphones, and a lot of services like music, TV and payments.

In 2024 Apple reportedly stopped a secret car project after about ten years.

So here is the question. Can a very big company still focus? Or was focus only the answer for a company in trouble?

True or false? Click your answer.

1. Tim Cook became Apple's boss after Steve Jobs died.

2. Today Apple makes only four products.

3. The story says Microsoft's help was not important at all.

Talk about it
  • Is Apple a focused company today? Why or why not?
  • Apple stopped its car project after about ten years. Was that a failure or a good decision?
  • Tell the story again in six sentences. Use: left · lost · came back · cut · has become · has made.

Present perfect or past simple? — Apple lost or Apple has become

This story has two times: the finished past (1985, 1997, 2011) and a time that continues until now.

Past simple — finished time, often with a date: I moved to this city in 2019.

Present perfect (have / has + past participle) — from the past until now, or life experience with no date: I have lived here since 2019. · Have you ever been to Japan?

Signal words: yesterday, last year, in 2010, ago → past simple. since, for, ever, never, yet, already → often present perfect.

Careful: never use the present perfect with a finished time. I saw him yesterday. (NOT I have seen him yesterday)
