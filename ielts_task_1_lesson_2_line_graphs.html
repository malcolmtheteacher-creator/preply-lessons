The Real Challenge with Line Graphs

The Problem: Most students can READ a line graph perfectly. The challenge is WRITING about it strategically. What do you include? What do you leave out? How do you organize 40+ data points into 150 words?

Line graphs typically contain far more information than you can describe. Look at this graph - 4 lines over 5 time periods = 20 data points. You cannot mention all of them.

Internet Users by Region (2000-2020) - millions

The Band 6 vs Band 8 Difference

Two students look at the same graph above. Compare how they write about it:

BAND 6 APPROACH (Lists everything)

"In 2000, Asia had 100 million users. In 2005, Asia had 400 million users. In 2010, Asia had 800 million. Europe started at 150 million in 2000. In 2005, Europe had 300 million..."

Problem: Just listing numbers. No analysis. No patterns. No comparisons.

BAND 8 APPROACH (Identifies patterns)

"Asia experienced the most dramatic growth, with users increasing 25-fold from 100 million to 2.5 billion. In contrast, more mature markets like North America showed modest growth, approximately tripling over the same period."

Strength: Compares RATES of change. Groups by pattern. Uses varied language.

Key Insight: Band 8 writers don't describe MORE - they describe SMARTER. They identify what's significant and ignore what isn't. Look at the graph again - the Band 8 writer noticed that Asia grew 25x while North America only tripled. That COMPARISON is the insight.

End point convergence

Understanding Significance

SIGNIFICANT (for overview):

  • Overall trends - "All groups increased" (affects everything)
  • Changing relationships - "Gap narrowed" (shows pattern evolution)
  • Rates of change - "65+ showed steepest increase" (comparative analysis)
  • End state - "All reached 75%+" (convergence = significant pattern)

NOT SIGNIFICANT (for overview):

  • Random mid-point figures (45% in 2012) - belongs in body paragraphs IF at all
  • Minor fluctuations (brief overtaking) - not the main story
  • Starting points alone - need context of change

Decision 2: How to Describe Trends Without Data

Critical Rule: Your OVERVIEW must describe main patterns but contain NO specific numbers. This is where most Band 6 students fail - they put data in the overview.

The Language of Trends (No Numbers)

Type of Observation Language to Use
Everything went up All categories showed upward trends / consistent growth
One thing changed most X experienced the most dramatic change / steepest increase
Lines got closer together The gap narrowed considerably / differences diminished
Lines spread further apart The disparity widened / divergence increased
One overtook another X surpassed Y / overtook Y to become the highest
Something stayed the same X remained relatively stable / showed little variation
Everything reached similar levels All categories converged / reached comparable levels

Decision 3: How to Organize Body Paragraphs

With multiple lines over multiple years, you must decide HOW to group information. There's no single "right" way - but some ways are better than others.

Energy Consumption by Source in Country Y (1990-2020)

Activity: Choose the Best Organization Strategy

Strategy A: By Time Period

Body 1: 1990-2005 (all sources)
Body 2: 2005-2020 (all sources)

Strategy B: By Source Type

Body 1: Fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas)
Body 2: Clean energy (nuclear, renewables)

Strategy C: By Trend Direction

Body 1: Declining sources (coal)
Body 2: Growing sources (gas, renewables)

Strategy D: One Source Per Paragraph

Body 1: Coal only
Body 2: Oil only
Body 3: Gas only... (etc)

Organization Analysis

Strategy B or C are both excellent choices.

Strategy B (By Source Type): Works well because it allows natural comparisons - "While fossil fuels generally declined, clean energy sources grew." Creates logical groupings.

Strategy C (By Trend): Also strong because it focuses on the STORY - what went down vs what went up. Good for graphs with clear opposing trends.

Strategy A (By Time): Acceptable but often leads to repetition. You end up describing each source twice.

Strategy D (One Per Para): Poor choice. With 5 sources, you'd need 5 body paragraphs. Too fragmented, prevents comparisons.

Key Principle: Group things that can be compared. Your organization should make comparisons EASIER, not harder.

Decision 4: Describing Rates of Change

Band 8 Skill: Don't just say what changed - describe HOW FAST or HOW MUCH it changed compared to other things. This shows analytical thinking.

Comparing Amounts vs Rates

WEAK: Just stating amounts

"Asia increased to 2.5 billion. Europe increased to 700 million."

STRONG: Comparing rates

"While Asia's users increased 25-fold, Europe saw more modest growth of approximately five times the original figure."

Useful Phrases for Rate Comparisons

Situation Phrases
Multiplied growth doubled / tripled / increased fivefold / grew tenfold / increased by a factor of X
Faster change grew at a faster rate / outpaced / showed steeper growth / accelerated more rapidly
Slower change grew more gradually / showed more modest growth / increased at a slower pace
Different patterns in contrast to / unlike / whereas / while
Similar patterns similarly / likewise / followed a comparable trajectory / mirrored

Activity: Upgrade These Sentences

Rewrite each sentence to include rate comparisons. Type in the box.

Original: "Coal went from 60% to 20%. Renewables went from 2% to 30%."

Model: "Coal's share declined by two-thirds, falling from 60% to 20%, while renewables showed even more dramatic change, increasing fifteen-fold from just 2% to 30%."

Model: "Although younger people maintained higher ownership throughout, the elderly demographic showed far steeper growth, with their rate increasing nearly tenfold compared to the younger group's approximate tripling."

Model Body Paragraphs:

BODY 1
London began the period with the highest prices at approximately 150,000 pounds, and this dominance continued throughout. By 2020, London house prices had reached around 480,000 pounds, representing more than a threefold increase. Edinburgh followed a similar pattern, starting at 100,000 pounds and climbing steadily to approximately 280,000 pounds, though the rate of growth was somewhat more moderate than London's.

BODY 2
In contrast, Birmingham and Manchester had considerably lower starting points of around 70,000 and 60,000 pounds respectively. While both cities saw their prices roughly triple over the period, Manchester's growth was particularly notable in proportional terms, with prices reaching approximately 200,000 pounds by 2020. Birmingham showed steadier growth, ending the period at around 210,000 pounds.

Key Takeaways

The 4 Decisions That Matter:
  1. What's significant? - Focus on patterns and relationships, not random data points
  2. Overview language - Describe trends WITHOUT numbers. Save data for body paragraphs
  3. How to organize - Group by type, trend, or logical category. Make comparisons easy
  4. Rate descriptions - Don't just state figures. Compare HOW FAST things changed
Remember:

The examiner knows you can read the graph. They want to see you can ANALYZE it. Every sentence should show thinking, not just copying numbers.

Common Band 6 Traps:
  • Putting numbers in the overview
  • Describing each line separately without comparison
  • Listing every data point you can find
  • Using "increased" and "decreased" repeatedly without variety
  • Not identifying the main story the graph tells
