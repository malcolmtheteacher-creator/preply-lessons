Words That Score

Small, simple words like good, very, nice and things are correct — but they don't show your best vocabulary. Swapping them for a slightly stronger word can lift your writing and speaking score without changing your meaning at all.
Easy upgrades:
  • good → useful / impressive
  • very + adjective → extremely + adjective
  • nice → enjoyable
  • things → aspects

Linking words worth knowing

Use these to join your ideas instead of starting every sentence with And or But:

  • Adding an idea: additionally, furthermore, in addition
  • Contrasting an idea: however, on the other hand
  • Giving a result: therefore, as a result
  • Giving an example: for example, for instance

Useful phrases to keep ready

  • “It is worth noting that…”
  • “One of the most useful aspects is…”
  • “This has a significant effect on…”
  • “In my experience,…”

Describe the Photo (in writing)

Look at the photo for a few seconds, then write three or four sentences describing it. Use the frame: general → details → a feeling or guess.
01:00
One good version: “This is a graduation ceremony, held outdoors in a large stadium on a sunny day. On the right there is a big stage with a banner that reads ‘Class of 2024’. In the middle, hundreds of graduates in black caps and gowns are throwing their caps into the air to celebrate. In the foreground, families and friends are standing, clapping and filming on their phones, and some look very proud and emotional. It feels joyful and full of achievement.”

Check before you finish

  • Verbs are in the right tense.
  • Singular/plural agree (a store sells, stores sell).
  • Articles are there where needed (a, an, the).
  • I used at least two linking words.
  • Opinion, two reasons + examples, a conclusion — and I filled the space.

Keep Improving

A short habit, done often, beats a long session done rarely. Use this checklist every time you finish a piece of writing.

Writing self-check

  • I upgraded at least one weak word (good, very, nice, things).
  • I used at least one linking word.
  • My tenses are consistent and correct.
  • My articles (a, an, the) are in the right places.
  • I filled the space and answered the actual question.

I can…

  • I can swap a weak word for a stronger one without changing the meaning.
  • I can find the answer to a gap-fill question inside a short passage.
  • I can describe a photo in writing using general → details → a feeling.
  • I can plan and write a four-part opinion answer in five minutes.
  • I can type a dictated sentence accurately, including contractions.
Strong vocabulary doesn't mean long words — it means the right word, used correctly. Keep upgrading one word at a time.
