Telling a Funny Story

B2 English Lesson | Storytelling & Emotions

What Makes a Story Funny?

Think about this:

What kinds of situations make people laugh? What about humour is different across cultures?

Humour can be silly (making people giggle), dry (understated, clever), rude (edgy), or physical. Different cultures find different things funny. In some cultures, self-mocking humour is popular; in others, making fun of situations is preferred. Understanding these differences helps when telling stories to people from different backgrounds.

Why Tell a Funny Story?

In everyday conversation, funny stories serve many purposes: they break the ice, build connection, show your personality, and keep people entertained.

What Makes a Good Story?

Surprise Element:
Something unexpected happens that the listener isn't prepared for. Example: "I was sure I locked my door, but when I got home, my cat had somehow opened it."
Emotional Connection:
You describe how you felt at different moments — scared, embarrassed, happy, confused. This makes the story relatable. Example: "I felt my face go completely red..."
Interesting Characters:
People or situations that are unusual or distinctive. Example: "My grandmother — who's in her 80s — decided to learn skateboarding."
A Satisfying Ending:
The story finishes with a clear resolution. The ending should either be funny, touching, or thought-provoking. Example: "And that's why I'll never order sushi online again."

Expressions for Telling a Story

Sequencing Phrases

Use these expressions to guide your listener through the story step by step:

To start:
So, this happened when... / It was about a month ago when... / There was this time...
To continue in order:
First of all, ... / Then, ... / After that, ... / Next, ... / At first, ... / Eventually, ...
To emphasize surprise or something unexpected:
Suddenly, ... / All of a sudden, ... / Unexpectedly, ... / Out of nowhere, ...
To finish the story (formal):
To make a long story short, ... / In the end, ... / To sum up, ... / Eventually, what happened was...
To finish the story (informal):
Anyway, ... / So basically, ... / The point is, ...

Expressing Emotions (Idiomatic Expressions)

Make your story more vivid by describing emotions and reactions:

Positive emotions:
I burst out laughing / I was thrilled / I felt relieved / I was over the moon / I couldn't believe my luck
Negative emotions:
I was mortified (very embarrassed) / I felt my heart sink / My face went bright red / I was mortified / I wanted the ground to swallow me up
Surprised or confused:
I couldn't believe my eyes / I was taken aback / I didn't know what to say / I was completely baffled

Practice: Complete the Story

"I was on my way to an important meeting. Suddenly, my car broke down. I felt my heart sink. To make a long story short, I never made it to that meeting."
You could also say: "Then, my car broke down" / "I was mortified" / "Anyway, I never made it" / "I couldn't believe what was happening" / "Eventually, I gave up and went home."

How to React When Someone Tells a Story

When someone tells you a story, show that you're engaged and interested by using the right responses. Click on each one to see when to use it:

"Oh no!"
When to use: Something bad, sad, or unfortunate happened. The person is sharing a difficult moment or loss.
"My bike was stolen!" / "I completely failed my driving test..."
"You're joking!"
When to use: You find the story surprising, unbelievable, or funny. You're expressing shock or amusement.
"I found €500 on the street!" / "My teacher fell down the stairs in front of the whole class!"
"What did you do then?"
When to use: You want to keep the story going and find out what happens next. You're engaged and curious.
(After they describe a problem or difficult moment)
"That's incredible!"
When to use: Something amazing, surprising, or impressive happened to them. You're expressing admiration or astonishment.
"I met my favourite actor!" / "I got the job I've always wanted!"
"How embarrassing!"
When to use: Someone describes a moment when they felt ashamed or self-conscious. You're showing empathy.
"I tripped in front of everyone at the party" / "I called my teacher 'Mum' by mistake"

Prepare Your Story

Choose one of these situations — or think of your own funny experience:

📝 Make Notes About Your Story

  • When did it happen? What day, time, or period? (Last month, when I was at university, etc.)
  • Who was involved? Describe the people in your story briefly.
  • What happened? Give a clear sequence of events.
  • How did you feel? Use emotional expressions — embarrassed, shocked, thrilled?
  • Why is it funny? What makes the story worth telling?
  • What was the result? How did it all end?

Checklist: Will Your Story Work?

✓ Does it have a clear beginning, middle, and end?
Can you explain what happened first, then second, then finally?
✓ Do you describe emotions or reactions?
Will the listener understand how you felt at different points?
✓ Does it have a surprise or unexpected moment?
Is there a moment where something unexpected happens?
✓ Have you chosen sequencing phrases?
Will you use "First of all," "Then," "Suddenly," "To make a long story short"?

Your Turn & Reflection

Tell Your Story

Now you're ready. Tell your story to your conversation partner(s) or to yourself on camera. Remember to:

Use sequencing language to guide your listener through the story.
Express emotions clearly so your listener understands how you felt.
Create a moment of surprise or humour that makes the story memorable.
Finish with a clear ending that leaves the listener satisfied.
