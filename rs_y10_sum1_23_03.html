God's Nature in Islamic Theology

Four Key Attributes of God

Islamic theology emphasises specific attributes of God that appear repeatedly in the Qur'an:

  • Al-Qadir (The Omnipotent) - God has absolute power; controls all creation
  • Al-Rahman (The Most Merciful) - God's compassion encompasses all beings
  • Al-Rahim (The Most Compassionate) - God's mercy is boundless and personal
  • Al-Adl (The Just) - God's justice is perfect; no wrong is done

Why these matter: These attributes assure Muslims that God rules justly and mercifully, providing comfort in hardship and ethical guidance in decisions.

Surah 59:22-24 - The Divine Names

This passage emphasises God's attributes:

Surah 59:22-24
"He is God, the Creator, the Maker, the Fashioner. To Him belong the most beautiful names (Asma al-Husna). All that is in the heavens and the earth glorify Him, and He is the Mighty, the Wise."

This verse references the 99 Names of Allah--a collection of divine names and attributes that reveal different facets of God's character.

The 99 Names of Allah (Asma al-Husna)

Islamic tradition teaches that God has 99 names, each revealing an aspect of God's nature:

God's Immanence (Closeness)

  • God is aware of all our actions
  • God listens to our prayers
  • God's mercy is always available
  • God guides believers personally
  • God is nearer than our jugular vein (Surah 50:16)

God's Transcendence (Otherness)

  • God is beyond human comprehension
  • God is not like creation
  • God exists beyond time and space
  • "There is nothing like unto Him" (Surah 42:11)
  • God's nature cannot be fully grasped

God's Nature in Islamic Theology

Mercy vs. Justice: God's Perfect Balance

One of Islamic theology's deepest questions is: How can God be both merciful and just?

  • God's Mercy (Rahma): God desires to forgive; the Qur'an opens each chapter with "In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate." This suggests God's default posture is compassionate.
  • God's Justice (Adl): God cannot overlook wrong; accountability is essential. The Day of Judgement reflects God's justice--each person answers for their deeds.

Islamic Resolution: These are not contradictory. God's justice is merciful--God gives humans freedom to choose, opportunities to repent, and forgives those who truly turn back. God's mercy is just--it does not excuse harm or cruelty. Together, they reflect a God who is both loving and fair.

Qur'anic evidence: Surah 7:56 - "My mercy encompasses all things"; yet Surah 3:140 affirms "God loves the just."

God's Nature and Human Suffering

The question "If God is merciful and just, why is there suffering?" is addressed in Islamic theology through several perspectives:

  • Testing: Suffering tests faith and purifies the soul (Surah 2:155: "With hardship comes ease.")
  • Consequence: Some suffering results from human sin; God's justice allows natural consequences (Surah 42:30)
  • Wisdom: God's wisdom may include purposes humans do not understand; trust in God's justice is required
  • Trust (Tawakkul): Reliance on God's mercy helps believers endure suffering with patience (Sabr)

This understanding encourages Muslims to maintain faith during hardship and seek God's guidance rather than despair.

God's Nature and the Problem of Evil: Islamic Response

Islam acknowledges the existence of evil but locates its source in human free will and Satan's influence, not in God:

  • Free Will: Humans choose good or evil; God does not compel sin (Surah 18:29)
  • Satan (Iblis): A rebellious being who tempts humans toward evil (Surah 7:16-17)
  • God's Permission: God permits evil to exist as a test, but never causes it directly
  • Accountability: On the Day of Judgement, humans answer for their choices

This framework preserves both God's justice and human moral responsibility.
