About You: Good At, Keen On, Worried About

For describing yourself and people you know — skills, interests, feelings.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday descriptions:

  • "My sister's really good at languages — she picks up new words in minutes."
  • "Dad's quite proud of how far my brother's come with his guitar playing."
  • "Honestly, I'm a bit worried about the interview tomorrow."

Before you check: what comes straight after "good", "proud" and "worried" in each one?

Check the pattern CLICK
A preposition (at / of / about), then a noun or an -ing form. Each adjective has its own fixed partner: good AT, proud OF, worried ABOUT. Swap the preposition and the sentence sounds foreign, even though the grammar around it looks fine.
be + adjective + preposition + noun / -ing
good at · keen on · worried about · afraid of · interested in · proud of

From plain to natural

"She doesn't want to try sushi."
→
"She's not keen on trying sushi."

"Keen on" + the -ing form is how people actually talk about interest and enthusiasm — much more common in speech than "want to."

What You Do: Depend On, Listen To, Wait For

For everyday habits, relationships and requests.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday habits:

  • "I still depend on my parents for a lift to the airport sometimes."
  • "We always listen to the radio in the car on long drives."
  • "Could you look after my plants while I'm away next week?"

Same question: what's the pattern here?

Check the pattern CLICK
verb + preposition + noun. depend ON, listen TO, look AFTER — each verb has its own fixed partner too. Miss the preposition ("listen the radio") and the sentence breaks; use the wrong one ("depend of") and it sounds foreign.
verb + preposition + noun
depend on · listen to · wait for · look after · agree with · apologise for

Reasons, Changes and Answers

For nouns — explaining why, describing a change, giving a solution.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday nouns with their prepositions:

  • "The real reason for the delay was a problem with the trains."
  • "There's been a small increase in the rent this year."
  • "I still don't know the answer to that question."

What's the pattern this time?

Check the pattern CLICK
reason FOR, increase IN, answer TO — nouns pair with prepositions too, just like adjectives and verbs. Learn the noun and its preposition as one chunk, and the same pattern also works after adjectives with the -ing form: "interested in growing vegetables", never "interested to grow."
noun + preposition + noun / -ing
a reason for · an increase in · a decrease in · the answer to · an interest in
