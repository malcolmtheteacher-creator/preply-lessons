Curious Conversations 07

Why We Can't Stop Checking Our Phones

Series 2 • C1-C2 Level | 50 minutes | 5 Key Expressions

How Many Times Did You Check Your Phone Today?

The average person checks their phone 96 times per day—that's once every 10 minutes

Meet Emma, a 28-year-old marketing manager from Toronto. She wakes up and the first thing she does—before even getting out of bed—is check her phone. Instagram, WhatsApp, email. "It's like my brain isn't fully awake until I've scrolled for a few minutes," she says.

Sound familiar? You're not alone. Research from the University of Chicago found that phantom vibrations—feeling your phone buzz when it didn't—happen to over 80% of smartphone users. Our brains are so attuned to our phones that we literally hallucinate notifications.

But here's what's fascinating: phone designers intentionally make apps as addictive as slot machines. Every time you pull down to refresh your feed, your brain gets a hit of dopamine—the same chemical released when people gamble. App developers call these "variable reward schedules," and they're incredibly powerful.

Michael, a former Google engineer, left his job specifically to warn people about phone addiction. "We've built the most sophisticated attention-grabbing machines in human history," he says. "And we've put one in everyone's pocket."

Studies show we now spend an average of 4.5 hours per day on our phones. That's more than 1,600 hours per year—equivalent to 67 full days. We're spending 2 months of every year staring at a screen.

Think About It:

  • When was the last time you left home without your phone? How did it feel?
  • Do you check your phone within 5 minutes of waking up?
  • Have you ever experienced a "phantom vibration"?
  • What would you do with 4.5 extra hours per day if you didn't use your phone?

Expressing Habits We're Not Proud Of

Click each expression to see how it's used

1. "I'm guilty of..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: Admitting that you do something you know you probably shouldn't do. Used to confess habits or behaviors with a slightly humorous tone.
Example:
"I'm guilty of checking Instagram first thing in the morning, before I even say good morning to my partner."
NOW YOU TRY:
Complete this sentence about your phone habits: "I'm guilty of whenever ."
2. "I can't help but..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: Describing automatic behaviors that feel beyond your control. It emphasizes that the action happens almost involuntarily.
Example:
"Even when I'm trying to focus on work, I can't help but check my phone every few minutes to see if I got any messages."
NOW YOU TRY:
Think of something you do with your phone automatically. Say: "I can't help but when ."

Describing Time and Patterns

3. "Before I know it..."
Formality: Neutral/Informal
Meaning: Describing how time passes quickly without realizing it. Often used when something happens faster than expected.
Example:
"I tell myself I'll just check social media for 5 minutes, but before I know it, an hour has passed and I've achieved nothing."
NOW YOU TRY:
Describe a time when phone usage made time disappear: "I started and before I knew it, ."
4. "I've been trying to..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: Describing ongoing attempts to change a behavior. Implies effort and struggle, often without complete success.
Example:
"I've been trying to stop checking my phone right before bed, but it's incredibly difficult. Those phantom vibrations keep pulling me back."
NOW YOU TRY:
What phone habit have you tried to change? Say: "I've been trying to , but ."
5. "It's gotten to the point where..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: Describing when a situation has reached an extreme or concerning level. Used to emphasize how serious something has become.
Example:
"My phone addiction has gotten to the point where I feel anxious if I can't find it for even 30 seconds."
NOW YOU TRY:
Describe an extreme phone behavior (yours or someone you know): "It's gotten to the point where ."

All 5 Expressions:

  • "I'm guilty of..." — Admitting behaviors you're not proud of
  • "I can't help but..." — Describing automatic actions
  • "Before I know it..." — Time passing without realizing
  • "I've been trying to..." — Ongoing attempts to change
  • "It's gotten to the point where..." — Describing extremes
