Key Vocabulary

Click each card to see the meaning. Then try to use it in a sentence about the mission.

a flyby A space flight that passes close to something (like the Moon) without landing on it
splashdown When a spacecraft lands in the ocean at the end of its mission
to crowd out When one thing takes so much money/attention that others lose out — e.g. "Space spending crowds out climate research."
a milestone An important event or achievement that marks progress — e.g. "The first woman on a lunar mission is a major milestone."
a make-or-break moment A critical point that will decide success or failure — "This was a make-or-break moment for NASA."
over budget Costing more than originally planned — "The programme ran $55 billion over budget."
a price tag The cost of something (often used when the cost seems surprising or controversial) — "The $4.1 billion price tag has drawn criticism."
to push the boundaries To go beyond what was previously thought possible — "Artemis pushes the boundaries of human exploration."

The Money Question

Here are some real numbers. Read them, then close your eyes and try to explain the situation from memory.

The Artemis Programme So Far

By 2026, NASA had spent more than $55 billion on the programme. By 2028, when Artemis astronauts are planned to actually land on the Moon, the total will reach roughly $105 billion.

Each single Artemis flight costs about $4 billion — roughly $2.2 billion for the rocket, $1 billion for the crew capsule, $300 million for the European service module, and $570 million for ground support.

For comparison: NASA's Apollo programme returned an estimated $7 to the US economy for every $1 spent.

SPEAK: Without looking at the numbers above — explain the cost situation in your own words. How much has been spent? How much will be spent? What does each flight cost?

What Could $4.1 Billion Buy Instead?

For context — the same amount of money could fund:

Perspective A: The Supporter

"There is value in establishing a long-term human presence on the lunar surface and mining for resources like water ice. Artemis offers a chance to establish a new lunar economy, conduct long-term science, and locate resources for missions to Mars."

— Retired NASA astronaut, April 2026

Perspective B: The Critic

"Rather than repeat accomplishments from half a century ago, NASA should focus on pushing deeper into the solar system — to Mars. Within NASA, human spaceflight spending has crowded out planetary science and other critical missions."

— Former NASA official, April 2026

Perspective C: The Sceptic

"Billions over the Moon — is Artemis II worth the price tag when Earth's crises go unfunded? We have climate change, poverty, disease. How do you justify $105 billion on repeating something we did in 1969?"

— Editorial headline, April 2026

SPEAK: Explain this view. Start with: "From this perspective..."

Useful Language for Evaluating

You might find these helpful for the debate coming up. Don't memorise them — just notice them.

WEIGHING UP On balance, I think the benefits outweigh / don't outweigh the costs.
CONCEDING I take the point that... but I still think...
CHALLENGING That assumes that... which isn't necessarily true.
REFRAMING It depends on how you define "worth it" / "progress" / "value."
EVIDENCE If you look at the figures, you can see that...
QUESTIONING The real question is whether... / who benefits from...

Discussion Cards

Choose 3 cards that interest you most. For each one, speak for at least one minute. Use evidence from the lesson and the expressions from the previous tab.

1

The Price Tag

$4.1 billion for one flight. $105 billion by 2028. Is this a reasonable investment in humanity's future, or an irresponsible use of public money while people on Earth struggle?

2

Representation

It took until 2026 for a woman and a person of colour to fly to the Moon. What does this tell us about who gets to explore? Does representation in space actually matter, or is it just symbolism?

3

Moon vs Mars

Some critics say we're repeating 1969 instead of pushing forward to Mars. Others say the Moon is a necessary stepping stone. Which side are you on, and why?

4

Private vs Public

SpaceX can launch rockets for a fraction of NASA's cost. Should governments still run space programmes, or should we leave it to companies like SpaceX and Blue Origin?

5

The "Lunar Economy"

NASA talks about mining water ice on the Moon and building a lunar economy. Is this exciting progress or the beginning of exploiting another place for resources?

6

Priorities

If you were in charge of a $4 billion budget, would you spend it on space, climate change, healthcare, education, or something else? Defend your choice.
