Key words

wonder (verb) — to think about something with curiosity and a little surprise, wanting to understand it. Example: "I sometimes wonder why there is anything at all."
take for granted (phrase) — to accept something without ever questioning it or noticing how valuable it is. Example: "We forget how lucky we are to have clean water from the tap."
curiosity (noun) — a strong wish to know or learn about something. Example: "Her questions came from pure curiosity, not from rudeness."
existence (noun) — the fact of being real or being alive. Example: "Nobody can fully explain the existence of the universe."
illusion (noun) — something that seems real or true but is not. Example: "The feeling that time is passing slowly was only an illusion."
doubt (verb) — to feel unsure whether something is true or possible. Example: "I don't doubt that he meant well."
perceive (verb) — to notice or become aware of something through the senses or the mind. Example: "We perceive colours differently in evening light."
free will (noun) — the power to make your own choices, not controlled by fate or by someone else. Example: "Did you decide that freely, or was it always going to happen?"
profound (adjective) — very deep, serious and important in meaning. Example: "It was a simple question with a profound answer."
speculate (verb) — to make guesses about something when you do not have all the facts. Example: "We can only speculate about who sent the letters."
