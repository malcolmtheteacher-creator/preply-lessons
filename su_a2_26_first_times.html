First Times

A2 English Lesson | Storytelling & Past Tense Verbs

Lesson Overview

In this lesson, you will:

  • Review irregular past simple verbs
  • Learn adjectives for describing feelings
  • Tell a story about the first time something happened to you
  • Listen and respond to stories

Let's Warm Up

Think about these questions. You don't need to answer now—just think about them. We'll come back to them later:

When was the first time you drove a car?

Do you remember your first day at secondary school?

How did you first meet your wife / husband / best friend?

When did you first travel abroad? How did you feel?

How was your first lesson on English Online?

These questions ask about moments that were new and memorable. When we tell stories about "first times," we need to use the past tense to describe what happened and also describe how we felt at that time. This lesson will help you tell these stories naturally!

Quick Check

Can you complete these sentences with the correct past tense verb? Try without looking at the table first:

1. Yesterday, I (go) to the supermarket.

went - "Yesterday, I went to the supermarket."

2. When I was young, I (have) many problems at school.

had - "When I was young, I had many problems at school."

3. Last week, my parents (pay) for a nice dinner.

paid - "Last week, my parents paid for a nice dinner."

4. I (drive) a car when I was 16 for the first time.

drove - "I drove a car when I was 16 for the first time."

5. My cousin (come) to my birthday party last year.

came - "My cousin came to my birthday party last year."

Adjectives for Feelings

When you tell a story about a "first time," you should describe how you felt. Here are some important adjectives:

😲Surprised — something unexpected happened. "I was surprised when I found out he was from Saudi Arabia too."

😳Embarrassed — you feel uncomfortable or ashamed. "I was embarrassed when I called her 'Mary'—her name is actually 'Maryam'."

😲✨Amazed — very surprised by something wonderful. "He was amazed when he found out his teacher speaks four languages!"

😨Terrified — very scared. "I felt terrified when I watched The Conjuring II—what a scary film!"

😟Nervous — worried or anxious. "We might feel nervous before a job interview or important exam."

😑Bored — not interested. "I am often bored at work because I do the same things every day."

Use These Adjectives in Your Stories

You can say:

  • "I was surprised when..."
  • "I felt embarrassed because..."
  • "I was amazed at..."
  • "I was terrified of..."
  • "I felt nervous before..."
  • "I was bored during..."

Quick Check

Which feeling would you use in these situations?

Situation: You see a ghost!

Terrified — "I was terrified!" (or nervous, or amazed—but terrified is strongest)

Situation: Someone you know has done something incredible.

Amazed — "I was amazed!"

Situation: You said something stupid by accident.

Embarrassed — "I felt so embarrassed!"

Story Structure Guide

Use this structure when you tell your story:

Beginning

  • When: "When I was ... years old" / "Last year" / "A few months ago"
  • What: "I had the first experience with..."

Middle

  • Use past tense verbs to describe what happened (went, took, drove, etc.)
  • Give details about the situation
  • Use adjectives to describe your feelings: "I was nervous...", "I felt surprised..."

End

  • What was the result?
  • How did it end?
  • Final feeling: "I was amazed!" / "I felt proud!"

Speaking Task

Now describe your experience. This time, give more information about how you felt. Use the adjectives and past tense verbs you learned in this lesson.

You have 3 minutes to tell your story.

3:00
  • Slow down: Speak clearly and not too fast
  • Use pauses: Give yourself time to think of the next word
  • Use feeling words: "I was nervous...", "I felt surprised..."
  • Use past tense: "I went...", "I drove...", "I found..."
  • Add details: Don't just say "I went abroad"—say where, when, and why
  • Don't worry about mistakes: Focus on communicating your story

Review & Reflection

What Did You Learn?

Think about these questions:

Which 3 new words or phrases do you want to remember from this lesson?

Maybe: surprised, terrified, nervous, embarrassed, amazed—or verbs like drove, paid, took, found, went.

Which activity helped you the most?

Some students say the verb table helps them remember. Others say telling the story is the best practice. Some like learning the feeling adjectives so they can describe emotions in English.

What do you want to review after this lesson?

Review: the irregular past tense verbs, the feeling adjectives, or the speaking structure. You can return to the British Council website and find more grammar practice on past simple verbs.
