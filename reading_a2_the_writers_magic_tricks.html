Key words

clue (noun) — a small thing that helps you find the answer. Example: "The open window was an important clue."
notice (verb) — see something and think about it. Example: "Did you notice the red light in the car?"
guess (verb) — give an answer when you are not sure. Example: "Guess what happens next."
surprise (noun) — something you did not expect. Example: "The ending was a big surprise."
ending (noun) — the last part of a story or film. Example: "I loved the film, but not the ending."
storyteller (noun) — the person who tells a story. Example: "The storyteller says it wasn't his fault."
lie (verb) — say something that is not true. Example: "He lied about the money."
hide (verb) — put something where people can't see it. Example: "The writer hides the clue in a boring sentence."
worried (adjective) — afraid that something bad will happen. Example: "I was worried when I heard the footsteps."
trick (noun) — a clever way to make people believe something. Example: "Writers use tricks, like magicians."

All six tricks

The Card · An early clueThe writer gives a small clue at the start. Later, it is important. A strange noise · a red light · "He didn't look at it."
The Cups · A false clueThe writer shows you the wrong person. The real clue is in a boring sentence.
The Hat · A surprise endingAt the end, everything changes. But the clues were there.
The Mirror · A lying storytellerThe person telling the story is not honest. Listen for small mistakes.
The Chest · WaitingShort sentences. Slow. Very slow. The writer makes you wait and feel worried.
The Crystal Ball · Show itDon't say "He was rich." Show it: "He paid with a 500-euro note."
