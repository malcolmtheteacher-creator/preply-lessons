The First Enquiry

Your phone rings. It is a new customer. They want a cleaner, and they are calling you. Time to answer well, ask the right questions, and win the job — in simple, polite English.

The Scene 🎬

You run a small cleaning business. You clean houses and flats, and your customers like you. Most of your new customers come from one place: a phone call. Someone finds your number online, or a friend gives it to them, and they call.

This first call is important. The customer does not know you yet. In two or three minutes, they decide: "Is this person friendly? Do they understand me? Can I trust them with my house?"

You do not need perfect English. You need a few good questions, a calm voice, and one clear promise at the end: "I'll get back to you." Today you practise exactly that.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. When you phone a business — a plumber, a taxi, a restaurant — what makes you think "yes, this is a good one" in the first minute?
  2. Do you like talking on the phone in English? What is the hardest part: understanding, or speaking?

Who's who

You
The Cleaner 🧽

You own the business. Friendly, calm, and organised. You ask questions, check the details, and promise a price later — not now.

The other person
The New Customer 📞

They need a cleaner for their home. They are polite but busy, and they want to know the price quickly. They speak fast sometimes!

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at what the cleaner does: they answer warmly, ask short questions, and check they understood. They never guess. Watch how, not just what.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases for any phone call from a new customer. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Slow is fine. On the phone, a calm, slow voice sounds professional. If you do not understand, ask again — nobody minds. If you are not sure, check. The customer will trust you more, not less.

📞 Answering the call

Phrase 1

The phone rings. You say hello. Now you want the customer to tell you what they need. What is the friendly question? Complete it: "How can I ___ you?"

"How can I help you?"
This is the standard way to answer a business call. Say your business name first, then this question. It tells the customer: "I'm listening — tell me what you need."

❓ Asking for details

Phrase 2

You need to know about the house — how many bedrooms, how many bathrooms. Which one is the polite way to ask?

A. "Bedrooms. How many?"
B. "Could you tell me how many bedrooms there are, please?"
C. "You must tell me the number of bedrooms."
B — "Could you tell me how many bedrooms there are, please?"
"Could you tell me…, please?" is a soft, polite frame for any question. Use it for everything: "Could you tell me your address, please?", "Could you tell me which day is best, please?" A sounds cold, and C sounds like an order.
Phrase 3

You need to know if someone will be at home on the first visit. You want to ask in a soft way. Put the words in order:

be able towould yoube at home?
"Would you be able to be at home?"
"Would you be able to…?" is a polite way to ask if something is possible for the other person. Use it for keys and access too: "Would you be able to leave a key?", "Would you be able to send me the address?"

✅ Checking you understood

Phrase 4

The customer speaks fast, or the line is bad, and you did not hear. Do not guess! Ask them to say it one more time. Complete it: "Sorry, could you ___ that again, please?"

"Sorry, could you say that again, please?"
Short, polite, and very normal — native speakers say this all the time on the phone. You can add a reason: "The line is a bit bad." Another version: "Sorry, can you repeat that?"
Phrase 5

You have all the details. Before the call ends, you want to say them back to the customer, so both of you are sure. Which one does this?

A. "OK, I think I understand everything."
B. "Fine. Anything else?"
C. "Just to be sure — every week, and one dog, right?"
C — "Just to be sure — every week, and one dog, right?"
"Just to be sure — …, right?" repeats the details and asks the customer to confirm. It is the best way to find a mistake now, not next Thursday at the wrong house. A and B do not check anything.

🤝 The price, and the end of the call

Phrase 6

The customer asks for a day that is difficult for you, or a job you are not sure about. You do not want to say "no", and you do not want to say "yes" too fast. Say it out loud first, then check.

"Let me see what I can do."
This is a friendly "maybe". It buys you time and sounds helpful. Use it when a customer asks for a special day, a special time, or something extra. Then check your diary and get back to them.
Phrase 7

The call is nearly finished. You need to contact the customer later, so you need two things from them. Complete it: "Could I have your ___ and ___?"

"Could I have your name and number?"
Always take the name and number before the call ends. Then say the number back: "Oh seven seven, four two one…" — this is the moment mistakes happen. You can also ask: "Could I have your address, please?"
Phrase 8

The customer wants a price now, but you need to see the house first. You want to promise to contact them later — and say when. Which one is clear and friendly?

A. "I'll get back to you this evening."
B. "I'll think about it."
C. "Maybe I can call you at some time."
A — "I'll get back to you this evening."
"I'll get back to you" means "I will contact you again". The important part is the time: "this evening", "tomorrow morning", "by Friday". A promise with a time makes the customer relax. B and C have no time — the customer will call another company.

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work on any call — a customer, a supplier, a school, a doctor. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"Could you tell me ___, please?" — What do you need to know from a new customer? (the address, the number of rooms, the best day…)

"Just to be sure — ___, right?" — Think of a real job. Say the details back.

"I'll get back to you ___." — When do you usually send your prices? Put a real time in.

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole call out loud — answer → ask about the house → ask about the day and pets → check the details → take the name and number → promise a time. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

The Twist

💷 "I need the price now"

One more time — no help this round. You are the cleaner again. The customer is in a hurry. They say: "Look, I'm calling three companies this morning. Can you just tell me the price now?" You cannot give a price before you see the house. What do you say? Stay friendly. Tell them why you need to see it first, promise a time, and try to keep the customer. Speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I answered with a warm hello and "How can I help you?"
  • I asked about the rooms, the day, and the pets — with short, polite questions.
  • When I didn't understand, I asked them to say it again. I didn't guess.
  • I said all the details back to check them.
  • I took the name and number, and I promised to get back to them at a real time.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Bigger Picture 🔭

The customer on the phone cannot see your work. They cannot see how clean your last kitchen was, or how careful you are with keys. They can only hear your voice — for three minutes.

So in those three minutes, your questions are your work. When you ask about the pets, the customer thinks: "This person is careful." When you say the details back, they think: "This person listens." When you promise a time and keep it, they think: "This person is reliable."

Nobody remembers perfect grammar on a phone call. They remember that you were calm, you asked good questions, and you called back when you said you would.

Two questions to talk about

  1. Some businesses give a price on the phone straight away. Others always want to see the job first. Which is better for the customer? Which is better for the business?
  2. Think about a time a business did not call you back. How did you feel? Did you use them again?
