Key Takeaway

The answer to "What is God?" depends entirely on who you ask. Christians, Muslims, Hindus, and Humanists give completely different answers based on their tradition's teachings, experience, and evidence. There is no single "correct" answer in a religious studies class. What matters is understanding why each tradition answers the way it does, and evaluating the evidence and arguments.

3. Assessment Task 1: The Knowledge Test

10 quick-fire questions testing your recall of key terms and facts from Unit 2. Work through them, then click "Check answers" for each one.

1. What is Tawhid?
Tawhid is the Islamic concept of the absolute oneness of Allah. It is the foundation of Islamic belief: there is only one God, and no other being should be worshipped or associated with Allah.
2. Which tradition believes in the Trimurti?
Hinduism. The Trimurti is the three main forms of Brahman: Brahma (creator), Vishnu (preserver), and Shiva (destroyer/transformer).
3. Complete: "Say: He is Allah..."
"Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal, Absolute. He begets not, nor is He begotten. And there is none like unto Him." (Surah Al-Ikhlas, 112:1-4) This is the core Islamic statement of monotheism.
4. What do Christians mean by the Trinity?
One God in three persons: God the Father (creator), God the Son (Jesus Christ), and God the Holy Spirit. They are distinct persons but one God.
5. What is shirk in Islam?
Shirk is associating partners with Allah or worshipping anything other than Allah alone. It is considered the greatest sin in Islam because it violates Tawhid (the oneness of Allah).
6. Name two of the 99 Names of Allah.
Any two from: Al-Rahman (The Merciful), Al-Rahim (The Compassionate), Al-Malik (The Sovereign), Al-Qudus (The Holy), As-Salaam (The Peace), Al-Aziz (The Mighty), Al-Hakeem (The Wise), Al-Adl (The Just), Al-Alim (The Knowing), Al-Wadud (The Loving), etc.
7. What is Brahman in Hinduism?
Brahman is the ultimate reality or divine force in Hinduism. It is infinite, eternal, and beyond full human understanding. It is sometimes described as "being, consciousness, bliss" (Sat-Chit-Ananda). All other gods and forms are expressions or manifestations of Brahman.
8. What do Humanists say about the source of morality?
Humanists say morality comes from human reason, empathy, and reciprocity — not from God or divine commands. They follow the Golden Rule ("treat others as you'd want to be treated") because it is rational and makes society work better.
9. State the Problem of Evil argument in one sentence.
If God is omnipotent (all-powerful) and omnibenevolent (all-good), then evil should not exist; but it does, so either God is not all-powerful, not all-good, or does not exist.
10. Name one similarity between Christian and Islamic views of God.
Both believe in one God (monotheism). Both believe God is creator, merciful, and has revealed himself to humanity. Both believe in an afterlife and divine judgment. Both emphasize morality and compassion as central to faith. (Any one of these is correct.)
