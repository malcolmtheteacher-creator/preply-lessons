Key Takeaway

The answer to "What is God?" depends entirely on who you ask. Christians, Muslims, Hindus, and Humanists give completely different answers based on their tradition's teachings. What matters is understanding why each tradition answers the way it does.

Assessment Task 1: The Knowledge Test

10 quick-fire questions. Work through them, then click "Show answer" for each one.

1. What is Tawhid?
Tawhid is the Islamic concept of the absolute oneness of Allah. There is only one God, and no other being should be worshipped or associated with Allah.
2. Which tradition believes in the Trimurti?
Hinduism. The Trimurti is the three main forms of Brahman: Brahma (creator), Vishnu (preserver), and Shiva (destroyer/transformer).
3. Complete: "Say: He is Allah..."
"Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal, Absolute. He begets not, nor is He begotten. And there is none like unto Him." (Surah Al-Ikhlas, 112:1-4)
4. What do Christians mean by the Trinity?
One God in three persons: God the Father (creator), God the Son (Jesus Christ), and God the Holy Spirit.
5. What is shirk in Islam?
Shirk is associating partners with Allah or worshipping anything other than Allah alone. It is the greatest sin in Islam because it violates Tawhid.
6. Name two of the 99 Names of Allah.
Examples: Al-Rahman (The Merciful), Al-Rahim (The Compassionate), Al-Malik (The Sovereign), Al-Aziz (The Mighty), Al-Hakeem (The Wise), Al-Adl (The Just), Al-Alim (The Knowing).
7. What is Brahman in Hinduism?
Brahman is the ultimate reality or divine force in Hinduism. It is infinite, eternal, and beyond full human understanding. All other gods and forms are expressions of Brahman.
8. What do Humanists say about the source of morality?
Humanists say morality comes from human reason, empathy, and reciprocity — not from God. They follow the Golden Rule because it is rational and makes society work better.
9. State the Problem of Evil argument in one sentence.
If God is omnipotent (all-powerful) and omnibenevolent (all-good), then evil should not exist; but it does, so either God is not all-powerful, not all-good, or does not exist.
10. Name one similarity between Christian and Islamic views of God.
Both believe in one God (monotheism). Both believe God is creator, merciful, and has revealed himself. Both believe in an afterlife and divine judgment.
