Blackbeard

The most famous pirate in history — and a man who frightened people with his face before he ever used a gun.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The man with the smoking beard

We do not know much about Blackbeard. We are not even sure of his real name. People usually call him Edward Teach, but old papers spell it in different ways. He was probably born in England, around the year 1680.

About three hundred years ago, there were many pirates in the seas near America. In 1717, Teach and his men took a big French ship. He kept it, put many guns on it, and gave it a new name: Queen Anne's Revenge. Now he was a real pirate captain.

But Teach's strongest weapon was not his guns. It was his face. He had a big, black beard. Before a fight, he put small fires in his beard, so smoke went around his head. He looked like a monster! Many sailors were so afraid that they did not fight — they just gave him their things. That was exactly what Teach wanted.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was the name of Blackbeard's big ship?

2. Why did Blackbeard put smoke in his beard?

1. Queen Anne's Revenge — a big French ship that he took and put many guns on.

2. To look frightening, like a monster. Then many sailors were too afraid to fight and just gave him their things.

Talk about it:
  • Blackbeard won because people were afraid of him. Is it good or bad to make people afraid of you? Why?

Part 2 — The medicine ransom

In 1718, Blackbeard was very powerful. In May, he took his ships to a busy town called Charleston, in America. He blockaded the port — his ships sat in front of it, so no other ship could come in or go out. He did this for about a week.

He stopped many ships and took some rich people as hostages. Then he sent a message to the governor. But he did not ask for gold! He asked for a box of medicine. Maybe his men were sick. The town was afraid, so they gave him the medicine. Blackbeard let the hostages go and sailed away.

After this, the governor of North Carolina gave Blackbeard a pardon. This means the government forgave his crimes. Blackbeard promised to stop. But it was not true. Very soon, he started robbing ships again.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did Blackbeard ask for at Charleston — gold or something else?

2. Did Blackbeard really stop being a pirate after his pardon?

1. Not gold — a box of medicine. (Maybe his men were sick.) The town gave it to him to free the hostages.

2. No. He promised to stop, but very soon he started robbing ships again.

Talk about it:
  • Blackbeard asked for medicine, not gold. What does this tell us about him? What things are more important than money?

Part 3 — The last fight

The government wanted to stop Blackbeard. They sent a navy officer, Lieutenant Robert Maynard, to find him. On 22 November 1718, Maynard found Blackbeard at a quiet place called Ocracoke, near North Carolina.

Maynard was clever. He hid most of his men under the deck of the ship. Blackbeard saw an almost empty ship, so he climbed on board to fight. It was a trap! The hidden sailors came up and surrounded him. After a hard fight, Blackbeard died. To show that the pirate was really dead, Maynard cut off his head and hung it on the front of his ship.

Blackbeard was a pirate for only about two years. But people never forgot him. Three hundred years later, he is still in many books, films and stories — the most famous pirate of all.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did Maynard trick Blackbeard?

2. Blackbeard was a pirate for only two years. Why do we still remember him?

1. He hid most of his men under the deck. The ship looked empty, so Blackbeard climbed on — and then the hidden sailors came up and surrounded him.

2. Because of his legend — the frightening image he made. He is still in many books and films, the most famous pirate of all.

Talk about it:
  • Blackbeard is now a "fun" pirate in films for children. But he was a real, dangerous criminal. Is that strange? Why?

Grammar: past simple & past continuous

To tell a story, we use two past tenses together.

Past simple = a finished action: "he took a ship," "Blackbeard died."
Past continuous (was/were + -ing) = a longer action, happening at that time: "while his ships were blocking the port…"
We often use them together with while and when: a long action (continuous) and a short action that happens inside it (simple).

1. Choose the right tense

1. While Blackbeard (blockade) the port, his men (take) money from the ships.

2. When Maynard's men (attack), Blackbeard (not expect) it.

3. Blackbeard (fight) when the hidden sailors (surround) him.

1. was blockading / were taking (both long actions happening at the same time)

2. attacked / did not expect (a short, finished action)

3. was fighting / surrounded (long action + short action inside it)

2. Tell a short part of the story

Put the verbs in the past simple or past continuous. Then read it out loud.

One morning, the pirates (sleep) on their ship. They (not know) that the navy was near. Suddenly, the ships (arrive). Blackbeard (jump) up and (start) to fight.

were sleeping · did not know · arrived · jumped · started

The long "background" action is in the past continuous; the sudden, finished actions are in the past simple.
