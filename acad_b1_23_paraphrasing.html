Paraphrasing: Saying It Differently

B1 Intermediate Academic English

⏱️ Time: 5:00

Why Paraphrasing Matters

Paraphrasing is restating someone else's ideas using your own words. It's essential for academic writing.

Three Important Reasons to Paraphrase

  • Show Understanding: When you can restate an idea, you prove you understand it
  • Avoid Plagiarism: Using someone's exact words without quotation marks is stealing. Paraphrasing gives them credit while using your own language
  • Improve Readability: Your essay flows better when the language matches your writing style

Paraphrasing vs Direct Quotes

  • Quote: Use exact words with quotation marks. Good for important or powerful statements.
  • Paraphrase: Use your own words. Good for explaining ideas and showing understanding.

Paraphrasing Techniques

1. Change Word Class

Original: "Climate change is significant."
Paraphrase: "The significance of climate change cannot be ignored."

2. Use Synonyms

Original: "Many students struggle with essay writing."
Paraphrase: "Numerous learners find composing academic essays challenging."

3. Restructure Sentences

Original: "Although remote work offers flexibility, it reduces team interaction."
Paraphrase: "Team collaboration suffers when workers are not co-located, despite the benefits of flexible working arrangements."

4. Change Sentence Structure

Original: "The student attended lectures, completed assignments, and passed the exam."
Paraphrase: "By attending lectures and completing assignments, the student successfully passed the exam."

Critical Rule: Always Cite Your Source!

Even when you paraphrase, you must cite the original source. Paraphrasing without citation is plagiarism.

Common Paraphrasing Mistakes

  • Too Close to Original: Changing just one or two words. This is still plagiarism.
  • Changing Meaning: Accidental misinterpretation of the original idea.
  • No Citation: Forgetting to acknowledge your source.
  • Mixing Quote and Paraphrase: Not clear which words are from the source.

Practice Paraphrasing

Read the original text and identify the better paraphrase.

Example 1

Original: "Social media has revolutionised how people communicate, allowing instant connection across vast distances."

Option A: "Social media has changed how people communicate, allowing instant connection across large distances."
Option B: "The way humans interact has been fundamentally transformed by social media platforms, which enable rapid communication regardless of geographical separation."
Which is better paraphrasing?

Option B. Option A only changes a few words (too similar to original). Option B genuinely paraphrases: restructures sentences, uses different word forms (revolutionised → transformed), and changes sentence structure while keeping meaning intact.

Example 2

Original: "The student's ability to concentrate improved significantly after exercise."

Option A: "After exercise, the student could concentrate much better."
Option B: "Physical activity had a positive effect on the student's concentration."
Which is better?

Option B. While both paraphrase, Option B shows more genuine reformulation. It changes word class (ability → effect, concentrate → concentration) and uses different structure. Option A is simpler but less sophisticated.
