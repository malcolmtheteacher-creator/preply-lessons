Key words

tone (noun) — the feeling your words give, for example friendly, cold or angry. Example: "I didn't mind what she said, but I didn't like the way she said it: her tone was icy."
register (noun) — how formal or informal your language is, chosen to suit the situation. Example: "That word is fine with friends, but it's the wrong register for a job interview."
blunt (adjective) — saying exactly what you think, without trying to be polite. Example: "His feedback was blunt, but it was useful."
tactful (adjective) — careful not to upset people when you say something difficult. Example: "She found a tactful way to tell him the plan would not work."
diplomatic (adjective) — careful and sensitive with people, so that you avoid arguments. Example: "Give a diplomatic answer, even if you disagree."
curt (adjective) — rudely short; using very few words. Example: "I asked for help and got a curt reply: 'No.'"
soften (verb) — make something sound less strong or less unkind. Example: "Start with 'I'm afraid' to make bad news gentler: it will soften the blow."
hedge (verb) — avoid saying something too strongly or too directly. Example: "Politicians often hedge when they are asked a difficult question."
come across (phrasal verb) — seem to other people in a particular way. Example: "Your email came across as quite angry."
pushy (adjective) — trying too hard to make other people do what you want. Example: "I don't want to sound pushy, but have you read my proposal yet?"
