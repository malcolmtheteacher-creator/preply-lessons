Torah and the Talmud

Remind yourself: What exactly is the Torah? And what makes the Talmud unusual?

Torah: The first five books of the Hebrew Bible, containing Jewish law and teachings. It's the most sacred Jewish text.

Talmud: Unusual because it's not a holy text itself, but recorded debates and discussions ABOUT the Torah. It shows Jews arguing, questioning, and interpreting the Torah across centuries.

The God Debate

Recall the arguments. Name 2 arguments FOR God's existence and 2 AGAINST.

For God: The Design Argument (the universe is too complex to be random), the Personal Experience Argument (people report experiencing God)

Against God: The Problem of Evil (why would an all-powerful God allow suffering?), Lack of Scientific Evidence (we can explain the universe without God)

What Is Prayer?

Prayer is communication with the divine. But what does that actually mean? It depends on what people believe exists beyond the material world, and what kind of relationship they think they can have with it.

Types of Prayer

Adoration
Praising or worshipping God/the divine

Expressing love, awe, respect. Example: "Glory be to God the Father, God the Son, and God the Holy Spirit."

Confession
Admitting wrongdoing and asking for forgiveness

Taking responsibility. Example: "I have sinned. I am sorry for what I have done."

Thanksgiving
Expressing gratitude

Acknowledging blessings and good things. Example: "Thank you for my family, my health, and this food."

Supplication
Asking for something (for yourself)

Making a request or petition. Example: "Please help me pass my exam," or "Give me strength."

Intercession
Praying for others

Asking God to help someone else. Example: "Please heal my grandmother," or "Help those affected by the flood."

Meditation & Contemplation
Listening, being still, reflecting

Not asking for anything—just being present. Example: Sitting in silence, thinking about God, or reflecting on a sacred text.

Why Do People Pray?

Sense of Connection: Prayer creates a feeling of connection to something bigger than themselves.
Comfort and Support: In difficult times, prayer can bring peace and help people feel less alone.
Guidance and Direction: Some people pray to seek wisdom or clarity about decisions.
Duty and Obligation: For many believers, prayer is a religious requirement or responsibility.
Community: Praying together with others creates belonging and shared identity.
Habit: Prayer can be a comforting routine, like brushing teeth before bed.
Gratitude: People pray to say thanks for what they have.

The Philosophical Question: Does Prayer Work?

The Problem: If God already knows everything (past, present, and future), why do we need to tell God what we need? If God doesn't intervene in the world, what's the point of asking?

Different believers answer this differently:

  • Some say prayer doesn't need to "work" in a magical way—it works because it changes the person praying.
  • Some believe God does intervene, but in ways we don't always see or understand.
  • Some argue that prayer is about trust and relationship, not getting what you ask for.
  • Atheists and agnostics might find value in prayer as meditation or reflection, even without believing in God.

Prayer vs Meditation: What's the Difference?

Prayer Meditation
Often involves talking to God or a divine being Usually silent, focused on breath or body
May ask for help, give thanks, or confess Aims to calm the mind and increase awareness
Usually connected to a religious faith Can be secular (non-religious)
Directed toward a being outside yourself Directed inward, toward yourself
Found across all religions Practised in Buddhism, Hinduism, and beyond
Fact: A 2018 Pew study found that 55% of Americans pray daily. In the UK, the figure is much lower—about 20%. Prayer is far more common in some countries and cultures than others.

Reading Activity

Read these three short prayers from different traditions. For each one, identify what TYPE of prayer it is and explain your choice.

Prayer 1: Christian Tradition

"Our Father in heaven, hallowed be your name. Your kingdom come, your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven. Give us today our daily bread. Forgive us our debts, as we also have forgiven our debtors."

Type of prayer: This includes elements of adoration ("hallowed be your name"), supplication ("give us our daily bread"), and confession ("forgive us"). It's a mixed prayer, but primarily focused on petition and praise.

Prayer 2: Islamic Tradition (from Salah)

The person stands, bows, prostrates, and says "Subhanallah" (Glory be to God). No words asking for anything—just physical movement and praise.

Type of prayer: This is adoration. The act of prostration itself is a form of worship and submission, without asking for anything.

Prayer 3: Buddhist Meditation

A person sits quietly, focuses on their breath, and observes their thoughts without judgment. No words spoken, just awareness.

Type of prayer: This is meditation. It's about being present and developing inner peace, not communicating with a divine being.

Discussion & Writing

Pick 2 Discussion Cards

Choose two of the following questions and think carefully about your answer. You can discuss with a partner, in small groups, or in your head.

If God already knows what you need, why ask?

If an all-knowing God is aware of everything you need before you pray, what's the purpose of asking? Is prayer about changing God's mind, or about something else?

Is meditation a type of prayer, or something completely different?

Some religions include meditation as a form of prayer, others see them as separate things. What's your view? Does it depend on the person's intention?

Can an atheist pray? What would that even mean?

If someone doesn't believe in God, can they still benefit from prayer? Is prayer only for believers?

Is praying together more powerful than praying alone?

Many religions emphasize communal prayer. Is there something special about praying with others, or is it just the same thing in a group?

Both Sides

"Prayer is just talking to yourself."

Arguments FOR this statement
  • There's no scientific evidence that anyone is "listening" to prayer
  • Prayer produces the same effects as self-reflection: it calms you, helps you think clearly, reduces stress
  • Talking through a problem out loud (even to an invisible being) helps people organize their thoughts
  • Believers often report feeling "guided" after prayer, but they might just be unconsciously making up their own minds
Arguments AGAINST this statement
  • Believers report experiences of being answered or guided that go beyond their own thinking
  • Prayer isn't about "getting a response"—it's about connection and relationship with God
  • Millions of people across centuries have found prayer life-changing in ways they couldn't explain otherwise
  • Just because we can't measure or prove God doesn't mean God doesn't exist
