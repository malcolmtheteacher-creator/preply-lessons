Emperor Nero

A young emperor of Rome who wanted to be a singer — and became famous for all the wrong reasons.

Today: tell the story of Emperor Nero, and talk about how true a famous story really is. Grammar: past simple and past continuous.

Key words — match them first

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A boy becomes emperor

In the year 54 AD, a boy of only sixteen became the emperor of Rome. His name was Nero. He ruled a huge empire, from Britain in the west to Syria in the east.

Nero did not win his power himself. His mother, Agrippina, was very clever and very ambitious. She married the old emperor and made sure that her son became the next ruler. At first, she wanted to control Rome through Nero.

For a few years, two wise men helped the young emperor. One was his teacher, Seneca. They kept him calm and helped him rule well. People say these were the "good years" of Nero. But Nero and his mother began to fight for power. In 59 AD, Nero did a terrible thing: he had his own mother killed. After that, he slowly became a much worse ruler.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How old was Nero when he became emperor? Who helped him get the power?

2. What terrible thing did Nero do in 59 AD?

1. He was only sixteen. His clever, ambitious mother Agrippina got the power for him.

2. He had his own mother killed.

Talk about it:
  • A boy of sixteen got total power, and no one could say "no" to him. Do you think that is good or dangerous? Why?

Part 2 — The fire and the singing emperor

In the year 64 AD, a big fire started in Rome. It burned for days and destroyed much of the city. There is a very famous story: people say that Nero played music and sang while Rome was burning. But this story is not true! In fact, Nero was not even in the city when the fire started. He was at his house by the sea, and he came back to help the people.

But Nero did make some people angry. After the fire, he built himself a giant golden palace on the burned land. And he needed someone to blame for the fire, so he blamed a small group of people — the Christians — and was very cruel to them.

Nero also loved music and acting. He wanted to be an artist! He sang, he played an instrument, and he even raced chariots — in front of crowds of people. To us, this is not strange. But the important people of Rome were shocked. They thought an emperor should lead armies, not sing on a stage like an actor.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. The famous story says Nero sang while Rome burned. Why is this story not true?

2. Why were the important people of Rome shocked by Nero?

1. Because Nero was not even in Rome when the fire started — he was at his house by the sea, and he came back to help. (Also, that kind of instrument did not exist yet.)

2. Because he performed in public — he sang, played music and raced chariots — and they thought an emperor should not do these things.

Talk about it:
  • The story about Nero singing is famous, but it is not true. Why do you think people still believe famous stories, even when they are not true?

Part 3 — The end of Nero

In the end, the people of Rome turned against Nero. He spent too much money and was cruel to many people. In the year 68 AD, a big rebellion started. Soldiers and leaders all over the empire said "no more!" Even the Senate, the leaders of Rome, said that Nero was an enemy.

Nero ran away from the city. He had no friends left, and he knew the soldiers would come for him. So he took his own life. He was only about thirty years old. People say his last words were: "What a great artist dies with me!"

After Nero died, the leaders of Rome tried to erase him. They pulled down his statues and took his name off buildings. But here is something important: almost all the stories about Nero were written later, by people who hated him. So we are not sure how much is true and how much is just a bad story made by his enemies.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did Nero's life end in 68 AD?

2. Why can't we be sure that the bad stories about Nero are all true?

1. There was a big rebellion, the leaders of Rome said he was an enemy, he ran away from the city, and then he took his own life (aged about thirty).

2. Because almost all the stories were written later by people who hated him, so some of it may be exaggerated or not true.

Talk about it:
  • His enemies wrote his story. Is it fair that the people who hated Nero decided how we remember him today?

Grammar: past simple & past continuous

To tell a story, we use two past tenses together.

Past simple = a finished action: "Nero became emperor," "the fire destroyed the city."
Past continuous (was/were + -ing) = a longer action, happening at that time: "while Rome was burning…"
We often use them together with while and when: a long action (continuous) and a short action that happens inside it (simple).

1. Choose the right tense

1. While the city (burn), Nero (rest) at his house by the sea.

2. When the news (arrive), the fire (already / spread) across Rome.

3. The people (watch) while the emperor (sing) on the stage.

1. was burning / was resting (both long actions at the same time)

2. arrived / had already spread (or: "spread") (a short, finished action)

3. were watching / was singing (both long actions at the same time)

2. Tell a short part of the story

Put the verbs in the past simple or past continuous. Then read it out loud.

One day, the young emperor (sing) for a big crowd. The important men (not like) it. Suddenly, everything (change). A few years later, the people (rebel) and Nero (run) away.

was singing · did not like · changed · rebelled · ran

The long "background" action is in the past continuous; the sudden, finished actions are in the past simple.
