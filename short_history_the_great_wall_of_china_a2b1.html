The Great Wall of China

Not one wall, but many — built and rebuilt over two thousand years.

Today: tell the story of the Great Wall of China, and talk about why people build walls. Grammar: the passive (present and past simple).

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The first wall

Many people think there is one Great Wall of China. But this is not true. There were many walls. For a long time, different parts of China built small walls to protect their land.

Then, about 2,200 years ago, one man became the first emperor of all China. His name was Qin Shi Huang. He gave a big order: join the small walls into one long wall in the north. He wanted to stop the enemies who came from the north on fast horses.

The first walls were not made of brick. They were made of earth. Workers pressed the earth down hard until it became like stone. Hundreds of thousands of workers built the wall. The work was very hard, and very sad: many workers died, far from their homes and families.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Is the Great Wall really only one wall? Explain your answer.

2. What did the first emperor, Qin Shi Huang, do?

1. No. There were many different walls. Different parts of China built their own small walls first.

2. He was the first emperor of all China. He ordered people to join the small walls into one long wall in the north, to stop enemies on horses.

Talk about it:
  • The wall is beautiful, but many workers died to build it. Can a thing be wonderful and sad at the same time?

Part 2 — Many walls, many years

The first emperor's wall did not last. Over the next 1,800 years, the wall was left, repaired, and built again many times. Each new emperor had his own enemies and his own ideas.

The wall we know today — the big grey wall of stone and brick on the green hills — is quite new. Most of it was built by the Ming emperors, between about 600 and 400 years ago. Before the Ming, people from the north (the Mongols) ruled China. The Ming did not want this to happen again, so they built a strong wall.

The wall was not only for fighting. It was also a border. Soldiers stood in tall towers and watched the land. When they saw danger, they made a big fire. The next tower saw the fire and made another fire. In this way, a warning travelled a very long way, very fast — faster than a man on a horse.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Which emperors built most of the wall we see today? Why?

2. How did soldiers send a warning a long way, very fast?

1. The Ming emperors (about 600–400 years ago). The Mongols from the north had ruled China before, and the Ming did not want this to happen again.

2. They made a big fire in a tower. The next tower saw it and made another fire, and so on — faster than a man on a horse.

Talk about it:
  • The wall was also a border, not only for fighting. Does this make it more interesting or less interesting for you?

Part 3 — The wall and its myths

People love to tell stories about the Great Wall. But some of these stories are myths — they are not true.

Here is the most famous myth: people say you can see the Great Wall from space, with your eyes. This is not true! The wall is very long, but it is only a few metres wide. It is also the same colour as the land around it. Astronauts say they cannot see it. The first Chinese astronaut in space looked, but he could not find it. Long is not the same as easy to see.

Today, most of the wall is old and broken. Slowly, it is going back into the hills. But people repaired some parts, and these parts are visited by millions of people every year. The wall was built to keep the world out. Now, it brings the world in.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Can you really see the Great Wall from space with your eyes? Why / why not?

2. What happens at the wall today?

1. No. The wall is very long, but it is thin (only a few metres wide) and the same colour as the land. Astronauts say they cannot see it.

2. Most of it is old and broken, but some parts are repaired and are visited by millions of people every year.

Talk about it:
  • Many people believe the "you can see it from space" myth, but it is not true. Why do you think some stories are so popular, even when they are wrong?

Grammar: the passive (present & past simple)

In this story, we often talk about the wall, not the people who built it. For this, we use the passive.

We make the passive with: be + past participle (the 3rd form of the verb).
Past simple passive = was / were + past participle: "The wall was built a long time ago." "Many workers were sent to the north."
Present simple passive = is / are + past participle: "It is visited by millions of people." "The towers are seen from far away."
We use the passive when the action is more important than the person who does it, or when we do not know who did it.

1. Choose the right passive form

Put the verb into the passive. Use the present or the past — read the sentence first!

1. The first walls (build) from earth, a long time ago.

2. Today, the most famous part of the wall (visit) by millions of people.

3. In the past, many workers (send) to the north to work.

1. were built (past — "a long time ago")

2. is visited (present — "today")

3. were sent (past — "in the past")

2. Make the sentence passive

Change each sentence into the passive. Keep the same time (present or past). Then read it out loud.

1. The Ming emperors built the stone wall. → The stone wall by the Ming emperors.

2. Soldiers watch the land. → The land by soldiers.

3. People repaired some parts of the wall. → Some parts of the wall .

1. was built (past simple passive)

2. is watched (present simple passive)

3. were repaired (past simple passive)
