Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous

Both tenses connect past and present, but they show different perspectives on an action.

Present Perfect

Use to show a completed action with present relevance:

NASA has detected ten large asteroids in the past year.

Scientists have brought out new research on asteroid deflection.

Present Perfect Continuous

Use to show an ongoing action from the past until now:

I've basically been spending the last 30 years studying meteorites.

NASA has been tracking this asteroid for months.

Practice: Choose the Right Form

Which verb form is correct?

1. I'm here with Chuck Larsson who's brought out / who's just been bringing out a book.

Answer: who's brought out — This is a completed action. The book is now published and available (present relevance). The Present Perfect Continuous would suggest he's still in the process of writing, which doesn't make sense here.

2. I've basically spent / I've basically been spending the last 30 years studying meteorites.

Answer: I've basically been spending — This emphasizes the ongoing, continuous nature of the action. It highlights the duration and commitment to research over 30 years.

Discourse Markers for Conversation

Use these markers to manage conversation smoothly and keep discussions organised:

To show we are considering what someone said:

Example: "Well, that's an interesting point, but..."

To show we are taking up an invitation to enter a conversation:

Example: "Right, so if we could use rockets to deflect the asteroid..."

To show we understand or agree:

Example: "OK, so the cost is the main problem with that option."

To suggest the listener is already familiar with something:

Example: "You know, we really only have a few viable strategies."

To express personal feelings or opinions:

Example: "I mean, deflecting is cheaper than evacuation."

To show what we're saying is related to the topic:

Example: "So, we need to decide which strategy is most effective."

To show we are about to disagree:

Example: "Actually, I think nuclear deflection is too risky."

To show we are getting back to the main topic:

Example: "Anyway, the key question is: which strategy can we implement?"

Modifying Adverbs with Collocations

Adverbs like "completely," "deeply," and "barely" modify adjectives and verbs. Some combinations are natural, others sound awkward:

Common Collocations

Bitterly: cold, ashamed, disappointed (but not hot)

Barely: alive, legible, comprehensible (but not healthy)

Completely: serious, open, honest, funny

Deeply: miserable, hurt, saddened, upset

Entirely: clear, convinced, satisfactory (but not clever)

Perfectly: capable, aware, reasonable (but not bad)

Tip: When describing risk or danger in the asteroid scenario, you might say:

  • "This strategy is completely viable"
  • "The cost is entirely prohibitive"
  • "We are deeply concerned about the timeline"

Think Tank Discussion: Asteroid Avoidance Strategies

You have been asked to evaluate different strategies for protecting your nation's capital from an incoming asteroid. Consider each option's viability, effectiveness, time required, and cost.

Strategy A: Kinetic Impact (Rocket Collision)

Description: Launch a rocket that collides with the asteroid to change its trajectory.

Viability: High — proven technology, successful tests

Effectiveness: Can deflect small to medium asteroids

Timeline: Possible within 10 years

Cost: Moderate — millions to billions

Strategy B: Nuclear Detonation

Description: Use a nuclear warhead to blast the asteroid off course or break it apart.

Viability: Moderate — controversial, untested at scale

Effectiveness: Very effective for large asteroids

Timeline: Could be implemented in 10 years

Cost: High — diplomatic and military implications

Strategy C: Gravitational Tractor Beam

Description: Position a spacecraft near the asteroid to gradually pull it off course using its gravitational field.

Viability: Low — experimental, requires new technology

Effectiveness: Very gentle and controlled

Timeline: Unlikely within 10 years — needs more research

Cost: Very high — cutting-edge technology

Strategy D: Evacuation

Description: Evacuate the capital and surrounding areas before impact.

Viability: High — readily available option

Effectiveness: Saves lives but doesn't prevent damage

Timeline: Can be done anytime in the 10-year period

Cost: Enormous — economic, social, logistical challenges

Check Your Understanding

What's the difference between Present Perfect and Present Perfect Continuous?

Present Perfect shows a completed action with present relevance ("I've studied meteorites"). Present Perfect Continuous emphasizes the duration and ongoing nature of the action ("I've been studying meteorites for years").

When would you use the discourse marker "I mean"?

Use "I mean" to clarify or rephrase something you just said. Example: "The cost is prohibitive. I mean, we simply don't have the budget for nuclear detonation."

What does "trajectory" mean in the context of asteroids?

Trajectory is the path or course an asteroid takes as it moves through space. Understanding the asteroid's trajectory is essential to predicting whether it will collide with Earth.

Which adverb collocation is correct: "completely viable" or "deeply viable"?

"Completely viable" is correct. "Completely" modifies adjectives that describe a state or condition (viable, possible, clear). "Deeply" modifies emotions or experiences (concerned, sorry, grateful).
