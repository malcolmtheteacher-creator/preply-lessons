Today we're working on the language of compliance jobs so you can read an advert closely, describe your experience clearly, and answer interview questions with confidence.

Why this matters

Compliance managers are in demand almost everywhere. Schools, hospitals, banks, and councils all have to prove they follow the rules, and someone has to organise that proof. People often move into the role from other countries, other sectors, and other kinds of regulatory work. The hard part is rarely the skill. It is saying the skill in the right English.

Guess first, then check

Try to explain each phrase in your own words. Then reveal the meaning.

"based in Central Services"
The head-office team that supports all the schools (finance, HR, IT, legal). The schools are the "front line"; Central Services is the support centre.
"provisions"
Places or services that provide education, not only traditional schools: for example, alternative provision for pupils who cannot attend a mainstream school.
"as required"
Whenever the work needs it. Not every week, and not never. Expect to be flexible. Similar: as needed, when necessary.
"travel across the Trust"
You will visit different sites. The job is not desk-based all week, so interviewers may ask how you manage your time and stay consistent across places.

What does a compliance manager actually do?

Most compliance roles in a school trust cover six areas. Each has its own vocabulary.

📜

Policy review

  • draft / update / approve a policy
  • keep a policy up to date with the law
  • roll out a policy to schools
🔍

Internal audit

  • carry out / respond to an audit
  • identify a control weakness
  • agree an action plan
🔒

Data protection

  • report / investigate a data breach
  • handle a subject access request
  • process personal data lawfully
🛡️

Safeguarding

  • carry out a background check
  • keep a single central record
  • raise a concern promptly
🦺

Health and safety

  • carry out a risk assessment
  • conduct a site inspection
  • log an incident
⚠️

Risk management

  • record / score / rank a risk
  • assign an owner to a risk
  • put a mitigation in place

Describing experience that doesn't match the advert exactly

Many strong candidates have done very similar work under a different system, in another sector, or in another country. The advert uses UK terms, so your job is to translate your experience, not to apologise for it.

Useful bridges

The closest UK equivalent would be…
This was equivalent to …
Although the framework was different, the principle was the same: …
I was responsible for ensuring that … met …
I worked under the oversight of …

Weak or strong?

Say out loud what is missing from the weak version before you read the strong one.

Weak
I worked in a government office checking rules.
For several years I worked for a public body that monitored organisations' compliance with national regulations. My role was to investigate possible breaches and recommend corrective action.

What improved: it names the type of organisation, the action, and the purpose.

Weak
I did many audits and found problems.
I led around thirty audits a year. When I found a weakness, I agreed an action plan with the manager responsible and checked that it had been completed.

What improved: a number, a clear process, and a result.

Now you: upgrade these sentences

Type a stronger version of each sentence. Use a bridge phrase or a specific number if you can. Then compare it with a possible model.

I helped companies follow the law.
I advised organisations on how to meet their legal obligations. I explained the requirements, reviewed their procedures, and recommended changes where they fell short.
I was in charge of a team.
I managed a team of six investigators. I set priorities, reviewed their reports, and made sure we met our statutory deadlines.
Sometimes there were problems with the rules.
When a rule was unclear or had changed, I raised it with the legal team, drafted updated guidance, and briefed the staff affected.

The cover letter: three sentences that go wrong

Each sentence contains one error that often appears in applications. Find it, type the corrected sentence, then check.

1. "I am writing for apply to the position of Compliance Manager."
I am writing to apply for the position of Compliance Manager. We use to + verb for purpose, and apply for a job.
2. "I have worked in regulatory compliance since five years."
I have worked in regulatory compliance for five years. For + a length of time; since + a starting point (since 2019).
3. "I look forward to hear from you."
I look forward to hearing from you. In look forward to, the to is a preposition, so the verb takes -ing.

A strong opening paragraph

I am writing to apply for the position of [job title]. I have [number] years of experience in [area], most recently as [role] at [type of organisation], where I was responsible for [main duty]. I am particularly drawn to this role because [specific reason connected to the organisation].
SAY: Tell me your version of this paragraph out loud. Then we'll improve it together.

A strong answer has a shape

1 · Situation
One sentence of context. "At that point, we were receiving a high number of late reports."
2 · Action
What you did, in order. "I mapped the process, identified where delays happened, and trained the team."
3 · Result
What changed. "Within six months, reports were on time 95% of the time."

Phrases that sound confident

The first thing I did was…I took the decision to…As a result,…What I learned from this was…I'd approach it by…In my experience,…
