Is the world slowly getting better, or does history just go round in circles?

Two friends argue about a new law. One says it's a disaster; the other says it's the best idea in years. After a long evening, they find a third position that keeps the best of both. About 200 years ago, a German philosopher said this is how the whole of history moves.

Today: You'll meet Hegel and his idea that history moves forward through conflict, so you can describe how things develop step by step and say whether you think the world is getting better.

Meet Hegel

Read one part at a time. After each part, close your eyes and answer the question before you check.

I

Three students in one room

Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel was born in Stuttgart in 1770. At university in Tübingen he studied religion and shared a room with two young men who also became famous: the poet Hölderlin and the philosopher Schelling. All three were excited by the French Revolution, which began in 1789, when Hegel was nineteen. For years after university he worked quietly as a private tutor, slowly building his own ideas.

Which big event excited Hegel and his friends at university?

The French Revolution, which began in 1789.
II

The world-soul on horseback

In 1806 Hegel was living in the town of Jena, finishing his first great book, the Phenomenology of Spirit. Then Napoleon's army won a battle nearby and took the town. Hegel saw Napoleon ride through the streets and called him 'the world-soul on horseback'. To Hegel, it felt as if he was watching history itself move forward. One man on a horse seemed to show that a whole age was changing.

What did Hegel call Napoleon?

'The world-soul on horseback': he felt he was watching history itself move forward.
III

Ideas that clash and grow

Hegel believed history is like a great story with a direction. One idea appears. Then an opposite idea challenges it. Out of the conflict comes something new, which keeps the best parts of both. Step by step, he said, humanity becomes more free and more aware of itself. In 1818 he became a professor in Berlin and the most famous philosopher in Germany. His ideas later inspired Karl Marx.

According to Hegel, what happens when two opposite ideas clash?

Something new comes out of the conflict, which keeps the best parts of both. That is how history moves forward.
Talk: Hegel saw Napoleon, a soldier who brought war to Europe, as a sign of history moving forward. Can something violent or painful ever lead to progress? Can you think of an example?

Key words

Each word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means. Then reveal and compare.

1. The two brothers wanted different things for the family farm, and the conflict between them lasted for years.
conflict (n) — a serious disagreement or fight between people, groups or ideas. Example: "There's a conflict between what the workers want and what the managers want."
2. My sister loves parties and I love quiet evenings at home: we're complete opposites.
opposite (n) — a person or thing that is completely different from another. Example: "Hot is the opposite of cold."
3. The change was so gradual that nobody noticed the river getting wider, centimetre by centimetre, over a hundred years.
gradual (adj) — happening slowly over a long period, not suddenly. Example: "There has been a gradual rise in prices all year."
4. After hours of talking, the neighbours finally resolved the problem of the noisy dog.
resolve (v) — to find an answer to a problem or end a disagreement. Example: "The two companies resolved their argument without going to court."
5. There is always some tension between keeping old traditions and trying new ideas.
tension (n) — a feeling of strain between people or ideas that pull in different directions. Example: "You could feel the tension in the room before the results were announced."
6. Over the centuries, the small fishing village evolved into a busy modern port.
evolve (v) — to develop slowly and change into something more advanced. Example: "Mobile phones have evolved from simple calling devices into small computers."
7. The final plan was a synthesis of both proposals: it took the best part of each and joined them together.
synthesis (n) — a new whole made by combining different ideas or things. Example: "Her cooking is a synthesis of Indian and Italian traditions."

The idea: The dialectic: ideas that clash and grow

1
IdeaOne idea or way of life appears: people must obey the king.
2
ChallengeAn opposite idea pushes back: people should rule themselves.
3
ConflictThe two clash, and the tension cannot last.
4
Something newOut of this comes a new idea that keeps the best of both.

See it in action

Here is the idea in action. In a family, the first rule is: "Children must obey their parents." As the children grow up, an opposite idea appears: "Teenagers should decide for themselves." The arguments start, and neither side can win completely. Step by step, something new comes out of the conflict: the teenagers make more of their own choices, but the family talks big decisions through together. Nobody simply defeated the other side. Both ideas were kept, changed and lifted to a higher level. People often call these steps thesis, antithesis and synthesis.

Your turn

Now you: explain Hegel's idea to a friend who has never heard of him. Speak for about a minute. Use the starters if they help.

Hegel believed that history...First, one idea appears, then...Out of this conflict comes...So, step by step, ...
Hegel believed that history is not random: it is going somewhere. First, one idea appears, then an opposite idea challenges it and the two clash. Out of this conflict comes something new, which keeps the best parts of both. For example, the idea of obeying kings met the idea of freedom, and gradually we got parliaments and laws that protect both order and freedom. So, step by step, he thought humanity becomes more free.

Connect

Compare

Heraclitus and Hegel: Heraclitus said everything flows and changes, and that conflict is the father of all things. Hegel also saw change and conflict everywhere, but he added one thing: a direction. What is the difference between saying 'everything changes' and 'everything is going somewhere'?
For Heraclitus, the world is like a river: always changing, but not necessarily improving. For Hegel, change has a purpose: each conflict leads to something higher, so history moves towards more freedom. One sees endless flow; the other sees a story with a direction.

Remember earlier thinkers

No looking back. Answer from memory first, then check.

From Immanuel Kant

What is Kant's test for right and wrong?

Before you act, ask: could I want everyone to do this? If the action would stop working if everyone did it, it's wrong. And never treat people only as tools.
From David Hume

Where does our belief in cause and effect come from, according to Hume?

From habit. We see one thing follow another many times, so we expect it again, but we never see the cause itself and cannot prove it.
From Thomas Hobbes

Why, according to Hobbes, do people agree to be ruled?

Because life without government would be "nasty, brutish and short", so we give up some freedom to a strong ruler in return for peace and safety.
From Boethius

What did Lady Philosophy teach Boethius about Fortune?

That Fortune is a turning wheel: it gives and takes away money, power and fame, but it can never take what is inside you, like your mind and your goodness.
