The Gladiators

The fighters of ancient Rome — feared, but loved by the crowd.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — Who the fighters were

A gladiator was a fighter in ancient Rome. The word comes from the Latin word gladius, which means a short sword.

Most gladiators did not choose this life. Many were slaves. Some were prisoners from wars. Some were criminals. A few men were free and chose to fight — for money or for fame. But all of them belonged to an owner. They were not free.

The fighters lived and trained in a special school. Life there was hard, but the men were not treated badly all the time. A good fighter cost a lot of money, so the owner did not want him to die. The men ate good food, doctors looked after them, and they trained for many hours every day. Different gladiators used different weapons. One man had a big shield and a sword. Another man had only a net and a long spear, like a fisherman. This made the fights more interesting to watch.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did most gladiators come from? Were they free?

2. Why did the men get good food and doctors?

1. Most were slaves, prisoners from wars, or criminals. A few were free men who chose to fight. But they all belonged to an owner — they were not free.

2. A good fighter cost a lot of money, so the owner wanted to keep him strong and alive.

Talk about it:
  • Some free men chose to fight, even though it was very dangerous. Why do you think they did this?

Part 2 — The games and the crowd

The fights were a big public show. Thousands of people came to watch. In Rome, they built a huge building called the Colosseum. It could hold tens of thousands of people. A day at the games had animal hunts in the morning and the gladiators in the afternoon.

The games were very expensive. Rich and powerful men paid for them. Why? Because a happy crowd was useful. If you gave the people a good show, they liked you. And if they liked you, they would help you in politics. People said the Romans only wanted two things: "bread and circuses" — free food and a good show.

The best fighters became famous, like film stars today. People wrote their names on walls. They painted their faces on cups and lamps. A winning gladiator could get money. After many years, a great fighter could even win his freedom. So a man who started as a slave could sometimes become free and famous.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did rich men pay so much money for the games?

2. What good things could a winning gladiator get?

1. A good show made the crowd happy. A happy crowd liked you and helped you in politics. People called this "bread and circuses".

2. Money, fame (people wrote their names on walls and painted their faces), and sometimes his freedom.

Talk about it:
  • "Bread and circuses" means leaders keep people happy with food and shows. Do you think this still happens today?

Part 3 — The myth and the revolt

Many people think that every fight ended in death. This is a myth — it is not true. Fighters cost a lot of money, so the owner did not want them to die. Many fights stopped when one man was hurt or said "stop". The crowd often let a brave loser live. Yes, gladiators did die sometimes. But it was not every fight.

You may know the picture of the emperor putting his thumb down to kill a fighter. We are not really sure this is true. It comes mostly from an old painting, not from Roman times. We do not even know which way the thumb pointed!

But one thing is true: sometimes the fighters fought back. In 73 BC, a gladiator called Spartacus and about seventy other men escaped from their school. More and more slaves joined them. Soon they had an army of thousands. For two years, they beat the Roman soldiers again and again. But in the end, a Roman general called Crassus stopped them. Spartacus died in the last big fight. Today, people still remember his name. He is a symbol of slaves who said "no" to their masters.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why is "every fight ended in death" a myth?

2. Who was Spartacus, and what did he do?

1. Fighters cost a lot of money, so owners did not want them to die. Many fights stopped when a man was hurt, and a brave loser was often allowed to live.

2. Spartacus was a gladiator. In 73 BC he and about 70 men escaped. They built an army of thousands and beat the Romans for two years, until a general called Crassus stopped them.

Talk about it:
  • The exciting story (every fight to the death, the thumbs-down emperor) is not really true. Why do you think people still believe it?

Grammar: the passive (present & past simple)

This story is about people that things were done to. The men were captured. They were trained. They were watched. For this, we use the passive.

How we make it: a form of be + the past participle (the "third form" of the verb, like trained, seen, paid).
• Present: "Gladiators are remembered today."
• Past: "Gladiators were trained in schools."
Why we use it: when the action is more important than the person who did it, or when we do not know who did it. If we want to say who, we add by: "the games were paid for by rich men."

1. Make it passive — past simple

Change each sentence to the passive. Put the underlined words first. Keep it in the past.

1. Roman soldiers captured many fighters. → Many fighters by Roman soldiers.

2. The school trained the gladiators. → The gladiators in the school.

3. Rich men paid for the games. → The games by rich men.

1. were captured

2. were trained

3. were paid for (the word "for" stays with the verb)

2. Present or past passive?

Put the verb in the passive. Choose present or past from the sentence. Then read it out loud.

1. Today, the gladiators (remember) in films and books.

2. Long ago, many gladiators (force) to fight.

3. The Colosseum (build) in Rome about 2,000 years ago.

1. are remembered (present — "today")

2. were forced (past — "long ago")

3. was built (past — one building, so "was")
