How Do People Really Spend Their Time?

📱 The average person checks their phone 96 times per day!

Meet Sarah, a teacher from Australia. Every morning, she wakes up at 6:30 AM. But before getting out of bed, she spends 15 minutes checking her phone. Then she makes coffee, gets ready, and drives to work. At work, she's very busy, but she still checks her phone about 40 times! After work, she goes home, makes dinner, watches TV, and checks her phone another 40 times before sleeping.

Now meet Miguel, a designer from Spain. His day is very different. He wakes up at 9:00 AM, works from home, takes a 2-hour lunch break, and finishes work at 7:00 PM. He checks his phone less than Sarah - only about 50 times a day. But he spends 3 hours every evening cooking and eating dinner with his family.

⏰ Most people spend 2 hours and 25 minutes on their phone every day!

Researchers studied daily routines in 15 different countries. They found some interesting facts:

  • People in Japan sleep only 6.5 hours per night (the least in the world)
  • People in France spend the most time eating - 2 hours and 13 minutes per day
  • People in the Netherlands exercise the most - almost 1 hour per day
  • Americans work the longest hours - an average of 8.8 hours per day

Think About Your Day:

  • What time do you usually wake up?
  • How many times do you check your phone each day?
  • What takes the most time in your daily routine?
  • Is your routine different on weekends?

5 Phrases for Talking About Daily Life

Click each phrase to see examples and try using it yourself!

1. "I usually..."
Formality: Neutral (use with anyone)
Meaning: Use this to talk about things you do most days, your regular habits and routines. It shows something happens 80-90% of the time.
Example from Sarah's story:
"I usually wake up at 6:30 AM."
"I usually check my phone before breakfast."
NOW YOU TRY:
Complete this sentence: "I usually _______ in the morning."
(Example: have coffee, take a shower, check my email)
2. "I tend to..."
Formality: Neutral to Formal
Meaning: Similar to "usually" but shows it's your natural behavior or habit. It's a bit more thoughtful and reflective.
Example:
"I tend to skip breakfast when I'm busy."
"Miguel tends to work late in the evening."
NOW YOU TRY:
What do you tend to do when you're stressed or tired? Tell your teacher: "I tend to _______"
(Example: eat snacks, watch TV, go to bed early)
3. "I can't get through the day without..."
Formality: Informal (friendly conversations)
Meaning: Something is so important to you that you need it every day. It shows something is essential for you.
Example:
"Sarah can't get through the day without her morning coffee."
"I can't get through the day without checking social media."
NOW YOU TRY:
What's something you really need every day? Say: "I can't get through the day without _______"
(Example: coffee, music, talking to my family, exercise)

More Phrases for Daily Routines

4. "I make time for..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: You choose to do something even when you're busy because it's important to you. You organize your schedule around it.
Example:
"Miguel makes time for cooking dinner with his family every evening."
"I make time for exercise three times a week."
NOW YOU TRY:
What do you always make time for, no matter how busy you are? Tell your teacher: "I make time for _______"
(Example: reading, calling my parents, walking, watching my favorite show)
5. "I've gotten into the habit of..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: You started doing something regularly, and now it's part of your routine. This can be a good habit or a bad habit!
Example:
"I've gotten into the habit of checking my phone first thing in the morning."
"She's gotten into the habit of taking a walk after lunch."
NOW YOU TRY:
What new habit have you started recently (good or bad)? Say: "I've gotten into the habit of _______"
(Example: going to bed late, drinking more water, watching videos before sleep)

Your 5 Phrases for Today:

  • "I usually..." — for regular routines (80-90% of the time)
  • "I tend to..." — for natural behaviors and patterns
  • "I can't get through the day without..." — for essential daily things
  • "I make time for..." — for things you prioritize
  • "I've gotten into the habit of..." — for new routines you've started
