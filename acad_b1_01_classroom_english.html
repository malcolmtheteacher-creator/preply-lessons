Classroom English: Asking for Help

Master essential phrases to ask questions, request clarification, and participate confidently in class

5:00

Asking for Help

Polite Questions

Could you explain that again, please? — Use when you don't understand something

Can I ask a question? — Permission to ask

What does this word mean? — Direct question about vocabulary

Could you speak more slowly, please? — Help with comprehension

Is this correct? — Check your understanding

Requesting Clarification

When You Don't Understand

I don't understand. — Clear, direct statement

Could you give me an example? — Ask for more information

Do you mean...? — Check your understanding

Can you repeat that, please? — Standard request

I'm not sure about this part. — Point to the specific problem

Participating in Discussion

Speaking Up in Class

I have a question. — Attract attention first

In my opinion... — Give your view

Can I add something? — Contribute to discussion

What do you think about...? — Ask another student

I disagree because... — Present different view

Essential Vocabulary

Clarify Make something clear or easier to understand
Participate Take part in an activity or discussion
Contribution Something you add to a discussion or project
Comprehension Understanding of what you hear or read
Confident Feeling sure and not nervous
Polite Respectful and considerate in manner
Emphasise Give special importance or attention to something
Peer Another student at your level; classmate

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: Complete the Dialogue

You didn't understand what the teacher said. Write a request for clarification:

"The teacher said: 'Today we will analyse the role of symbolism in modern poetry.'"

Your question: ___________________

Possible answers: "Could you explain what symbolism means?" or "I don't understand. What is symbolism?"

Exercise 2: Match Situations to Phrases

Situation: You want to give your opinion in a discussion.

What do you say? ___________________

"In my opinion..." or "I think..." or "Can I add something?"

Exercise 3: Role Play Scenario

You are in a lecture. The teacher speaks very quickly and you cannot follow. Write what you would say to ask for help.

"Could you speak more slowly, please?" or "I'm having difficulty following. Could you repeat that?"

Classroom Dialogue

Sample Conversation

Student: "Can I ask a question?"

Teacher: "Of course."

Student: "What does 'analyse' mean in this context?"

Teacher: "Good question. 'Analyse' means to examine something carefully and explain the different parts."

Student: "Could you give me an example?"

Teacher: "Certainly. For instance, if I ask you to analyse a poem, you should look at the language, the images, and the message."

Student: "I think I understand now. Thank you."

Key Takeaways

✓ Always use polite phrases when asking questions

✓ Don't be afraid to ask for clarification

✓ Active participation shows you are engaged

✓ Teachers appreciate students who ask thoughtful questions

✓ Confidence comes from practice — keep speaking!
