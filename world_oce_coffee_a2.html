The Flat White and the Café: A Surprising Coffee Culture

Australia and New Zealand love good coffee. Migrants started the café culture.

Key words

Read the sentence. What does the bold word mean? Then click Reveal.

1. I drink a cup of coffee in the morning.
coffee (n) — a hot, dark drink made from beans. Example: “I drink a cup of coffee in the morning.”
2. A flat white has a lot of milk.
milk (n) — a white drink from a cow. Example: “A flat white has a lot of milk.”
3. The café is busy at breakfast.
café (n) — a small place where people drink coffee. Example: “The café is busy at breakfast.”
4. She has a piece of cake with her coffee.
cake (n) — a sweet food baked in an oven. Example: “She has a piece of cake with her coffee.”
5. A migrant opened the first espresso bar.
migrant (n) — a person who moves to a new country. Example: “A migrant opened the first espresso bar.”
6. I order a flat white every day.
order (v) — to ask for food or a drink in a café. Example: “I order a flat white every day.”

The stereotype: “Australians only drink beer and eat meat pies.”

The story

Part 1 — Good coffee

Many visitors expect only beer and meat pies in Australia and New Zealand. But the coffee is very good in Melbourne, Sydney and Wellington. People queue for their favourite café. They know the baristas by name. A famous drink is the flat white. It has coffee and soft milk.

Quick check:

1. Where is the coffee very good?

2. What is a flat white?

1. In Melbourne, Sydney and Wellington.

2. Coffee with soft milk.

Discuss:
  • Do you have a favourite café?

Part 2 — Migrants and coffee

After the Second World War, many people from Italy and Greece moved to Australia. They brought their coffee and café life. In 1954, a man opened Pellegrini's Espresso Bar in Melbourne. It had one of the first coffee machines. The bar is still open today. Melbourne now has many different foods from around the world.

Quick check:

1. Where did the migrants come from?

2. When did Pellegrini's open?

1. Italy and Greece.

2. In 1954.

Discuss:
  • Which foods in your town come from other countries?

Part 3 — Who invented it?

Australia and New Zealand both say they invented the flat white. Nobody knows who is right. Today, people drink flat whites in London, New York and Tokyo. In the café, you can say: “A flat white, please” or “Can I have a piece of cake?” Some baristas will draw a picture in the milk.

Quick check:

1. Which two countries say they invented the flat white?

2. What can you say in a café?

1. Australia and New Zealand.

2. A flat white, please.

Discuss:
  • What do you say when you order a drink?

Grammar — a cup of, a piece of and some

Uncountable things need a container word: a cup of coffee, a glass of milk, a piece of cake.
Use some in positive sentences: “I'd like some milk.”
Ordering: “Can I have a flat white, please?”
