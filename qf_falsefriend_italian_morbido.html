☁️ Morbido ≠ Morbid

B1 • Italian speakers • False friends: morbido (soft) ≠ morbid (macabre)

🔍 Let's notice what we see

Look at this sentence. What's the problem?

"This pillow is very morbid."

(The speaker wants to say the pillow feels nice and soft, not that it's creepy!)

Does this sentence sound strange? What's happening here?

⚠️ The False Friend:

The Italian word "morbido" looks like English "morbid", but they mean DIFFERENT things!

In Italian: morbido = soft, gentle (morbido al tatto = soft to touch)

In English: morbid = related to death/disease, unhealthy fascination. "A morbid curiosity about death."

In English: soft = gentle, not hard (soft pillow, soft fabric)

🛠️ Understand the difference

📊 Italian morbido vs English morbid:

🛋️ Italian: MORBIDO

  • Soft pillow
  • Soft fabric
  • Soft to the touch
  • Gentle and smooth

💀 English: MORBID

  • Morbid curiosity
  • Morbid thoughts
  • Morbid fascination
  • Dark and unhealthy

🚨 Context matters

Watch for these contexts:

morbido → English: "soft"
Example: "Il tessuto è molto morbido" = "The fabric is very soft" ✓
NOT "The fabric is very morbid" ✗
morbid → Italian: "macabro/lugubre"
Example: "He has a morbid fascination with horror" = "Ha una curiosità macabra per l'orrore" ✓
