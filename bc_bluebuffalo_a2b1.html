The Promise on the Bag

A family made a pet food with one big promise on the label: “no by-product meal.” Then a competitor took them to court. What happens to a company when one promise is wrong?

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then answer the questions before you read the next part.

Part 1 — A dog called Blue

In 2003, a family in the United States, the Bishops, started a pet food company. They called it Blue Buffalo.

The idea came from their dog. His name was Blue, and he was very ill with cancer. The family wanted the best food for him. They read the labels of many pet foods, and they did not like some ingredients. One of them was “by-product meal” — a cheap powder made from parts of an animal.

So they made a different food. It was natural, with real meat as the first ingredient. And on every bag, there was a big promise: no chicken by-product meal, no corn, no wheat, no soy.

The food cost more than other brands. But many owners were happy to pay. They trusted the promise — and they loved their pets.

Blue Buffalo grew very fast and became one of the best-known pet food brands in the country.

True or false? Click your answer.

1. The company was started in 2003.

Talk about it
  • Would you pay more for pet food with a clear promise on the bag? Why or why not?
  • Why do you think the family used their dog’s story in their marketing?

Part 2 — The test

In May 2014, a big competitor, Purina, started a lawsuit against Blue Buffalo.

Purina said: “Some Blue Buffalo food contains chicken by-product meal. The promise on the bag is not true.” Purina did laboratory tests on the food and took the results to court.

Blue Buffalo was in a very difficult position. A supplier had sent it the wrong ingredients and, it said, had not told the truth about them. The company did not know. It stopped buying from that supplier.

But for the customers, that did not matter. The label on the bag said “no by-product meal”, and it was not always true.

Some owners were angry. They started their own lawsuit. In the end, Blue Buffalo paid about thirty-two million dollars to settle it.

True or false? Click your answer.

1. Purina is a competitor of Blue Buffalo.

2. Blue Buffalo knew about the wrong ingredients.

3. Some customers also started a lawsuit.

Talk about it
  • Blue Buffalo says it did not know. Is it still responsible for the label? Why or why not?
  • How would you feel if you found a wrong ingredient in your food?

Part 3 — Can you trust a promise?

In November 2016, Purina and Blue Buffalo agreed to end their lawsuits. The details are secret.

The story did not end the company. Many customers kept buying Blue Buffalo. In 2018, a big food company, General Mills, bought it for about eight billion dollars.

But the story still teaches a hard lesson. A strong promise can win trust very fast — and one mistake can put that trust in danger.

The bigger the promise on the bag, the more you must check. A good company tests its ingredients and visits its suppliers. A smart customer reads the label — and asks questions.

So: was Blue Buffalo’s promise a clever idea, or a dangerous bet?

What is in the food? contains / no / not any / without

Labels say what is inside a product — and what is not inside. We use no, not any and without for this.

Something is inside: This biscuit contains chicken. / This biscuit is made with chicken.

Something is not inside — use no OR not any (same meaning):
This biscuit contains no corn.
This biscuit doesn’t contain any corn.

Never use both: This biscuit doesn’t contain no corn. ✗ — one negative is enough!

without + noun means “not having”: I like tea without sugar. / a food without artificial colours

Remember the -s: It contains chicken. (he / she / it) but The biscuits contain chicken.

Speaking

Take your time. Short sentences are fine.

Time: 3:00
1 · Tell the story

Tell me the story in your own words. Six or seven sentences is enough.

Use these: The Bishops started…Their promise was…In 2014, Purina…A supplier…The customers felt…In the end…
2 · Read the label

Here is a label: NATURAL DOG BISCUITS — contains chicken, rice and carrots. No corn. No soy. No artificial colours.

Tell me what is in the biscuits and what is not. Then say: what would you check before you believe this label?

Use these: It contains…It doesn’t contain any…There is no…I would ask the company to…
3 · A promise you know

Think of a product you buy: food, a drink, a phone, clothes.

What does the company promise on the box or in its advertising? Do you believe it? Why or why not?

Use these: The box says…They promise that…I trust / don’t trust it because…
4 · The difficult call

Role play. Person A is a customer. Person B works for the company.

A: Your test found a wrong ingredient in the food, but the label says it is not inside. You are angry. Ask for an answer.

B: Say sorry, explain what happened and say what you will do now.

Use these: I’m very sorry about…It was a mistake. Our supplier…We have stopped…From now on, we will test…
