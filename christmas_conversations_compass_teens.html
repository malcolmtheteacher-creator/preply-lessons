Why Adult Conversations Are So... Painful

💀 Sound familiar? "So, what subjects are you taking?" "What do you want to do after school?" "When I was YOUR age..." "Are you seeing anyone?" "You've grown so much!"

Every Christmas, you face the same torture: relatives you barely know asking the SAME boring questions, expecting you to perform like you're in a job interview.

Here's the thing though — according to Harvard behavioral scientist Alison Wood Brooks, it's not entirely their fault. Most people are just... really bad at conversations. They get stuck asking the same boring questions because they don't know any better.

The good news? You can actually steer these conversations somewhere less painful. And maybe — just maybe — have some that are actually interesting.

The Problem: Most adults are stuck in the INTERROGATION quadrant — "What subjects? What grades? What plans?" It feels like a job interview, not a conversation.

Your Options:

  • Escape to the bathroom every 10 minutes
  • OR: Learn to steer conversations to CONNECTION — where they're actually enjoyable

Escape Routes: Moving OUT of Interrogation

These expressions help you redirect boring conversations into something more interesting — without being rude. Adults actually appreciate it when you make the conversation better.

Click each one to see how it works:

1. "Actually, I've been really into..."
→ Moves to CONNECTION Formality: Casual
Why it works: Instead of just answering their boring question, you pivot to something YOU actually want to talk about. This takes control of the conversation in a natural way.
Example:
Aunt: "So what are you studying?"
You: "Math, English, Science..." (dies inside)
Better: "The usual subjects, but actually, I've been really into photography lately — I started editing my own stuff."
NOW YOU TRY: Someone asks "How's school going?" Redirect to something you're actually interested in.
2. "What about you? What are you into these days?"
→ Moves to CONNECTION Formality: Casual/Friendly
Why it works: Flip the script! Adults almost NEVER get asked about themselves by teenagers. This surprises them (in a good way) and takes the spotlight off you.
Example:
Uncle: "So what do you want to do after school?"
You: "Um... I don't know yet?" (awkward silence)
Better: "Honestly, I'm still figuring it out. What about you — what are you into these days? Any hobbies?"
NOW YOU TRY: A relative finishes asking you questions. Flip it back to them.
3. "Tell me more about that..."
→ Keeps them talking (less work for you!) Formality: Universal
Why it works: Harvard research found this is literally the most powerful phrase in conversation. When you say this, people like you more AND they keep talking — which means you don't have to.
Example:
Uncle: "I've been getting into woodworking."
You: "Cool." (conversation dies)
Better: "Really? Tell me more about that — what kind of stuff do you make?"
NOW YOU TRY: Your aunt mentions she just came back from a trip. Use this phrase.

Pro Tip: The Flip

Adults expect to interrogate YOU. When you ask THEM questions, it completely changes the dynamic. Suddenly you're not the awkward teenager being examined — you're someone they want to impress.

Try it. It's kind of satisfying.

Staying Chill When It Gets Awkward

Sometimes adults say things that are... a lot. Maybe they give unsolicited advice, share a "hot take," or start the "when I was your age" speech. These phrases help you stay chill without being rude.

Click each one to see how it works:

4. "That's an interesting perspective..."
→ Keeps things warm (without agreeing) Formality: Polite/Neutral
Why it works: This acknowledges what they said WITHOUT agreeing or disagreeing. It buys you time and keeps them from getting defensive. Works great for "hot takes" you don't want to fight about.
Example:
Grandpa: "Young people today are always on their phones. They don't know how to talk anymore."
You: "That's not true!" (argument starts)
Better: "That's an interesting perspective. What was it like before phones though? How did you stay in touch with friends?"
NOW YOU TRY: Someone says "Your generation has it too easy." Respond without starting a fight.
5. "Going back to what you mentioned earlier..."
→ Redirects to safer ground Formality: Neutral
Why it works: This is a "callback" — you reference something from earlier in the conversation. Perfect for escaping awkward topics and showing you were actually listening (adults love that).
Example:
The conversation has somehow turned to politics and it's getting weird...
"Oh hey — going back to what you mentioned earlier about your new dog, what breed is it? I've been thinking about getting one."
NOW YOU TRY: Someone mentioned earlier they're learning to cook. The conversation has now turned awkward. Redirect!

All 5 Navigation Phrases:

  • "Actually, I've been really into..." — Pivot to what YOU want to talk about
  • "What about you? What are you into these days?" — Flip the script
  • "Tell me more about that..." — Keep them talking (less work for you)
  • "That's an interesting perspective..." — Acknowledge without agreeing
  • "Going back to what you mentioned..." — Escape to safer topics
🧠 Science Hack: Nervous Before a Party? Your body can't tell the difference between anxiety and excitement — both feel the same (racing heart, sweaty palms, etc.). Instead of telling yourself "calm down," tell yourself "I am excited." Sounds dumb, but research says it actually works.

🎯 Practice: Navigate These Scenarios

Your mission: Take a stuck or awkward conversation and steer it somewhere better. Choose a scenario, then practice navigating it!

Scenario 1: The Grade Interrogation
Aunt you barely see: "So! How are your grades? What subjects are you best at? What do you want to study at university?"
Your Challenge: Answer briefly, then redirect. Flip the script — ask HER something, or pivot to something you actually care about.
Scenario 2: The "When I Was Your Age" Speech
Uncle: "When I was your age, we didn't have phones. We actually talked to each other! Kids today don't know how good they have it."
Your Challenge: Don't argue. Use "That's an interesting perspective" and follow up with a genuine question about what life was like then.
Scenario 3: The Awkward Cousin
You're stuck at the "kids table" with a cousin you haven't seen in years. You have nothing in common. Silence is deafening.
Your Challenge: Use a question from the Question Bank to break the ice. Keep them talking with follow-up questions.
Scenario 4: The Escape
The conversation has somehow turned to something really awkward — maybe politics, or someone's divorce, or something you really don't want to discuss.
Your Challenge: Use a callback to redirect. "Hey, going back to what you said about [something from earlier]..."
