Assessment: Building an Ethical Framework

What are your ethical principles?

Knowledge Check

Answer the following questions to show your understanding:

Question 1: Define the three ethical frameworks we've studied (consequentialism, deontology, virtue ethics) and give an example of each.
Question 2: What are the Five Precepts in Buddhism, and what virtue or value does each one promote?
Question 3: What is Shariah in Islam, and what are its four main sources?
Question 4: What is the difference between a religious and a humanist approach to ethics?
Question 5: Define virtue ethics and give three virtues that most traditions value.

Scenario Analysis

Read this scenario and analyse it from different ethical perspectives:

The Scenario: Your friend is struggling financially. A large company near your school is hiring, but the company has documented a history of poor environmental practices and worker exploitation. Your friend really needs the job. Should they take it?
Question 6: How would a consequentialist analyse this?

Hint: What outcomes matter? How would they weigh the friend's financial need against environmental and worker harm?

Question 7: How would a deontologist analyse this?

Hint: What duties or rules are relevant? Is it wrong to work for an unethical company?

Question 8: How would a virtue ethicist analyse this?

Hint: What virtues (like integrity, compassion, courage) are in tension here?

Notice how the same situation looks different depending on the framework you use. This is why ethics is complex—we care about outcomes, rules, and character.

Building Your Own Ethical Framework

Now it's time to synthesise what you've learned and develop your own approach to ethics.

Question 9: Of the frameworks we've studied (consequentialism, deontology, virtue ethics, religious approaches, humanism), which do you find most convincing and why?

You don't have to pick just one. You might blend approaches. Explain your reasoning.

Question 10: What are your core ethical principles?

For example: "I believe honesty is important because...", "I think we have a duty to...", "I value compassion because..."

Question 11: Can someone who doesn't believe in God still be ethical? Explain your answer.
Question 12: Reflection: What's the most important thing you've learned from this unit about ethics?
Challenge extension: Think of an ethical dilemma in your own life (big or small). How would you approach it using the frameworks you've learned? What principles would guide you?
