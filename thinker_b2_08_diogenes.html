Meet Diogenes

Read one part at a time. After each part, close your eyes and answer the question before you check.

🪙

Forced to leave home

Diogenes was born around 412 BC in Sinope, a Greek city on the Black Sea coast. His father was a banker. According to ancient writers, there was a scandal about the city's coins, which were perhaps damaged or faked, and Diogenes had to leave. He went to Athens with almost nothing. Later, when people laughed at him for being sent away, he is said to have answered that this was how he became a philosopher.

Why did Diogenes have to leave Sinope?

Because of a scandal about the city's coins. He went to Athens with almost nothing.
🏺

Life in a jar

In Athens, Diogenes lived in a large clay jar, the kind used to store wine or oil. He owned a cloak, a stick and a bowl. One day, the story goes, he saw a boy drinking water from his hands, and he threw his bowl away: a child had shown him that even a bowl was too much. People called him 'the dog' because he ate and slept in public. He was proud of the name, and his followers became the Cynics, from the Greek word for dog.

Why did Diogenes throw away his bowl?

Because he saw a boy drinking from his hands and realised that he didn't even need a bowl.
☀️

Stand out of my sunlight

Diogenes loved to shock people into thinking. He is said to have walked through Athens in the daytime carrying a lamp, saying, 'I am looking for an honest man.' In the most famous story, Alexander the Great, the most powerful man in the world, came to meet him and offered him anything he wanted. Diogenes, lying in the sun, answered: 'Stand out of my sunlight.' He died in Corinth around 323 BC.

What did Diogenes ask Alexander the Great for?

Nothing, except that he should move: 'Stand out of my sunlight.'
Talk: Diogenes broke rules to make people think, but he also made many people angry and uncomfortable. Is shocking people a good way to change their minds? When does it work, and when does it fail?

Key words

Each word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means. Then reveal and compare.

1. There's no law against wearing pyjamas to the office; it's just a convention that we don't.
convention (n) — a way of behaving that most people in a society accept as normal, although it is not a law. Example: "In many countries, people shake hands when they meet someone for the first time; it's a strong social convention."
2. He refused to conform: while everyone else wore dark suits, he came in a bright yellow jumper.
conform (v) — to behave in the way that most people, or the rules, expect. Example: "Teenagers often feel pressure to conform and dress like their friends."
3. She took the job for the status: a big office and an important title, even though the pay was low.
status (n) — the position or importance a person has in society compared with others. Example: "For some people, an expensive car is mainly about status, not about driving."
4. After the fire, his only possessions were the clothes he was wearing and his phone.
possession (n) — something that you own. Example: "When they moved abroad, they sold most of their possessions and took only two suitcases."
5. Living alone on the island, she had to be self-sufficient: she fixed, cooked and built everything on her own.
self-sufficient (adj) — able to get everything you need without help from other people. Example: "The family grows its own vegetables and is almost self-sufficient."
6. The other children mocked his old shoes, laughing and pointing every time he walked past.
mock (v) — to laugh at someone or something in an unkind way, often by copying them. Example: "The comedian mocked politicians who promise everything and do nothing."
7. Wearing a heavy suit and tie on a 40-degree day seemed completely absurd to him.
absurd (adj) — so silly or unreasonable that it is almost funny. Example: "It's absurd that a small bottle of water costs more at the airport than a whole meal at home."

The idea: Living by nature, not by convention

👀
NoticeSpot a rule that everyone follows without thinking.
❓
QuestionAsk: who says so, and why?
🌿
CompareIs it a real need of nature, or just a habit of society?
✂️
Drop itIf there's no good reason for it, live without it.

See it in action

Here is the idea in action. Everyone at your office says you need the newest phone. Diogenes asks: "Why should I? Who says my old phone isn't good enough?" You check: it makes calls, sends messages and takes photos. The only thing a new phone would add is status. So by nature you don't need it; the 'need' comes from convention. Diogenes would keep the old phone, or throw it away completely, and enjoy the money and the freedom.

Your turn

Now you: explain Diogenes' idea to a friend who loves shopping. Speak for about a minute. Use the starters if they help.

Diogenes believed that...He divided our needs into...For example, he threw away...His point was that freedom means...
Diogenes believed that most of the rules and things we worry about are just conventions. He divided our needs into real needs, like food and shelter, and invented ones, like status and fashion. For example, he threw away his only bowl when he saw a boy drinking from his hands. His point was that freedom means needing as little as possible, so that nothing and nobody can control you.

Thought experiment: The rule-free week

Imagine you could spend one week completely free of social conventions: no dress code, no polite small talk, no rules about when or where to eat. Nothing illegal, and nobody would remember it afterwards. Or you could spend the week exactly as normal.

Now give two reasons for your choice. Then argue for the other option for thirty seconds. Which conventions would you miss most, and why?

Connect

Compare

Socrates and Diogenes: Socrates questioned people in the market but still lived as a citizen, with family, friends and duties. Diogenes questioned the whole city and lived outside its rules. Plato is said to have called him 'a Socrates gone mad'. Was Diogenes taking Socrates' idea further, or too far?
Both believed you should examine your life and not just follow the crowd. Socrates used questions and stayed inside society; Diogenes used shocking actions and rejected society's rules almost completely. You could say he turned Socrates' questions into a whole way of life.
Plato and Diogenes: Plato is said to have defined a human being as 'an animal with two legs and no feathers'. Diogenes brought him a chicken with its feathers pulled out and said, 'Here is Plato's human!' What does this story show about the difference between them?
Plato built careful theories and definitions in his school; Diogenes tested ideas against real life and laughed at theories that forgot it. For Diogenes, philosophy was something you live and show, not just something you define.

Remember earlier thinkers

No looking back. Answer from memory first, then check.

From Aristotle

What is Aristotle's golden mean?

The idea that a virtue, a good quality, is the middle point between two extremes, too much and too little. For example, courage lies between cowardice and recklessness.
From Socrates

Why did the oracle call Socrates the wisest man in Athens?

Because he knew that he knew nothing, while others believed they knew things they didn't.
From Thales

What new question did Thales ask about the world?

Not who made the world, but what it is made of. He looked for a natural answer instead of a story about the gods, and his answer was water.

Final review

From memory, out loud. Then check.

1. What did Diogenes believe we need in order to be free?

Very little: only what nature really requires, like food, water and shelter. Most other 'needs' are just conventions, and they make us less free.

2. What did Diogenes say when Alexander the Great offered him anything he wanted?

'Stand out of my sunlight.'

3. Why were Diogenes and his followers called Cynics?

From the Greek word for 'dog', because he ate and slept in public like a dog, and he was proud of the name.

4. What does 'convention' mean? Give an example.

A way of behaving that most people accept as normal, although it isn't a law. For example, shaking hands when you meet someone.
