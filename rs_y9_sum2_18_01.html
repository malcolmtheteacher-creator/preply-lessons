Before you start GCSE RS, let's remind ourselves of the big questions you explored last term. These are the foundations of religious studies.

Question 1: What is meant by the term "meaning"?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Meaning is the sense of purpose or significance in life — what makes life feel worthwhile or important to you.

Question 2: What do some people believe happens after death?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Some believe in an afterlife (heaven, reincarnation, etc.), while others believe death is final. Different religions and philosophies offer different answers.

Question 3: Why do you think people ask questions about death and the afterlife?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Because death is universal and mysterious, people naturally seek comfort, understanding, and hope about what happens next.

Question 4: How can studying Religion help you understand different perspectives on life?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Religion reveals how different cultures and communities find meaning, value, and answers to big questions — it builds empathy and critical thinking.

What is AQA GCSE RS A (8062)?

AQA GCSE Religious Studies A is a 2-year exam course (Years 10–11) that teaches the beliefs, practices, and teachings of Christianity and Islam, alongside four philosophical and ethical themes that apply to all religions.

You'll study how religions answer big life questions: What is God? Why do we suffer? What is right and wrong? How should we live together?

The Two Papers

Paper 1: Religion (1 hour 45 minutes)

  • Section A: Christianity — beliefs, scriptures, practices, key figures
  • Section B: Islam — beliefs, Qur'an, Five Pillars, key concepts
  • Question format: 1 mark + 1 mark (factual recall) → 4 marks (explain with examples) → 6 marks (evaluate with reasoning)

Paper 2: Thematic Studies (1 hour 45 minutes)

  • Theme A: Relationships & Families
  • Theme B: Religion & Life (meaning, purpose, suffering)
  • Theme D: Peace & Conflict (war, peace, forgiveness, justice)
  • Theme E: Crime & Punishment (justice, rehabilitation, capital punishment)
  • Question format: 1 mark → 1 mark → 4 marks → 6 marks → 12 marks (extended evaluation)

What Makes RS Different from KS3?

KS3 (What you've been doing): Broad survey of many religions; focus on what people believe and practise.

GCSE (What's coming): Deep dive into TWO religions; focus on WHY believers hold their views; developing evaluation and critical thinking skills; exam technique matters.

Why RS Matters

Religious Studies isn't just about religion. It develops:

  • Critical thinking: Evaluating arguments, considering multiple viewpoints
  • Empathy: Understanding how beliefs shape people's lives and choices
  • Communication: Explaining complex ideas clearly and persuasively
  • Cultural literacy: Understanding the 2.4 billion Christians, 1.9 billion Muslims, and billions of others who shape the world
  • Ethics: Wrestling with questions about right and wrong, justice, suffering, family, peace

Careers that value RS: Law, journalism, social work, medicine, international relations, counselling, teaching, politics.

Skills You'll Develop in GCSE RS

Knowledge (AO1): Learning facts — scripture, beliefs, practices, key terms. ~40% of exam marks.

Understanding (AO2): Explaining why believers hold views and what beliefs mean in practice. ~50% of exam marks.

Evaluation (AO2 evaluation): Judging arguments, weighing different perspectives, writing 12-mark extended responses. ~10% of exam marks on Paper 1; ~40% on Paper 2.

How RS Helps in Life & Work

  • Lawyer: Understanding religious freedom laws, family law (marriage, inheritance), criminal justice
  • Doctor: Understanding patients' religious beliefs about medical treatment, end-of-life care, dietary needs
  • Journalist: Reporting on religious news accurately without bias; understanding religious communities
  • Social Worker: Supporting families with different beliefs; recognising how religion affects wellbeing
  • Politician: Understanding voters' values; designing policies that respect religious freedom
  • Teacher: Creating inclusive classrooms; teaching diverse students with different worldviews

Review: Key Facts from This Lesson

What are the two papers in AQA GCSE RS A (8062)?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Paper 1 (Religion): Christianity and Islam beliefs & practices. Paper 2 (Thematic Studies): Relationships & Families, Religion & Life, Peace & Conflict, Crime & Punishment.

What is the question format on Paper 1?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: 1 mark + 1 mark + 4 marks + 6 marks = 16 marks total for Christianity and Islam sections.

What is AO1 and AO2?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: AO1 = knowledge (learning facts). AO2 = understanding (explaining why) and evaluation (judging arguments).

Name three careers that value GCSE RS.

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Any of: law, medicine, journalism, social work, politics, international relations, counselling, teaching.

What's the difference between KS3 and GCSE RS?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: KS3 covers many religions broadly. GCSE focuses deeply on two religions (Christianity & Islam) and teaches evaluation skills.
