The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — Gold in the river

It was January 1848, in California. At that time, California was quiet and had very few towns. A man called James Marshall was building a mill by a river. One morning, he looked in the water. He saw some small, bright, yellow pieces. They were heavy. It was gold!

The land belonged to a man called John Sutter. Sutter was not happy. He knew that gold could bring many people and problems. So he tried to keep the gold a secret. But a secret like this is very hard to keep.

Soon a shopkeeper called Sam Brannan heard the news. He was clever. First, he bought a lot of shovels and pans. Then he walked through the town of San Francisco with a bottle of gold. He shouted, "Gold! Gold from the river!" Now everybody wanted to go and find gold — and Brannan sold them the tools.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did James Marshall find, and where?

2. What did Sam Brannan do to get rich?

1. He found gold in the water of a river, while he was building a mill in California.

2. He bought a lot of tools first, then told everyone about the gold. So he sold the tools to the crowds and became rich.

Talk about it:
  • Sutter wanted to keep the gold a secret. Brannan wanted to tell everyone. Who do you understand more? Why?

Part 2 — Thousands of people come

In 1849, the news went all around the world. Thousands and thousands of people came to California to find gold. People called them the "forty-niners", because it was the year 1849. They came from America, China, Mexico, Chile, Ireland and many other places.

The journey was very hard. Some people came in wagons across the mountains. Some people came on ships. The trip took many months, and some people died on the way. But everybody wanted the same thing: gold.

Life in the goldfields was difficult. At the start, there was gold in the rivers, and it was easy to find. People lived in small tents. New towns grew very fast. But there was not much food, and the shops sold things for a lot of money. One egg cost a lot! Most people did not get rich. The people who got rich were often the shopkeepers, not the miners.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who were the "forty-niners", and where did they come from?

2. Why did most miners not get rich?

1. They were the thousands of people who came to California in 1849 to find gold. They came from all over the world — America, China, Mexico, Chile, Ireland and more.

2. The gold was hard to find after a while, and food and things in the shops cost a lot of money. The shopkeepers got rich, not the miners.

Talk about it:
  • The shopkeepers got rich, not the miners. Why do you think this happened?

Part 3 — What the gold changed

The Gold Rush changed California very fast. So many people came that, in 1850, California became a state of the United States. San Francisco grew from a small town into a big city. There were new roads, new banks and new shops everywhere.

But the Gold Rush was not good for everyone. Native people already lived on this land. When the miners came, they took the land. Many Native people lost their homes and their food, and very many of them died. It was a terrible time for them. Chinese people and Mexican people also had many problems, because other miners were not kind to them.

The land changed too. When the easy gold was gone, big companies used water to break the hills and move rivers. This made a mess of the land. The wild years of the Gold Rush were short — only a few years. But they made a new state, changed a country, and hurt many people and the land.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What good things did the Gold Rush bring to California?

2. Who and what had big problems because of the Gold Rush?

1. California became a state in 1850, San Francisco became a big city, and there were new roads, banks and shops.

2. The Native people lost their land, their food and their homes, and many died. Chinese and Mexican people had problems too. The land was also damaged.

Talk about it:
  • The Gold Rush was good for some people and very bad for others. Is this true for many big changes in history?

Grammar: there was / there were

This story tells us what was in a place in the past — gold in the river, people in the towns, food in the shops. We use there was and there were to say this.

there was = one thing, or a thing we do not count (like gold, food, water): "There was gold in the river." "There was a man by the river."
there were = more than one thing (things we count): "There were thousands of people." "There were new towns everywhere."
some / any / a lot of:
• some = a small amount, in a "yes" sentence: "There was some gold in the water."
• any = in a "no" sentence or a question: "There wasn't any food." "Was there any gold?"
• a lot of = a big amount: "There were a lot of people." "There was a lot of gold."

1. there was or there were?

Read the sentence and choose the correct one.

1. gold in the river. (gold = we do not count it)

2. thousands of people in California. (people = many)

3. a lot of new towns. (towns = many)

4. not much food in the shops. (food = we do not count it)

1. There was (gold — one uncountable thing)

2. There were (people — more than one)

3. There were (towns — more than one)

4. There was (food — uncountable)

2. some, any or a lot of?

Choose the correct word for each sentence.

1. There was gold in the water, so people were happy. (a small amount, "yes")

2. There wasn't food in the town. ("no" sentence)

3. There were people — many thousands of them! (a big amount)

4. Was there gold in the river? (a question)

1. some (a "yes" sentence)

2. any (a "no" sentence)

3. a lot of (a big amount)

4. any (a question)

3. Tell a short part of the story

Fill each gap with there was, there were, some, any or a lot of. Read it aloud when you finish.

In 1849, thousands of people in California. gold in the rivers, so people were happy. But new people every day, and soon there wasn't food in the shops. There were problems for everyone.

there were (people) · There was some (gold) · there were a lot of (new people) · any (food, "no" sentence) · a lot of (problems)

Use there was for uncountable things (gold, food) and there were for things we count (people, problems).
