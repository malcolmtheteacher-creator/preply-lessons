God Sees the Truth, But Waits

Leo Tolstoy's story of an innocent man, a long wait, and a great act of forgiveness.

Now — the words for today's story

Ten words open the story. Do the four short activities. Say your answer out loud before you reveal it.

A · What does the word mean here?

1. Ivan was a merchant — he bought and sold things, and he had two shops.
a merchant — a business person who buys and sells things.
2. Ivan did nothing wrong. He was innocent — but nobody believed him.
innocent — you did nothing wrong. Opposite: guilty.
3. The court sent him to a prison camp far away in Siberia, in the cold east of Russia.
a prison camp — a place far away where prisoners live and work.

B · Which word is it?

Three words: truth · lie · secret. Say the right word for each meaning, then reveal.

1. Which word means what really happened — the real facts?
the truth — "Only God knows the truth," said Ivan. The word is in the title!
2. Which word means something you say that is not true?
a lie — "He never told a lie in his life."
3. Which word means something you keep hidden from other people?
a secret — one man in this story keeps a terrible secret for 26 years.

C · Choose the meaning — a / b / c

1. "The police blamed Ivan." To blame someone means…
  (a) to say they did something wrong   (b) to help them   (c) to forget them
(a) — to blame = to say "you did it!" The police blamed the wrong man.
2. "In the end, the real culprit confessed." To confess means…
  (a) to run away   (b) to say "yes, I did it"   (c) to say nothing
(b) — to confess = to tell the truth about the bad thing you did.

D · Word families

Noun: forgiveness. Past: forgave (forgive → forgave → forgiven). The most important word in this story.
2. You know to wait. Complete: "He ___ for twenty-six years" (past). The title says the truth also ___s.
"He waited for twenty-six years." And the truth waits — it comes late, but it comes.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then answer the quick questions from memory, and talk about the discussion question.

Part 1 — The journey to the fair

Ivan Aksionov was a young merchant in a Russian town. He had two shops, a nice house, a wife and small children. He was friendly and funny, and he loved to sing. One summer, he prepared to travel to a big market in another city, to sell his things.

"Please don't go today," his wife said. "I had a bad dream about you. In my dream, your hair was completely grey." Ivan laughed. "Dreams mean nothing!" And he drove away. On the road, he met another merchant, a man he knew. They stopped at the same small hotel for the night, drank tea together, and slept in rooms next to each other. Early in the morning, Ivan continued his journey alone.

Later that day, police officers stopped him on the road. They asked strange questions: Where did you sleep? Who was with you? Then they told him terrible news: the other merchant was dead, and his money was gone. The police searched Ivan's bag — and found a knife in it. It was not Ivan's knife. Somebody had put it there. "It's not mine! I don't know how it got there!" Ivan cried. But his face was white and his voice was shaking, and to the police he looked like a guilty man. The court did not believe him. Ivan was sent far away, to a prison camp in Siberia — an innocent man.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was Ivan's life like at the beginning?

2. Why did the police think Ivan was guilty?

1. Very good: he was a young merchant with two shops, a house, a wife and children. He was happy and loved singing.

2. They found a knife in his bag (somebody put it there), and he looked afraid — his face was white and his voice was shaking. Fear looked like guilt.

Discuss:
  • Ivan looked guilty because he was afraid. Can an innocent person look guilty? How?

Part 2 — Twenty-six years

Before Ivan was sent away, his wife visited him one time. She asked him quietly: "Ivan, tell me the truth. Did you do it?" That question hurt Ivan more than anything. Even his own wife was not sure! "Only God knows the truth," he said to himself. "People will not help me. I will pray, and I will wait."

Ivan lived in the prison camp in Siberia for twenty-six years. His hair became white — exactly like in his wife's dream. He worked, he read his one book, and he sang in the church choir on Sundays. Slowly, something surprising happened: everybody began to respect him. The other prisoners called him "Grandfather" and "the man of God". When they argued, they came to Ivan, and he decided fairly who was right. But he heard nothing from his family — nothing, for twenty-six years.

One day, a new group of prisoners arrived. One of them, Makar, was a big, loud man of about sixty — from Ivan's home town! Ivan asked him about his family. And when Makar heard Ivan's name, he looked very surprised and said strange things. Slowly, Ivan understood something terrible: this man knew about that night at the hotel, twenty-six years ago. Maybe — maybe Makar himself was the real culprit. Old memories filled Ivan's heart, and for two weeks he was so unhappy he could not sleep.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did Ivan's wife ask him, and why did it hurt so much?

2. Who was Makar, and what did Ivan understand about him?

1. She asked: "Did you do it?" It hurt because even his own wife was not sure he was innocent. After that, he put his hope only in God.

2. A new prisoner from Ivan's home town. From Makar's surprised face and strange words, Ivan understood: Makar was at the hotel that night — he was probably the real culprit.

Discuss:
  • After 26 years, Ivan had found peace. Then Makar arrived. Why was this meeting so hard for Ivan?

Part 3 — "God will forgive you"

One night, Ivan saw something: Makar was secretly digging a tunnel under the wall — he wanted to escape! Makar whispered: "Keep quiet, old man, and I'll take you with me. If you tell, you will be sorry." Ivan answered: "I don't want to escape. And I'm not afraid of you. You took my life from me long ago. I will do what God tells me to do."

The next day, the guards found the tunnel. The commander asked all the prisoners: "Who did this?" Nobody spoke. Then the commander turned to Ivan: "Old man, you always tell the truth. Tell me: who dug the tunnel?" Here was Ivan's chance! He could finally pay Makar back for everything! Ivan looked at Makar… and said: "I cannot say it, sir. Do what you want with me." He protected the man who had destroyed his life.

That night, Makar came to Ivan's bed — crying. He fell on his knees. "Ivan, forgive me! It was me, that night at the hotel. I put the knife in your bag. I will confess everything — they will let you go home!" Ivan said sadly: "It is easy to say. But where can I go now? My wife is dead. My children have forgotten me. I have nowhere to go." But Makar cried and cried: "Forgive me! Please, forgive me!" And when Ivan saw his tears, his own heart changed. "God will forgive you," he said softly. "Maybe I am no better than you." And suddenly, Ivan felt light and free — the pain of twenty-six years was gone. Makar confessed everything to the court. The order came: Ivan was free. But when the paper arrived at the camp, Ivan had already died — quietly, and at peace.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Ivan had a chance to punish Makar. What did he do?

2. How does the story end?

1. He said nothing. He protected Makar and did not tell the commander about the tunnel.

2. Makar confessed everything, crying, and Ivan forgave him: "God will forgive you." Ivan felt free at last. The court ordered his release — but when the paper arrived, Ivan had already died, at peace.

Discuss:
  • Ivan forgave the man who took everything from him. Could you do that? Should a person do that?

Grammar — past simple + time words: ago, for, later, one day

This story covers twenty-six years. To tell a long story clearly, we use the past simple with time words that show when and how long.

The time words:
• ago — counting back from now: "It happened twenty-six years ago."
• for — how long: "He lived there for twenty-six years."
• later — after some time: "Two weeks later, everything changed."
• One day… — how stories continue: "One day, a new prisoner arrived."
Everyday examples:
• I moved to this city five years ago.
• We lived in that house for ten years.
• I started a new job. Two months later, I got my first big project.

Going deeper

Leo Tolstoy, one of the greatest writers in history, wrote this story in 1872 — for a school book for children. He said it was one of the best things he ever wrote. The title is an old Russian saying.

The heart of it. Everything failed Ivan: the court, the police, even his wife's trust. So the story asks a hard question: what can a person do when nobody helps? Ivan's answer came in three steps: he did not give up hope (he found faith), he did not try to escape, and — hardest of all — he did not take revenge when it was offered to him for free. And here is the beautiful surprise of the ending: when Ivan forgave Makar, it was Ivan who became free. The paper from the court came too late. The peace in his heart did not.

Talk it through:
  • Is the ending sad or peaceful for you? Why?
  • Ivan said: "Maybe I am no better than you." Why did he say that?
  • Makar confessed only after Ivan protected him. Did Ivan's kindness change him?
  • Tolstoy wrote this story for school children. What did he want them to learn?
