Part 1 — Check-in

Tuesday, six in the morning. Leo is at the airport. He has a meeting in London with a new customer — a big customer. He has a small suitcase and his laptop. He is nervous. His English is not perfect.

Agent: Good morning. Where are you flying to?

Leo: To London.

Agent: Can I see your passport, please?

Leo: Here you are.

Agent: Thank you. Any bags to check in?

Leo: One suitcase. This is my hand luggage.

Agent: Put the suitcase here, please. Twenty kilos — that's fine.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why is Leo going to London?

2. How many bags does he check in?

1. For a meeting with a new customer — a big one.

2. One suitcase. The other bag is hand luggage.

Talk about it:
  • And you? Do you fly a lot? Where to? Do you check in a suitcase, or only hand luggage?

Part 2 — The seat

Leo: Thank you.

Leo goes through security. Belt, watch, shoes. The laptop goes in a box. Then coffee. He needs coffee.

2. What time is the flight? What time is boarding?

Part 3 — Delayed

At 9:50, Leo looks at the screen. Flight 507 to London: DELAYED. New time: 12:10. Two hours. The meeting is at three. Leo calls Anna.

Leo: Anna, the flight is delayed. Two hours.

Anna: Don't worry. Call the customer. Say: "I'm sorry, my flight is delayed. Can we meet at four?"

Leo: In English?

Anna: In English. You can do it.

Leo: OK. OK.

He calls. The customer says: "No problem. See you at four." Leo smiles. Maybe his English is not so bad.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why does Leo call Anna?

2. What does the customer say?

1. The flight is delayed two hours, and the meeting is at three. He doesn't know what to do.

2. "No problem. See you at four."

Talk about it:
  • And you? Is your flight ever delayed? What do you do? Do you make phone calls in English?

Grammar: polite questions

At an airport, a hotel or a restaurant, people ask you questions, and you ask for things. These four forms do most of the work.

Can I…? = you want something, politely: "Can I see your passport?" "Can I have a coffee, please?" Answer: "Here you are." / "Of course."
Would you like…? = the other person offers you something: "Would you like a window seat?" — "A window seat, please." / "Yes, please." / "No, thank you."
Where can I…? / What time is…? "Where can I get a coffee?" "What time is boarding?" Start with Excuse me to stop a person: "Excuse me, is this gate 22?"
Here you are / Here's your… = you give something: "Here you are." (your passport) · "Here's your boarding pass."
