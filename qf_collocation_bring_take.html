🔄 Bring vs Take

Master the direction | A2 Level

Let's see what you notice...

Read this sentence. What's wrong with it?

Don't forget to bring an umbrella when you go out.

What's the correction?

✓ Correction

Don't forget to take an umbrella when you go out.

📌 Why?

BRING = toward the speaker, here, or where we are. TAKE = away from the speaker, there, or where someone else is.

If you're leaving (going out), you TAKE something with you. If someone is coming HERE, they BRING something.

Key point: BRING = here, TAKE = there. Like lend/borrow, these are perspective verbs!

📚 Direction Matters: BRING vs TAKE

✓ BRING ➡️ HERE

➡️

Bring it to me

Bring your friend to dinner

Bring a gift to the party

Can you bring the documents?

Direction: toward speaker

Movement: to here, to me

✓ TAKE ➡️ THERE

➡️

Take it to her

Take your coat when you leave

Take your umbrella with you

Take your friend to the station

Direction: away from speaker

Movement: to there, away

Practice: BRING or TAKE?

💡 Quick Rule: BRING = Come here with this. TAKE = Go there with this. Think about where YOU are when you speak!
