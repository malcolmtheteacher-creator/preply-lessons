Basic Presentation Structure

The Three Parts

Introduction (1-2 minutes): Greet the audience, state your name and topic, outline what you will cover

Body (3-4 minutes): Present three main points with examples and explanations

Conclusion (1 minute): Summarise key points, thank the audience, open for questions

Introduction Examples

Starting Strong

"Good morning, everyone. My name is [name], and I'm going to talk about [topic]."

"Hello, I'd like to present my research on [topic]. I'll cover three main points..."

"Thank you for being here today. My presentation focuses on..."

Body: Presenting Points

Organising Your Ideas

Point 1: Clearly state the point, give an example, explain its importance

Point 2: Use transition words (Secondly, Furthermore, Additionally)

Point 3: Complete your argument with final evidence

Conclusion Examples

Finishing Your Presentation

"In conclusion, we've seen that..."

"To summarise the main points..."

"Thank you for listening. Do you have any questions?"

Managing Nerves

Confidence Tips

✓ Practice several times before the presentation

✓ Speak slowly and clearly

✓ Pause between points to breathe

✓ Make eye contact with different audience members

✓ Stand confidently; don't pace back and forth

✓ It's normal to feel nervous — your audience is on your side

Essential Vocabulary

Audience The group of people listening to your presentation
Signpost Verbal markers that guide listeners (e.g., "next point")
Outline Brief summary of the main points of a presentation
Deliver Present or perform a speech or presentation
Articulate Speak clearly and distinctly
Engagement The attention and interest of your audience
Visual aid Picture, diagram, or object that helps explain ideas
Reiterate Repeat or restate something for emphasis

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: Plan a Short Presentation

Choose a topic you know well (your hobby, hometown, favourite subject). Plan three main points in 2 minutes.

Example: Topic = Football. Point 1: History, Point 2: Rules, Point 3: Why it's popular

Exercise 2: Write an Introduction

Write an introduction for a presentation about "The Importance of Sleep for Students."

"Good morning. My name is [name]. Today I'm presenting on sleep and student health. I'll discuss three key benefits: academic performance, mental health, and physical well-being."

Exercise 3: Create a Conclusion

Write a conclusion that summarises three points about climate change.

"In conclusion, we've covered rising temperatures, environmental impacts, and solutions. Taking action now is critical. Thank you for your attention. Are there any questions?"

Signposting Language

Guide Your Audience

Moving Between Points

First/Firstly: "First, I want to talk about..."

Second/Secondly: "My second point is..."

Finally/Lastly: "Finally, the third aspect..."

Additionally/Furthermore: "Furthermore, it's important to note..."

In addition to: "In addition to this, we should consider..."

Adding Examples

For example: "For example, schools in Europe..."

To illustrate: "To illustrate this point, consider..."

For instance: "For instance, young people today..."

Concluding

In conclusion/To conclude: "In conclusion, all these factors..."

In summary: "In summary, we've examined..."

To summarise: "To summarise the main points..."

Key Takeaways

✓ Follow a clear introduction-body-conclusion structure

✓ Use signposting words to guide your audience

✓ Practice to build confidence

✓ Speak clearly and at a natural pace

✓ Make eye contact and engage your audience

✓ Nerves are normal — they show you care
