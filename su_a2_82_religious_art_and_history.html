Stained Glass: The Poor Man's Bible

The History

In medieval times, most people in the world could not read. They also could not afford to buy their own Bibles. Stained glass windows in churches served a very important purpose:

  • Stained glass windows were like picture books for people who couldn't read
  • They told biblical stories through colorful images and patterns
  • Churches used these windows as an educational tool to teach people about religion

This is why stained glass became known as "the Poor Man's Bible" — it made the Bible accessible to everyone, not just those who could read.

Symbolism in Color

Colors in stained glass had special meaning:

  • Blue: Often represented the Virgin Mary or the heavens
  • Red: Represented love, sacrifice, or the blood of saints
  • Gold: Symbolized divine light, heaven, or importance
  • Green: Represented life, hope, or nature
  • White: Symbolized purity or innocence

Gothic Stained Glass

Example: King's College Chapel, Cambridge

This famous cathedral contains beautiful stained glass windows that tell two important stories:

  1. The death of Jesus Christ — the central event of Christian faith
  2. The reign of King Henry VIII — an important historical figure for England

By combining these two stories, the windows show how religious history and political history are connected. For people who cannot read, these images make both stories clear and memorable.

Modern Stained Glass

Example: The Baptistry Window at Coventry Cathedral

This modern stained glass window demonstrates how the tradition continues today:

  • The window is 81 feet tall — as tall as five double-decker buses stacked on top of each other!
  • It stands above a font carved into the Stone of Bethlehem, a rock from the place of Jesus's birth
  • This placement makes the window deeply symbolic — it connects ancient biblical history with modern worship
  • Even modern churches continue using stained glass to tell sacred stories

Discussion Questions

Discuss these questions with a partner or write your thoughts:

1. Why were stained glass windows so important in medieval churches?

Show Answer
Because most people could not read, stained glass windows served as visual teaching tools. They told biblical stories through pictures and colors, making religion accessible to everyone.

2. What does the phrase "Poor Man's Bible" mean?

Show Answer
It means that stained glass windows provided a way for poor people (and anyone who couldn't read) to learn about the Bible through visual images instead of reading text.

3. Describe what you think happens in the King's College Chapel windows. Why would someone choose to show both religious and political history?

Show Answer
The windows show the story of Jesus's death and the reign of King Henry VIII. This connects religious and political history, showing how they are intertwined. It shows that both are important to the nation's story.

4. What makes the Baptistry window at Coventry Cathedral "symbolic"?

Show Answer
The window is placed above a font made from the Stone of Bethlehem — the place where Jesus was born. This creates a powerful symbol connecting the ancient birth of Christ with modern Christian baptism rituals.

Speaking Activity 1: Describe an Image

Imagine you're looking at a stained glass window. Describe what you see:

  • What colors do you see?
  • What shapes or figures are shown?
  • What biblical story do you think it tells?
  • How does the artwork make you feel?

Target: Speak for 60-90 seconds using descriptive vocabulary like beautiful, bright, colorful, sacred, peaceful.

Speaking Activity 2: Compare Art Forms

Compare how stained glass teaches stories compared to other art forms:

Consider:

  • How is stained glass different from a painting?
  • How is it different from a written book?
  • What makes stained glass special for teaching in churches?
  • Would you prefer to learn from stained glass or from books? Why?

Target: Use comparing language like "unlike," "whereas," "in contrast," "similar to."

Speaking Activity 3: Role Play

Two roles: A visitor to a church and a guide

Visitor: Ask questions about the stained glass windows:

  • What story does this window tell?
  • What does the color symbolize?
  • How old is this window?
  • Why is stained glass important?

Guide: Answer using vocabulary and information from the lesson.

Target: Use polite language, questions, and descriptive details. Speak for 2-3 minutes total.

Speaking Activity 4: Personal Opinion

Record or speak your answer to this question:

"If you could design a stained glass window to tell a story that's important to you, what would it show? Describe your design, the colors you would use, and why each color matters."

Target: Speak for 90-120 seconds. Use "I would," "I would choose," "the symbolism of," and other planned language from the lesson.
