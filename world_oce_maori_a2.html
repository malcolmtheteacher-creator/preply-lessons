More Than the Haka: Māori Culture Today

Māori culture is alive today. It has its own language, television and festivals.

The stereotype: “Māori culture is only the haka.”

The story

Part 1 — More than the haka

The haka is a Māori chant with strong movements. The All Blacks do a haka before rugby games. But Māori culture is more than the haka. Māori sailors came to New Zealand about 700 years ago. They had a rich culture of stories, carving and weaving. They did not write books, but they told stories out loud.

Quick check:

1. When do the All Blacks do a haka?

2. When did Māori sailors come to New Zealand?

1. Before rugby games.

2. About 700 years ago.

Discuss:
  • Does your country have a famous sports tradition?

Part 2 — A treaty and a loss

In 1840, Māori chiefs and the British signed the Treaty of Waitangi. The Māori and English texts did not say the same thing. Later, Māori lost a lot of land. Children went to schools in English. Many families did not speak Māori at home. In 1982, Māori people opened the first “language nests”. Small children learned Māori there.

Quick check:

1. When did people sign the Treaty of Waitangi?

2. What did children learn in language nests?

1. In 1840.

2. The Māori language.

Discuss:
  • Did you learn a second language at school?

Part 3 — Today

Today, about one in six New Zealanders is Māori. Māori is an official language. There is a Māori television channel. Matariki, the Māori new year, became a public holiday in 2022. People greet each other with “Kia ora”. At a marae, two people may press their noses together. This is called a hongi.

Quick check:

1. What is the Māori new year called?

2. What is a hongi?

1. Matariki.

2. A greeting with noses together.

Discuss:
  • How do you greet your friends?

Grammar — past simple (did / didn't and questions)

Past simple for finished actions: “They signed the Treaty in 1840.”
Negative: didn't + base verb: “They didn't write books.”
Questions: Did + subject + base verb: “Did children speak Māori at school?”
