At the Library

You moved to a new town. Now you want to join the library, find a book, and — oh! — pay a very old fine. Time to be polite.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

You moved to a new town this month. Your new home is nice, but it is very quiet — you have no books to read. So today you walk to the local library. It is big and calm, and there is a friendly librarian at the desk.

You want three things: to join the library, to find one special book, and to ask what else the library can do. It is easy. You just need to be polite and ask nicely.

But there is a small surprise. When the librarian looks at the computer, they find an old fine on your name — from many years ago! Don't worry. You can fix it, and stay friendly.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like libraries? When did you last go to one?
  2. What book do you want to read next? Why?

Who's who

You
The New Member 📖

New in town, and you love books. You want to join, find a book, and — oops — pay an old fine. You are polite and friendly.

The other person
The Librarian 🗂️

Kind and helpful. They know every book. They can sign you up, find your book, and help you with the old fine.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the polite words: please, thank you, I would like, can I, sorry. These small words do a lot of work. It is OK to speak slowly, or to ask the librarian to say it again.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the polite words.

LibrarianGood morning! How can I help you?
YouGood morning. I would like to join the library, please. I am new in the town.
LibrarianWelcome! That is easy. Can I see some ID, and a letter with your address on it?
YouHere you are — my ID, and a letter from my bank. I think I had a card here before, a long time ago.
LibrarianLet me look… Yes, you are here! But there is a small problem. There is an old fine on your card — two pounds, from 2016.
YouA fine? Sorry, I don't remember… Oh! Yes, I remember now. It was a book. I did not bring it back. That's my mistake. Can I pay it now, please?
LibrarianThank you, that is very kind. It is done. You can pay when you like — no problem. Now you are a member again! Do you need anything else?
YouYes, please. I am looking for a book, but I only remember half the title. Can you help me find it? It is about a light between two seas.
LibrarianI know that book! Let me check the computer… I am sorry, it is out on loan now. It comes back next week.
YouOh, that's a shame. Can I reserve it, please?
LibrarianOf course. We will send you a message when it comes back. It will wait here for you, with your name on it.
YouThank you so much for your help!
LibrarianYou're welcome! Enjoy your new library. See you soon.

From memory — don't look up!

1. To join, the librarian asks for two things. What are they?

2. There is an old fine on the card. What does the new member say and do?

3. The book is out on loan. What does the librarian do so the member can still get it?

1. Some ID, and a letter with the member's address on it (a letter from the bank).
2. First they don't remember it. Then they remember it is theirs — a book they did not bring back — and they say "that's my mistake" and ask to pay.
3. The librarian reserves it, and will send a message when it comes back. It will wait at the desk with the member's name on it.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use in any library. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it with a smile. Be calm and polite. When the old fine comes up, don't worry — just say sorry and ask to pay. A friendly voice and the words please, thank you and sorry do a lot.

📖 Joining the library

Phrase 1

You are at the desk. You want to become a member. Which sentence is polite?

A. "Give me a library card."
B. "I would like to join the library, please."
C. "I want a card now."
B — "I would like to join the library, please."
"I would like… please" is soft and polite. A and C sound like an order. People help you more when you are polite. To join = to become a member.
Phrase 2

The librarian needs to see your address. You give them a bank letter. Complete it: "Here is my ID, and a ___ with my address on it."

"Here is my ID, and a letter with my address on it."
A letter or a bill (for example from the bank) shows where you live. "Here you are" is a nice, friendly way to give something to someone.

🔍 Finding a book

Phrase 3

You are looking for a book, but you don't know where it is. You want the librarian to help you. Complete it: "Can you ___ me find a book, please?"

"Can you help me find a book, please?"
"Can you help me…?" is an easy, polite way to ask for help. Use it everywhere: "Can you help me carry this?", "Can you help me find the station?"
Phrase 4

The book is out on loan (another person has it now). You want to keep it for later. Put the words in order:

reserveCan Iit, please?
"Can I reserve it, please?"
To reserve a book = to ask the library to keep it for you. Then, when it comes back, it is yours to borrow first.

🧾 The old fine (a small problem)

Phrase 5

The librarian says there is an old fine on your card. You don't remember it. What is the calm, polite thing to say?

A. "That is wrong. I never had a fine!"
B. "Sorry, I don't remember this fine. Can you check, please?"
B — "Sorry, I don't remember this fine. Can you check, please?"
B is calm and polite. It asks a question, it does not start a fight. A sounds angry — and maybe the fine is really yours! B leaves room to be nice.
Phrase 6

Now you remember: the fine IS yours. The nice thing to do is say sorry and take the blame. Complete it: "Oh yes — that's my ___. Sorry!"

"Oh yes — that's my mistake. Sorry!"
When you make a small error, saying "that's my mistake" is honest and kind. People like it. It is much better than making excuses.
Phrase 7

You want to pay the fine now, so your card is clean again. Complete it: "Can I ___ it now, please?"

"Can I pay it now, please?"
To pay a fine = to give the money you owe. "Can I…?" is the polite way to ask. You can also say: "Can I pay by card?"

💛 Saying thank you

Phrase 8

The librarian helped you a lot — the card, the fine, the book. You want to say a warm, big thank you. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you so much for your help!"
Add "so much" to make your thank you bigger and warmer. You can add "for your help" to say exactly what they did. A smile helps too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in libraries, shops, offices — everywhere. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"I would like ___, please." — What do you want to ask for next time you go out?

"Can you help me ___?" — Think of a time you need help. What do you say?

"That's my mistake. Sorry!" — Think of a small mistake. What happened?

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — join → find a book → the old fine → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your library. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the new member 📖

The librarian speaks — you answer. Be polite: use please, thank you, and I would like. When the fine is yours, say sorry with a smile.

LibrarianGood morning! How can I help you?
LibrarianWelcome! Can I see some ID, and a letter with your address on it?
LibrarianLet me look… Ah, there is a small problem. You have an old fine — two pounds, from years ago.
LibrarianThat is very kind, thank you. All done — you are a member again! Do you need anything else?
LibrarianI know that one! But I am sorry — it is out on loan. It comes back next week.
LibrarianOf course. We will send you a message when it is back. Is that everything?

Round 2 — now YOU are the librarian 🗂️

Change places. You work at the library. Be friendly and helpful. Say "Welcome", "Of course", "No problem", "I'm sorry".

VisitorHere you are. I think I had a card here a long time ago.
VisitorOh… yes, that's my mistake. Sorry! Can I pay it now, please?

The Twist

🌙 A bigger fine

One more time — no help now. The librarian looks at the computer and says: "I am sorry, the old fine is bigger than we thought. It is ten pounds." But you do not have ten pounds with you today. What do you say? Stay calm and polite. Say sorry, and ask a soft question — can you pay some now and some next week? Or can you pay next time you come in? Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I asked to join the library politely.
  • I showed my ID and a letter, and answered clearly.
  • When the fine was mine, I said sorry and asked to pay.
  • I asked to reserve the book nicely.
  • I said "thank you" warmly at the end.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

More than two thousand years ago, in the city of Alexandria in Egypt, people had a big dream: to put every book in the world in one place. When ships came to the city, the people looked on them — not for gold, but for books to copy for the library.

That great library is gone now. But the dream is still here. Every time you use a card and take home a book that is not yours, you are part of the same idea: a place that lets you borrow the whole world's books, and only asks you to bring them back on time.

Your old fine was two pounds. In old Alexandria, a late book could cost you much more!

Two questions to talk about
