The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. George was Elector of Hanover.
an elector (n) — a German prince who elected the emperor.
2. He was chosen to succeed Anne.
to succeed (v) — to come next in a position.
3. The 1715 Jacobite rising failed.
a Jacobite rising (n) — a rebellion to restore the Stuarts.
4. The cabinet met without the king.
a cabinet (n) — a group of top ministers.
5. Walpole is seen as the first prime minister.
a prime minister (n) — the head of government.
6. The South Sea Bubble burst in 1720.
a bubble (n) — a financial boom that collapses.
7. Many chose to speculate in shares.
to speculate (v) — to invest hoping for quick profit.
8. Many were ruined.
to ruin (v) — to lose everything.
9. He chose to imprison his wife for 30 years.
to imprison (v) — to put in prison.
10. He was unpopular in England.
unpopular (adj) — not liked.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The foreign king

George, Elector of Hanover, became king in 1714 because he was the nearest Protestant heir. He spoke limited English, though he could communicate in French. In 1715 a Jacobite rising tried to restore James II's son but failed.

George had divorced his wife, Sophia Dorothea, and kept her confined in Germany for over thirty years.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did George become king?

2. What happened in 1715?

1. He was the nearest Protestant heir.

2. A failed Jacobite rising.

Discuss:
  • How might a king who rarely spoke English rule?

Part 2 — The bubble

In 1720 shares in the South Sea Company soared and then collapsed — the South Sea Bubble. Many investors were ruined, and politicians and the court were implicated.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was the South Sea Bubble?

2. Who was implicated?

1. A share boom and collapse in 1720.

2. Politicians and the court.

Discuss:
  • Why do people keep falling for bubbles?

Part 3 — Walpole

Robert Walpole managed the crisis and led the government from 1721 to 1742. He is often considered Britain's first prime minister. Cabinet meetings increasingly took place without the king.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who is seen as the first prime minister?

2. How long did he lead?

1. Robert Walpole.

2. 1721–1742.

Discuss:
  • Was it good for power to move from the king to ministers?

Grammar — The Passive Voice

Historians often care more about what was done than who did it — or they simply do not know who did it. That is the job of the passive.

How it works:
• Form: be + past participle: "The king was crowned at Westminster."
• Add by only when the doer matters: "The castle was built by Norman masons."
• Reporting passives hedge a legend: "He is said to have…", "It was believed that…"

1. Make it passive

Complete each sentence about George I. Use the word in brackets.

1. George (choose) because he was Protestant.

2. The rising (defeat) in 1715–16.

3. Many investors (ruin) by the bubble.

1. was chosen

2. was defeated

3. were ruined

2. Choose the right passive form

1. Walpole (consider) the first PM.

2. Cabinet meetings (hold) without the king.

3. Sophia Dorothea (confine) for years.

1. is considered

2. were held

3. was confined

Going deeper

George I's reign shows how a weaker royal role allowed cabinet government to grow.

Worth noticing: Handel's Water Music was first performed for George on the Thames in 1717.
Learning from the past — for today: Beware the bubble. If an investment promises fast and easy profit, ask hard questions. The South Sea Bubble is still taught as a warning.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Was George a good king?
  • Why do bubbles happen?
  • Have you seen a modern bubble?
