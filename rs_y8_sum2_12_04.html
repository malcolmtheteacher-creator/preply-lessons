Encounter: The Ganges and Varanasi

The River Ganges (Ganga Ma)

The Ganges is the longest river in India and one of the holiest rivers in Hinduism. Hindus call it "Ganga Ma" — Mother Ganges — because they believe the river is a goddess, not just water. Bathing in the Ganges is believed to purify sins and wash away spiritual impurity. Millions of Hindus travel to the Ganges every year to bathe, pray, and scatter the ashes of family members who have died.

Varanasi — The Holiest City

Varanasi sits on the banks of the Ganges and is the oldest continuously inhabited city in India. It is sacred to Lord Shiva and is considered the holiest city in Hinduism. The city is built around ghats — stone steps leading down to the river where pilgrims perform rituals.

At the cremation ghats, Hindus believe that being cremated at Varanasi breaks the cycle of reincarnation and achieves moksha — final liberation from rebirth. Many Hindus travel to Varanasi near the end of their lives, hoping to die and be cremated there.

Kumbh Mela

The Kumbh Mela is the world's largest religious gathering. Held every 12 years at sacred river locations, it attracts over 100 million pilgrims. Hindus believe that bathing during the Kumbh Mela is especially powerful for washing away sins and gaining spiritual blessings.

Why Hindus make these pilgrimages: Purification (washing away sins), moksha (liberation from reincarnation), dharma (fulfilling religious duty), connection to ancestors (scattering ashes), and spiritual growth (deepening faith through ritual and prayer).

Evaluate: Sacred River, Polluted Water

The Ganges is now one of the most polluted rivers in the world — full of sewage, industrial waste, and plastic. Does this affect its sacredness?

Religious perspective: Sacredness comes from God, not physical condition

Hindus believe the Ganges is a goddess. Her spiritual power to purify comes from the divine, not from the physical state of the water. No amount of pollution can remove the Ganges' sacred nature because sacredness is spiritual, not material.

Environmental perspective: Pollution disrespects the sacred

If Hindus truly believe the Ganges is sacred, they should protect her. Allowing pollution shows a failure to honour what is holy. A sacred river deserves care, not neglect. Pollution also harms the millions of pilgrims who bathe in the water.

Practical perspective: It can be both sacred and in need of protection

The Ganges can be spiritually sacred and physically in danger at the same time. Many Hindu leaders now campaign to clean the river, arguing that protecting the Ganges is itself a sacred duty. Caring for the environment and respecting the divine go hand in hand.

🔒Try to answer from memory first!

Key Terms

Ganges
The holiest river in Hinduism, believed to be the goddess Ganga Ma. Bathing in it purifies sins.
Varanasi
The oldest and holiest city in Hinduism, on the banks of the Ganges. Sacred to Lord Shiva.
Moksha
Liberation — the soul's release from the cycle of death and rebirth. The ultimate goal in Hinduism.
Karma
The belief that actions in this life affect the next. Good actions lead to a better rebirth; bad actions lead to suffering.
Reincarnation
The cycle of death and rebirth. The soul (atman) is reborn into a new body after death until moksha is achieved.
Puja
Hindu worship — prayer, offerings, and rituals performed at a shrine, temple, or sacred place like the ghats.
Kumbh Mela
The world's largest religious gathering, held every 12 years at sacred river locations in India.
Ghat
Stone steps leading down to a river, used for bathing, prayer, and cremation rituals in Hindu practice.
