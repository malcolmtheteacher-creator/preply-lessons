📚 10 Major Philosophers

ANCIENT GREECE (400-300 BC)
1Click to reveal
Socrates (470-399 BC)
Socratic Method & "Know Thyself"
The method of asking questions to reveal contradictions in thinking. Famous for saying he knew nothing, which made him the wisest person in Athens. Executed for corrupting youth.
2Click to reveal
Plato (428-348 BC)
Theory of Forms & The Republic
Believed in ideal, perfect forms of things beyond our physical world. Founded Academy. The cave allegory shows how humans see shadows of reality.
3Click to reveal
Aristotle (384-322 BC)
Logic, Virtue Ethics & Realism
Student of Plato. Believed virtue is the mean between extremes (courage between cowardice and recklessness). Founded the Lyceum. Grounded philosophy in observation.
ENLIGHTENMENT (1600s-1700s)
4Click to reveal
René Descartes (1596-1650)
"Cogito, Ergo Sum" (I think, therefore I am)
Founder of modern philosophy. Used systematic doubt to establish certainty. If you can doubt everything, at least you know you exist as a thinking being.
5Click to reveal
John Locke (1632-1704)
Tabula Rasa (Blank Slate) & Social Contract
Believed people are born as blank slates and knowledge comes from experience. Influenced democratic theory with social contract ideas.
6Click to reveal
Immanuel Kant (1724-1804)
Categorical Imperative & Critique of Reason
Duty-based ethics: act only if you'd want it to be universal law. "Act on maxims you'd will to be universal." Revolutionized how we think about knowledge.
MODERN (1800s)
7Click to reveal
Friedrich Nietzsche (1844-1900)
Will to Power & "God is Dead"
Rejected traditional morality. Humans strive for power and self-overcoming. Proposed "beyond good and evil." Influenced modern thought profoundly.
EASTERN PHILOSOPHY (Ancient-Modern)
8Click to reveal
Confucius (551-479 BC)
Five Relationships & Filial Piety
Emphasized harmony in five relationships: ruler-subject, father-son, husband-wife, older-younger brother, friend-friend. Respect for ancestors and parents is central.
9Click to reveal
Gautama Buddha (563-483 BC)
Four Noble Truths & Eightfold Path
Suffering exists, it has a cause (craving), it can end, the path is the Eightfold Path. Rejected rigid belief systems; emphasized personal experience and enlightenment.
20th CENTURY
10Click to reveal
Simone de Beauvoir (1908-1986)
Existentialism & Gender Philosophy
"One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman." Explored how gender and freedom intersect. Key work: "The Second Sex." Revolutionary existentialist thinker.

🏛️ Guided Practice: Memory Palace of Wisdom

Build a memory palace through famous buildings/locations where these philosophers lived or taught.

1️⃣ Athens Agora (Socrates)

Scene: Socrates is surrounded by young students in the marketplace, asking them tricky questions that make their heads explode with confusion and enlightenment.

2️⃣ Plato's Academy (Plato)

Scene: Above the Academy entrance: "Let no one ignorant of geometry enter here." Inside, Plato draws perfect FORMS on the wall — ideal shapes that cast shadows like his cave allegory.

3️⃣ The Lyceum (Aristotle)

Scene: Aristotle walks in circles (peripatetic school) with students, lecturing about VIRTUE — the golden mean between two extremes on either side of him.

4️⃣ René's Study (Descartes)

Scene: Descartes sits in a dark room. He doubts EVERYTHING he sees — maybe it's a demon tricking him. But then he realizes: he's THINKING, so he must EXIST. Light floods in.

5️⃣ Locke's Library (John Locke)

Scene: Books are being written on blank pages (TABULA RASA) as experiences pour in from windows. Knowledge builds from experience, not innate ideas.

6️⃣ Kant's Königsberg (Immanuel Kant)

Scene: Kant walks the same route every day (he was famously predictable). On every street corner: giant stone tablets with the CATEGORICAL IMPERATIVE written in gold.

7️⃣ Nietzsche's Mountain (Friedrich Nietzsche)

Scene: Nietzsche stands on a high mountain peak. Below, the world is "beyond good and evil." He's reached the "overman" status. A statue of God shatters at his feet.

8️⃣ Confucius's Courtyard (Confucius)

Scene: Five circles intersect in the center — the FIVE RELATIONSHIPS. Students bow deeply to elders and ancestors. Respect and harmony radiate outward in concentric circles.

9️⃣ Buddha's Bodhi Tree (Gautama Buddha)

Scene: Buddha sits under the tree. He nods "yes" four times (FOUR NOBLE TRUTHS), then traces an eight-fold path on the ground with his staff.

🔟 Simone's Parisian Café (Simone de Beauvoir)

Scene: Simone sits at a café table. A mirror shows her image, but it keeps CHANGING (she's not born, she BECOMES). The words "The Second Sex" float in cigarette smoke.

💡 Memory Anchor Principle

Each location is tied to a real place the philosopher was known for. This uses the Method of Loci combined with spatial memory. The more vivid and strange your mental images, the stronger the memory!

🔗 Elaboration: Why Philosophers Matter

Understanding the IMPACT of each philosopher helps your memory stick. These ideas shaped the world.

How They Build on Each Other

  • Socrates → Plato → Aristotle = Foundation of Western thought
  • Descartes = Shifted from faith to reason (modern philosophy begins)
  • Locke = Grounded knowledge in experience, influenced democracy
  • Kant = Synthesized Descartes and Locke; revolutionized ethics
  • Nietzsche = Rejected ALL previous morality (radical rupture)
  • Eastern Philosophers = Different paths to wisdom (not better/worse, just different)

"I am not afraid of storms, for I am learning how to sail my ship."

— Attributed to various sources; reflects philosophical resilience

Real-World Impact

Confucius: Shaped Asian governance and family structures for 2,500 years. Confucianism is still taught in schools.

Locke: Inspired the American Declaration of Independence ("life, liberty, and pursuit of happiness").

Kant: Every ethics class worldwide teaches his Categorical Imperative. It's the foundation of human rights.

Buddha: 500 million Buddhists worldwide live by Four Noble Truths. His ideas shaped Eastern psychology and medicine.

Descartes: "Cogito ergo sum" is THE most famous philosophical statement. It anchors modern thought in the individual mind.

Simone de Beauvoir: Inspired second-wave feminism. Women's rights movements worldwide cite her work.
