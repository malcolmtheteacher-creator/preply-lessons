Key words

hardship (noun) — a situation that makes life very difficult, especially not having enough money or food. Example: "The family faced real hardship after the factory closed."
lament (verb) — to express deep sadness or disappointment about something. Example: "In the song, she laments the loss of her home town."
raw (adjective) — strong, natural and honest, without anything polished or controlled. Example: "There is a raw energy in the band's early recordings."
understatement (noun) — a way of describing something that makes it seem smaller or less serious than it really is. Example: "To say the exam was 'a bit tricky' is an understatement: half the class failed."
exaggerate (verb) — to make something sound bigger, better or worse than it really is. Example: "He always exaggerates: the fish was ten centimetres long, not a metre."
hit rock bottom (phrase) — to reach the lowest and worst point of a difficult time. Example: "She says she had to hit rock bottom before she could start again."
pay your dues (phrase) — to work hard for a long time before you get success or respect. Example: "He spent ten years in tiny clubs, so nobody can say he hasn't paid his dues."
hard up (adjective) — having very little money, usually for a short time. Example: "We were so hard up in our student days that we shared one coat."
down on your luck (phrase) — going through a period when things keep going wrong, especially with money or work. Example: "He helped an old friend who was down on his luck."
at a crossroads (phrase) — at a point where you must make an important choice about what to do next. Example: "After university I was at a crossroads: more study, or a job?"
