Key words

cliffhanger (noun) — an ending that stops at an exciting moment, so you badly want to know what happens next. Example: "The chapter stopped just as the door opened."
suspense (noun) — the nervous, excited feeling of waiting to find out what will happen. Example: "The slow footsteps on the stairs kept us on the edge of our seats."
foreshadow (verb) — to give a small sign early in a story of something that will happen later. Example: "The storm in chapter one warns us that trouble is coming."
ominous (adjective) — suggesting that something bad is going to happen. Example: "There was a strange silence in the house when she came home."
narrator (noun) — the voice that tells a story. Example: "We see everything through the eyes of a frightened seven-year-old boy."
red herring (noun) — a clue that seems important but is there to lead you the wrong way. Example: "Everyone suspected the gardener, which was exactly what the author wanted."
gripping (adjective) — so exciting that you cannot stop reading or watching. Example: "I stayed up until two in the morning because I couldn’t put it down."
eerie (adjective) — strange and frightening in a quiet way. Example: "The empty road looked silver and unreal in the moonlight."
unravel (verb) — to slowly become clear, or to slowly fall apart. Example: "As the detective found more clues, the mystery began to come undone."
benefactor (noun) — a person who gives money or help to someone, often secretly. Example: "An unknown friend paid for his education."
