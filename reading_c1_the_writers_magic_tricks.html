Key words

foreshadowing (noun) — a small hint early in a story about something that will happen later. Example: "The broken lock in chapter one was clever foreshadowing."
misdirection (noun) — making somebody look at one thing so that they miss another. Example: "Magicians and mystery writers both depend on misdirection."
red herring (noun) — a false clue that leads you towards the wrong answer. Example: "The butler's muddy boots were just a red herring."
twist (noun) — a sudden, unexpected change in a story, often near the end. Example: "Nobody saw the twist coming: the detective was the thief."
narrator (noun) — the person or voice that tells a story. Example: "The narrator of the book is a twelve-year-old girl."
unreliable (adjective) — that cannot be trusted or believed. Example: "By chapter three I realised the storyteller was completely unreliable."
suspense (noun) — the excited, worried feeling of waiting to find out what will happen. Example: "The last chapter kept me in suspense until midnight."
cliffhanger (noun) — an ending that stops at the most exciting moment, so you must read on. Example: "Every episode ends on a cliffhanger."
clue (noun) — a piece of information that helps you solve a mystery. Example: "The crumbs on the rug were the real clue."
plot (noun) — the series of events that make up a story. Example: "The plot is simple, but the characters are brilliant."

All six tricks

The Card · ForeshadowingPlant a small clue early that pays off later. Nobody paid any attention to… · a warning sign · an object that matters later
The Cups · MisdirectionShine a light on the wrong suspect (a red herring); hide the real clue in a dull sentence.
The Hat · The TwistTurn the story upside down at the end. Surprising, but fair: the clues were there.
The Mirror · The Unreliable NarratorA storyteller we can't trust. Watch for protesting too much, small corrections and contradictions.
The Chest · SuspenseMake the reader wait and worry: short sentences, slow motion, sounds, delay, a cliffhanger.
The Crystal Ball · Show, Don't TellDon't name the feeling. Give the evidence, and let the reader work it out.
