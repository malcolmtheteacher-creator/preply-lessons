💥 Brick 3: The Problem
No problem = no story. Something has to go WRONG.
The rule

Every single story you've ever loved follows the same pattern:

Character + World + PROBLEM = Story

Without a problem, nothing happens. The problem is the engine. It's the reason the reader keeps going.

Think about it: Harry Potter without Voldemort? Just a kid at school. Mario without Bowser? Just a plumber. The Avengers without Thanos? Just some people in costumes having lunch.
Pick your problem type
Write the moment the problem hits

Write 3-4 sentences. Show the moment your character discovers the problem. Make the reader feel the danger/urgency.

🗣️ Brick 4: Dialogue
Make your characters TALK. This is where stories come alive.
Dialogue Rules — 3 things to remember
Rule 1: New speaker = new line
Every time a different person talks, start a new line.
Rule 2: "Said is dead" — use better verbs
Instead of "said", try: whispered, shouted, muttered, snapped, begged, stammered, laughed, hissed, groaned
Rule 3: Show character through speech
A scared person speaks differently from a brave one. Short sentences = panic. Long sentences = confidence or rambling.
Quick Practice — Fix the dialogue (30 seconds each)

Rewrite each line using a better verb than "said":

"We need to get out of here," said Alex.

"I'm not scared," said Marco.

"Give me the key," said the villain.

Write a dialogue scene

Your character meets someone. They talk about the problem. Write 4-6 lines of dialogue. Remember: new speaker = new line, no "said".

🔀 Brick 5: The Twist
The moment everything changes. The reader didn't see this coming.
What is a twist?

A twist is when the story suddenly goes in a direction nobody expected. It makes the reader gasp, laugh, or say "WHAT?!"

Classic twists:
🔄 The helper is actually the villain
🔄 The "weakness" turns out to be the solution
🔄 The character's secret saves/ruins everything
🔄 The problem was caused by someone they trust
🔄 What they were searching for was inside them all along
Remember your character's SECRET? This is where it comes back. The best twists connect to something set up earlier.
Choose your twist type
Write the twist moment

Write 3-5 sentences. This is the BIGGEST moment in your story. Build up to it, then drop the bomb. (Remember: short sentence for impact!)
