Your CONNECT Chain
Five tools for sustaining extended, coherent turns.
The Architecture of Extended Speech

These five move from local to global: extend a point → return to an earlier thread → synthesise → identify the throughline → broaden to the bigger picture. Master these and you never lose your audience.

When to Use Each One
Buffalo — "Building on that point..." Extends what was just said without repeating it. You're adding a new layer. Use when the current thread has energy and you want to push it further. Signals to your audience that the argument is gaining weight.
Cicada — "To circle back to what I mentioned earlier..." Returns to a previous point with fresh context. The audience now sees it differently because of what's been discussed since. Use when an earlier point gains new relevance. Signals strategic thinking and memory.
Tiger — "What ties this together is..." Synthesises multiple threads into a single insight. Use after a discussion has generated several ideas and the audience needs to see the pattern. This is the moment of clarity everyone has been waiting for.
Thorny Devil — "The throughline here is..." Names the underlying thread that runs through everything. Use when the conversation feels scattered and needs grounding. This phrase says: "I've been tracking the deeper logic beneath all of this."
Quail — "This connects to the broader question of..." Zooms out. Elevates a specific discussion to a wider strategic or philosophical level. Use when you want to show that the current conversation has implications beyond the immediate topic.
