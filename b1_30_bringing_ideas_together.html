Ways to Connect Ideas

These phrases help you join in conversation and show you\'re listening:

Function Phrases
Agree "I think you\'ve got a point."
"I know what you\'re saying."
Disagree "I just don\'t think that…"
Give an opinion "I think it\'s also the case that…"
"I might be wrong, but…"
Find middle ground "Maybe there\'s room for a compromise."
"We need a bit of balance."
"You know, that may also be true."

Practice

For each scenario, which phrase would you use?

Someone says: "Homework should be banned."

If you agree, which phrase might you choose?

"I think you\'ve got a point." or "I know what you\'re saying."
Someone says: "Students study too much."

If you disagree, what could you say?

"I just don\'t think that…" Then add your reason. For example: "I just don\'t think that. Students need to prepare for university."
Someone says: "Teachers should only teach, not manage behaviour."

If both sides have a point, which phrase works well?

"Maybe there\'s room for a compromise." or "We need a bit of balance." or "You know, that may also be true."

School Debate

Imagine you\'re in a parent-teacher meeting at a secondary school. Each person presents an idea, and everyone discusses whether it\'s a good suggestion.

The Ideas

For each idea, think about the pros and cons. Would you support it? Why or why not?

1. Separate Boys and Girls

"The school should be divided into two sites, one for each gender."

What might be the advantages? The disadvantages?

2. Shorter School Days

"School days should begin at 9am and finish at 1pm. We should move to a 4-day school week."

Who would benefit? Who might not?

3. Ban Mobile Phones

"Mobile phones should be banned inside the school."

What are the reasons to support this? To oppose it?

4. Require Extra-Curricular Activity

"All students should be required to do at least one extra-curricular activity."

What would be the benefits? The challenges?

5. Zero Tolerance for Bullying

"Bullies should be expelled from school permanently."

Is this fair? What else could the school do?

Your Task

Choose one idea above. Prepare to:

  1. Explain the idea clearly
  2. Give reasons why it\'s a good idea (or why you disagree)
  3. Use the discussion phrases you learned
  4. Listen to other viewpoints
  5. Try to find common ground

The Vote

After discussing, the school will vote on whether to accept or reject the idea. Be ready to explain your final vote.

Deeper Questions

Use these questions to keep the conversation going:

Do you think students are under too much stress?

What causes the stress? How could schools help?

Are you satisfied with the school system in your country?

What works well? What would you change?

Did you enjoy your time at school? Why or why not?

Think about the friendships, the learning, the rules, the teachers.

Should students spend more time studying than socialising?

Is there a right balance? What does it depend on?

Are online-only schools a good idea?

What are the pros and cons? Would you attend one?

Remember

In a good discussion:

  • You don\'t need to agree with everything
  • Different views are valuable
  • Listen to understand, not just to reply
  • Use the phrases you learned to show you\'re engaged
