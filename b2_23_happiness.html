Reporting Survey Results

When we report data from surveys, we use specific phrases to describe percentages. These phrases make our language more natural and conversational than just saying numbers.

Percentage Phrases

the vast majority
~95%
almost all
~98%
nearly everyone
~97%

~75%
about three quarters
~75%

Expressing Attitude to Results

We don't just report survey results—we also express our emotional reaction or attitude toward those results. Here are useful phrases:

Attitude Expression Phrases

I'm glad to say that...
Positive result — pleased
It came as no surprise that...
Expected result — not shocked
As expected...
Predicted outcome — unsurprising
Amazingly enough...
Unexpected positive result — surprising
Somewhat unexpectedly...
Slightly surprising result
Funnily enough...
Curious or ironic result — amusing
Interestingly enough...
Noteworthy result — worthy of attention
Predictably...
As one would expect — unsurprising
I was sad to see that...
Negative result — disappointed

Examples

Example 1: I'm glad to say that most people reported feeling satisfied with their relationships.
Example 2: It came as no surprise that financial stress was cited as a major barrier to happiness.
Example 3: Interestingly enough, those who spent more time outdoors reported higher overall well-being.
Example 4: Somewhat unexpectedly, younger people didn't necessarily report lower happiness levels than older participants.
Example 5: I was sad to see that roughly a third of respondents felt their work-life balance was unsatisfactory.

Create Your Own Survey

Now it's time for you to create and conduct your own happiness survey.

Step 1: Design Your Survey

Create 6-8 questions about happiness. Consider these areas:

  • Work/Career: Satisfaction, fulfillment, work-life balance
  • Relationships: Family, friends, social connections
  • Health: Physical fitness, mental well-being
  • Hobbies/Interests: Leisure time, activities, passions
  • Finances: Money, financial security, financial stress
  • Living Situation: Home, living environment, location

Example Survey Questions

1. On a scale of 1-10, how satisfied are you with your current job or studies?

2. How important is financial security to your overall happiness?

3. Do you feel you have enough time for hobbies and interests?

4. How would you rate your relationships with family and close friends?

5. How much time do you spend on health and fitness each week?

6. Would you describe your living situation as comfortable and appropriate for your needs?

7. What is the single most important factor in your personal happiness?

8. What one change would make you significantly happier?

Step 2: Analyze Your Results

After collecting responses, organize your data:

  • Count the responses for each question
  • Calculate percentages where appropriate
  • Identify patterns and interesting trends

Step 3: Present Your Findings

Write a short report (150-200 words) presenting your survey results. Use:

  • At least 5 percentage phrases from the Survey Language section
  • At least 3 attitude expression phrases from the Attitude Phrases section
  • Natural, fluent English describing your findings
Remember: The goal is to present information in a natural, conversational way while expressing your genuine reaction to the data. Avoid simply listing numbers.

Discussion Questions

These questions help you think more deeply about happiness and build your fluency:

1. Money and Happiness

Is money the most important happiness factor? At what point does having more money stop increasing happiness? Is the relationship between money and happiness different in different cultures or countries?

2. Your Country's Happiness

In general, how happy are people in your country compared to other places you know about? What factors contribute to overall happiness or unhappiness in your society? What would change if it were significantly different?

3. Essential Happiness Factors

In your view, what is the most important aspect of happiness? Is it health, relationships, financial security, meaningful work, personal freedom, spiritual belief, or something else? Why?

4. Future Happiness

How do you think the factors that contribute to happiness will change in the future? Will technology, environmental changes, or social shifts alter what makes people happy? Will younger generations prioritize different things than older generations?

5. Personal Change

If you could make one significant change in your life right now, what would it be? Why would this change increase your happiness? Is it something within your control?
