Talking About Sport

You watched a big game last night. Your friend watched it too — but they like a different team! Time to talk about it, and stay friends.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

Last night there was a big football game. You watched it on TV. It was very exciting! There were goals, big saves, and a winner at the end. You have your ideas about the game.

This morning you see your friend. Your friend watched the same game. But there is one funny thing: your friend likes a different team! You liked one team, and they liked the other one. You do not have the same ideas.

That is OK. Talking about sport is fun. You can say "I think…", you can agree, and you can say "I don't agree" — but nicely. At the end, you are still good friends.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like sport? Which sport do you like to watch or play?
  2. Do you have a favourite team or a favourite player? Who is it, and why?

Who's who

You
The Fan 🧣

You watched the game and you have your ideas. You like to talk about sport. You are friendly, and you like your team!

The other person
Your Friend ⚽

They watched the same game, but they like a different team. They are happy to talk. You do not always agree — and that is fine.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. It is OK to speak slowly. It is OK to say "Sorry, again please?" if you do not understand. Good talking is not fast — it is friendly.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the nice words: I think, I agree, I'm not sure.

FriendDid you watch the game last night? It was so good!
YouYes, I watched it! I think it was a great game. So exciting!
FriendMe too! The red team played very well. For me, they are the best.
YouHmm, I'm not sure. I like the blue team. I think they played well too.
FriendReally? But the red team scored three goals!
YouThat's true. You're right about that. But the blue team was a little unlucky.
FriendMaybe. Who is your favourite player?
YouFor me, the best player is number ten. He is very fast!
FriendI don't think so! Number seven is better. He scored two goals.
YouThat's a good point. But I still like number ten!
FriendHa ha! We like different teams and different players.
YouYes! But that's OK. It's only a game. Let's watch the next one together.
FriendGood idea! Come to my house on Saturday.
YouGreat! Thank you. See you on Saturday.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use when you talk about sport — or anything! Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it in a friendly way. When you talk about sport, it is OK to have different ideas. Smile. Use a soft voice. You can disagree and still be friends — this is the whole game!

🗣️ Saying what you think

Phrase 1

You want to give your opinion about the game. Complete it: "I ___ it was a great game."

"I think it was a great game."
"I think…" is the easy way to give your opinion. You can use it for anything: "I think it's a good film", "I think she is right". Easy cousins: "For me,…" and "In my opinion,…".
Phrase 2

You want to say who your favourite player is. Which one sounds friendly and simple?

A. "Number ten is the best. End of story."
B. "For me, the best player is number ten."
C. "You are wrong about number seven."
B — "For me, the best player is number ten."
"For me,…" shows it is your idea, not a fact. This leaves space for your friend to think differently. A and C sound too strong — they can start a fight, not a chat.

👍 Agreeing

Phrase 3

Your friend says something, and you feel the same. Complete it: "Yes, I ___ with you."

"Yes, I agree with you."
"I agree" means "I think the same as you". Quick, warm cousins: "Me too!" and "You're right!"
Phrase 4

Your friend says something and you think it is completely true. Put the words in order:

rightyou'reso
"You're so right!"
A happy, strong way to agree. "So" makes it bigger. You can also say: "That's true!" or "Good point!"

🤔 Not agreeing (nicely)

Phrase 5

Your friend says the red team was the best. You do not agree — but you want to say it softly. Complete it: "Hmm, I'm not ___."

"Hmm, I'm not sure."
This is a soft, kind way to say "I don't agree". It does not sound angry. Say "Hmm…" first to give yourself a second to think. Cousin: "I'm not so sure about that."
Phrase 6

Your friend says their team is the best. You think a different team is better. Which answer is polite?

A. "No! You are wrong."
B. "Really? I don't think so. I like the other team."
C. "That is a silly idea."
B — "Really? I don't think so. I like the other team."
"I don't think so" says "no" in a soft way, and then you give your own idea. A and C are not kind — they can hurt your friend. Stay friendly!

😄 Staying friends

Phrase 7

Your friend makes a good point. You do not fully agree, but you want to be nice about it. Complete it: "Maybe you're ___."

"Maybe you're right."
This gives your friend a little "yes". It keeps the chat warm, even when you still think a bit differently. Cousin: "That's a good point."
Phrase 8

You like different teams and you do not agree. How do you finish in a happy, friendly way? (Say it out loud first.)

"That's OK! It's only a game."
This tells your friend: we don't agree, and that's fine — we are still friends. You can also say: "We like different teams — no problem!"

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work for sport, films, food — everything! Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"I think ___." — What is your opinion about something? (a team, a film, a food…)

"I'm not sure. I like ___." — Say "no" softly, then give your own idea.

"That's OK! ___." — Think of a friendly way to finish when two people don't agree.

Quick test: think of one team, film, or food you love. Say the whole thing out loud — give your opinion ("I think…"), agree ("You're so right!"), say no softly ("I'm not sure…"), and stay friends ("That's OK!"). When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your chat about the game. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the fan 🧣

Your friend speaks — you answer. Be friendly: use I think, I'm not sure, and you're right.

FriendDid you watch the game last night? It was amazing!
FriendThe red team was the best. For me, they played perfectly.
FriendBut the red team scored three goals!
FriendWho is your favourite player?
FriendReally? I don't think so. I like a different player.
FriendHa ha! We never agree, do we?

Round 2 — now YOU are the friend ⚽

Change places. Now you like a different team. Say what YOU think, and stay friendly. Use "I think", "For me…", "I'm not sure".

FanThat was a great game! My team was clearly the best.
FanCome on — we won three to one! How can you say that?
FanOkay, maybe your team was a little unlucky.
FanSo, who wins the next game, do you think?
The Twist

🌙 Your team lost

One more time — no help this round. Last night, your team lost the big game. Your friend's team won! Your friend is very happy and talks about it a lot. This is hard — but stay friendly. Say the other team played well ("They were good"). Say one good thing about your team too. Do not be angry. A good friend can lose and still smile. Start the timer and talk!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I gave my opinion with "I think…" or "For me…".
  • I said "yes" warmly when I agreed.
  • I said "no" in a soft, kind way ("I'm not sure").
  • I agreed with a true part before I said my idea.
  • I stayed friendly the whole time.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

The World Cup is the biggest football game in the world. It started a long time ago, in 1930. The first one was in a small country called Uruguay.

Uruguay won the first World Cup! Some people were very happy. Other people were very sad, and some were angry. People still talk about that game today.

People love to talk about football. They agree, and they don't agree. This is very old and very normal — and it is part of the fun. You are doing the same thing today!
