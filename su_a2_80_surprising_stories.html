What Makes a Good Story?

Good stories capture our attention. They have interesting elements that keep listeners engaged.

Think about this:

Do you like telling stories or listening to stories? What makes a good story?

Adverbs That Add Interest to Stories

Adverbs help make stories more interesting by showing how or when things happened. Notice the stress in each sentence - the stressed word adds emphasis.

Read These Sentences

Notice which words are stressed (emphasized):

  • I rode happily for about an hour.
  • I carefully locked up my bike and went into the café, but unfortunately I'd dropped my keys on the way in.
  • Luckily, my friends saw me drop my keys.
  • Suddenly I realized my bike was missing. It was gone!
  • ...when strangely I heard someone laughing.
Happily In a happy or joyful manner; with a smile
Carefully With attention to detail; cautiously
Unfortunately Sadly; something bad happens
Luckily By chance; something good happens
Suddenly Quickly; as a surprise or without warning
Strangely In an unusual or surprising way; oddly

When to Use These Adverbs

  • Strangely = when something is unusual
  • Suddenly = when something happens quickly or is a surprise
  • Luckily = when something good happens
  • Unfortunately = when something bad happens
  • Happily = when we do something with a smile

Where Can We Put Adverbs?

Adverbs can go in different places in a sentence:

  • At the beginning: Suddenly, I realized my bike was missing.
  • Before the verb: I carefully locked up my bike.
  • After the verb: I rode happily for about an hour.

Continue the story using adverbs:

I spent an hour looking for the missing phone.

  • Suddenly, ...
  • Unfortunately, ...

Continue this story:

I called my friend and she picked up the phone.

  • Strangely, ...
  • She shouted happily ...

How to Respond When Someone Tells You a Story

These phrases show that you're interested and help the storyteller feel supported. Click on the phrases to see them.

Oh no!
You're joking!
And what did you do?
That's lucky!
Yes, it's nice there.

When telling a story:

Your teacher will tell a short story or begin a story. Listen carefully and quickly respond with one of these phrases to show you're interested!

These Phrases Show:

  • Oh no! — Sympathy or surprise at something bad
  • You're joking! — Disbelief; you think the story is exaggerated or funny
  • And what did you do? — Interest; you want to hear more
  • That's lucky! — Pleasure; something good happened
  • Yes, it's nice there. — Agreement; you know about the place or topic

Prepare Your Story

Think of a real story that happened to you. Choose one of these topics:

Story Ideas

  • A day you were late for something
  • A day you lost something
  • A day you won something
  • A day you were embarrassed
  • A day you had an argument
  • A day you were very lucky
  • A day you were very unlucky
  • A day you were very surprised

Make Notes About Your Story

Answer these questions about your story:

1. When did it happen? What day/time was it?

2. What did you do? (Main events)

3. What happened in the end?

4. What adverbs can you use? (happily, suddenly, unfortunately, luckily, strangely)

Tell Your Story

Now you're ready to tell your story to your teacher. Remember to use adverbs to make it more interesting!

Story time: 3:00

As You Listen to Stories

Your teacher may tell a story or ask you to listen to another story. As you listen:

  • React using the phrases: "Oh no!", "You're joking!", "And what did you do?", "That's lucky!"
  • Listen for adverbs: unfortunately, luckily, happily, suddenly, unluckily
  • Notice the best parts of the story

After stories are told:

Who had the funniest/most interesting/saddest story? Which adverbs did you hear?
