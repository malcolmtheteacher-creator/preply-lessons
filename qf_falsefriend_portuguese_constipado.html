🤧 Cold vs Constipated

A2 • Portuguese speakers • False friends: constipado ≠ constipated

🔍 Let's notice what we see

Look at this sentence. What's the problem?

"I am constipated."

(But they mean: I have a runny nose and I'm sneezing!)

⚠️ VERY EMBARRASSING MIX-UP! This is one of the most famous false friends that can cause awkward misunderstandings!
⚠️ The False Friend (MAJOR!):

The Portuguese word "constipado" LOOKS like English "constipated", but they mean COMPLETELY DIFFERENT things!

In Portuguese: constipado = having a COLD, a runny nose, sneezing (estou constipado = I have a cold)

In English: constipated = unable to go to the bathroom (a digestive problem)

In English: have a cold = sick with a runny nose, sore throat

🛠️ Understand the difference

📊 COMPLETELY DIFFERENT MEANINGS:

🇵🇹 Portuguese "constipado"

🤧 HAVE A COLD

Runny nose, sneezing, sore throat

"Estou constipado" = I have a cold

🇬🇧 English "constipated"

🚽 DIGESTIVE PROBLEM

Difficulty going to the bathroom

This is embarrassing if you say it wrong!

🎯 CRITICAL RULE:

Portuguese constipado → English HAVE A COLD / ILL

NEVER use "constipated" when talking about a cold!

This is one of the most embarrassing false friends!

✏️ Guided practice

1. "I can't go to work today. I _____ with the flu." (have a cold / am constipated)
2. "My throat is sore and I'm sneezing. I think I _____ a _____." (have / cold)
3. "She's been sick with a _____ all week." (cold / problem)

→ A respiratory illness, not a digestive issue.

→ Sneezing = respiratory symptoms = cold.
