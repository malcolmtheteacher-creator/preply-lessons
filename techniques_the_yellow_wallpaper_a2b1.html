The Yellow Wallpaper

A big summer house. A doctor husband. A room with yellow wallpaper — and a wife who is not allowed to write.

Today: tell the story of a woman who must rest in one room for a whole summer. Her husband is a doctor. He says she is not sick — only tired. Grammar: get + adjective — get worse, get better, get stranger and stranger.

Key words — learn them, then make your guess

First, let's learn eight words. Read each word and its sentence, guess the meaning, then click Reveal to check. Say each word out loud.

1. Wallpaper is the paper on the walls of a room. It can have colours and patterns.
wallpaper (noun) — paper with colours or patterns on the walls of a room.
2. A pattern is a design that repeats again and again — lines, flowers or shapes — for example on paper or on clothes.
a pattern (noun) — a design that repeats: lines, shapes or flowers.
3. When you rest, you stop working and stay quiet. Doctors sometimes say: "Rest in bed for a week."
to rest (verb) — to stop working and stay quiet, for example in bed.
4. A nursery is a room for small children. They sleep and play there.
a nursery (noun) — a room in a house for small children.
5. Bars are strong pieces of metal in front of a window. With bars, nobody can climb in — or out.
bars (noun) — strong metal pieces in front of a window or door.
6. When you creep, you move slowly and quietly, low, near the floor. A cat creeps when it hunts.
to creep (verb) — to move slowly and quietly, low, near the ground.
7. When you tear paper, you pull it into pieces with your hands. (Past: tore.)
to tear (tore) (verb) — to pull paper or cloth into pieces.
8. A secret is something you know, but you do not tell other people.
a secret (noun) — something you know but do not tell other people.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — The summer house

A young woman and her husband, John, come to a big old house for the summer. John is a doctor. The woman feels ill and very tired. But John says: "You are not sick. You are only a little nervous. You must rest."

So she must rest all day. She must not work. She must not write. But she loves writing. So she writes in secret, in a little diary. This diary is her story.

Their room is at the top of the house. Long ago it was a nursery — a room for children. The windows have bars. The bed is heavy and stands fixed to the floor. And on the walls there is old yellow wallpaper. It is torn in places. The pattern is strange, and the colour is ugly — an old, dirty yellow. "I never saw a worse paper in my life," she writes.

She does not like the room. She asks for a different one. But John says no. This room is big and full of air, he says. It is good for her.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What must the woman do all summer? What must she not do?

2. What is strange about the room?

1. She must rest all day. She must not work and must not write — so she writes in secret, in a diary.

2. It was a nursery. The windows have bars, the bed is fixed to the floor, and the yellow wallpaper is old, torn and ugly.

Talk about it:
  • The woman says: "I am sick." The doctor says: "You are not sick." Who should we listen to — the patient or the doctor? Why?

Part 2 — The woman in the wallpaper

Weeks go by. John works in town all day, so the woman is alone for many hours. She has nothing to do. So she looks at the wallpaper. She looks at it for hours and hours.

Slowly, the wallpaper gets more and more interesting to her. She starts to see things in the pattern. Behind the pattern, there is a shape. Every day, the shape gets clearer. It is a woman! A woman is behind the pattern, and she is creeping — moving slowly, low, near the floor. At night, the woman shakes the pattern, like the bars of a window. She wants to get out.

The wife tells John: "Please, take me away from here." But John says: "You are getting better! You look stronger every day. We stay." So she stops telling him things. Now the wallpaper is her secret.

But she is not getting better. Things are getting stranger and stranger.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What does the wife see behind the pattern?

2. What does she ask John — and what does he answer?

1. A woman. She is behind the pattern, creeping low near the floor. At night she shakes the pattern — she wants to get out.

2. She asks him to take her away. John says no: "You are getting better. We stay." So she stops telling him things.

Talk about it:
  • The wife stops talking to John about the wallpaper. Why do people stop talking when nobody listens to them?

Part 3 — The last day

It is the last day of summer. Tomorrow they leave the house. In the night, the wife helps the woman behind the pattern. She pulls the paper, and the woman shakes it. Together they tear off metres and metres of yellow paper.

In the morning, she locks the door and throws the key into the garden. Now the room is hers. She creeps around and around the room, low, with her shoulder near the wall. She feels free.

In the evening, John comes home. The door is locked! He shouts and knocks. "The key is in the garden, near the front door," says the wife in a soft, calm voice. She says it again and again. At last John gets the key and opens the door.

He stops. He cannot believe his eyes. His wife is creeping around the room. "I got out at last," she says, "and I tore off most of the paper, so you cannot put me back!" And John — the strong doctor — falls down on the floor in a faint. So now she creeps over him, every time she goes around the room.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What do the wife and the woman do to the wallpaper in the night?

2. What happens when John opens the door?

1. They tear it off together — the wife pulls and the woman shakes — metres and metres of yellow paper.

2. He sees his wife creeping around the room. She says: "I got out at last — you cannot put me back!" John faints, and she creeps over him every time she goes around.

Talk about it:
  • At the end, the wife feels free — but John falls down. Is this a sad ending or not? What do you think?

Grammar: get + adjective

This story is about slow change. Nothing happens fast — things simply get worse, day by day. In English, get + adjective means become.

get + adjective = become: get worse, get better, get tired, get dark, get angry.
From the story: "The shape gets clearer every day." · "It gets dark, and the woman shakes the pattern." · John says: "You are getting better!" — but he is wrong.
For slow change, repeat the comparative: get + -er and -er (short adjectives) or get more and more + adjective (long adjectives).
"Things are getting stranger and stranger." · "The wallpaper gets more and more interesting." · "She is getting worse and worse."

Going deeper — talk about the big ideas

Now take the story's ideas into the real world. Talk about each one out loud. There are no right answers — say what you think, and say why.

1 · Doctors then and now

In 1892, a doctor could say: "Rest. Do nothing. No books, no writing, no visitors." Today, doctors often say the opposite: "Move, meet people, do things you love." Why do you think the advice changed? What is good advice for a very tired person today?

2 · One room, one month

In the story, an empty room with nothing to do makes everything worse. Imagine a friend must stay in one room for a month — for example, after breaking a leg. Choose five things to put in the room to help them. Explain each thing: "I put … in the room because …".

3 · Writing helps

The woman writes in secret, and writing helps her. Many people write diaries, lists or letters. Why is writing things down helpful? Do people write more or less today, with phones? Is that good or bad?

4 · Who do we believe?

The woman says: "I am ill." Her husband, the doctor, says: "No, you are fine." In the story, everybody believes the doctor. Why? Is it different today? What should a good doctor do when a patient says "Something is wrong"?

Use get + adjective at least three times ("she gets worse", "the shape gets clearer", "things get stranger and stranger").

Task B · A better summer (3 minutes)

You are the wife's friend. It is the first week of summer, and you can change everything. Make a better plan for her: What does she do every morning? Who visits her? What is in her room? Tell the new summer, week by week: "First she…, then she…, and slowly she gets stronger…"

Task C · The perfect room (3 minutes)

Describe a room you know well — your kitchen, a classroom, a café. The walls, the window, the furniture. Then change it: you must live in this room for a long, happy summer. What do you change, add or take away? Why?

To finish — easy questions

  1. Which picture from the story stays in your head: the yellow wallpaper, the creeping woman, or John on the floor? Why?
  2. John loves his wife, but he does not listen to her. What is more important: to love someone, or to listen to them? Why?
  3. The wife's diary was a secret. Is it good to have small secrets? When is a secret a problem?
