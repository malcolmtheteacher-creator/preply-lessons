Building Your Brand

Creating Your Professional Identity and Elevator Pitch

Warm-Up: Building Blocks of Brand

Before we talk about your personal brand, let's think about how brands work in the world around you.

Reflect on these questions:

  • Name 5 brands you admire — companies, people, or organizations.
  • What do you like about each of them? What makes them stand out?
  • Now think: How do those qualities relate to you? What similar values do you share?

Take a moment to jot down your thoughts. There's no "right" answer — this is about discovering patterns in what attracts you.

Language Focus: Understanding Personal Brand

What is a Personal Brand?

A personal brand is built from three core elements:

Three Pillars of Personal Brand:
  1. Beliefs & Core Values — What you stand for and what matters most to you
  2. Passions — What excites you, what you care deeply about
  3. Skills & Strengths — What you're good at and what you can offer
"Personal branding is the story people tell about you when you're not in the room." — Aaron Orendorff

How to Describe Your Brand

You can present your brand in different ways depending on who you're talking to. Here are two examples:

Example 1: Detailed Brand Story (Career Transition)

Background: "I spent eight years in HR, managing recruitment and employee development for a tech company. I was passionate about understanding what makes people tick, and I realized I wanted to help businesses grow through their people. That led me to transition into sales leadership — a move that combined my deep HR knowledge with my growing passion for building customer relationships. Today, I focus on helping sales teams understand their customers as people, not just transactions. It's about applying human-centered thinking to business growth."

What makes this effective:
  • Shows a progression (not a random job change)
  • Reveals values and passions (people, relationships, growth)
  • Explains the why behind career moves
  • Connects past experience to current focus
Example 2: Brief Brand Introduction (30 Seconds)

Simple version: "My background is in teaching. I'm passionate about helping people develop critical thinking skills and communicate more effectively. I combine that passion with expertise in curriculum design and professional development. I'm most excited when I can create learning experiences that actually change how people think."

What makes this work:
  • Clear and concise — gets to the point quickly
  • Includes skills (teaching, curriculum design)
  • Shows passion (helping people develop, creating experiences)
  • Memorable closing statement

Your Turn: Create Your Elevator Pitches

You'll now write three 30-second elevator pitches, each tailored to a different audience. Think about how to emphasize different aspects of your brand depending on who you're talking to.

Pitch 1: Meeting Someone Senior at a Social Event

In this context, emphasize credibility, accomplishments, and your vision for the future. Show confidence and professionalism, but remain warm and authentic.

Pitch 2: Networking Event with Someone from Another Organization

Here, focus on what makes you unique and what you're passionate about. Create genuine interest and find common ground. Keep it friendly and relatable.

Pitch 3: Virtual Meeting with People from Other Departments

In this setting, emphasize collaboration and how your skills complement theirs. Highlight shared values and how you can work together effectively.

Great work! You've completed all three pitches. Each one shows how you can adapt your brand to different audiences while staying authentic to who you are.

Discussion: Reflecting on Your Brand

Use these questions to deepen your thinking about personal branding and how it shapes your career identity:

1. Authenticity vs. Adaptation

You've written three different pitches for three different contexts. How do you keep your brand authentic while adapting it for different audiences? Where's the line between genuine adaptation and being fake?

2. The Story You Tell

Think about the quote: "Personal branding is the story people tell about you when you're not in the room." What story do you want people to tell about you? How does that differ from the story they're currently telling?

3. Values in Action

You identified your core values earlier. Can you give a specific example from your career where one of those values guided an important decision or action? How did that reflect your personal brand?

4. Building Credibility Over Time

Credibility isn't something you claim in an elevator pitch — it's built through consistent actions. What are you doing right now to build credibility in your field? What could you do more of?

5. Career Identity and Change

As your career evolves, does your personal brand change too? Have you ever had to redefine who you are professionally? How did that feel?

6. Networking as Relationship Building

Networking isn't about collecting business cards — it's about building genuine relationships. How do you approach networking from the perspective of your personal brand? What kind of connections are you looking for?
