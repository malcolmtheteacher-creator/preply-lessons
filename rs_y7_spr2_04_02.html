The Guru Granth Sahib

Recall: How is the Guru Granth Sahib treated as a "living Guru"? Name 2 practices that show this.

The Guru Granth Sahib is treated as a living presence:

  • It is placed on a throne and honored with ritual respect each day
  • Sikhs bow before it with full devotion, as they would bow to a living guru
  • It is covered with cloth each night and uncovered each morning (like preparing someone for the day)
  • Langar (communal meals) are shared at the Gurdwara in the presence of the Guru Granth Sahib

Sacred Texts

Recall: What is a "sacred text"? Name 2 ways that sacred texts are used in religious practice.

Sacred Text: A religious text believed to be holy, revealed, or inspired by God or the divine. It is foundational to a faith tradition.

2 ways sacred texts are used:

  • For guidance on how to live a moral and ethical life
  • In worship and prayer, read aloud during religious ceremonies
  • For studying theology and understanding God's will
  • To settle disputes or answer questions within the community

What Is Salah?

Salah is the second Pillar of Islam—a fundamental religious duty for all Muslims. It is obligatory prayer, performed 5 times daily at set times. Salah is not optional; it is a non-negotiable part of being Muslim.

The 5 Daily Prayers

Fajr

Dawn Prayer

Before sunrise. Often prayed just as the day begins, to set intentions for the day ahead.

Dhuhr

Midday Prayer

Just after the sun has passed its highest point. Often prayed at lunch time.

Asr

Afternoon Prayer

In the late afternoon. A reminder to pause from daily work.

Maghrib

Sunset Prayer

Just after the sun sets. Marks the transition from day to night.

Isha

Night Prayer

After darkness falls. Often prayed in the evening before sleep.

Preparing for Salah: Wudu (Ritual Washing)

Before praying, a Muslim must be in a state of ritual purity. This means performing wudu—a washing ritual that purifies body and mind.

1 Wash the hands up to the wrists (3 times)
2 Rinse the mouth with water (3 times)
3 Rinse the nose with water (3 times)
4 Wash the face from hairline to chin, ear to ear (3 times)
5 Wash the right arm from wrist to elbow (3 times)
6 Wash the left arm from wrist to elbow (3 times)
7 Wipe the head with wet hands (once, or until it feels right)
8 Wash the right foot up to the ankle (3 times)
9 Wash the left foot up to the ankle (3 times)
Why wudu? It's not just about physical cleanliness. Wudu is a moment to pause, focus, and prepare yourself mentally and spiritually for prayer. The water reminds Muslims of their need to be pure in body and heart when approaching God.

The Movements of Salah

Salah is not just words—it's a physical ritual with specific movements. Each movement has meaning.

Standing (Qiyam)

القيام

The worshipper stands upright, hands placed together or at the sides. This position shows readiness and attentiveness before God.

Bowing (Ruku)

الركوع

Bending at the waist with hands on knees. This movement expresses respect and humility. The worshipper says "Glory be to God."

Prostrating (Sajdah)

السجدة

Kneeling and touching forehead to the ground. This is the deepest form of submission—the closest part of your body to the earth. It shows complete surrender to Allah.

Sitting (Julsa)

الجلسة

Sitting upright between prostrations. A moment of rest and reflection before the next cycle of movements.

One complete cycle is called a Rak'ah (ركعة). Each of the 5 daily prayers consists of 2, 3, or 4 rak'ahs depending on which prayer it is. All prayers include standing, bowing, and prostrating.

Requirements for Salah

Clean Body & Mind

You must be in a state of ritual purity. This usually means performing wudu.

Clean Clothing & Clean Space

Your clothes should be clean, and you should pray in a clean place.

Facing the Qiblah

You must face the direction of the Kaaba (the cube-shaped building in Makkah, Saudi Arabia). For Muslims worldwide, this means turning toward the same point on Earth.

Set Times

You must pray at the correct time for each of the 5 prayers. Prayer outside these times is not valid salah.

Salah vs Du'a (Personal Prayer)

Salah (Formal Prayer) Du'a (Personal/Informal Prayer)
Obligatory (required 5 times daily) Voluntary (whenever you want)
Set words in Arabic Can use any language, your own words
Set times (Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha) Any time of day or night
Physical movements required (standing, bowing, prostrating) Can be said while walking, sitting, or lying down
Ritualized structure (specific order) Free-form, personal requests or gratitude

Jumu'ah — Friday Congregational Prayer

The Weekly Gathering

At midday on Fridays, Muslims gather in a mosque or prayer space for a special congregational prayer called Jumu'ah. This is a deeply important part of Islamic life.

  • Who must attend? All adult men are required to attend. Women may attend but are not obligated. Children can attend with family.
  • What happens? The Imam (prayer leader) leads the salah. Before the prayer, there is a khutbah (sermon)—a speech that teaches about Islam, ethics, or current events affecting the Muslim community.
  • Why gather? Jumu'ah creates community. Praying together reminds Muslims that they are part of a global ummah (community of believers). It's also a chance to hear teaching and support each other.

Why 5 Times Daily?

  • Discipline: Praying 5 times a day structures your entire day. It trains you in obedience and commitment.
  • Remembrance of Allah: The 5 prayers are spread throughout the day and night, so Allah is in your mind constantly—not just on Sundays or at special times.
  • Connection: Each prayer reconnects you to God and to your faith, breaking the routine of daily life.
  • Community: Praying together (especially at the mosque) creates unity and belonging.
  • Submission: Obeying the command to pray 5 times daily is an act of submission (islam) to God's will.
Fact: Muslims worldwide face the Kaaba in Makkah when praying. Roughly 1.9 billion Muslims around the globe turn toward the same point—a powerful visual of global unity and shared faith.

Both Sides

"Praying 5 times a day shows true devotion. Other religions could learn from this discipline."

Arguments FOR this statement
  • Discipline strengthens faith. Regular practice becomes ingrained, not optional.
  • 5 prayers daily keep God at the center of every moment, not just on holy days.
  • The physical routine (wudu, movements, specific times) creates structure and commitment.
  • Muslims who pray 5 times daily report deep spiritual connection and sense of purpose.
  • Global synchronization (all Muslims praying at the same times) creates powerful unity.
Arguments AGAINST this statement
  • Devotion isn't measured by frequency. Some religions find depth in one long prayer or meditation.
  • Mandatory repetition can become rote (just going through motions) rather than deeply felt.
  • Not all people can pray 5 times daily (work, school, disability, etc.). Does that make their faith less genuine?
  • Quality over quantity—one sincere prayer might mean more than 5 routine ones.
  • Other faiths show equal devotion in different ways: monastic communities, pilgrimage, service.
