Grammar Patterns

Notice how the grammar changes depending on the verb:

offer / refuse / threaten: + TO DO
"She offered to help me."
invite: + sb + TO DO
"He invited her to go to the party."
deny / admit: + DOING
"She admitted making a mistake."
regret: + (NOT) DOING
"He regretted not spending time with his family."
blame: + sb + FOR
"She blamed him for the accident."
accuse: + sb + OF DOING
"They accused him of stealing."
claim: + sb + DID
"She claimed he was late."

Pronunciation & Intonation

When telling a news story, use pausing and intonation to create interest and credibility:

Strategy 1: Pause at the beginning
[PAUSE] "Apparently, the celebrity was offered a new film role..."
This makes the listener pay attention.
Strategy 2: Use a hedge phrase
"All I know is... the deal fell through."
This shows you're reporting, not stating fact.
Strategy 3: Cite your source
"According to reports... she threatened to leave the country."
This adds authority to your news report.

  • Use at least 3-4 word cards from the list
  • Be 50-100 words
  • Sound like a real news report

Writing Space

Example Report

Breaking News: Celebrity actor Jake Williams has had a tumultuous year. He reportedly threatened to sell his mansion for ten million pounds after his divorce. Sources claimed he hired an English teacher while in prison. He was later accused of buying a diamond necklace instead of paying his debts. However, he admitted making mistakes and offered to donate a helicopter to charity. The year certainly proved dramatic for Mr. Williams!
Tips for better news delivery:
  • Start with "Breaking news..." or "In today's headlines..."
  • Use reporting verbs to introduce different claims
  • Vary your sentence structure to keep it interesting
  • End with a summary statement

Discussion & Reflection

Think about these questions and share your ideas:

What makes a good news report?
Is it more important to be accurate, or to be interesting? What's the difference between news and gossip?
How do we know what to believe?
When someone says "apparently" or "reportedly," are they less sure? How can we tell if a report is trustworthy?
Celebrity news around the world
Are celebrities from your country in the news? What kinds of stories get reported most? Are they positive or negative?
Reporting verbs in everyday life
When a friend tells you gossip, which reporting verbs do you use? ("She claimed..." or "She said...") Does it change the meaning?
