Quick Fire

Four statements. Guess which are true, which are made up. Click to reveal the answer.

Question 1
"Honey never goes bad. Archaeologists found 3,000-year-old honey in Egyptian tombs and it was still edible."
✓ TRUE
Honey's chemical composition (sugar, water, enzymes) means it never spoils. Archaeologists have found edible honey in sealed Egyptian tombs from 3,000+ years ago.
Question 2
"The Great Wall of China is visible from space with the naked eye."
✗ FALSE
This is a common myth. The Great Wall is about 20 feet wide — too narrow to see with the naked eye from space. You'd need a telescope or zoomed satellite image.
Question 3
"Octopuses have three hearts."
✓ TRUE
Octopuses have three hearts: two pump blood to the gills, one pumps it to the rest of the body. When they swim, the heart pumping to the body actually stops, which is why they prefer crawling.
Question 4
"Lightning never strikes the same place twice."
✗ FALSE
Lightning strikes the same places repeatedly. The Empire State Building is hit about 20-25 times per year. Tall structures are struck much more often than random locations.

Round 1: The Human Body

Six statements. Three are true, three are false. Guess first, then explain why you think so.

Score: 0 / 6
Statement 1
"Your brain uses 20% of your body's total energy, even though it's only 2% of your weight."
✓ FACT
The brain is energy-hungry. Even at rest, it uses about 20% of your body's oxygen and calories — disproportionate to its 2% weight.
Statement 2
"Humans share 60% of their DNA with bananas."
✓ FACT
Many basic cellular processes are shared across life. Humans and bananas share about 60% of genes because we both need to grow, metabolize, and reproduce. But this doesn't mean we're 60% banana.
Statement 3
"The average person swallows 8 spiders in their sleep per year."
✗ BLUFF
This is an internet myth. Spiders actively avoid humans. The vibrations and smell of breath keep them away. There's no scientific evidence for this claim.
Statement 4
"Your stomach acid is strong enough to dissolve metal."
✓ FACT
Stomach acid (pH 1.5-3.5) can dissolve zinc and other soft metals. It's one of the most acidic environments in your body — designed to break down food and kill bacteria.
Statement 5
"Fingernails continue to grow after you die."
✗ BLUFF
Nails don't grow after death. But as the body dehydrates, skin shrinks back, making nails appear longer. It's an optical illusion.
Statement 6
"Humans can survive for up to 2 months without food, but only 3-4 days without water."
✓ FACT
The body can store energy as fat and protein, lasting weeks or months without food. But water loss affects every cell immediately. Survival without water: typically 3-5 days.

Round 2: Money & Society

Six statements. Three are true, three are false. Guess, explain your reasoning, then we'll debate the real ones.

Score: 0 / 6
Statement 1
"The richest 1% of people own more than half of all the world's wealth."
✓ FACT
According to Oxfam and recent wealth studies, the top 1% owns approximately 32-40% of global wealth (some studies show over 50% including hidden assets). Meanwhile, the bottom 50% owns about 2%.
Statement 2
"The average CEO earns 300 times more than their average worker."
✓ FACT
In the U.S., CEO-to-worker pay ratios average 270-370:1 depending on the year and industry. In 1965, this ratio was about 20:1. In Europe, the ratio is typically 50-100:1.
Statement 3
"Countries with higher taxes consistently have lower levels of happiness."
✗ BLUFF
The opposite is often true. Nordic countries (Denmark, Finland, Norway) have the highest taxes AND the highest happiness levels. Tax rates alone don't determine well-being — how taxes are spent matters more.
Statement 4
"More people die from taking selfies each year than from shark attacks."
✓ FACT
Selfie-related deaths (typically from falling or risky situations) number around 250-300 per year globally. Shark attacks cause about 10 deaths per year. The statistics are shocking but real.
Statement 5
"Cash will be completely replaced by digital payments in most countries by 2030."
✗ BLUFF
Cash use is declining but nowhere near elimination. Most countries will still use cash widely beyond 2030. Older generations, unbanked populations, and those without digital access depend on physical money.
Statement 6
"If you earn more than $34,000 a year, you're in the richest 1% globally."
✓ FACT
Global income distribution is extremely unequal. An annual income of $32,400-$40,000 puts you in the top 1% globally when accounting for global wealth and cost of living disparities.

Round 3: Technology & The Future

Six statements. Three are true, three are false. Then we'll debate one using a flip argument format.

Score: 0 / 6
Statement 1
"There are more mobile phones on Earth than toothbrushes."
✓ FACT
About 6.6+ billion mobile phone users exist. Toothbrushes: approximately 3-4 billion (many people don't have regular access). Phones are more ubiquitous than dental care tools.
Statement 2
"AI can now write music that is indistinguishable from music written by humans."
✗ BLUFF
AI music is improving rapidly, but experts can still distinguish it from human composition. AI can create technically correct but emotionally repetitive pieces. True indistinguishability is not yet achieved.
Statement 3
"The first computer virus was created in 1986."
✓ FACT
The "Brain" virus (also called Pakistani virus) was created in 1986 by two Pakistani brothers. It infected IBM PCs and marked the start of the computer virus era.
Statement 4
"90% of all the data in the world was created in the last 2 years."
✓ FACT
Data creation is exponential. Every two years, more data is produced than in all of human history before. This statistic remains true each year because generation accelerates.
Statement 5
"Self-driving cars have never caused a fatal accident."
✗ BLUFF
Autonomous vehicles have been involved in fatal accidents. For example, Uber's self-driving car struck and killed a pedestrian in Arizona in 2018. The technology is still developing safety systems.
Statement 6
"The average person checks their phone 150 times a day."
✗ BLUFF
Studies suggest the average person checks their phone 80-100 times per day, not 150. Still shockingly frequent — about once every 7-10 minutes. The number varies by age and country.
