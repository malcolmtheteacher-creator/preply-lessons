Lesson 4 — See & Say

IELTS Task 1 Comparing Data · B2 · Lesson 1 of 3 (Set 2)

What you'll be able to do by the end of this lesson: look at a chart with two or more things on it, and say how they compare in three different shapes — comparative (A is higher than B), superlative (A is the highest), and proportion (A is twice as much as B). One fact, three sentences. That's how Band 7 writers describe bar charts and pie charts.

Three ways to compare any two numbers

For every fact in a chart, you can write it three ways. Mix all three in your essay and you tick the box for grammatical range and lexical resource in one go.

TypePatternExample (London £60m, Leeds £35m)
1. ComparativeA was [adv] [adj-er / more adj] than BLondon was considerably higher than Leeds.
2. SuperlativeA was the [adj-est / most adj] of allLondon had the highest sales of the four cities.
3. MultipleA was [twice / three times / nearly half] as [adj] as BLondon was nearly twice as high as Leeds.

The four proportion phrases (for pie charts especially)

NumberPhrase 1Phrase 2
50%half ofaccounted for 50%
25%a quarter of / one in fourmade up 25%
33%a third of / one in threeaccounted for around a third
75%three-quarters of / three in fourmade up the majority, at 75%
10%one in ten / a small minorityaccounted for just 10%
Watch out — common B2 errors:
❌ more higher than → ✅ higher than (or much higher than)
❌ the most highest → ✅ the highest
❌ twice more than → ✅ twice as much as (or double)
❌ less people → ✅ fewer people (countable nouns take fewer)

Mini-quiz — write all three for: Bristol £58m vs Hull £20m

1. Comparative: Bristol was significantly higher than Hull.

2. Superlative: Hull had the lowest figure of the four cities.

3. Multiple: Bristol was almost three times as high as Hull.

Now you try — Cats 25% vs Fish 15%

1. Comparative: Cats were a little more popular than fish.

2. Proportion: Cats made up a quarter of pets, while fish accounted for just 15%.

3. Multiple: There were nearly twice as many cat owners as fish owners.

How big is the gap?

Pick the right modifier for the size of the difference. If the gap is huge, use far or much. If it's tiny, use slightly. Get this wrong and the comparison sounds weird to the examiner.

Tiny gap (~1–5%)
slightly · a little · marginally
A was slightly higher than B.
Medium gap
considerably · significantly
A was considerably higher than B.
Big gap
much · far
A was far higher than B.
Multiple (2×, 3×…)
twice · three times · nearly four times
A was twice as high as B.

Apply it — describe this bar chart

Read the paragraph and choose the right word for each gap. Try to use a mix of all three comparison shapes.

Average weekly hours spent on activities by UK teenagers, 2024

Social media 28h · Studying 14h · Sport 7h · Reading 3h

Word bank — use each option once: the most · twice as many · far · a little · the least · significantly
1. Of the four activities, social media took up time, at 28 hours per week.
2. Teenagers spent hours studying as they did doing sport.
3. Time spent reading was lower than time spent on any other activity.
4. Studying took up more time than sport — 14 hours compared to 7.
5. Reading came in last, with time of all four activities.

1. the most

2. twice as many

3. far

4. significantly

5. the least

Notice the mix: superlative (1, 5), multiple (2), big-gap modifier (3, 4). All three shapes in one short paragraph.

Now say each fact a DIFFERENT way

Original: "Teenagers spent twice as many hours studying as doing sport." — Rewrite as a comparative.

Studying took up considerably more time than sport. (Or: Teenagers spent far more time studying than doing sport.)

Original: "Reading came in last, with the least time." — Rewrite as a comparative.

Reading was far lower than every other activity. (Or: Far less time was spent on reading than on any other activity.)

The compare drill — flip the comparison

Ten cards. Read the left side, cover the right, say (or type) the comparison the OTHER way around (or in a different shape), then reveal. Aim for 8 out of 10 before moving to Lesson 5.

Comparative
London was higher than Leeds.
Flip it
Leeds was lower than London.
Multiple
Dogs were twice as popular as cats.
Flip it
Cats were half as popular as dogs.
Superlative
Reading was the lowest activity.
As comparative
Reading was lower than every other activity.
Proportion
Cats made up a quarter of all pets.
In numbers
Cats accounted for 25% of all pets. (or: One in four pets was a cat.)
Comparative
France was slightly higher than Germany.
Flip it
Germany was slightly lower than France. (Tiny gap → keep "slightly".)
Multiple
Sales in Tokyo were three times as high as in Osaka.
Flip it
Sales in Osaka were a third of those in Tokyo. (or: only one-third as high as.)
Superlative
Social media took up the most time.
As comparative
Social media took up far more time than any other activity.
Proportion
Three-quarters of households owned a dog.
In numbers
75% of households owned a dog. (or: Dogs were owned by 75% of households.)
Fix it
"Bristol is more higher than Hull."
Correct
Bristol is much higher than Hull. (Drop "more" before "higher" — "higher" is already comparative.)
Fix it
"There were less students than last year."
Correct
There were fewer students than last year. ("Students" are countable → "fewer".)
