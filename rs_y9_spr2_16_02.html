Encounter: Religious Teachings on Marriage

Christianity

In Christian tradition, marriage is a sacred covenant between a man and woman.

"What God has joined together, let no one separate." (Mark 10:9)

The purposes of marriage:

  • Companionship: Emotional support and partnership
  • Procreation: Raising children in faith
  • Faithfulness: Mutual commitment and exclusivity

Cohabitation (living together before marriage): Traditional Christian teaching opposes this. Many liberal Christians are more accepting, viewing it as a practical step toward marriage.

Islam

Nikah is the Islamic marriage contract. It is a legal and spiritual agreement witnessed by the community.

Mahr: A gift from the groom to the bride, symbolising his commitment and her right to financial security.

Marriage as worship: "Marriage is half the faith" — a famous hadith (saying of the Prophet). It is seen as a way to fulfil religious duty and build a just community.

Polygamy: Islam permits a man to have up to four wives, but only under strict conditions — equal financial support, equal emotional attention, and the consent of all parties (Qur'an 4:3). Polygamy is not common in modern Islamic societies and is illegal in many Muslim-majority countries.

Judaism

Kiddushin: The Jewish marriage ceremony. The groom gives something of value (usually a ring) to the bride, declaring her to be his wife.

Ketubah: The marriage contract, outlining the groom's obligations to his bride (support, respect, conjugal relations). The ketubah is signed by witnesses and is a legal document.

Family as sacred: In Jewish tradition, the family is the foundation of community and faith. "Be fruitful and multiply" (Genesis 1:28) expresses the value placed on marriage and children.

Humanism

Marriage is a personal commitment between consenting adults, not a religious requirement. Humanists value:

  • Mutual love and respect
  • Equality and partnership
  • Freedom to choose one's partner, regardless of religion or background
  • Acceptance of diverse relationship structures (marriage, civil partnership, cohabitation)
