A Retrieved Reformation

O. Henry's story of a safe-robber who wants to be a good man — and one locked door that changes everything.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — Out of prison

Jimmy Valentine was a burglar. He was very good at opening safes and stealing money. One day the police caught him, and he went to prison. But Jimmy had important friends. After ten months, they helped him, and he got a pardon. This meant he was free. He could leave prison.

The prison boss said to him, "Now, Jimmy, be a good man. Stop opening safes. Live an honest life." Jimmy smiled and said, "I never opened a safe in my life!" But this was not true, and everyone knew it.

The next morning, Jimmy left prison. He ate a good meal and felt happy to be free. Then he went to his old room. There, he took out his special tools for opening safes. A few days later, someone robbed safes in three cities. The police knew it was Jimmy — but they could not prove it.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did Jimmy go to prison, and how did he get out?

2. What did Jimmy do a few days after he left prison?

1. He was a burglar who opened safes and stole money. His important friends helped him, and he got a pardon and left prison.

2. He took his special tools and robbed safes in three cities again.

Talk about it:
  • The prison boss asked Jimmy to be a good man. At the start, does Jimmy want to change? Why, or why not?

Part 2 — A new name and a new life

A detective called Ben Price wanted to catch Jimmy. Ben knew how Jimmy worked. So Ben started to follow him, quietly and carefully.

One day, Jimmy arrived in a small town called Elmore. He walked down the street, and he saw a young woman. Her name was Annabel. Her father had a bank in the town. When Jimmy looked at Annabel, something changed in his heart. Suddenly, he did not want to be a burglar any more. He wanted to be a good man, for her.

So Jimmy took a new name: Ralph Spencer. He opened a shoe shop in the town. He worked hard, and the shop did well. People liked him and trusted him. He and Annabel fell in love, and soon they were going to get married. Jimmy was very happy. He wrote a letter to an old friend and said, "I am giving away my old tools. I do not need them now. I am a new man."

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who was Ben Price, and what did he do?

2. Why did Jimmy change his life in Elmore?

1. He was a detective. He wanted to catch Jimmy, so he followed him quietly.

2. He fell in love with Annabel. He took a new name (Ralph Spencer), opened a shoe shop, and became an honest man.

Talk about it:
  • Jimmy changed for love. Do you think love can really change a person? Why?

Part 3 — The locked door

One day, Jimmy and Annabel's family went to the bank. Annabel's father showed them a new vault — a big, strong room with a heavy door and a strong lock. Two little girls were playing near the door. Then something terrible happened. One girl closed the heavy door, and the other girl, May, was inside. The door locked. May was trapped in the dark. There was not much air, and no one could open the door.

Everyone was afraid. Annabel looked at Jimmy and said, "Please, can you do something?" Jimmy knew what he had to do. If he opened the vault, everyone would see that he was really Jimmy Valentine, the burglar. But he did not stop. He took out his tools and started to work. And Ben Price, the detective, was there. He watched Jimmy from the door.

Fast and sure, Jimmy opened the vault. The little girl was safe! Then Jimmy put on his coat and walked to the door. He was ready to go to prison with Ben Price. He said, "Hello, Ben. Let's go." But Ben Price said, "I don't know you, Mr. Spencer. I think you are wrong." And Ben Price turned and walked away. He let Jimmy go free.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What terrible thing happened at the bank?

2. What did Ben Price do at the end? Why is this surprising?

1. A little girl, May, was locked in the new vault. There was little air, and no one could open the door. Jimmy opened it with his tools, but this showed everyone he was really a burglar.

2. Ben Price said he did not know Jimmy, and he walked away. This is surprising because his job was to catch Jimmy — but he let him go free.

Talk about it:
  • Jimmy saved the girl, but he lost his secret. Was it a good choice? What would you do?

Grammar: Past Simple & Time Order

We use the past simple to tell a story — to say what happened. And we use small "order words" to show what happened first, and what happened next.

Past simple: for a finished action in the past.
• Regular verbs add -ed: "Jimmy opened the safe." "He worked hard."
• Many common verbs are irregular: go → went, take → took, see → saw, meet → met.
Order words tell us the order of events:
First, Jimmy left prison. Then he saw Annabel. After that, he opened a shop. In the end, he saved the girl.

1. Put the verb in the past simple

Write the past simple form of the verb in brackets.

1. Jimmy (leave) prison in the morning.

2. He (see) Annabel in the street.

3. He (open) a shoe shop in the town.

4. Ben Price (walk) away and let Jimmy go.

1. left (leave → left, irregular)

2. saw (see → saw, irregular)

3. opened (regular, + -ed)

4. walked (regular, + -ed)

2. Choose the order word

Choose First, Then, After that, or In the end. (More than one answer can be correct — say why.)

1. Jimmy left prison. It was the start of the story.

2. he met Annabel and fell in love.

3. , the detective let Jimmy go free. It was the last part.

1. First (it is the beginning)

2. Then / After that (the next event)

3. In the end (the last event)

3. Tell a short part of the story

Fill each gap with a past simple verb or an order word. Read it aloud when you finish.

First, a little girl (go) into the vault. Then the door (close) and locked. , Jimmy opened the door with his tools. In the end, the detective (let) him go.

Going deeper

This story has a famous surprise ending. Jimmy is a burglar, but he uses his skill to do something good — he saves a child. And the detective, whose job is to catch him, decides to let him go. The story asks a big question: can a bad person really become a good person?

Think about this: Jimmy has to show everyone his secret — that he is really a burglar — to save the little girl. His bad skill becomes a good thing. And Ben Price, the detective, chooses to be kind, not to follow the rules.
Talk about these — out loud:
  • Jimmy uses his burglar skill to do a good thing. Is he a good man or a bad man now? What do you think?
  • Ben Price is a detective, but he lets Jimmy go free. Was this the right thing to do? Why?
  • Do you think Jimmy and Annabel stayed together after this? Why, or why not?
  • What would you do if you were Jimmy? Would you save the girl, or keep your secret?

Speak

Useful language: use the past simple to tell the story ("Jimmy left prison", "he met Annabel", "he opened the vault"). Use order words: first, then, after that, in the end. To give an opinion: "I think…", "In my opinion…", "For me…".
