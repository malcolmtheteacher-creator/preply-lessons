Politician Mode

Someone asks: "Did you lie about the budget?"

SPEAK: Answer this question in 3 completely different ways. Try direct denial, deflection, and reframing. No timer — just give it a go.

Why This Is Hard

The question assumes guilt. A direct "no" sounds defensive. Smart communicators use several techniques:

  • Change the question
  • Lead with emotion
  • Promise without committing
  • Add context that reframes the story
  • Dodge gracefully

CEO Mode

A journalist asks: "Why did you fire 500 people?"

SPEAK: Again, give 3 different answers. Make them sound authentic each time — not robotic.

Today's Goal

You'll play a public figure under pressure. Questions will be tough. You need to:

  • Stay calm — panic shows
  • Control the narrative — answer WHAT you want, not what they ask
  • Use hedging language — "exploring options", "actively working", "committed to"
  • Lead with emotion — show you understand the concern
  • Bridge and pivot — acknowledge, then change the topic
  • Know when to stay silent — sometimes "no comment" is the answer
You'll need quick thinking and composure

Crisis Communication Moves

Click each to see the technique and an example.

Move 1: The Redirect

Answer a different (better) question instead. Don't ignore the question — just answer the one you prefer.

"What I think is really important here is..." / "The real issue is..." / "Let me address what matters most..."

↓ Tap for example

Q: "Have you cut corners on safety?"

A: "What matters here is that we're committed to industry-leading safety standards. Our record speaks for itself — zero incidents in three years."

You didn't answer the corner-cutting question. You answered why safety is non-negotiable.

Move 2: The Empathy Shield

Lead with feeling before facts. Show you understand the concern, then explain.

"I completely understand the concern..." / "I hear what you're saying..." / "That's a fair question because..."

↓ Tap for example

Q: "People are angry about the price increase."

A: "I completely understand the frustration. Price increases hurt families. What's important to know is that we've invested every penny from the last three years into making this product safer and more reliable."

Empathy first = you're not heartless. Then pivot to your message.

Move 3: The Vague Commitment

Promise action without promising a specific outcome. Sounds positive but buys you time.

"We're actively exploring..." / "We're committed to..." / "We're looking at every option..." / "We're determined to..."

↓ Tap for example

Q: "Will you fix this by next month?"

A: "This is our top priority. We're actively working with our teams to resolve this as quickly as possible."

You didn't promise "next month" — but you sound committed and urgent.

Move 4: The Context Bomb

Add context that completely changes how people see the story. Not a lie — just incomplete reporting.

"What hasn't been reported..." / "To put this in perspective..." / "The full picture is..." / "What people don't realize..."

↓ Tap for example

Q: "Your company's carbon footprint is massive."

A: "What hasn't been reported is that we've cut our carbon footprint by 40% in five years — faster than any competitor. We've also invested 200 million in renewable energy."

The footprint is huge — true. But the context makes you look proactive, not guilty.

Move 5: The Bridge

Acknowledge the question, then pivot to your message. Small agreement = you're not dismissive.

"That's a fair question, and what I'd add to that is..." / "You're right, and..." / "That's important, and here's what else matters..."

↓ Tap for example

Q: "The project is over budget."

A: "That's true, and what's important to understand is why. We discovered structural issues that weren't visible at the start. We've done them right rather than quickly. The final result is stronger and safer."

Yes, over budget — you don't deny it. But now people see the reason and the benefit.

Move 6: The Non-Answer

When you genuinely can't say anything. Sounds professional, not evasive — if you do it right.

"I'm not in a position to comment on that at this time." / "That's still under review." / "I can't speak to that publicly yet."

↓ Tap for example

Q: "Are you closing the London office?"

A: "That's confidential business information, and I'm not in a position to comment on that at this time. What I CAN tell you is that we're committed to our UK operations and our people."

You're not lying. You're protecting sensitive info while staying positive.

Choose Your Scenario

Pick one of these 3 crisis roles. You'll have 2 minutes to prepare, then deliver an opening statement (90 seconds), then answer 6 live questions from the teacher (no script — pure improvisation).

Option A: Football Manager

You're the manager of a national football team that just lost badly in a major tournament. Everyone expected you to win. The media is furious. Fans are calling for your resignation.

Option B: Hospital Director

You're a hospital director. A documentary has revealed poor conditions, understaffing, and neglect in your hospital. Patients are angry. Families are demanding accountability. The government is investigating.

Option C: City Mayor

You're the mayor of a city. A major bridge has collapsed due to poor maintenance. Fortunately no one died, but it was a close call. Construction companies are blaming budget cuts. The public is terrified.
