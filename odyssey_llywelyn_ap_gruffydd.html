The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. Henry III recognised him as Prince.
to recognise (v) — to accept officially.
2. He refused to do homage.
homage (n) — a public promise of loyalty to a lord.
3. The Treaty of Montgomery was signed in 1267.
a treaty (n) — a formal agreement.
4. His bride was captured at sea.
to capture (v) — to take as a prisoner.
5. A revolt began in 1282.
a revolt (n) — a rebellion.
6. He died, perhaps in an ambush.
an ambush (n) — a surprise attack.
7. His daughter was sent to a nunnery.
a nunnery (n) — a community of nuns.
8. A memorial stands at Cilmeri.
a memorial (n) — something that helps people remember.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The prince

Llywelyn ap Gruffydd fought his own brothers for power. By 1267, in the Treaty of Montgomery, Henry III recognised him as Prince of Wales — the only native Welsh ruler to receive that title from an English king.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did the Treaty of Montgomery do?

2. Why is this unique?

1. Recognised him as Prince of Wales.

2. No other native Welsh ruler got the title from an English king.

Discuss:
  • Why would Henry agree?

Part 2 — Edward I

Henry's son Edward I was tougher. Llywelyn refused to do homage to him. In 1275 his bride, Eleanor de Montfort, was captured at sea on her way from France. In 1277 Edward invaded, and Llywelyn lost most of his lands. Edward said that Llywelyn must give up territory and pay; they finally married in 1278 at Worcester, with Edward present.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why did Edward invade in 1277?

2. What happened to Eleanor?

1. Llywelyn refused homage.

2. She was captured at sea; they married in 1278.

Discuss:
  • Was refusing homage brave or foolish?

Part 3 — The end

In 1282 Llywelyn's brother Dafydd began a revolt, and Llywelyn joined. Eleanor died after giving birth to a daughter, Gwenllian. On 11 December 1282, near Cilmeri, Llywelyn was killed — the exact circumstances are disputed. His head was displayed at the Tower of London. Gwenllian was sent to a nunnery in Lincolnshire, where she lived until 1337.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did he die?

2. What happened to Gwenllian?

1. Near Cilmeri.

2. She lived in a nunnery for her whole life.

Discuss:
  • Why put a baby in a nunnery?

Grammar — Reported Speech

Much of what we know about the past comes from chroniclers reporting what people said. Reported speech moves the tense back and changes the pronouns.

How it works:
• "I will return," he said → He said that he would return.
• "We have lost," she said → She said that they had lost.
• Reporting verbs: claimed, promised, warned, insisted, denied.

1. Report the words

Complete each sentence about Llywelyn ap Gruffydd. Use the word in brackets.

1. "I recognise you as Prince," Henry said. → Henry said that he him as Prince.

2. "I will not do homage," Llywelyn said. → He said that he not do homage.

3. "You must give up your land," Edward said. → Edward said that Llywelyn give up his land.

1. recognised

2. would

3. had to / must

2. Choose the reporting verb

1. "We have lost the prince," the poet wrote. → The poet wrote that they the prince.

2. "I will join the revolt," Llywelyn said. → He said he join the revolt.

3. "She has died," the messenger said. → The messenger said that she .

Going deeper

After 1282 Edward built great castles — Caernarfon, Conwy, Harlech, Beaumaris — and in 1301 gave his own son the title Prince of Wales. It has been given to the heir to the English (later British) throne ever since.

Worth noticing: The poet Gruffudd ab yr Ynad Coch wrote a famous elegy for Llywelyn, imagining the end of the world.
Learning from the past — for today: Know when to negotiate. Llywelyn's refusal to bend cost him everything. Principles matter — but so do timing and the power you actually have.
Dig into it — talk these through:
