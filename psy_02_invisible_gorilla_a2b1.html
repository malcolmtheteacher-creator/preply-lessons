The Invisible Gorilla

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, answer the quick check from memory first, then take the discussion question.

Part 1 — The video

In 1999, two scientists, Daniel Simons and Christopher Chabris, made a short video. In it, six students were passing basketballs. Three students wore white shirts and three wore black shirts.

People watched the video and had one job: count how many times the white team passed the ball. While they were counting, something strange happened. A person in a gorilla costume walked into the middle of the room. The gorilla stopped, hit its chest, and walked out. It was on the screen for about nine seconds.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What was the job?

2. What happened in the middle of the video?

1. To count the passes of the white team.

2. A person in a gorilla costume walked in, hit its chest and walked out.

Discuss:
  • Do you think you would see the gorilla? Why or why not?

Part 2 — The surprise

About half of the people did not see the gorilla. They were looking at the screen, and the gorilla was right there. But they were busy with the passes, so they didn't notice it.

When people watched the video again, with no job to do, they saw the gorilla easily. Many said, "That's a different video!" It wasn't. Scientists call this inattentional blindness: when you don't notice something because your attention is somewhere else.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How many people missed the gorilla?

2. What do scientists call it?

1. About half.

2. Inattentional blindness: not noticing something because you are paying attention to something else.

Discuss:
  • Have you ever said, 'That's impossible, I didn't see it!'?

Part 3 — Why it matters

This idea is important in real life. A driver can look at the road, but not see a cyclist if he is thinking about something else. A person who is using a phone while walking can miss a lamp post!

In one study, doctors who were looking at medical scans missed a small picture of a gorilla on a scan, even when their eyes were looking right at it. The lesson is not that people are stupid. It is that attention is limited: we cannot see everything at once.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Give one example from real life.

2. What is the main lesson?

1. A driver who doesn't see a cyclist; a person who walks into a lamp post.

2. Attention is limited: we cannot notice everything at once.

Discuss:
  • When is it dangerous not to notice something? What can you do about it?

Grammar — past continuous and past simple

Use the past continuous for an action in progress and the past simple for a short action that interrupted it.

The pattern:
• I was counting (long action) when the gorilla walked in (short action).
• Past continuous: was / were + verb-ing.
• Past simple: verb + ed or an irregular verb (walked, hit, saw).
Use while and when:
• While + past continuous: While they were counting, the gorilla walked in.
• When + past simple: They were counting when the gorilla walked in.
With a verb like know, see, like we usually use the past simple only.

Did you notice?

Five situations. Say whether the person probably noticed or missed it and why. Then reveal an idea.

1. A man was talking on his phone while he was walking. A friend waved at him from the other side of the road. Did he see her?
Idea. Probably not. His attention was on the phone.
2. A teacher was writing on the board. A student at the back was drawing. Did the student hear the question?
Idea. Maybe not. The student was busy with the drawing.
3. A driver was looking for a street name. A child ran out. Did the driver see the child?
Idea. It is possible that the driver missed the child. That is why drivers must pay attention.
4. You were reading a book on a train. Your station was called. Did you hear it?
Idea. It depends how interesting the book is!
5. Is it easier to notice something if you know it is coming?
Idea. Yes. When people know about the gorilla, they see it. Knowing what to look for helps attention.
