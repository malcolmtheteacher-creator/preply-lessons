Encounter: The Fifth Pillar of Islam

Hajj is the annual Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca — one of the Five Pillars of Islam. Every Muslim who is physically and financially able must complete Hajj at least once in their lifetime.

When and Who

Hajj takes place during Dhul Hijjah, the final month of the Islamic calendar. Over 2 million Muslims travel to Mecca each year. Every Muslim, regardless of age, gender, or background, must go if able. This creates profound spiritual equality.

The Key Rituals of Hajj

Ihram

Pilgrims wear two plain white garments with no decoration. This symbolises equality before Allah — a king and a poor person look identical.

Tawaf

Circling the Kaaba seven times. The Kaaba is Islam's holiest site, a cube-shaped building at the centre of the Sacred Mosque.

Sa'i

Running seven times between the hills of Safa and Marwa. This commemorates Hagar's desperate search for water for her son Ishmael.

Standing at Arafat

Pilgrims gather on the plain of Arafat to stand in prayer. Muslims believe this is a rehearsal for the Day of Judgement — the spiritual climax of Hajj.

Stoning of Jamarat

Pilgrims throw pebbles at three stone pillars representing Satan. This symbolises rejecting evil, recalling Ibrahim's rejection of temptation.

Eid ul-Adha

A feast celebrating the completion of Hajj. Animals are sacrificed to remember Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son. Meat is shared with the poor.

Evaluate: Does Completing Hajj Make Someone a Better Muslim?

For: Spiritual renewal transforms the pilgrim

Pilgrims report feeling closer to Allah, gaining forgiveness, and being emotionally transformed by standing with millions of other Muslims in prayer.

For: It means following Allah's direct command

Allah commanded Hajj in the Quran as one of the Five Pillars. Completing it shows obedience and commitment to Islamic faith — itself a form of spiritual growth.

Against: Actions after Hajj matter more

How someone lives after Hajj is more important than the pilgrimage itself. A person could complete Hajj but then behave unethically. True improvement comes from sustained moral effort.

Against: Inner faith is what truly counts

Outward ritual alone doesn't guarantee spiritual growth. Two people can perform the same rituals with completely different hearts. Sincere faith and good character matter more.

Key Terms

Hajj
The Islamic pilgrimage to Mecca, one of the Five Pillars of Islam.
Kaaba
The cube-shaped building in Mecca that is Islam's holiest site, towards which all Muslims pray.
Tawaf
Circling the Kaaba seven times as an act of devotion and submission to Allah.
Ihram
The state of ritual purity entered before Hajj, symbolised by wearing two plain white garments.
Arafat
The plain where pilgrims gather to stand in prayer, the spiritual climax of Hajj.
Sa'i
Running between the hills of Safa and Marwa, commemorating Hagar's search for water.
Eid ul-Adha
The festival celebrating Hajj's completion, involving animal sacrifice and sharing meat.
Ummah
The global community of all Muslims, united in shared faith and purpose during Hajj.
