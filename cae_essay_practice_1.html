What is a CAE Part 1 Essay?

In Part 1 of the CAE Writing paper, you must write a semi-formal discursive essay of 220-260 words. You are given a topic and three bullet points, and you must choose TWO of them to discuss. You then evaluate both and reach a reasoned conclusion about which one is more important.

Today's Question

In your English class you have been discussing different factors that influence a person's career choice. Now your teacher has asked you to write an essay on this topic.

Write an essay discussing TWO of the factors below. Explain which one you think has the most influence on career choice, giving reasons in support of your answer.

  • salary
  • personal interest
  • family expectations

Some opinions expressed in the discussion:

"Money is the main reason people choose a job."
"I'd do what I love, whatever the pay."
"Your family shapes your choices more than you realise."

Write your essay in 220-260 words in an appropriate style.

Key rule: You must discuss exactly TWO of the three bullets. Pick the two you have the most ideas for. Do not mention all three — doing so will split your focus and cost you marks.

Model Essay: Band C1+

Read carefully. Notice how the writer picks two factors (not three), evaluates both in turn, and delivers a clear verdict at the end. Highlighted phrases are the advanced structures the examiner rewards.

INTROThe question of what drives a person to follow a particular career is one that has interested sociologists and ordinary workers alike. Although several factors come into play, this essay will focus on two of the most powerful: financial reward and genuine personal interest. It will argue that, in the long term, the latter exerts the stronger influence.
BODY 1 — SALARYIt is undeniable that earning potential carries significant weight, particularly for young people burdened with student debt or family responsibilities. A well-paid role offers security, social status and the freedom to shape one's lifestyle. That said, the motivating power of money tends to fade once basic needs are met; research consistently shows that pay rises beyond a certain threshold produce little lasting satisfaction.
BODY 2 — INTERESTPersonal interest, by contrast, sustains engagement across decades rather than months. When people pursue work that genuinely fascinates them, they invest more effort, recover more quickly from setbacks and are far less likely to burn out. A doctor drawn to medicine by curiosity about the human body, for example, will almost certainly outperform one who chose the profession solely for its salary.
CONCLUSIONIn conclusion, although financial considerations cannot be dismissed, it is personal interest that shapes a meaningful and sustainable career. Were more young people encouraged to follow their curiosity, workplaces would be filled with more motivated, creative and resilient professionals.

Total: 252 words ✓

Nominalisation (intro): "The question of what drives a person..." — turns a verb idea into an elegant noun phrase

Emphatic opener (Body 1): "It is undeniable that..." — concedes the obvious before qualifying

Concessive linker (Body 1): "That said,..." — pivots from advantage to limitation

Contrastive linker (Body 2): "by contrast" — signals a new angle

Conditional inversion (Conclusion): "Were more young people encouraged..." — instead of "If more young people were encouraged..."

Pattern: concede then rebut: "Although financial considerations cannot be dismissed, it is personal interest that..." — the examiner's favourite conclusion pattern.
