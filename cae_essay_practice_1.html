What is a CAE Part 1 Essay?

In Part 1 of the CAE Writing paper, you write a semi-formal discursive essay of 220-260 words. You are given a topic and three bullet points.

✓ REQUIRED by the Cambridge rubric:
  1. Discuss TWO of the three bullets in turn.
  2. Reach a reasoned conclusion about which factor is most important overall.
★ USEFUL — but NOT required:

Adding ONE point of your own (an idea NOT in the bullets) is a strong technique we teach because it demonstrates independent thinking and helps you hit the word count. However, Cambridge does not require it — a well-argued essay discussing two bullets with a reasoned verdict can achieve Band 5. Top-band candidates often use the "own point" to pull ahead on Content and Communicative Achievement, so it's a recommended strategic choice.

Today's Question

In your English class you have been discussing different factors that influence a person's career choice. Now your teacher has asked you to write an essay on this topic.

Write an essay discussing TWO of the factors below. Explain which one you think has the most influence on career choice, giving reasons in support of your answer.

  • salary
  • personal interest
  • family expectations

Some opinions expressed in the discussion:

"Money is the main reason people choose a job."
"I'd do what I love, whatever the pay."
"Your family shapes your choices more than you realise."

Write your essay in 220-260 words in an appropriate style.

Key rule: Discuss exactly TWO of the three bullets — never all three. Develop both in depth. Your opinion in CAE is which of the two bullets matters more, woven through the evaluation and stated in the conclusion. Adding a third "own point" paragraph is a stretch technique for Band 5+ candidates only — it is not what Cambridge asks for, and it carries word-count risk.

Essay Structure — the orthodox shape

This is the structure Cambridge expects. Four paragraphs, two bullets developed in depth, opinion delivered through evaluation and verdict — not through a fourth invented idea.

Introduction

Paraphrase the topic + name the two bullets you'll discuss + hint at your verdict.

50-60 words
Body 1: First bullet

Topic sentence + advantages + concession (limits/qualifier) + example. Develop in depth.

75-85 words
Body 2: Second bullet

Topic sentence + evaluation + explicit comparison with Body 1 + your evaluative angle.

75-85 words
Conclusion

Clear verdict — which of the two bullets matters more, and why. No new arguments.

30-40 words
Where your "opinion" lives: not in a separate fourth paragraph — but woven through the evaluation in Bodies 1 and 2 (concession, comparison, qualifier) and stated cleanly in the conclusion. Which of the two bullets matters more? That is your opinion in CAE.
⭐ STRETCH option (Band 5+ only): a confident candidate who naturally writes short can add a 4th body paragraph (45-55 words) introducing an idea NOT in the bullets — see Tab 6. This is a strategic move, not a Cambridge requirement, and it carries word-count risk. Most candidates do better keeping the four-paragraph spine and developing the two bullets in depth.

Model Essay: Band C1+

Read carefully. Notice how the writer (1) discusses two of the three bullets, (2) adds a fresh point of their own, and (3) delivers a clear verdict. Highlighted phrases are the advanced structures examiners reward.

⭐ Note — this model demonstrates the STRETCH version (with an optional fourth body paragraph adding the writer's own point). For most candidates, the safer orthodox structure is intro + 2 bullets developed in depth + conclusion. Read this model for the language and evaluation moves, but you do not need to copy the four-body structure. See Tab 6 for guidance on when the stretch own-point is worth adding.
INTROThe question of what drives a person into a particular career has long divided opinion. While several forces are at play, this essay will focus on two of the most powerful — financial reward and genuine personal interest — before adding a further factor of my own. I will argue that interest ultimately wins out.
BODY 1 — SALARYIt is undeniable that earning potential carries significant weight, particularly for young people burdened with debt or family responsibilities. A well-paid role offers security, status and the freedom to shape one's life. That said, the pull of money tends to fade once basic needs are met; studies consistently show that pay rises beyond a certain threshold produce little lasting satisfaction.
BODY 2 — INTERESTPersonal interest, by contrast, sustains engagement across decades rather than months. When people pursue work that genuinely fascinates them, they invest more effort and recover more quickly from setbacks. A doctor drawn to medicine by curiosity about the human body will almost certainly outperform one who chose the profession solely for its salary.
BODY 3 — OWN POINTA further factor worth adding to the discussion is exposure to real role models in childhood. Young people who grow up seeing engineers, teachers or entrepreneurs at close range are far more likely to picture themselves in those roles — a quiet but powerful influence that often predates both salary calculations and personal taste.
CONCLUSIONIn conclusion, although salary cannot be dismissed, it is interest — reinforced by early exposure to role models — that shapes a meaningful career. Were young people given richer contact with working professionals, workplaces would become far more motivated and resilient.

Total: 257 words ✓ (within 220-260)

Nominalisation (intro): "The question of what drives a person..." — turns a verb idea into an elegant noun phrase

Emphatic opener (Body 1): "It is undeniable that..." — concedes the obvious before qualifying

Concessive linker (Body 1): "That said,..." — pivots from advantage to limitation

Contrastive linker (Body 2): "by contrast" — signals the second bullet

Own-point signal (Body 3): "A further factor worth adding to the discussion is..." — the examiner IMMEDIATELY recognises independent thinking

Conditional inversion (Conclusion): "Were young people given richer contact..." — instead of "If young people were given..."

Concede-then-rebut (Conclusion): "Although salary cannot be dismissed, it is interest that..." — the examiner's favourite pattern.

⭐ Stretch: Your Own Point (Band 5+ only)

This is the paragraph that lifts a good essay to a top-band one. You add a factor NOT in the three bullets — showing independent thinking. Keep it shorter than the others (45-55 words).

What counts as "your own point"?

Any genuine factor that influences career choice but isn't "salary", "personal interest" or "family expectations". For this task, that could be: early exposure to role models, school and teachers, peer influence, the local economy, luck, personal values, the rise of social media careers, etc.
