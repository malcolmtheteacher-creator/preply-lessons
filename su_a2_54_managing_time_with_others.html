Strategies for Staying Focused at Work

These are five common strategies to help you concentrate better. Which ones do you already use? Which ones might help you?

Strategy 1: Find a Quiet Space

Work away from your usual office. You might work from home, a library, or a café. Less interruptions = more focus.

Strategy 2: Turn Off Notifications

Close your email, silence your phone, and disable app notifications. Check these only at set times.

Strategy 3: Use Time Blocks

Work in focused blocks (e.g., 90 minutes). Tell colleagues when you are available and when you are "in focus mode".

Strategy 4: Set Clear Boundaries

Tell people your working hours, when you check email, and when you are available for meetings.

Strategy 5: Plan Your Day

List your top 3 priorities at the start of the day. Focus on these before helping others with their tasks.

Clear or Courteous?

Look at these responses. Which ones are clear? Which are courteous? Are they both?

RESPONSE A
"No, I can't do this."
Clear: Yes, very clear. Courteous: No — it's too blunt and might hurt the relationship.
RESPONSE B
"I'm too busy, sorry."
Clear: Not very clear — why are you busy? What's your priority? Courteous: Yes, it sounds apologetic.
RESPONSE C
"I appreciate the request. Right now I'm focused on finishing my report. Could we discuss this tomorrow?"
Clear: Yes — you explain your priority. Courteous: Yes — you appreciate the request and offer a solution.

The Five-Step Process to Say 'No' Politely

Follow these steps to refuse a request while maintaining positive relationships.

Step 1: Ask Questions

Understand the request fully before responding. This shows you care about what they need.

Example: "When do you need this? How long will it take? What's the deadline?"

Step 2: Think About the Issue

Consider your priorities, your workload, and your deadlines. Is this something you can reasonably do?

Example: "I need to finish my own project by Friday. Can I take on this task?"

Step 3: Be Clear But Courteous

Say 'no' directly, but politely. Don't make excuses or over-apologize.

Example: "I appreciate you asking, but I'm not able to take this on right now."

Step 4: Explain Why

Give a brief reason so they understand it's not personal.

Example: "My current project has a tight deadline, and I want to give it my full attention."

Step 5: Offer Alternatives

Help them find a solution. Offer a different time, suggest someone else, or provide a partial solution.

Example: "I could help after next Friday, or maybe Sarah has capacity?"

Complete Example

"Hi, I'd like to understand more about this task. When do you need it? ... I appreciate you thinking of me, but I'm not able to take this on right now. My current deadline is Friday, and I need to focus fully on it. I could help you after next Friday, or I know that Carlos might have some capacity. Would either of those options work?"
