The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — A new home

One day there was a big flood. The water carried a small, wet mongoose into a garden in India. An English family lived in the house. A little boy called Teddy found the mongoose in the sun. He picked him up and took him inside.

The family gave the mongoose food and made him warm and dry. His name was Rikki-Tikki-Tavi. Soon Rikki was happy in his new home. He walked all around the house and sat on Teddy's shoulder. He decided that this was his family now, and he wanted to keep them safe.

But the garden was not safe. Two big cobras, Nag and his wife Nagaina, lived in the tall grass. They did not like the new mongoose. On his first morning outside, Rikki heard a cold hiss. He turned and saw a huge black snake. The family did not know it yet, but they would soon need this small, brave animal very much.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did Rikki come to live with the family?

2. Who lived in the garden?

1. A flood carried him into the garden. The boy Teddy found him and the family took him in, gave him food and made him warm.

2. Two cobras, Nag and Nagaina, who did not like the new mongoose.

Talk about it:
  • Rikki quickly felt that this was his family. Why do you think he wanted to protect them so soon?

Part 2 — The first fight

Rikki learned that the two cobras made all the small animals in the garden afraid. He knew the family was in danger too. One day, Nag came near Teddy in the garden. Rikki was small and fast. He jumped in and made the big snake move away, and the boy was safe.

Then, one dark night, Rikki heard the two cobras talking. They had a bad plan. They wanted to go inside the house and make the family leave for ever. Then the garden would be theirs. Rikki understood that he had to stop them.

So Rikki waited quietly in the dark. He waited until Nag fell asleep. Then he took hold of the big snake and did not let go. It was a long, hard fight. Rikki was very tired at the end — but the first cobra was gone, and the house was safer now.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did Rikki do when Nag came near Teddy?

2. What plan did the two cobras have?

1. He jumped in, made the snake move away, and kept the boy safe.

2. They wanted to go inside the house and make the family leave, so the garden would be theirs.

Talk about it:
  • Rikki was alone in the dark, fighting something much bigger than him. Why do we like brave people who help when no one is watching?

Part 3 — The last snake

Now only Nagaina was left, and she was very angry and very clever. She had hidden her eggs in the garden, under the melon plants. Baby cobras would come out of them. She wanted to hurt the family the next morning. Rikki knew he had to stop her for good.

So Rikki worked fast. He found the eggs and broke them, but he kept one. When Nagaina came to attack the family, Rikki showed her the last egg. She looked only at Rikki, not at the family. Then she took the egg and went down into her hole in the ground. Rikki went straight down after her, into the dark.

The whole garden was quiet. Everyone thought the little mongoose would not come back. Then the ground moved, and Rikki came out alone. The last cobra was gone. The family was safe at last, and everyone felt happy and calm. After that day, Rikki always kept the garden safe, and no snake ever came near the house again.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did Nagaina hide her eggs, and why did they matter?

2. How did Rikki stop the last cobra?

1. Under the melon plants in the garden. Baby cobras would come out of them, so they were the next danger.

2. He broke the eggs but one, used the last egg to get her attention away from the family, then followed her into her hole and did not let her get away.

Talk about it:
  • Rikki went down into the dark hole, where it was very dangerous. Was this brave, or too dangerous? What do you think?

Grammar: past simple & time order

We tell stories in the past. We also use small words to show the order of the events — what happened first, next and last.

Past simple = a finished action in the past. Many verbs add -ed: "Rikki waited." "He jumped in." But some verbs are irregular and change: find → found, take → took, come → came, see → saw, run → ran, go → went, hear → heard.
Time order words show the order of the story: first, then, next, after that, suddenly, finally. We usually put them at the start of the sentence, with a comma: "First, he waited. Then, he jumped. Finally, the family was safe."

Going deeper

Rudyard Kipling wrote this story a very long time ago, in 1894. It is in a famous book called The Jungle Book. It is an exciting animal story, but it is also about big ideas: being brave, helping your family, and keeping other people safe.

Why we still love it. A very small animal beats a very big enemy. He wins not because he is big, but because he is brave and he does not give up. People all over the world have loved this story for more than a hundred years, because we all like to see a small hero win.
Talk about it:
  • Rikki protects a family that is not the same kind of animal as him. Why do you think he cares about them so much?
  • The cobras want to protect their home and their babies too. Does this make you feel a little sorry for them? Why or why not?
  • Rikki is a small hero who beats a big enemy. Do you know other stories where a small or weak hero wins?
  • What would you have done if you were Rikki? Would you fight the snakes, or run away?
