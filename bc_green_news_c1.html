How to categorise

Positive words describe stories we find interesting, genuine, or hopeful.

Negative words describe stories we find unfair, boring, or frustrating.

Positive:

sexy, winning, sincere, passionate, relevant, legit, attention-grabbing, current

Negative:

tuned-out, vicious, silly, hypocritical, formulaic, hackneyed, weird, crass

How We Judge News

When we talk about news stories with others, we use phrases that show what we think. Let"s learn and practise them.

Key phrases for evaluating news

"That could work really well"

Use this when you think a story has real potential—it"s interesting, newsworthy, has all the right elements.

Practice: Say it with rising intonation on "well"—sounds optimistic and convinced.
"That"s crass!"

A quick, sharp response. Use this when a story is vulgar, offensive, or tasteless. Usually disapproving.

Practice: Say it with sharp stress on "crass" and falling intonation—sounds dismissive.
"Excellent!"

Simple, strong approval. The story is strong, relevant, engaging. Could be enthusiastic or understated depending on tone.

Practice: Try it enthusiastically (high pitch, strong volume), then quietly satisfied (lower pitch, slower pace).
"That doesn"t really grab me"

Polite criticism. The story isn"t engaging enough, doesn"t have enough "pull" or interest factor.

Practice: Say it with level intonation and slow pace—sounds thoughtful but unconvinced.

How to add more layers

You can make your evaluation stronger with:

  • Stress: "That"s REALLY crass" (more emphatic)
  • Pitch: Higher pitch = more excited; lower pitch = more serious
  • Volume: Louder = more convinced; quieter = more hesitant
  • Pace: Faster = more excited; slower = more thoughtful

Your Turn: Green News Editor

You work for "Green News", a trendy environmental news programme. Your job: review four environmental stories and pick the two best ones using the evaluative language you"ve learned.

3:00

The four stories

Read each story. Use the buttons to mark it as Selected (you want to run it) or Rejected (not right for the show). Choose exactly 2 stories.

Story 1: City Bans Single-Use Plastics

Amsterdam, Netherlands — Starting next month, the city has banned all single-use plastic bags, bottles, and takeaway containers. Businesses will switch to compostable alternatives. The move is expected to reduce ocean plastic by 40 tonnes per year. Local restaurants support the decision.

Sources: Environmental Ministry, Business Council

Story 2: Corporate Greenwashing Scandal

Global — A major fashion company promised to be "carbon neutral by 2025." An investigation reveals they"ve cut emissions by less than 5%, while spending millions on PR campaigns claiming green credentials. Environmental groups call it greenwashing—appearing green while doing little.

Sources: Investigative journalists, Environmental NGOs

Story 3: Teen Inventor Creates Water Filter

Kenya, Africa — A 16-year-old has designed a low-cost water filter using clay and sand that removes 99% of bacteria. With funding from a local charity, she"s installing 200 filters in villages without clean water. Already 5,000 people have access to clean drinking water.

Sources: Local community, Charity foundation

Story 4: Rainforest Rewilding Project Succeeds

Brazil — After ten years of replanting, a 50,000-hectare area of the Amazon has been restored to health. Wildlife is returning, soil is recovering, and carbon storage has increased. Local communities have created 300 jobs managing the forest sustainably.

Sources: Conservation International, Local leaders

Now, tell the stories

Using the phrases below, present your two chosen stories. Show why you picked them.

Telling:

"Have you heard...?" / "There"s been..." / "Apparently..."

Example: "Have you heard about the city that"s banning single-use plastics? There"s been a real push in Amsterdam..."

Evaluating:

"This one looks promising..." / "Sounds interesting..." / "It"s got potential..."

Example: "The teen inventor story sounds interesting—it"s got real human interest and shows innovation."

Deciding:

"I"m not convinced..." / "Let"s go with..." / "You"ve got a point..."

Example: "I"m not convinced by the rainforest story alone—it"s too general. Let"s go with the greenwashing scandal. That"s got conflict, and it matters now."

Why these stories work for "Green News":

Story 1 (Plastics): Practical, actionable, shows real change—relevant and current.

Story 2 (Greenwashing): Conflict, scandal, hypocrisy exposed—winning and sincere, calls out corporate dishonesty.

Story 3 (Teen Inventor): Human interest, innovation, positive impact—sincere, passionate, winning.

Story 4 (Rainforest): Large scale, success story, hope—winning and relevant, but less immediate.
