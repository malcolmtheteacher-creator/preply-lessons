Two Truths & A Lie

The Game Room — Spot the lie!

Can you spot the lie?

Three statements. One of them is not true. Can you guess which one? Click "Reveal" to check.

Set 1

1"I have been to 15 countries."
2"I can cook Italian food very well."
3"I once met a famous actor on a plane."
The lie is: #3

Meeting a famous actor on a plane is fun, but #1 and #2 sound more real about everyday life.

Set 2

1"I have two cats and a dog."
2"I have never eaten sushi."
3"I can speak 4 languages."
The lie is: #1

Having three pets is more unusual. #2 and #3 are common things people do.

Set 3

1"I broke my arm when I was 10."
2"I have never driven a car."
3"I won a singing competition at school."
The lie is: #2

Most people have driven a car. #1 and #3 are more specific memories.

Think about this: What made you think that was the lie? What makes something sound true?

How to Be a Good Liar

Tips for making your lie believable. Click each tip to see the secret.

Tip 1: Add Details

"I broke my arm" is easy to doubt.

"I broke my arm playing football when I was 10 — I was in hospital for 3 days" is much harder to doubt.

Details make lies sound real.

Tip 2: Keep the Same Energy

If your truths are exciting and your lie is boring, people will notice.

If all three statements sound the same way — calm, fast, detailed, funny — it's harder to spot which one is fake.

Same energy = harder to tell the difference.

Tip 3: Don't Over-Explain

If you talk too much about one statement, people think you're trying too hard.

Say all three statements with the same amount of time. Then stop. Don't keep talking about the lie.

Over-explaining = red flag.

Tip 4: Look Confident

The lie should feel as normal as the truth.

Speak clearly. Make eye contact. Believe what you're saying for 60 seconds. Even if it's a lie, you're telling a story.

Confidence makes it believable.

Useful Language Chips

Use these phrases to make your stories sound natural:

I've always...

A few years ago...
Every weekend, I...
