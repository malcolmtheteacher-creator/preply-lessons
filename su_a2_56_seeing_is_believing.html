Types of Slides

Click on each slide type to reveal more information. These are common structures used in presentations.

Common Slide Types
SLIDE TYPE
Title Slide
Introduces the presentation with main heading and presenter name. Sets the tone and attracts attention.
SLIDE TYPE
Welcome Slide
Greets the audience, explains what they will learn, and motivates them to listen carefully.
SLIDE TYPE
Bullet Points
Lists key information as short sentences. Helps audience follow main ideas without overwhelming them with text.
SLIDE TYPE
Diagrams
Shows relationships, processes, or structures visually. Helps explain complex ideas clearly and quickly.
SLIDE TYPE
Data/Numbers
Uses charts, graphs, or tables to present information. Makes statistics and comparisons easy to understand.
SLIDE TYPE
Visuals/Images
Photographs or illustrations that make the message real and memorable. Connects emotionally with the audience.
SLIDE TYPE
Bullet with Visual
Combines text points with images side by side. Balanced approach that informs and engages.
SLIDE TYPE
Comparison (Side by Side)
Shows two or more options or ideas next to each other. Makes differences and similarities clear.
SLIDE TYPE
Word Cloud
Displays important words in different sizes. Shows which topics are most important at a glance.
SLIDE TYPE
Dark Background
Uses dark colors with light text. Creates dramatic effect and is easier to read in dark rooms.
SLIDE TYPE
Numbers/Sections
Shows the presentation structure with numbered sections. Helps audience know where they are in the talk.
SLIDE TYPE
Title & Quote
Displays a powerful quote or key message. Emphasizes important ideas or creates memorable moments.

What Makes an Effective Slide

Click on each design tip to learn more. These principles help create slides that actually work.

Key Design Principles

DESIGN PRINCIPLE
Use bullets to help limit your text
Instead of paragraphs, use short bullet points (5-7 words each). Keeps slides simple and readable. Your audience should read the slide in 5 seconds.
DESIGN PRINCIPLE
Don't overuse visual effects and animation
Limit animations. Too many moving elements distract from your message. Use animation only for important points. If an animation doesn't help explain, remove it.
DESIGN PRINCIPLE
Think about the size and colour of your text
Make text large enough to read from the back of the room. Use high contrast between text and background. Dark text on light background (or light on dark). Avoid red/green combinations for colorblind viewers.
DESIGN PRINCIPLE
One main idea per slide
Each slide should communicate ONE idea. If you need to explain multiple ideas, create multiple slides. Helps audience follow your logic step by step.
DESIGN PRINCIPLE
Use consistent design
Keep the same fonts, colors, and layout throughout. Consistency makes the presentation feel professional and helps the audience focus on content, not the design.
DESIGN PRINCIPLE
Include high-quality images
Use clear, relevant images. Bad quality or unrelated images distract and look unprofessional. High-quality visuals support your message.

Storyboarding Your Slides

What is Storyboarding?

Storyboarding is a planning technique. You draw simple sketches of your slides BEFORE creating the real presentation. This helps you:

  • Plan the order of your ideas
  • Decide what type of slide you need (bullets, diagram, image, etc.)
  • See the flow of the presentation
  • Catch problems early
  • Save time when creating the real slides

Below is what a simple storyboard looks like. You don't need to be a good artist — just draw boxes and stick figures!

How to Storyboard

Step 1: Get paper or use a template. Draw rectangles for each slide.
Step 2: Write the slide number and type (Title, Bullets, Diagram, Image, etc.)
Step 3: Sketch the layout. Draw where text and visuals will go.
Step 4: Add notes below. Write what you will say when showing this slide.

Design Your Presentation

Your task: Storyboard a presentation titled "What makes an effective slide"

Task Instructions:

  • Explain your design choice — why did you choose this type of slide?

Slide 3: Example or Visual

What example will you show? Good slide or bad slide comparison? Image with explanation?
