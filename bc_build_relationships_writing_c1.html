Building Rapport in Written Communication

Rapport is about quickly building connection and trust through written words. Effective business writing humanises language and demonstrates sincerity and emotion.

What is Rapport?

Rapport in written communication means creating a sense of understanding, trust, and connection with your reader through your choice of words, tone, and approach.

Why Does It Matter for Business Writing?

Strong rapport helps you:

  • Build lasting professional relationships
  • Increase trust and credibility
  • Improve the effectiveness of your messages
  • Create positive workplace connections

Emotional Intelligence in Business Writing

Emotional intelligence (EQ) in business writing means understanding and expressing emotions appropriately to build stronger relationships.

Common Business Writing Tones

Choose your tone based on your relationship with the reader and the situation:

  • Apologetic: Acknowledging a mistake or concern
  • Professional: Formal and respectful, maintaining boundaries
  • Collaborative: Working together toward common goals
  • Supportive: Encouraging and helping the reader
  • Enthusiastic: Showing genuine interest and positivity
  • Diplomatic: Respectfully disagreeing or managing conflicts

Think about it: Which tone do you naturally use in your workplace emails? How could you adjust your tone to build stronger relationships?

Reflecting on Your Learning

Check your understanding by answering these reflection questions:

What is rapport in written communication?

Rapport is creating a sense of understanding, trust, and connection with your reader through your choice of words, tone, and approach. It means humanising your language and demonstrating sincerity and emotion.

Why is emotional intelligence important in business emails?

Emotional intelligence helps you understand and express emotions appropriately, which builds stronger relationships, increases trust, and makes your messages more effective and impactful.

Name three common tones used in business writing.

Any three of the following are correct: professional, collaborative, supportive, enthusiastic, diplomatic, or apologetic. The tone you choose depends on your relationship with the reader and the situation.

What should you avoid in writing that builds relationships?

You should avoid damaging behaviour such as: being insincere, using overly formal or cold language, dismissing the reader's feelings, being unclear about expectations, and failing to acknowledge the human side of communication.
