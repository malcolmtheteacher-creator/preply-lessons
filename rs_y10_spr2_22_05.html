Understanding Global Christianity

Christianity: A Global Religion

Christianity is the world's largest religion with nearly 2.4 billion adherents. But it's not evenly distributed geographically.

Historically, Christianity was centered in Europe and North America. Today, Christianity is growing fastest in Africa, Asia, and Latin America—the "Global South."

The Shifting Center of Christianity

Past (1900-1950): Christianity was primarily Western European and North American.

Present (2020+): Most Christians live in Africa, Asia, and Latin America. By 2050, it's estimated that only about 35% of Christians will be in Europe/North America.

Implications: Christian theology, worship styles, and priorities are being shaped by these growing communities.

Growth and Persecution

Growth in the Global South

Christianity is growing rapidly in Africa, Asia, and Latin America.

  • Africa: Fastest-growing continent; nearly 500 million Christians
  • Latin America: Majority Catholic; growing Pentecostalism
  • Asia: China and Southeast Asia seeing rapid growth

Reasons: Missionary work, grassroots conversion, cultural adaptation, addressing local spiritual needs.

Impact: New forms of worship, theology from liberation and contextual perspectives.

Persecution of Christians

Millions of Christians face persecution globally.

  • Open Doors World Watch List: Tracks persecution annually
  • Forms of persecution: Violence, imprisonment, discrimination, property destruction
  • Main persecutors: Authoritarian regimes, extremist groups, competing religions
  • Hotspots: North Korea, Nigeria, Pakistan, Iraq, Syria, China

Impact: Millions live in fear; some face martyrdom.

Key Resources

Open Doors International: Advocates for persecuted Christians and ranks countries by persecution severity.

Persecution Facts: Annually, over 300 million Christians face discrimination or violence. 4,000+ are killed for their faith.

Global Christianity in Focus

Case Study: Christianity in Nigeria

Nigeria has Africa's largest Christian population (around 90 million). But it's also a country of religious complexity, where Christian-Muslim tensions are significant.

Growth: Pentecostalism has exploded, with megachurches in Lagos attracting thousands.

Persecution: In the north, Christians face violence from extremist groups like Boko Haram. Church bombings have killed hundreds.

Theological response: Nigerian theology emphasizes spiritual warfare, healing, and prosperity—reflecting local contexts.

Case Study: Christianity in China

Christianity is growing despite government restrictions. Official estimates: 100+ million Christians (including both state-approved and underground churches).

Government control: Churches must register with state; Bible study groups are monitored; evangelism is restricted.

Underground churches: House churches operate secretly, risking imprisonment.

Theological shift: Chinese Christianity is developing its own theology, less dependent on Western missionaries.

Ecumenism: Unity Among Christians

The Challenge: Christianity is divided into thousands of denominations (Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant, Pentecostal, etc.). This fragmentation can weaken witness.

Ecumenical Movement: Efforts to promote unity and understanding among denominations.

World Council of Churches: Founded 1948, brings together 380+ churches from 120 countries to dialogue and collaborate.

Practical cooperation: Joint charities, interfaith dialogue, shared advocacy on social issues.

Reconciliation Work: Coventry Cathedral and Corrymeela

Coventry Cathedral (England): Bombed in WWII; rebuilt as a symbol of reconciliation. Its ministry focuses on peace and healing from conflict.

Corrymeela Community (Northern Ireland): Christian community working on reconciliation during and after the Troubles. Brings Protestants and Catholics together for dialogue.

Christian witness: These communities embody Jesus's teaching on forgiveness and peace-making, showing Christianity's potential to heal even deep communal wounds.

Apply Your Knowledge

Activity: Evaluate Global Christianity

Write a detailed response to this question:

Is Christianity growing or declining globally? Evaluate.

Consider:

  • Growth in Africa, Asia, Latin America vs. decline in Europe/North America
  • Changing forms of Christianity in different regions
  • Impact of persecution
  • Secularization in developed countries

Aim for 12-mark essay level (approximately 400-500 words).

Words: 0/600

Evaluate: Is Christianity a Dying Religion?

Essay question (12 marks): "Christianity is a dying religion." Evaluate this statement.

Essay Guidance

What to include:

  • Arguments that Christianity is declining (secularization in West, nominal Christianity, church attendance decline)
  • Arguments that Christianity is growing (Global South expansion, conversion rates, 2.4 billion adherents)
  • Regional differences (Europe vs. Africa vs. Asia)
  • Changing forms (Pentecostalism, prosperity gospel, house churches)
  • Impact of persecution and diaspora
  • A nuanced conclusion balancing global and regional perspectives

Structure: Introduction → Point 1 + evidence → Point 2 + evidence → Counterargument → Conclusion

Key Statements to Consider

  • "Church attendance is plummeting in Europe and North America. Christianity is dying in the developed world."
  • "Christianity is the world's largest religion with 2.4 billion adherents growing fastest in Africa and Asia."
  • "While the West secularizes, Christianity is being reborn in the Global South with vibrant, growing churches."
  • "Persecution is actually strengthening faith in many contexts—suffering produces commitment."
