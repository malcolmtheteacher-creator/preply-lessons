1. What is the design argument?
The design argument suggests that the complexity and order in the universe point to an intelligent designer (God), just as a watch implies a watchmaker.
2. What is the first cause argument?
The first cause argument claims that everything that exists must have been caused by something, and this chain of causes must begin with God as the first uncaused cause.
3. What is a religious experience?
A religious experience is a personal encounter or moment that someone believes shows them something divine — such as a vision, feeling of presence, or transformation.
4. Give one argument that people use to suggest God does not exist.
The problem of evil: if God is all-powerful and all-good, why would he allow suffering and evil in the world? This inconsistency suggests God may not exist.

The Relationship Between Religion and Science

Throughout history, people have understood the relationship between religion and science in different ways. Here are four major models used by scholars and believers.

Four Models

Conflict

View: Science and religion are at war. As science advances, religion retreats.

Key thinker: Richard Dawkins argues that religious belief is incompatible with a scientific worldview.

Example: Evolution seems to disprove the need for a creator.

Independence

View: Science and religion ask different questions and operate in different domains.

Key concept: Stephen Jay Gould called this NOMA — Non-Overlapping MAgisteria. Science answers "how?" Religion answers "why?"

Advantage: No conflict because they never directly compete.

Dialogue

View: Science and religion inform each other. They ask different questions that can deepen understanding.

Example: Scientific discoveries about the universe's age and structure inspire new theological thinking.

Strength: Acknowledges both as valuable without forcing agreement.

Integration

View: Science reveals how God creates and sustains the universe.

Key thinker: Teilhard de Chardin saw evolution as God's creative process unfolding.

Perspective: Science shows the method; faith shows the meaning.

A Brief History

Many people believe religion and science have always been enemies. But historians show this is partly a myth. Yes, Galileo faced trouble from the Church for supporting heliocentrism — but he was also a person of faith. The idea of permanent "warfare" was built up by 19th-century writers like Thomas Huxley, who wanted to promote science over religion.

Scientists of Faith

Many great scientists have been deeply religious:

  • Isaac Newton — Mathematician and physicist; wrote more about theology than physics.
  • Francis Collins — Genetic scientist who led the Human Genome Project; devout Christian.
  • Abdus Salam — Pakistani physicist and Muslim who won the Nobel Prize in Physics (1979).

Today, many scientists are religious, and many believers embrace scientific understanding. The relationship is more complex than simple conflict.

Essay Question: Critical Thinking

Statement: "Science and religion will always be in conflict."

Do you agree? Write 200–300 words. Structure your answer with evidence for the statement, evidence against it, and your own conclusion.

Structure your answer:
  • Introduce the statement — what does it mean?
  • Give evidence that supports it (an argument for conflict)
  • Give evidence that challenges it (reasons there might not be conflict)
  • Say what you think, with your reasoning
Remember: The strongest essays acknowledge both sides. Show that you understand why someone might disagree with you.

Check Your Knowledge

Click each question to test what you've learned.

  1. What does NOMA stand for, and who developed the concept?
    NOMA stands for Non-Overlapping MAgisteria. Stephen Jay Gould developed this concept to argue that science and religion operate in separate domains.
  2. Name the four models of the religion–science relationship.
    Conflict, Independence (NOMA), Dialogue, and Integration.
  3. What did Galileo face, and why is his story often misunderstood?
    Galileo was tried by the Inquisition for supporting heliocentrism. His story is often used to show "warfare" between science and religion, but he was himself a person of faith, and the conflict was more nuanced than popular versions suggest.
  4. Name one major scientist who was (or is) religious.
    Isaac Newton, Francis Collins, or Abdus Salam are all valid answers. Each was a significant scientist and a person of faith.
  5. Which model suggests that science and religion are fundamentally incompatible?
    The Conflict model. Thinkers like Richard Dawkins argue that as science advances, religious belief must retreat because they offer competing explanations.
