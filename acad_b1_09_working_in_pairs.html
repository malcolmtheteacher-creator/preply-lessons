Working in Pairs and Groups

Learn collaboration skills: turn-taking, suggesting, and reaching agreement

5:00

Turn-Taking

Speak and Listen Equally

Getting a turn: "Can I say something?" / "May I add a point?"

Giving someone a turn: "What do you think?" / "Would you like to contribute?"

Letting someone finish: "Let me just finish my thought..."

Interrupting politely: "That's interesting, but I'd like to add..."

Making Suggestions

Propose Ideas

Tentatively: "We could try..." / "What if we...?"

More confidently: "I suggest we..." / "I propose that..."

Building on ideas: "That's good. We could also..."

Questioning suggestions: "Do you really think that would work?"

Asking for Opinions

Include Everyone

Direct questions: "[Name], what do you think about this?"

Open questions: "Does anyone have ideas?"

Encouraging participation: "We haven't heard from everyone yet."

After silence: "I know it's difficult, but let's brainstorm together."

Reaching Agreement

Come to Decisions

Agreement: "I agree with that." / "That works for me."

Partial agreement: "That's mostly right, but we should also consider..."

Compromise: "What if we combine both ideas?"

Decision-making: "So, shall we go with this option?"

Dividing Tasks

Fair Responsibility

Volunteering: "I'll research the first part." / "I can write the introduction."

Assigning work: "Could you handle the graphics?" / "Would you be responsible for the conclusions?"

Sharing fairly: "Let's divide it so everyone has equal work."

Checking progress: "How is your section coming along?"

Essential Vocabulary

Collaborative Done together with others working as a team
Compromise Settlement reached by both sides giving something up
Consensus General agreement by all group members
Delegate Assign responsibility or work to someone
Contribute Give or add something to a common effort
Brainstorm Generate many ideas quickly without criticism
Liaison Communication or contact between groups
Mediate Help resolve a disagreement between parties

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: Make a Suggestion

In a group project about climate change, suggest researching solar energy.

"I suggest we research solar energy as a renewable solution. What do you think?"

Exercise 2: Divide Tasks

Your group needs to create a presentation on three topics. How do you divide work?

"Let's divide the topics equally. Sarah, would you cover topic 1? Marco, can you handle topic 2? I'll research topic 3. Sound fair?"

Exercise 3: Find a Compromise

Two group members disagree: one wants text-heavy slides, the other wants mainly graphics. What do you suggest?

"What if we combine both ideas? We could use clear text for key points and add graphics to illustrate them. That way both approaches work together."

Sample Group Conversation

Dialogue Example

Maria: "Okay, we need to plan our project on renewable energy. What should we focus on?"

David: "I think we should research solar and wind power."

Lisa: "That's good, but we could also include hydroelectric power."

Maria: "Great suggestions. So we have three energy sources. Let's divide the work. David, would you handle solar? Lisa, wind? And I can cover hydro?"

David: "That works for me."

Lisa: "Yes, that's fair. When should we meet to share our findings?"

Maria: "How about this weekend? Saturday afternoon?"

Both: "Perfect!"

Key Takeaways

✓ Listen actively to everyone's ideas

✓ Take equal turns speaking

✓ Suggest ideas respectfully

✓ Ask for others' opinions directly

✓ Find compromise when disagreeing

✓ Divide tasks fairly based on abilities

✓ Follow through on your responsibilities
