The task

The chart below shows the number of trips made by children in one country in 1990 and 2010 to travel to and from school using different modes of transport.

Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.

Data (millions of trips)

Car: 4m → 12m
Bus: 7m → 5m
Cycling: 6m → 2m
Walking: 5m → 4.8m
Walking + bus: 4m → 2m

Words: 0

Model answer (167 words)

The chart shows the number of trips made by children aged 5–12 to and from school in one country in 1990 and 2010, across five different modes of transport.

Overall, car use rose dramatically over the period, while every other mode of transport declined. Cars changed from being the least-used to the most-used method.

Car trips nearly tripled, climbing sharply from 4 million in 1990 to 12 million in 2010. By contrast, cycling saw the most dramatic drop, plummeting from 6 million to just 2 million — a fall of around two-thirds. Bus trips declined only slightly, from 7 million to 5 million. Walking alone remained relatively stable at around 5 million, dropping only marginally. Meanwhile, combined walking and bus trips fell by half, from 4 million to 2 million.

While car use surged, all other modes lost ground, showing a clear shift towards private vehicles over the 20-year period.

Set phrases used: nearly tripled, climbing sharply, plummeting, fell by (around two-thirds / half), declined only slightly, remained relatively stable, dropping only marginally, While X…, Y… (Frame 5), Meanwhile (Frame 6), changed from being the least-used to the most-used (Frame 4).
