What is Just War Theory and who developed it?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Aquinas developed Just War doctrine. Criteria: just cause, right intention, legitimate authority, last resort, reasonable chance of success, proportionality, discrimination (civilian protection).

What are the three Aquinas criteria for Just War?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Right authority (ruler, not individual), just cause (defense, recover stolen goods, punishment of wrongdoing), right intention (true cause, not greed). All three must be present.

What is Holy War (Jihad) in Islam and when is it justified?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Jihad = struggle against injustice. Greater jihad = inner spiritual struggle. Lesser jihad = armed defense. Justified to defend faith, protect Muslims, respond to aggression. Requires declaration and conditions.

What conditions must be met for Islamic Holy War (Jihad)?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Permission from legitimate imam/authority, just cause (defense of faith/people), last resort, discrimination (no killing non-combatants), proportionality, no treachery. Killing civilians forbidden.

What is pacifism and what do Christians believe?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Refusal to participate in violence/war. Quakers (Religious Society of Friends) oppose all wars. Jesus teaching: "turn the other cheek" (Matthew 5:39), "blessed are peacemakers" (Matthew 5:9).

What is the Islamic concept of Greater Jihad?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Inner spiritual struggle against ego, sin, and worldly temptation. More important than military jihad. Hadith: "the greater jihad is against your own soul." Personal moral development.

What do Quakers teach about war and pacifism?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Absolute opposition to war and violence. Inner Light (God presence in all people) makes killing wrong. Testimony of Simplicity, Peace, Integrity. Alternative service instead of military.

What are Weapons of Mass Destruction (WMDs) and why are they problematic?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Nuclear, chemical, biological weapons causing mass indiscriminate death. Violate just war principles (discrimination, proportionality). Both religions condemn WMDs as immoral and destabilizing.

What is terrorism and how do religions view it?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Deliberate violence against civilians to create fear and achieve political/religious aims. Both Christianity and Islam condemn terrorism. Violates just war theory and Islamic military ethics. Unjust means.

What do religions teach about refugees and displaced persons?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Christian: Good Samaritan parable (Luke 10:25-37) - help those in need. Catholic teaching: hospitality as moral duty. Islamic: Surah 9:6 - grant refuge to polytheists who seek protection.

What peace organizations exist from Christian and Islamic perspectives?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Christian Aid (Christian relief and development), Islamic Relief (Muslim humanitarian aid). Pax Christi (Catholic peace movement). Muslim Aid. Focus on conflict prevention, peacebuilding, justice.

Perspectives on Conflict & Peace

Just War Theory (Christian Perspective)

Developed by: Augustine, Aquinas refined it. Attempts to balance pacifism and realism.

Three categories: (1) Jus ad bellum (right to go to war), (2) Jus in bello (right conduct in war), (3) Jus post bellum (right after war).

Jus ad bellum criteria: Right authority, just cause, right intention, last resort, reasonable chance success, proportionality.

Modern application: Used to evaluate Gulf War, Kosovo, Afghanistan by Christian ethicists. Often disputed whether criteria truly met.

Holy War & Jihad (Islamic Perspective)

Two forms of Jihad: Greater jihad (spiritual struggle within self), Lesser jihad (defensive military struggle).

Conditions for military jihad: Legitimate authority declares it, just cause (defense against aggression, protection of faith), last resort tried, combatants distinguished from civilians, no excessive force.

Protection of non-combatants: Quran 2:190 - "fight those who fight you but do not transgress." Killing civilians strictly forbidden (Haram).

Modern debates: Extremists misuse jihad concept. Mainstream Islamic scholars condemn terrorism and suicide attacks as violating Islamic military ethics.

Pacifism: Christian Approach

Quaker witness: All violence is wrong. Inner Light (God) in every person makes killing immoral.

Biblical foundation: Jesus teaching on non-resistance (Matthew 5:38-42), love of enemies (Matthew 5:43-47), "put away the sword" (Matthew 26:52).

Cost: Pacifists historically persecuted. Some killed rather than fight in wars (conscience objection).

Modern relevance: Peace movements, conflict resolution, restorative justice draw on pacifist principles.

WMDs & Terrorism: Religious Responses

WMD condemnation: Both religions prohibit weapons that cannot discriminate between combatants and civilians.

Terrorism definition: Deliberate targeting of civilians to create fear for political/religious aims.

Religious condemnation: Christianity and Islam reject terrorism as unjust means. Violates sanctity of life, proportionality, discrimination principles.

Extremism issue: Some religious extremists justify terrorism wrongly. Mainstream religious leaders consistently oppose it.

Key Terms & Concepts

Essential Vocabulary

Just War Theory: Framework for evaluating whether war is morally justified.

Jus ad bellum: Right to go to war (authority, cause, intention, last resort, proportionality).

Jus in bello: Right conduct during war (discrimination, proportionality, no prohibited weapons).

Jus post bellum: Right after war (just terms, restitution, discrimination in punishment).

Jihad: Islamic concept of struggle. Greater jihad = spiritual struggle. Lesser jihad = armed defense.

Discrimination: Military principle: distinguish combatants from civilians; never target civilians deliberately.

Proportionality: Good achieved by war must outweigh harm caused. Excessive force forbidden.

Pacifism: Absolute opposition to violence and war. All disputes resolved non-violently.

WMD: Weapons of mass destruction (nuclear, chemical, biological); indiscriminate killing forbidden.

Terrorism: Deliberate violence against civilians to create fear for political/religious aims; condemned by both religions.

What is the difference between jus ad bellum, jus in bello, and jus post bellum?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Ad bellum = criteria for entering war justly. In bello = ethical conduct during combat. Post bellum = fair peace terms and treatment after war. Three stages of just war framework.

Why is discrimination a key principle in both Just War and Jihad?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Civilians are non-combatants; deliberately killing them is immoral. Just war and Islamic law both protect innocents. Weapons that cannot discriminate (WMDs) therefore forbidden.

How do peace organizations like Christian Aid and Islamic Relief practice peacebuilding?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Development projects addressing poverty and injustice; conflict prevention through dialogue; disaster relief; advocacy for justice. Prevent conflict by addressing root causes.
