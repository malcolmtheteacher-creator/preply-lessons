Essential University Vocabulary and Procedures

Studying abroad brings new experiences and vocabulary. Here are key terms and processes you'll encounter.

Accommodation

Where you live while studying. Options: student residence halls (dorms), private housing, host families, apartments.

Registration / Enrolment

The process of signing up for courses at the beginning of each term. Usually done online or at the registrar's office.

Tuition

The fee you pay for classes and university services. Does not include accommodation (housing) or meals.

Syllabus

The course outline provided by teachers. Shows what will be covered, required readings, assessment methods, and deadlines.

Prerequisites

Classes you must take before enrolling in a higher-level course. You cannot skip prerequisites.

GPA (Grade Point Average)

A number (usually 0.0-4.0) representing your overall academic performance. Important for scholarships and job applications.

Office Hours

Times when teachers are available to meet with students. Use these to ask questions or discuss your work.

Dealing with Culture Shock

Culture shock is normal when living in a new country. Understanding it helps you manage it.

Stages of Culture Shock

  • Excitement Phase (Week 1-2): Everything feels exciting and new
  • Confusion Phase (Weeks 2-8): Differences become frustrating. You miss home, feel tired, angry, or sad
  • Adjustment Phase (Months 2-6): You begin understanding the new culture and adapting
  • Acceptance Phase (Months 6+): You feel comfortable; the new culture becomes normal

Tips to Manage Culture Shock

  • Keep a routine (regular sleep, exercise, meals)
  • Meet people—join clubs or study groups
  • Learn the language basics, even if studies are in English
  • Explore the city systematically (find the library, grocery store, etc.)
  • Allow yourself to feel homesick—it's normal
  • Stay in touch with family, but don't isolate yourself
  • Try local food and experiences

Making Friends and Building Connections

  • Attend orientation: Meet other international and local students
  • Join clubs and societies: Share interests with people
  • Form study groups: Academic connections become friendships
  • Attend university events: Sports, cultural events, parties
  • Be open and friendly: Introduce yourself, ask questions, show interest
  • Use online platforms: Facebook groups, Discord, campus apps for connecting
  • Visit your country's student association: Meet others from your home country

Understanding University Situations

How would you handle each situation? What words would you use?

Situation 1

You want to attend office hours to discuss an assignment, but you're nervous about talking to your professor. What would you say?

Example: "Hello Dr. [Name], thank you for having office hours. I wanted to discuss my recent essay. I'm not sure if my argument is clear enough. Could you provide some feedback?"

Situation 2

You're feeling homesick and struggling academically. You want to talk to an international student advisor.

Example: "I've been feeling homesick since arriving, and I'm finding it difficult to concentrate on my studies. My grades are suffering, and I'm not sure how to adapt. Could you suggest some resources or support services available to international students?"

Situation 3

You want to join the student photography club but are nervous about being the only international student.

Example: "I'm interested in joining the photography club. I'm an international student, new to the university. What should I do to get involved? When and where do you meet?"

Situation 4

You don't understand the registration process for next semester's courses.

Example: "I need help understanding the course registration process. How do I access the registration system? Are there any prerequisites I should know about before enrolling? What if I'm unsure which courses to take?"
