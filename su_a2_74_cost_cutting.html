A2 Business Communication — Vocabulary, Opinions & Suggestions

Let's Talk About Workplace Spending

Think about a workplace you know — maybe your current job, a previous one, or a friend's workplace.

1. How does (or did) that workplace waste money?

Examples: leaving lights on, printing unnecessarily, heating/cooling empty rooms, single-sided printing, unused subscriptions, takeaway coffee cups instead of reusable ones.

2. How could that workplace be more environmentally-friendly?

Ideas: recycling programmes, energy-efficient equipment, remote work options, paperless systems, public transport incentives, reusable cups and containers.

3. Do you think workplaces will be more environmentally-friendly in the future?

Your answer — there's no single correct response. Consider regulations, climate concerns, cost savings, technology, and generational change.

Vocabulary in Practice

1. If a company is struggling, is it more important to reduce costs or boost income?

Usually both are necessary, but it depends on the situation. Reducing costs is often faster; boosting income takes longer. In a crisis, cost reduction might be the priority.

2. What can a company do if their overheads triple?

They could negotiate lower prices with suppliers, move to cheaper offices, reduce staff, cut non-essential services, or find ways to increase revenue to match the higher costs.

3. In a wedding in your country, who usually foots the bill?

This varies by culture. Traditionally in some countries the bride's family pays; in others it's shared or the couple pays themselves. Today, it's often a combination.

4. When you plan a holiday, do you budget before you go?

Your answer — do you plan how much to spend? How detailed is your holiday budget?

5. When you retire, would you prefer to receive a lump sum from your pension, or regular payments?

A lump sum gives you immediate control but you must manage it; regular payments provide security and steady income. Many people choose one or the other based on their situation.

Language for Suggestions

How do we suggest ideas in English?

Common Phrases

  • How about...? — How about closing the windows?
  • Why don't we...? — Why don't we ask people to wear warmer clothes?
  • What about...? — What about turning off the lights?
  • It might be worth... — It might be worth using less paper.
  • We should think about... — We should think about energy-saver mode on the photocopiers.

Grammar Pattern

Fill the gaps. Use the correct form (gerund -ing or infinitive):

1. How about the windows?

2. Why don't we people to wear warmer clothes?

3. What about less paper?

4. It might be worth off computer screens.

5. We should think about air-con costs.

Pattern Summary

With -ING (gerund): How about...? / What about...? / It might be worth... / We should think about...

With infinitive: Why don't we...?

Example: "Why don't we ask people to bring their own mugs?" (NOT "Why don't we asking...")

Meeting Task: Cost Cutting Plan

Scenario: Your office overheads have tripled in the past year. You need to find the best ways to cut costs.

Step 1: Brainstorm Ideas (2 minutes)

Look at these suggestions. Think: which ones would work best in your workplace or a workplace you know?

  • Wear less or more clothes to reduce air-con or heating costs
  • Turn off lights at the end of the day
  • Print on both sides to reduce paper costs
  • Open windows instead of using air-con
  • Use energy-saver mode on photocopiers
  • Avoid printing unless absolutely necessary
  • Turn off computer screens when not in use
  • Bring your own mug instead of using plastic or paper cups

Step 2: Choose the 3 Best Ideas

Discuss each option. Use the language you learned:

  • "How about turning off lights at the end of the day?"
  • "Why don't we ask people to bring their own mugs?"
  • "It might be worth using energy-saver mode..."
  • "What about printing on both sides?"

Step 3: Present Your Ideas

Be ready to explain why you chose these three. Use language like:

  • "We think this will save money because..."
  • "This is practical because..."
  • "Most people would accept this because..."

Reflection & Discussion

Think about these questions. There are no right or wrong answers — these are your opinions.

Question 1

What's an example of cost-cutting you've seen at home or at work?

Question 2

Is recycling important in your workplace or school?

Question 3

How often do you use air-con at work or at home? Is it expensive where you live?

Question 4

Do your colleagues or family members use their own mugs or reusable cups at work or school?

Question 5

What's one way you could reduce energy costs in your home? How would you do it?

Key Takeaway

In business, cost-cutting is often necessary, but it must be practical and fair to employees. The best ideas are those that save money and are easy to implement.
