RSVP: Répondez s'il vous plaît — "Please confirm if you will attend"

Regards: A polite closing phrase used in professional/semi-formal letters

Hereby: An old-fashioned, formal word meaning "by this letter" or "officially"

Attached: Included with the letter; often used for documents or photos

Enquire: British English for "ask" or "request information"

Sincerely: A formal closing phrase, often used in British English as "Yours sincerely"

Venue: The place or location where an event will be held

Refreshments: Food and drinks served at an event

Sign-Off Phrases: Formality Levels

Formal (Professional, Official)
Yours sincerely, — Traditional British formal closing
Respectfully yours, — Very formal, old-fashioned
Yours faithfully, — British formal when you don't know the person's name
Semi-Formal (Invitations, Professional Acquaintances)
With warm regards, — Friendly and professional
Best regards, — Professional but approachable
Kind regards, — Polite and professional
Informal (Friends, Casual)
See you soon, — Casual and friendly
Cheers, — British informal, friendly
Take care, — Warm and relaxed

RSVP Language

How to ask people to confirm they will attend:

Please RSVP by [date] — Standard invitation request
Please confirm your attendance by [date] — Clear and direct
We would appreciate confirmation by [date] — More formal and polite
Please let us know if you can join us — Casual and friendly

Identifying Tone: Three Versions

Read these three versions of the same message. Which one is semi-formal?

Version A: Too Formal
"I hereby respectfully invite you to attend a social gathering at my residence on the 15th of June at 18:00 hours. Your prompt confirmation of attendance is requested at your earliest convenience."
Version B: Semi-Formal ✓
"I would like to invite you to a housewarming party at my home on June 15th at 6:00 pm. We would appreciate it if you could let us know if you can attend. Hope to see you there!"
Version C: Too Informal
"Hey! Come over to my place on June 15th at 6 for a housewarming thing. Just let me know if you're coming. It'll be fun!"
Why is Version B Semi-Formal?
Version B balances politeness with warmth. It uses "would like to invite," acknowledges the guest, and includes "appreciate" — all semi-formal markers. But it's not stiff like Version A, and it includes warmth ("Hope to see you there!") that Version A lacks. Version C is too casual and lacks respect for the occasion.

Practice: Rewrite in Semi-Formal Tone

Each formal sentence below needs a semi-formal rewrite. Try to keep the meaning but make it more approachable.

Formal: "I hereby request your attendance at a social event on the aforementioned date."

Suggested Semi-Formal Rewrite
Semi-Formal: "I would like to invite you to an event on June 15th. Please let me know if you can join us."

Changes: "hereby request your attendance" → "would like to invite you"; "aforementioned date" → specific date; added warmth with "join us"

Formal: "Please provide confirmation of your attendance at your earliest convenience."

Suggested Semi-Formal Rewrite
Semi-Formal: "Could you please let us know if you can attend? We would appreciate your confirmation by June 10th."

Changes: "provide confirmation at your earliest convenience" → "let us know... by [date]"; added specific deadline; "we would appreciate" sounds warmer than passive formal language

Formal: "Refreshments and light sustenance will be provided throughout the duration of the event."

Suggested Semi-Formal Rewrite
Semi-Formal: "We will provide food and drinks throughout the evening."

Changes: "Refreshments and light sustenance" → "food and drinks"; "throughout the duration of" → "throughout the"; more direct and natural

The Housewarming Scenario

You have just moved into a new flat. You want to invite friends and colleagues to a housewarming party. Your letter should:

  • Explain the purpose of the event (housewarming)
  • Suggest items they could bring
  • Give clear directions or location details

Checklist: What to Include

Model Letter: Fill in the Gaps

Below is a model housewarming invitation with key phrases missing. Try to guess what should go in the gaps.

Dear [Friend's name],

I am delighted to invite you to a housewarming party at my new home. I have just moved to a lovely flat in the city centre and would very much like to celebrate this exciting occasion with friends.

The party will take place on Saturday, June 22nd at 6:00 pm at 25 Maple Street, London, SW11 2QP. If you need directions, please feel free to contact me.

If you would like to bring something, I would be most grateful. Flowers, wine, or snacks would all be wonderful.

We will serve food and drinks throughout the evening, so please come hungry!

Please RSVP by June 18th so I know how many guests to expect. You can reach me by email at sarah@example.com or by phone at 020 7946 0958.

I really hope you can make it. It would not be the same without you.

With warm regards,
Sarah

Show Complete Model Letter

Gaps filled:

  • "a housewarming party" — States the purpose clearly
  • "25 Maple Street, London, SW11 2QP" — Complete location with postal code
  • "Flowers, wine, or snacks" — Specific items (plural), not vague suggestions
  • "food and drinks" — Tells guests what to expect
  • "Please RSVP by June 18th" — Specific deadline for confirmation
  • "With warm regards," — Semi-formal sign-off, warm but not casual

Reflection: Your Formality Choices

After writing, consider these questions:

Did you use a semi-formal greeting? ("Dear...") or something else?
Which sign-off did you choose? Is it appropriate for the scenario?
Did you include specific details? (date, time, location, contact)
Are there any words that feel too formal or too casual? Could you adjust them?
Does your tone match the occasion? (celebratory for birthday, warm for farewell, welcoming for welcome event)
