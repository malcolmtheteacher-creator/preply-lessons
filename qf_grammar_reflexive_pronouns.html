🪞 Quick Fix: Reflexive Pronouns

Myself, yourself, himself... — when the subject and object are the same person

Level: B1

What's wrong here?

I really enjoyed on the trip.

I really enjoyed on the trip.

Missing a reflexive pronoun. The verb "enjoy" in English always needs a reflexive:

I really enjoyed myself on the trip.

When do we use reflexive pronouns?

1. Subject and object are the same person:

  • "She hurt herself." (She = the one who got hurt)
  • "He blamed himself." (He = the one blamed)

2. Certain verbs always take a reflexive: enjoy yourself, hurt yourself, introduce yourself, express yourself, behave yourself, help yourself, teach yourself

3. Do NOT use reflexive with these verbs: feel, relax, concentrate, wake up, sit down, get up, wash (British English)

Common error: "I feel myself tired" ✗ → "I feel tired" ✓

The reflexive pronouns

Pronoun Reflexive
I myself
you yourself
he himself
she herself
it itself
we ourselves
you (plural) yourselves
they themselves

Compare the two uses of reflexive pronouns. Then fix the sentences below.

✗ No Reflexive Needed

😴

"I feel tired." (not "feel myself")

"She relaxed." (not "relaxed herself")

"He woke up." (not "woke himself up")
