Reflexive Pronouns

Master myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves

Spot the Difference

Look at the wrong and correct sentences. What's the difference?

❌ Not correct
I hurt me when I fell.
✅ Correct
I hurt myself when I fell.

The Pattern

When the subject does something to themselves, use a reflexive pronoun:

I hurt myself

❌ Not correct
She did it by herself own.
✅ Correct
She did it by herself.

The Pattern

Use reflexive pronouns after "by" to mean "alone" or "without help":

by herself = alone, no one helped

❌ Not correct
We enjoyed.
✅ Correct
We enjoyed ourselves.

The Pattern

Some verbs need a reflexive pronoun to be complete:

enjoy → enjoy ourselves (not just "enjoy")

❌ Not correct
I washed myself hands.
✅ Correct
I washed my hands.

The Pattern

Don't use a reflexive pronoun when a possessive pronoun (my, your, his, her, etc.) is better:

I washed my hands. (not "myself hands")

Reflexive Pronoun Reference

Match each subject pronoun with its reflexive form:

Subject Pronoun Reflexive Pronoun Example
I myself I cut myself shaving.
you yourself Be careful! Don't hurt yourself.
he himself He made the decision himself.
she herself She surprised herself with her strength.
it itself The engine shut itself down.
we ourselves We made this ourselves without help.
you (plural) yourselves You solved the problem yourselves.
they themselves They taught themselves to code.

Three Key Uses

1. Subject = Object (the person acts on themselves)

She blamed herself for the mistake.

She = subject; herself = object (the same person)

2. By + Reflexive (meaning alone, no help)

He lives by himself in the city.

= He lives alone, no one with him

3. Emphatic Reflexive (for emphasis)

I myself don't believe it.

= I, personally, don't believe it (emphasis)

She made the cake herself.

= She, not someone else, made it (emphasis)
