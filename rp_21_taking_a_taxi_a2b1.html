Taking a Taxi

It is late, and your train is soon. A taxi stops next to you. Time to get in, say where you want to go, and be friendly.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is night. It is cold, and you are tired. Your last train leaves in twenty minutes. You have a heavy bag, and you are far from the station. You cannot walk — there is no time.

Then a taxi stops next to you. The window opens, and the driver says, "Where do you want to go?" This is good news! Now you need to say the place, be friendly, and pay at the end.

The driver knows the city very well. Tonight there is a football game, so the roads are busy. The driver may take a small road you do not know. That is OK — you can ask, in a nice way, "Is this the quick way?"

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you take taxis? Do you like to talk to the driver, or do you like to be quiet? Why?
  2. The driver takes a road you do not know. Do you say something, or do you wait and trust them?

Who's who

You
The Passenger 🧳

Tired and in a hurry. You want to get to the station fast — and you want to be friendly and polite.

The other person
The Taxi Driver 🚕

Kind and calm. They drive all day and know the city very well. They know the fast roads — trust them.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the friendly words: please, thank you, can you. It is OK to speak slowly. You can also say "Sorry, again please?" if you do not understand.

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the friendly words.

DriverGood evening! Where do you want to go?
YouHello. Can you take me to the station, please? My train is soon.
DriverNo problem. Get in! I can take you there.
YouThank you! Is it busy tonight?
DriverYes, very busy — there is a big football game. I will take a small road, OK?
YouA different road? Is this the quick way to the station?
DriverThe big road is full of cars tonight. My road is longer, but it is faster. Trust me!
YouOK. You know the city better than me.
DriverLook — the big road is full. No cars are moving! My road is better, you see?
YouYou are right! Sorry, I was worried about my train.
DriverNo problem! Here is the station. You have ten minutes. Easy!
YouGreat, thank you! How much is it, please?
DriverIt is eleven pounds.
YouHere is twelve pounds. Keep the change! Thank you very much!

From memory — don't look up!

1. Why does the driver take a small road, and not the big road?

2. The passenger is not sure about the new road. How do they ask about it, in a nice way?

3. The taxi is eleven pounds. How much does the passenger give? Why do they give more?

1. The big road is full of cars because of the football game. The small road is longer, but tonight it is faster.
2. They ask a question, not an order: "Is this the quick way to the station?" A question is polite — it is not angry.
3. They give twelve pounds — one pound more. They say "Keep the change." The extra money is a small thank you, because the driver was fast and kind.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use in any taxi. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Ask, don't order. A question is friendly: "Can you…?" and "please" and "thank you" do a lot of work. It is OK to speak slowly. If you do not understand, just say "Sorry, again please?"

🚕 Where you want to go

Phrase 1

You get in. You want to go to the station. Complete it: "Can you take me to the ___, please?"

"Can you take me to the station, please?"
"Can you take me to…?" is the easy, polite way to say the place. Change the last word for anywhere: "…to the airport", "…to the hotel", "…to this address".
Phrase 2

You are in a hurry, but you do not want to be rude. Which one is polite?

A. "Go fast! Faster!"
B. "Can you be quick, please? I have a train."
C. "Drive quickly now."
B — "Can you be quick, please? I have a train."
"Can you be quick, please?" asks nicely, and "I have a train" tells them why. A and C sound like an order. When you say why, people want to help you.

💬 A little small talk

Phrase 3

You want to say a friendly word. You ask about the traffic. Complete it: "Is it ___ tonight?"

"Is it busy tonight?"
"Busy" means "a lot of cars / a lot of people". It is an easy small-talk question. You can also ask: "Is it a long day for you?" or "Is it cold out there?"
Phrase 4

The driver says the traffic is bad tonight. You want to sound friendly, like a real person. Which reply is warm?

A. "Yes. Correct."
B. "Oh yes, I see! It is very busy."
B — "Oh yes, I see! It is very busy."
"Oh yes, I see!" shows you are listening, and adding your own words keeps the chat going. A is not wrong, but it is cold — it stops the chat.

🛣️ Asking about the road

Phrase 5

The driver turns onto a road you do not know. You want to ask — in a nice way. Put the words in order:

is thisthe quickway?
"Is this the quick way?"
A question is soft and polite — it is not "You are wrong!" You just want to know. You can also ask: "Is this the fast way to the station?"
Phrase 6

The driver explains, and you see they are right. You agree in a nice way. Complete it: "OK, you ___ the city better than me."

"OK, you know the city better than me."
This is a kind way to say "You are right, I trust you." It makes the driver feel good. Saying "you know best" is friendly, not weak.

💷 Paying and goodbye

Phrase 7

You arrive. Now you want to pay. Which question is polite?

A. "Money?"
B. "How much is it, please?"
C. "Give me the price."
B — "How much is it, please?"
"How much is it, please?" is the easy, polite way to ask the price. A and C sound cold or like an order. You can also say: "What do I pay?"
Phrase 8

The driver was fast and kind. You want to say a warm thank you and goodbye. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you very much! Have a good night!"
Add "very much" to make your thank you bigger. "Have a good night!" is a warm, easy goodbye. A smile helps too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in taxis, buses, shops — everywhere. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"Can you take me to ___, please?" — Where do you go by taxi? (the airport, work, home…)

"Is it ___ tonight?" — Think of a friendly small-talk question for a driver.

"How much is it, please?" — Where else do you ask this? (a shop, a café, a market…)

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole ride out loud — get in → say the place → small talk → ask about the road → pay → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your taxi. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the passenger 🧳

The driver speaks — you answer. Be polite: use please, thank you, and can you.

DriverGood evening! Where do you want to go?
DriverNo problem, get in! Busy night tonight.
DriverI will take a small road — the big road is full of cars.
DriverTrust me — the big road hasn't moved for twenty minutes. My way is clear.
DriverHere we are — the station, with time to spare. That's ten pounds.
DriverVery kind, thank you! Run for your train!

Round 2 — now YOU are the driver 🚕

Change places. You drive the taxi. Be friendly and calm. Say "No problem", "Get in", "Trust me", "Have a good night".

PassengerHello. Can you take me to the airport, please? My plane is soon.
PassengerThank you! Is it busy tonight?
PassengerIs this the quick way? I don't know this road.
PassengerYou're right! How much is it, please?
The Twist

🚧 The road is closed

One more time — no help this round. You are halfway to the station. Then you see it: the road is closed! There is a sign for a different way. Your train leaves in ten minutes. What do you say? Stay calm and friendly — do not blame the driver. Work together: Is there another road? Can you stop near the station and run? Agree a plan, and pay a fair price. Start the timer and talk!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said the place clearly, with "please".
  • I said one friendly, small-talk thing.
  • I asked about the road in a nice way — a question, not an order.
  • I asked the price and said "thank you".
  • I stayed friendly and calm the whole time.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

Long ago, in the American West, there was no taxi and no train. To go far, people took a stagecoach — a big cart with horses. The driver knew every road, every river, and every safe place.

You got on, you said the town, and you trusted the driver — a stranger. You did not know the way, but they did. When you arrived safe, you said a big thank you.

Tonight's taxi is the same, but easier: a stranger, a hard road, and you, trusting the person at the wheel.

Two questions to talk about

  1. We often trust a stranger — a driver, a pilot — to take us somewhere. Why? When do you stop trusting and say something?
  2. The old stagecoach driver was famous — everyone knew their name. Today's taxi driver is a stranger you see once. Is that better or worse? Why?
