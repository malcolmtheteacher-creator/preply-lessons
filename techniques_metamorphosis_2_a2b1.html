The Metamorphosis — Episode 2

Franz Kafka's story continues: a bowl of milk, a sister who helps, a picture on the wall — and an apple.

Today: you'll tell how life changes for Gregor and his family, and practise comparatives — bigger, slower, more tired, better, worse — to compare life before and after.
Previously… Gregor Samsa is a travelling salesman. He works very hard, and his money pays for his parents and his sister, Grete. One morning he wakes up and he is a big insect. He is late for work, and the chief clerk from his office comes to the flat. When Gregor opens his door, the chief clerk runs away. Gregor's father pushes him back into his room with a stick, and Gregor is hurt.
Before we read:
  • Last time you predicted what would happen next. Were you right?
  • Think of a big change in your life — a new job, a new home, a new baby. What was better before? What is better now?
  • When somebody in a family is ill for a long time, how does life change for the others?

Key words — think first, then check

Ten words for this episode. Think first — then check with Reveal.

A · What do they mean here? Look at the sentence

Read the sentence. What does the word in bold mean? Say it, then reveal.

1. The baby is hungry, so her father gives her some warm milk in a bottle.
milk (n) — the white drink that comes from cows. a glass of milk; milk in my coffee.
2. My grandmother is very kind. She makes us soup when we are ill and she always has time to listen. She looks after the whole family.
to look after (phrasal v) — to take care of someone; to give them what they need. Who looks after your dog when you're on holiday?
3. He didn't want anybody to see him, so he went under the table and stayed very still. He hid there for an hour.
to hide (v), past: hid — to go where nobody can see or find you, or to put something there. She hid the money under the bed.
4. The chair was too heavy for one person, so two of us carried it into the other room.
to carry (v) — to hold something and take it to another place. Can you carry this bag for me?

B · Which word means…?

Choose from: furniture · a wall · to faint. Say the word, then reveal.

5. …one side of a room — you can put pictures on it?
a wall (n) — There is a clock on the wall.
6. …to fall down suddenly and not know what is happening for a short time, because you are ill or have had a shock?
to faint (v) — It was very hot and she fainted.
7. …tables, chairs, beds, cupboards — the big things in a room?
furniture (n, uncountable) — We bought some new furniture. Not “furnitures”! One thing = a piece of furniture.

C · Choose the meaning — a / b / c

8. The family has not got much money now. They must be very careful with it. Life is hard.
  (a) easy and nice   (b) cold   (c) difficult
(c) — hard (adj) = difficult (a hard question) — and also = not soft (a hard bed). Adverb: to work hard.
9. He threw the ball, and it hit the window. Crash!
  (a) touched something quickly and hard   (b) missed   (c) went over
(a) — to hit (v), past: hit (no change). The car hit a tree.

D · Word families & two meanings

10. Look at these two sentences. Throw — which is the past form? What does it mean?
(1) Can you throw me the keys?
(2) He threw the ball to his dog.
to throw (v) — to make something fly through the air from your hand. Forms: throw – threw – thrown. throw away = put in the rubbish.

Episode 2, in three parts

Kafka's story, told again in easier English. Read each part; after it, try the quick check from memory, then talk about the question.

Part 1 — Milk and bread

It is evening when Gregor wakes up. He is hungry. Next to the door there is a bowl of milk with some white bread in it. Gregor is happy — milk was his favourite drink. His sister, Grete, is thinking of him!

He puts his head in the milk. But he can't eat well, because his side hurts. And the milk tastes horrible to him now. He turns away.

In the living room, it is very quiet. Before, his father always read the newspaper aloud in the evening. Now nobody reads. Gregor thinks: What a quiet life my family has. And I paid for this nice flat.

The next morning, Grete opens the door. She sees the milk. Gregor didn't drink it. So she brings him lots of different food: old vegetables, bones, cheese, dry bread. Gregor eats the old cheese first. He likes it much more than fresh food now.

Grete comes twice a day with food. When she comes in, Gregor hides under the sofa, because he knows she doesn't want to see him. When she goes, he comes out and eats.

Nobody in the family can understand Gregor. But Gregor can understand them. He listens at the door. One day he hears his father talk about money. There is some old money in a box — more than Gregor thought. Good, thinks Gregor. They can live on it for a year or two. But after that, somebody must work. His father is old and fat. His mother is ill. Grete is only seventeen.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why doesn't Gregor drink the milk?

2. What does Gregor like to eat now?

1. His side hurts, and the milk tastes horrible to him now — before, it was his favourite drink.

2. Old food — he eats the old cheese first. He doesn't like fresh food any more.

Discuss:
  • Grete brings lots of different food to find out what Gregor likes. What does this tell you about her?

Part 2 — The window and the walls

Weeks go by. Gregor sits at the window a lot. He looks out, but every day he can see less. Before, he could see the hospital across the street. Now he can't see it. The street looks grey and empty.

Nobody in the family comes into his room, only Grete. She opens the window for fresh air. She does it very fast, and she doesn't look at him. One day Gregor is at the window when she comes in. She jumps back and closes the door! Gregor understands: she is afraid of him. So he puts a sheet over the sofa, and he hides under it all the time.

Gregor finds a new game. He walks on the walls and on the ceiling! He likes hanging from the ceiling. It is more fun than the floor, and he can breathe better up there.

Grete sees the marks on the walls. She has an idea. Gregor needs more space. Let's take the furniture out of his room. She asks their mother to help.

Their mother comes into the room for the first time. She is afraid, but she wants to see her son. “But maybe he likes his furniture,” she says quietly. “Maybe it helps him remember his old life. When he gets better, he can find everything the same.”

Gregor hears this. He thinks: Mother is right. I don't want an empty room. I want to remember that I am a person.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why does Gregor hide under the sheet all the time?

2. Why does Grete want to take the furniture out? Why does their mother not want to?

1. Because Grete is afraid of him — once she saw him at the window and jumped back.

2. Grete thinks Gregor needs more space to walk on the walls. Their mother thinks the furniture helps him remember his old life.

Discuss:
  • Their mother says, “When he gets better…” Does she really believe he will get better? Do you?

Part 3 — The picture and the apple

But Grete wants to take everything out, and the two women carry the heavy furniture out of the room. It is hard work. Gregor watches. They take the big cupboard. They take his desk — he did his homework at that desk when he was a boy.

Gregor can't watch any more. He comes out. What can he save? On the wall there is a picture of a lady in a fur hat. He climbs up and holds on to the picture. They can't have this!

His mother comes back into the room. She sees a big brown insect on the wall. “Oh God, oh God!” she cries, and she faints onto the sofa.

“Gregor!” shouts Grete. It is the first time she speaks to him. She runs to get medicine for their mother. Gregor wants to help. He runs after her, into the next room. Grete turns and sees him. She drops a bottle and it breaks. She runs back to her mother and shuts the door. Now Gregor can't go back.

Then his father comes home. Gregor looks at him. His father is different! Before, he was a tired old man in a dressing gown. Now he stands tall in a blue uniform with gold buttons. He looks stronger and younger. He has a new job.

“Gregor broke out,” says Grete. His father is very angry. He takes some apples from the table and he throws them at Gregor, one after another. One apple hits Gregor's back and goes into it. The pain is terrible.

The door opens. His mother runs to his father and holds him. “Please,” she cries, “don't kill him!” And then Gregor can't see any more.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What does Gregor try to save, and why does his mother faint?

2. How is Gregor's father different now?

1. The picture of the lady in the fur hat. His mother sees him on the wall — a big brown insect — and faints.

2. Before, he was a tired old man in a dressing gown. Now he wears a blue uniform, stands tall and looks stronger and younger — he has a job.

Discuss:
  • The father throws apples at his own son. Is he angry, afraid, or both? What would you do in his place?

Grammar — comparatives: before and after

In this episode, everything changes. To compare before and now, we use comparatives: older, bigger, more tired, better, worse + than.

1 · Short adjectives: + -er (than)
• old → older   • cheap → cheaper   • big → bigger (one vowel + one consonant → double it)   • busy → busier (y → ier)
My new phone is smaller than my old one.
2 · Long adjectives: more + adjective (than)
• beautiful → more beautiful   • expensive → more expensive   • tired → more tired
The city is more expensive than the village.
3 · Irregular: good → better, bad → worse, far → further
The weather today is worse than yesterday.   Her English is better now.
Careful: never “more better” or “gooder”.

4. Hanging from the ceiling is (interesting) than sitting on the floor.

5. Gregor's father looks (young) in his uniform.

6. The furniture is (heavy) than the women think.

Going deeper

Gregor is an insect, but inside he is still Gregor. He still loves his family, and he still worries about money. But his body wants different things now — old food, dark places, the ceiling.

And look at his family. Before, Gregor worked and everybody else rested. Now the father works, the sister works, and they are stronger. It is almost as if Gregor's problem makes them better.

The picture of the lady is Gregor's last thing from his old life. He holds it with his whole body.

Go deeper — talk it through

Discuss:
  • Grete helps Gregor more than anybody. But she also takes his furniture away. Is she kind, or is she starting to forget her brother?
  • Why do you think Gregor wants to keep the picture of the lady?
  • The father is stronger now that he has a job. Why do you think work changes him so much?
