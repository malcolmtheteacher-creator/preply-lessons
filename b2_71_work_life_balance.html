Work-Life Balance Vocabulary

Click each card to reveal the definition.

Turn up To arrive or appear at a location; show up
Get someone down To make someone feel sad, depressed, or discouraged
Strike a balance To find an equilibrium between two competing things or responsibilities
Take over To assume control or dominate; to expand beyond appropriate limits
Miss out on To lose the opportunity to experience or benefit from something
Flexi-time A work arrangement allowing flexible hours, as long as total hours are worked
Overworked Having too much work to handle; working excessively without adequate rest
Stress-related Caused or worsened by anxiety and pressure from work or life circumstances

Global Work-Life Initiatives

United States: Some companies allow employees to take a nap whenever they want during work hours.
United Kingdom: By law, people must have a break every 4 hours of work.
Sweden: Some companies have introduced a 4-day working week.

Question: Do you have any initiatives like these in your country? What does your workplace do to help with work-life balance?

Giving Advice with Multiword Verbs

Scenario 1: Working Long Hours

Problem: "I work long hours and never see my children during the week."

Possible Solutions: flexi-time, fewer hours

You could try to negotiate flexi-time so you can adjust your hours. Or you could look for a role with fewer working hours.

Scenario 2: Double Workload

Problem: "My colleague has just left and now I'm doing two jobs!"

Hint: Use "overworked" or "changes"

You shouldn't carry on being so overworked. You should speak up about the need for changes in your workload or additional staffing.

Scenario 3: Weekend Work

Problem: "I have to take work home every weekend to meet deadlines."

Hint: Use "make an effort" and "strike a balance"

You need to make an effort to strike a better balance. Discuss workload priorities with your manager to manage deadlines more effectively.

Scenario 4: Sleep Problems

Problem: "Work-related stress keeps me awake at night!"

Hint: Use "less" and "take a break"

You should aim for less work or take a break. Consider stress management techniques or talking to a manager about workload reduction.

Management Meeting

You will participate in a management meeting to address staff work-life balance issues. Duration: 5 minutes.

5:00

Meeting Objective

Discuss staff work-life balance issues in your department and agree on solutions that can be implemented.

Manager Roles

Manager A: Flexible Working Hours

People in your department want flexible working hours. Many are parents who need to fit work around school times.

  • Identify the problems
  • Decide on solutions
  • Consider objections others might raise
  • Think of multiword verbs and collocations to use

Manager B: Stress Management

People in your department are extremely stressed. More people than ever have taken sick leave due to stress-related conditions. Morale is low.

Manager C: Remote Working

People in your department want to work partly from home. Much of their work is online (data inputting, project design, analysis) and doesn't require constant office presence.

Manager D: Workload Management

People are unhappy about working late or weekends to meet deadlines. They feel their workload is too heavy and impossible to achieve within normal hours.

Manager E: Email Communication

People complain about being expected to check emails outside work hours. Important information affecting their roles is often sent at these times.

Meeting Process: Each manager describes their department's problem and suggests solutions. Listen to others and offer solutions to their problems. Decide on one solution to each problem.

Useful Meeting Language

  • "I appreciate your concerns about..."
  • "We could try to solve this by..."
  • "That's a valid point. What if we..."
  • "Let me turn to the issue of..."
  • "So we've agreed that..."
