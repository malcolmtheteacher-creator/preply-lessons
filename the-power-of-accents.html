🎙️ The Power of Accents

Why the Way You Sound Changes How People See You

🎯 Can 30 Seconds of Speech Change Your Life?

Within moments of hearing someone speak, we make instant judgments about their intelligence, trustworthiness, education, and even their competence at their job. But how fair are these judgments—and what happens when the accent doesn't match the expectation?

7 Seconds

The time it takes for listeners to form judgments about a speaker's social class, education level, and personality—based purely on accent.

Source: University of Chicago, Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, 2023
28% Less Likely

In one study, job candidates with regional British accents (Yorkshire, Scouse, Geordie) were rated 28% less competent than candidates with "Received Pronunciation"—despite identical qualifications.

Source: Queen Mary University London, 2022
The "Foreign Doctor" Paradox

Research shows patients initially rate doctors with foreign accents as less competent—but after treatment, rate them as MORE caring and trustworthy than native-accented doctors.

Source: BMJ Open, Patient Perception Study, 2021
🎬 Fun Fact: Hollywood's Accent Problem Why do movie villains so often have British accents? Researchers call it "accent typecasting"—we associate certain accents with sophistication AND coldness. Meanwhile, American Southern accents get typecast as "friendly but simple." It's all unconscious bias!

Real Stories

Dr. Fatima Khalil, Cardiologist, Manchester: "When I first moved from Cairo, a patient actually asked to see 'a proper English doctor.' Now, 15 years later, patients specifically request me. My accent hasn't changed—but their assumptions have."

James, Software Engineer from Newcastle: "I toned down my Geordie accent for years in London meetings. Then I watched a TED talk about 'accent authenticity' and thought—why am I hiding who I am? Now I lean into it. People actually remember me!"

Professor Elena Varga, Hungarian linguist at Leeds: "The fascinating thing is that 'accent prestige' is completely arbitrary. In the 1800s, the accent we now call 'posh' was considered vulgar by the aristocracy. Accents gain and lose status like fashion trends."

🗣️ Expressions for Talking About Accents & Identity

Click each expression to reveal its meaning and practice it.

"to code-switch"

+
Meaning
To change the way you speak (accent, vocabulary, tone) depending on who you're talking to
Form
code-switch / code-switches / code-switched / code-switching
Register
Academic/professional; increasingly common in everyday conversation
"I find myself code-switching automatically—I speak differently with patients than I do with colleagues from back home."
🎯 NOW YOU TRY: Do you code-switch? In what situations do you notice yourself changing how you speak?

"to have preconceptions about"

+
Meaning
To have ideas or opinions formed before knowing the facts; assumptions made in advance
Form
have preconceptions about [noun/gerund]
Register
Neutral to formal; often used when discussing bias
"People often have preconceptions about doctors with foreign accents—they assume we trained somewhere less rigorous."
🎯 NOW YOU TRY: What preconceptions do you think British people might have about Egyptian professionals? Are any of them accurate?

"to win someone over"

+
Meaning
To gradually gain someone's support, trust, or approval—especially when they were initially skeptical
Form
win [someone] over / won over / winning over
Register
Informal to neutral; positive and warm
"The patient was skeptical at first, but Dr. Hassan won her over with his warmth and expertise."
🎯 NOW YOU TRY: Can you think of a time when you had to win someone over? What did you do?

🗣️ More Expressions About Identity & Perception

"to be judged on first impressions"

+
Meaning
To be evaluated based on someone's initial reaction to you, before they know you properly
Form
be judged on first impressions / judge on first impressions
Register
Neutral; often used when discussing fairness or bias
"It's frustrating to be judged on first impressions when you know you have so much more to offer."
🎯 NOW YOU TRY: In medicine, do you think first impressions matter more or less than in other professions? Why?

"to own something" (informal)

+
Meaning
To confidently embrace something about yourself instead of hiding or apologising for it
Form
own [noun] / own it
Register
Informal; empowering, modern usage
"Instead of trying to hide my accent, I decided to own it—it's part of who I am."
🎯 NOW YOU TRY: What's something about yourself that you've learned to "own" rather than hide?

Quick Review: All 5 Expressions

  1. to code-switch – changing how you speak for different audiences
  2. to have preconceptions about – assumptions made in advance
  3. to win someone over – gaining trust despite initial skepticism
  4. to be judged on first impressions – evaluated on initial reactions
  5. to own something – confidently embracing who you are
