Real Stories

Maya, 28, marketing manager, London: "I have 400 matches I've never messaged. It sounds insane, but when you have that many options, talking to any single one feels like closing a door. So I just... keep swiping."

Dr. Helen Fisher, biological anthropologist: "The brain isn't designed to process hundreds of romantic options. We evolved to choose from maybe a dozen people in our village. Apps have created a situation we have no evolutionary preparation for."

Carlos, 33, software engineer, Barcelona: "I met my wife on Tinder in 2019. But I also spent three years before that going on 100+ dates that went nowhere. The app didn't find me love - it just gave me access to enough people that eventually one worked out."

Think about it...

Dating apps promised to make finding love easier. Instead, many users report exhaustion, disappointment, and endless swiping. Have we solved loneliness, or just industrialized it?

C1 Vocabulary for Critical Discussion

Today's words help you analyze modern dating beyond surface-level chat. They're useful for discussing technology, consumerism, and social change more broadly.

commodify (verb)

▼
Meaning
To turn something into a product that can be bought, sold, or traded. Often used critically to describe treating people, relationships, or experiences as market goods.
Pronunciation
/kuh-MOD-ih-fy/ - stress on second syllable
"Critics argue that dating apps have commodified romance - we swipe through people like products in an online store."
"Social media has commodified attention. Our eyeballs are the product being sold to advertisers."
Word Family
commodity (n) - a product commodification (n) - the process commodified (adj) - made into a product

Now You Try

What else has been commodified in modern life? (Think: experiences, emotions, personal data, identity...) Use the word naturally in your answer.

curated (adjective)

▼
Meaning
Carefully selected and organized to present a particular image or experience. Originally from museums (a curated exhibition), now widely used for online self-presentation.
Pronunciation
/KYOOR-ay-tid/ - stress on first syllable
"His dating profile was heavily curated - only photos from flattering angles, only achievements, nothing vulnerable or real."
"Instagram shows us a curated version of people's lives, not reality."
Word Family
curate (v) - to carefully select curator (n) - person who selects/organizes curation (n) - the process of selecting

Now You Try

How curated is your online presence? What do you show, and what do you hide? Why?

the paradox of choice (noun phrase)

▼
Meaning
The psychological phenomenon where having more options leads to less satisfaction, more anxiety, and difficulty making decisions. Coined by psychologist Barry Schwartz.
Pronunciation
/PAIR-uh-doks uhv CHOYS/
"Dating apps are the perfect example of the paradox of choice - thousands of potential partners, yet people feel more single than ever."
"Netflix demonstrates the paradox of choice perfectly. I spend 30 minutes scrolling and then watch nothing."
Related Concepts
decision fatigue - exhaustion from too many choices FOMO - fear of missing out on better options analysis paralysis - inability to decide

Now You Try

Where do you experience the paradox of choice in your own life? How do you deal with it?

transactional (adjective)

▼
Meaning
Based on exchange - "what can you do for me?" Rather than genuine connection, relationships are treated as business deals where both parties calculate what they're getting.
Pronunciation
/tran-ZAK-shuh-nul/ - stress on second syllable
"Modern dating can feel very transactional - people evaluate partners based on height, salary, and social status like they're checking boxes."
"I don't want transactional friendships where we only help each other when it's convenient."
Word Family
transaction (n) - an exchange transact (v) - to conduct business
Contrast
Opposite: authentic, genuine, unconditional - relationships based on real connection rather than exchange value.

Now You Try

Is some level of "transactional" thinking normal in relationships? Where's the line between reasonable expectations and treating people like products?

superficial (adjective)

▼
Meaning
Concerned only with surface-level qualities rather than depth. In dating: judging people by appearance, job title, or bio rather than personality, values, or compatibility.
Pronunciation
/soo-per-FISH-ul/ - stress on third syllable
"Swiping based on photos alone is inherently superficial - but what's the alternative when you have 3 seconds per profile?"
"He seemed perfect on paper, but the conversation was superficial. We talked for hours without learning anything real about each other."
Word Family
superficiality (n) - the quality of being shallow superficially (adv) - in a shallow way surface-level (adj) - synonym
Nuance
Not always negative: "At a superficial level, the plan looks good" means "on first glance" - neutral, not critical. Context determines whether it's judgmental.

Now You Try

Are humans naturally superficial when choosing partners? Is physical attraction "superficial" or just... human?

Quick Review: 5 Words

  2. curated (adj) - carefully selected to present an image
  3. the paradox of choice (n) - more options = less satisfaction
  4. transactional (adj) - based on exchange, "what can you do for me?"
  5. superficial (adj) - concerned only with surface-level qualities
