Language Focus: Making and Accepting Excuses

Making Excuses

I really want to but...
I'd love to but...
You see the thing is...
I'm really sorry but...
It's really kind of you to ask, but...
Well, in actual fact...
I'm terribly sorry but...

Accepting Excuses

That's all right.
It's not a problem.
Never mind.
Don't worry about it.
No problem.
Please don't worry about it.

Practice Scenarios

Someone asks to borrow your phone.

"I'd love to help, but I need it right now. My battery's almost dead."

A friend invites you to dinner, but you have other plans.

"It's really kind of you to ask, but I've already got something planned that night. I'm terribly sorry!"

Your colleague suggests going to the cinema, but you don't want to go.

"I really want to, but I've got to finish a project. Don't worry about it though!"

You accidentally spill coffee on someone's desk.

Speaker A: "I'm really sorry! That was completely my fault."
Speaker B: "That's all right. It happens. Never mind."

You accidentally deleted someone's important notes from the computer.

Speaker A: "I'm terribly sorry. I've deleted your notes by accident. I feel awful."
Speaker B: "It's not a problem. I've got a backup. Please don't worry about it."

Your Turn: Role-Play Practice

For each situation, plan what you'd say. Use the phrases you've learned.

Situation 1: Forgotten Name

You meet your colleague's partner for the first time at a party three months ago. You see them again, but you can't remember their name. How do you handle this politely?

Situation 2: Bad Food at Dinner

A friend invited you to dinner at their house. The food tastes terrible, but they're clearly proud of it. They ask, "Do you like it?" How do you respond tactfully?

Situation 3: Spilled Drink

You accidentally spill your drink on your boss's white rug during a meeting. What do you say? How do you apologize properly?

Situation 4: Unwanted Invitation

Your neighbor invites you to a golf weekend you don't want to attend. They seem offended by the idea that you might say no. How do you decline politely but firmly?

Discussion Questions

Think about these questions. There are no wrong answers!

Q1: Cultural differences
Are there differences in how people handle awkward situations in different cultures? What's acceptable in one culture might be rude in another. What examples can you think of?
Q2: Honesty vs. politeness
Is it better to be completely honest ("I don't like your gift") or polite but dishonest ("It's lovely, thank you")? When might you choose one over the other?
Q3: Power dynamics
Does the relationship affect how you handle awkwardness? Is it harder to be tactful with your boss than with a friend? Why?
Q4: Immediate vs. later
Is it always better to address awkwardness immediately, or are there times when it's better to wait? Give examples.
Q5: Your experience
Tell me about an awkward situation you've experienced. How did you handle it? Would you do anything differently now?
Q6: Recovery
What's the best way to recover if you've already handled an awkward situation badly? Can you save the situation, or is it too late?
