Press Conference vs Boardroom

In a press conference, the audience does not know you. In a boardroom, they do. They know your track record, your weaknesses, your previous promises. That makes it harder.

↓ Tap to see the key differences

Press conference: You can spin. They do not have the data. You control the narrative.

Boardroom: They HAVE the data. They have seen your slides. They know the numbers. Spin does not work — strategy does.

The same 6 moves still apply, but you need to use them more carefully. Today you also get 3 new boardroom-specific techniques.

Boardroom Moves

You still have the original 6 moves. But boardroom situations need 3 more.

These work specifically when your audience already has the facts.

Move 7: The Pre-Empt

Address the problem BEFORE they bring it up. This takes away their power and shows you are in control.

"I know the first thing on everyone's mind is..." / "Before we get to questions, I want to address..." / "Let me get the difficult part out of the way first..."

↓ Tap for example

Situation: Your project is 3 months behind schedule.

You: "Before I walk you through the progress, I want to address the timeline directly. We are running 12 weeks behind the original schedule. I am going to explain exactly why, what we have done about it, and what the revised timeline looks like."

You named the problem first. Now they are listening to your explanation instead of waiting to attack you with it.

Move 8: The Trade-Off Frame

Present the problem as a choice, not a failure. There were two options — you chose the responsible one.

"We had a choice between X and Y..." / "The alternative was..." / "We could have done Z, but that would have meant..."

↓ Tap for example

Situation: You overspent your budget by 20%.

You: "We had a choice. We could have stayed within budget and launched a product that did not meet regulatory standards. Or we could invest in getting it right. We chose to get it right. The additional spend covers compliance testing that will save us significantly more in the long run."

The overspend is now a responsible decision, not a failure. The alternative was worse.

Move 9: The Numbers Pivot

When they throw a bad number at you, come back with a different number that tells a better story.

"That is one way to look at it. Another way is..." / "If we zoom out..." / "The number that matters more here is..."

↓ Tap for example

Board member: "Your cost per acquisition has gone up 40%."

You: "That is correct for Q3. And if we look at lifetime customer value, each new customer is now worth 3 times what they were last year. We are spending more to acquire higher-value customers. The ROI has actually improved."

Their number was real. Your number is also real — but it tells the opposite story. This is not spin; it is choosing which metric matters.

The Full Toolkit

You now have 9 moves:

1. Redirect 2. Empathy Shield 3. Vague Commitment 4. Context Bomb 5. Bridge 6. Non-Answer
7. Pre-Empt 8. Trade-Off Frame 9. Numbers Pivot

Moves 1-6 are for when people do not have the facts. Moves 7-9 are for when they do.

The Scenario

You are the Head of Product Development. Your department has overspent its annual budget by 25%. The board wants to cut your funding by 30% next year. You are presenting a case for why they should not. You have 5 senior executives in the room. One of them — David, the CFO — is openly hostile.

Your Opening (2 minutes)

You need to acknowledge the overspend, explain why, and argue for keeping your budget. Consider using the Pre-Empt — address the problem before they attack you with it.

The Scenario

You are a Regional Director. Your region's Q3 results are the worst in the company. Revenue down 18%. Two key clients lost. Your team is overworked and morale is low. You are presenting at the quarterly review. The CEO and five other regional directors are on the call. Everyone else had a good quarter. You did not.

Pressure Level

Before You Speak

Everyone else has just presented strong results. Now it is your turn. You have 90 seconds for your opening. The room already knows your numbers are bad.

Think about this: do you lead with the bad news or build up to it? Do you show vulnerability or confidence? Do you blame external factors or take ownership?
