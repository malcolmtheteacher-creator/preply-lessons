Warm-up

Out loud, before anything else — don't worry about mistakes, just talk.

SPEAK

Imagine you meet an old friend on the street. You haven't seen them for a long time. Say two or three things you would say first.

Listen in on this catch-up

Mia: Hi! Long time no see! What's new with you?

Tom: Hi! Well, I've started a new job. And guess what — my bike got stolen last week!

Mia: Oh no, that's terrible!

Tom: I know. Anyway, I'm meeting my sister this weekend. Do you want to come too?

Look at what Tom says. Three different grammar forms, all in a few short lines. Can you find all three? Say them before you check.

Check your answer CLICK
1. "I've started a new job" — have/has + done, for news with no exact time.
2. "my bike got stolen" — the passive, for something that happened to a thing.
3. "I'm meeting my sister this weekend" — a future form, for a fixed plan.

These three forms are the whole catch-up conversation. Learn them and you can talk about your life, your news, and your plans in simple English.

What's New?

The tense for sharing life news — you don't need to say exactly when.

Spot the pattern

  • "I've started a new job."
  • She's moved to a new flat."
  • "Have you seen Anna lately?"

What's the shared pattern? Say it, then check.

Check the pattern CLICK
have / has + past participle. Use it for news when you don't say exactly when. Compare: "I've started a new job" (recent news, no exact time) with "I started the job in May" (an exact time — this needs the simple past).
have / has + past participle  (+ lately / recently / just / already / yet)
One trap: if you say the exact time (yesterday, last week, in May), use the simple past, not the present perfect. "I started it in May." NOT "I've started it in May."

Guess What Happened

News about things — when we don't know or don't care who did it.

Spot the pattern

  • "The little cinema was closed down last year."
  • "The new supermarket was opened in March."
  • "My phone got stolen yesterday."

What do these have in common, and what's different about the last one? Say it, then check.

Check the pattern CLICK
All three put the thing first, because we don't know or don't care who did it.
was / were + past participle — simple news, finished.
got + past participle — the spoken favourite for bad luck: got stolen, got broken, got lost.
was / were + past participle  (finished news)
got + past participle  (spoken, often bad luck)

Quick check

"My phone screen yesterday."

What's Next?

Three ways to talk about the future — and each one tells your listener how fixed the plan is.

Spot the difference

  • "I'm going to visit my parents next month." (a plan)
  • "I'm meeting Tom on Friday." (fixed — it's in the diary)
  • "I'll call you later." (decided right now)

Three futures, three levels of "how fixed is this?". Talk it through, then check.

Check the idea CLICK
going to = a plan, decided before now. -ing = a fixed arrangement — the time and place are already set. will = a decision made right now, or an offer.
going to + verb  (a plan)
am / is / are + verb-ing  (a fixed arrangement)
will + verb  (a decision now / an offer)

The Catch-Up

Everything together, out loud. This is the whole point of the lesson.

Round 1 — you talk

A good friend you haven't seen for months sits down next to you: "So, tell me everything!"

Talk for one minute. Cover all three:

  • your news — what you've started, finished, or done recently
  • what's changed around you — what's been closed, opened, or lost
  • what's coming — your plans, arrangements, and offers
01:00

Round 2 — you ask

SPEAK

Now interview me — the same catch-up, other direction. Build your questions out loud with today's grammar:

  1. "Have you...?" — two different questions
  2. "Was anything... closed / opened / changed?" or "Did anything get lost / broken / stolen?" — one question about my town
  3. "Are you going to...?" / "Are you doing anything...?" — one question about my plans
  4. Finish with an offer using will, based on something I said.

Round 3 — pin it down in writing

Write the 3–5 sentence message you'd send that friend afterwards ("Great to see you — ..."). Use all three of today's forms; the checker will tell you which ones it finds.

Mix It Up

Real conversation never tells you which form to use. Here everything's shuffled.

Which form fits?

1. You see your friend's new haircut. "Wow, your hair looks different!" — "Yeah, I ___ it yesterday."

2. Your friend asks about the old cinema. "Oh, it ___ last year — it's a supermarket now."

3. The phone rings mid-dinner. "Leave it — I ___ them back later."
