Let's Think About This

Imagine you haven't seen an old friend in 10 years. They suddenly send you a message online. What would you say back to them?

Consider:

  • What tone would you use – formal or friendly?
  • What kinds of questions would you ask?
  • How would your writing be different from a formal email to a business contact?

Today's focus: We're going to explore how to write informal, friendly emails that sound natural and bring you closer to people you care about.

Informal Writing Style

When writing to old friends, the style is completely different from formal writing. It should sound like you're actually talking to them.

Key Features of Informal Style

Contractions & Short Forms

Why? They make your writing sound more natural and conversational.

  • "How is your mother?" → "How's your mum?"
  • "I have two children..." → "I've got two children..."
  • "It has been a long time" → "It's been ages"

Conversational Language

Use everyday phrases:

  • "Are you still in touch with anyone from school?" (NOT "Do you still have contact...")
  • "I go back to see my parents from time to time" (NOT "I visit my parents occasionally")
  • "It's been ages" (NOT "Yes, it has been a long time")
  • "Stay in touch!" or "Looking forward to catching up" (NOT "I am looking forward to hearing from you")

Punctuation in Informal Writing

Yes to these:

  • Exclamation marks (!) – to show enthusiasm: "That's amazing!"
  • Question marks (?) – for genuine questions: "How's life treating you?"
  • Dashes (–) – to add extra info: "I've got two kids – Lizzie's 6 and Jack's 4"

Usually not these:

  • Colons (:) – too formal
  • Semicolons (;) – too formal

Emphasis Words

Add personality with these:

  • "How on earth did you find me?" (emphasis on surprise)
  • "It's been ages!" (emphasis on how long it's been)
  • "That's brilliant!" (showing genuine pleasure)

Language Practice

Let's work through seven key transformations. For each one, we're making formal sentences sound natural and friendly.

1Contractions with "is" and "how"

Original: "How is your mother?"

Informal: "How's your mum?"

What changed: "is" → "is" (contraction) + "mother" → "mum" (more familiar)

2Contractions with "have" + Adding details

Original: "I have two children, but I haven't mentioned their names."

Informal: "I've got two children – Lizzie's 6 and Jack's 4."

What changed: "have" → "I've got" (natural spoken form) + adds specific info with dashes

3Changing "Do you still have contact..." to conversational form

Original: "Do you still have contact with anyone from school?"

Informal: "Are you still in touch with anyone from school?"

What changed: "have contact" → "in touch" (much more natural)

4Changing formal frequency expressions

Original: "I visit my parents occasionally."

Informal: "I go back to see my parents from time to time."

What changed: "visit" + "occasionally" → "go back to see" + "from time to time" (sounds more conversational)

5Adding emphasis with "on earth"

Original: "How did you find me?"

Informal: "How on earth did you find me?"

What changed: Added "on earth" to show surprise and emotion (very natural in speech)

6Using "it's been ages"

Original: "Yes, it has been a long time."

Informal: "Yes, it's been ages!"

What changed: "has been" → "it's been" (contraction) + "a long time" → "ages" (colloquial, friendly)

7Making the closing more natural

Original: "I am looking forward to hearing from you."

Informal: "Stay in touch!" or "Looking forward to catching up!"

What changed: Removed "I am," shortened to action phrase (much more friendly and direct)

Your Task

Here's the situation: You receive an email from John, an old school friend you haven't spoken to in 10 years. He moved to America when you were both 16, and now he's living in North Carolina. He's reached out to reconnect.

From: John Wilson <j.wilson@email.com>
To: You
Subject: Long time no talk!
Hi! It's been absolutely ages! I can't believe it's been 10 years since we left school. I've been thinking about my old friends back home, and I found you on Facebook. How on earth have you been all this time? I'm living in North Carolina now, working in tech. It's great, but I do miss the UK sometimes. I'd love to hear what you've been up to. Are you still in the same area? Do you ever see anyone from school? Would be brilliant to catch up properly! John

What You Need to Do

  1. Plan your reply: What key information do you want to include? (Where you live, what you do, people you keep in touch with, questions for John?)
  2. Write your email reply: Aim for 100-150 words
  3. Use informal style: Contractions, conversational language, appropriate punctuation
  4. Ask questions: Use the natural question forms we practised

Check Your Work Against These

  • Relevant information included (about you, your life, questions)
  • Logical organization (greeting, main content, closing)
  • Informal, friendly tone throughout
  • Everyday language and contractions
  • Correct question forms (not too formal)
  • Uses exclamation marks, question marks, and dashes appropriately

  • How does adding small words like "on earth" or "from time to time" change the feeling of a message?

Optional Extension

Challenge: Take one of John's questions and create 2-3 different informal ways to ask it. For example:

  • "How's life been treating you these days?"
  • "What've you been up to?"
  • "Are you still in the same place?"

Notice how the same information can be expressed in many natural ways. Pick the one that sounds most like you.

Key Takeaway

Informal writing brings people together. When you write like you talk, your friends feel like they're actually hearing from you. That's what reconnecting is all about.
