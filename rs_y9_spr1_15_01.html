Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948)

After World War II, the world saw the horrors of the Holocaust and mass atrocities. In 1948, the United Nations created the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR) — a promise that such things would never happen again.

What Are Human Rights?

Human rights are basic freedoms and protections that belong to every person, simply because they are human. They are not given by governments — they exist regardless of where you live.

Key Rights in the UDHR

  • Right to life: Everyone has the right to live
  • Freedom of religion: Everyone can believe or not believe as they choose
  • Freedom from torture: No one can be tortured or treated cruelly
  • Right to education: Everyone has a right to learn
  • Freedom of thought and expression: Everyone can think and speak freely
  • Right to family life: Family relationships are protected

Inalienable Rights

The word "inalienable" means these rights cannot be taken away. Even if a government tries to remove them, they still belong to you.

But Where Do Rights Come From?

This is the big question: Are human rights given by God? By nature? Or are they just something humans invented to protect each other?

Religious View

Many believers argue rights are God-given — they come from our creation in God's image (Genesis 1:27) or from the divine nature within us.

Humanist View

Humanists say rights are human creations — agreements we make to live peacefully together. They don't need a god to be real.

Think about:

  • Can something be "real" even if humans created it? (e.g., money, language)
  • Does a right need to come from God to matter?
  • What's the difference between a "real" right and an "invented" right?
  • Is the UDHR powerful because it's binding law, or because people believe in it?

Quick-Fire Recall

1. What does UDHR stand for?
Universal Declaration of Human Rights (created by the UN in 1948)
2. What does "inalienable" mean?
Cannot be taken away — rights that belong to everyone automatically
3. What event led to the creation of the UDHR?
World War II and the Holocaust — people wanted to prevent such atrocities from happening again
4. Name two key rights from the UDHR
Any two of: right to life, freedom of religion, freedom from torture, right to education, freedom of expression, right to family
5. Do humans have rights only when governments recognize them?
No — human rights are universal and inalienable, meaning they exist regardless of government recognition
