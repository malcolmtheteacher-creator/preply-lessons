Justice Across Religious Traditions

Christianity: Love and Liberation

Core teaching: "Love your neighbour as yourself" (Mark 12:31)

The Parable of the Sheep and Goats (Matthew 25:31–46) teaches that Jesus sees himself in the hungry, homeless, and sick. Serving them is serving God.

Liberation Theology (Gustavo Gutiérrez, 1970s): God has a "preferential option for the poor" — God stands with those who suffer injustice and calls believers to fight oppression.

Modern example: Christian Aid, CAFOD work for justice for the world's poorest people.

Islam: Zakah and Community

Core teaching: Justice (adl) is a fundamental Islamic value.

Zakah (obligatory charity): 2.5% of a Muslim's wealth goes to those in need. It's not optional — it's a duty.

Sadaqah (voluntary charity): Giving beyond what's required, from compassion.

Ummah (global Muslim community): All Muslims are one body. If one part suffers, all feel it.

Modern example: Islamic Relief operates in over 80 countries, providing aid in disasters and development.

Sikhism: Sewa and Equality

Core teaching: Guru Nanak rejected the caste system. All people are equal before Waheguru (God).

Sewa (selfless service): Serving others without seeking reward is a spiritual practice.

Langar (free community kitchen): Open to everyone, rich or poor, any religion. Everyone eats together, proving equality.

Vand Chakna (sharing with others): Wealth and time should be shared with the community.

Modern example: Khalsa Aid provides humanitarian aid worldwide, from earthquakes to food insecurity.

Humanism: Reason and Empathy

Core teaching: Social justice through reason and empathy, without needing a divine mandate.

Peter Singer's "effective altruism": Use reason to figure out how to reduce suffering most effectively.

Modern example: Humanist organisations support causes like education, healthcare access, and poverty reduction through evidence-based approaches.

Statement to Evaluate

"You don't need religion to care about justice."

Do you agree or disagree? Why?

Think about:

  • Do secular approaches to justice (like humanism) work just as well as religious ones?
  • Does it matter whether justice comes from faith or reason?
  • Are religious believers more committed to justice? More effective?
  • What's the difference between doing good because God says so vs. because it's right?

Quick-Fire Recall

1. What is Liberation Theology?
Christian theology teaching that God stands with the poor and calls believers to fight injustice (Gustavo Gutiérrez)
2. What is Zakah?
Obligatory charity in Islam — 2.5% of wealth given to those in need
3. What's the difference between Zakah and Sadaqah?
Zakah is obligatory, Sadaqah is voluntary charity given from compassion
4. What is Sewa?
Selfless service in Sikhism — serving others without expecting reward
5. What is Langar and why is it important?
A free community kitchen open to all. It demonstrates Sikh belief in equality — everyone eats together regardless of status
