What is the Cosmological Argument?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The argument that the universe had a beginning, so it must have a cause, and that cause is God.

What is a contingent being?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: A contingent being is something that depends on something else for its existence; it might not have existed.

What is the infinite regress objection?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The objection that there might be an infinite chain of causes without needing a first cause.

How does the Big Bang support the Cosmological Argument?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The Big Bang theory shows the universe had a beginning, suggesting it must have a cause.

What is Religious Experience?

A religious experience is a personal encounter with God, the divine, or the sacred. Rather than using reason to argue for God's existence (as in the Design and Cosmological arguments), this argument says: people actually experience God directly, and this experience is evidence for His existence.

Religious experiences vary widely. Some are dramatic and sudden; others are quiet and gradual. Some are shared communally; others are deeply personal. Psychologist William James, in his classic work The Varieties of Religious Experience, identified four key characteristics of genuine religious experiences:

  1. Ineffability: The experience cannot be fully described in words
  2. Noetic Quality: The experience seems to reveal truth or knowledge
  3. Transience: The experience is brief, though its effects may last
  4. Passivity: The experience feels like something happening to you, not something you control

Types of Religious Experience

Numinous Experience: Encounters with something 'wholly other' and transcendent. Otto Rudolf described this as experiencing the 'mysterium tremendum' — something awesome, powerful, and even frightening. A believer might experience this in nature (standing before a vast mountain), in prayer, or in church.

Mystical Experience: A sense of union with God or ultimate reality. The mystical tradition in Christianity, Islam, Judaism, and other religions involves seeking direct experiential knowledge of the divine. Mystics describe feelings of unity, peace, and dissolution of the self into the divine.

Conversion Experience: A sudden, radical change in belief or commitment. Paul's conversion on the Damascus Road (Acts 9) is a classic Christian example — he was converted from persecuting Christians to becoming Christianity's greatest apostle. Conversion experiences are common across religions.

Healing or Answered Prayer: Experiences where prayer seems to result in healing or where a seemingly impossible situation improves. Believers interpret these as God's direct intervention.

Case Studies Across Traditions

Christian Example: St. Teresa of Ávila (16th century) described mystical experiences of profound intimacy with God. She wrote of feeling God's presence so intensely that everyday concerns dissolved. Such experiences shaped her understanding of prayer and deepened her faith.

Islamic Example: The Prophet Muhammad's experience in the cave of Hira, where he experienced revelation of the Quran, is understood by Muslims as God's direct communication. Sufi mysticism in Islam also emphasizes direct experiential knowledge (gnosis) of God.

Hindu Example: Mystical traditions in Hinduism speak of experiencing Brahman (ultimate reality) directly through meditation. Advaita Vedanta philosophy emphasizes the non-dual realization that the individual self (Atman) is identical with Brahman.

Arguments Supporting Religious Experience as Evidence

  • Widespread and consistent: People across cultures and centuries report similar experiences, suggesting they tap into something real
  • Transformative: Religious experiences genuinely change people's lives for the better (increased compassion, purpose, peace)
  • Verifiable effects: While we can't verify the experience itself, we can verify that believers report real psychological and behavioral changes
  • Experiential knowledge: Just as you know what love feels like through experience, not just from reading about it, believers know God through encounter

Psychological and Naturalistic Objections

Hallucinations and Psychology: Neuroscientist Michael Persinger showed that stimulating certain brain regions can trigger experiences similar to religious ones. This suggests religious experiences are brain states, not encounters with God.

Expectation and Culture: People's expectations, shaped by their religious tradition, may cause them to interpret ordinary experiences (a sense of peace, a coincidence) as divine encounters.

Emotional Disturbance: Some religious experiences occur in states of extreme emotion or stress, when people are vulnerable to delusion.

Contradictory Claims: Different religions report contradictory experiences (Christians experience the Trinity; Muslims experience absolute monotheism). They can't all be true, so perhaps none is reliable.

What is a religious experience?

Answer: A personal encounter with God, the divine, or the sacred, reported across cultures and religions.

List William James's four characteristics of religious experience.

Answer: Ineffability (cannot be described in words), noetic quality (seems to reveal truth), transience (brief but lasting effects), passivity (feels like something happening to you).

What is a numinous experience?

Answer: An encounter with something transcendent and 'wholly other' — something powerful, awesome, and potentially frightening.

What is a mystical experience?

Answer: A sense of union with God or ultimate reality, characterized by peace, unity, and sometimes dissolution of individual identity.

Name one psychological objection to religious experience as evidence for God.

Answer: Brain stimulation can trigger experiences similar to religious ones (showing they're brain states). OR: Expectations and cultural beliefs shape interpretation. OR: Contradictory experiences across religions suggest they're not reliable.
