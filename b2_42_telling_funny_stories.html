Sequencing & Summing Up

Key phrases for telling a story in order:

To start with At the beginning of the story
Suddenly Unexpectedly; without warning
Then The next thing that happened
Finally At the end of the story
In the end The final result or conclusion
To make a long story short To summarize quickly without all the details
To sum up To summarize the main points (formal)

Types of Funny Situations

Here are common situations that make great funny stories. Which appeal to you?

  • A misunderstanding between two people
  • A difficult or chaotic journey
  • A special event like a birthday that went wrong
  • An embarrassing moment (yours or someone else's)
  • When someone got unexpectedly angry
  • An incident during a holiday or trip
  • A time you lost something important (and found it in a funny way)
  • A surprise that caught everyone off guard

Reflect on your own experience:

Can you think of a funny situation from your own life? What made it funny? Was it the way things went wrong, the people involved, or how it ended?

Telling Your Story

Remember when you tell your story:

1. Use your sequencing phrases (To start with, Then, Suddenly, Finally)

2. Describe people's emotions to make it more interesting

3. Build to the funny moment—don't rush the punchline

4. Keep it 2-3 minutes long (you can use "To make a long story short..." if needed)

5. Make eye contact and enjoy telling it—your enthusiasm makes it funnier!

After Your Story

Ask for feedback using these questions:

Did the story make you laugh?

What was the funniest moment?

Which sequencing phrases did I use well?

Should I have explained something differently?
