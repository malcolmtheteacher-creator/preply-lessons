Understanding Nanotechnology

Nanotechnology represents the groundbreaking science of working with atoms and molecules at scales of billionths of a meter. This multidisciplinary field combines chemistry, physics, and biology to create innovative solutions to modern problems.

At the nanoscale, materials can be manipulated to have entirely new properties that differ from the same materials at larger sizes. For example, nanoscale particles can conduct electricity or light differently, opening up extraordinary applications in medicine, energy, and materials science.

Scientists use electron microscopes and other advanced techniques to observe and work with matter at this incredibly small level, understanding how atoms and molecules interact and influence the properties of substances.

Comprehension Questions

What is nanotechnology?

Nanotechnology is the science of working with matter at nanoscale (billionths of a meter) to create new materials and applications with unique properties.

Why is nanotechnology multidisciplinary?

It combines chemistry, physics, and biology to create innovative solutions, requiring knowledge from multiple scientific fields.

What are three applications of nanotechnology?

Medicine, energy production, and materials science are three key application areas for nanotechnology.

Error Correction Practice

Review these common grammar patterns used when discussing scientific topics.

Passive Voice with Complex Subjects

When discussing nanotechnology, passive constructions are frequent. For example:

Correct these sentences

1. "Atoms can be manipulated to creating new materials."

Atoms can be manipulated to create new materials. (Use infinitive form, not gerund)

2. "The microscope is used for to observe particles."

The microscope is used to observe particles. (Remove the extra "for")

3. "Properties is changed at nanoscale."

Properties are changed at nanoscale. (Use plural verb for plural subject)
