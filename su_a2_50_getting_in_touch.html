Features of Informal Writing:

  • Contractions: I'm, don't, we'll, haven't
  • Exclamation marks for enthusiasm!
  • Short sentences and paragraphs
  • Everyday vocabulary: mum, kids, loads of
  • Dashes — instead of semicolons
  • Questions to keep it conversational

Make It Informal

Rewrite these formal sentences in an informal style:

1. "How is your mother?"
How's your mum?
2. "I have two children. There is Elizabeth who is six and James who is nine."
I've got two kids — Lizzie's 6 and Jimmy's 9.
3. "Do you still maintain contact with people from school?"
Are you still in touch with anyone from school?
4. "I visit my parents occasionally."
I go back to see my parents from time to time.
5. "Yes, it has been a very long time."
Yeah, it's been ages!
6. "Please do not hesitate to contact me."
Drop me a message any time!

Read the Email

You received this email from an old school friend:

Hi!

How are you? It's been a long, long time since we saw each other. I can't believe I found you on Facebook!

As you know, my family moved to America when I was 16. Since then I've moved all around the US. I'm now living in Portland, Oregon. I've been here for three years now.

Anyway, I got married five years ago! My wife's name is Sarah and we've got a little girl called Maya — she's nearly two. What about you?

I'm working for a tech company now. It's OK but the hours are crazy sometimes! Remember when we used to talk about starting our own business? Ha!

Anyway, what have you been up to? Are you still in [your city]? Drop me a message — I'd love to catch up!

John

Review & Reflect

3 Informal Expressions to Remember

1. Contractions Use them everywhere: I'm, don't, we've, haven't. They make your writing feel natural and friendly.
2. Exclamation marks! Show enthusiasm and emotion. Compare "That's great" (neutral) with "That's great!" (excited).
3. Questions for conversation Ask questions to keep the dialogue going: "What about you?" "Are you still...?" "Do you remember...?"

2 Key Differences: Formal vs Informal

Grammar & Vocabulary Formal uses complex structures and advanced words. Informal uses simple sentences and everyday language: "Hi" instead of "Dear Sir/Madam", "mum" instead of "mother", "drop me a line" instead of "please contact me".
Tone & Structure Formal is serious and organized. Informal is conversational with short paragraphs, dashes, and lots of punctuation marks (!).

1 Thing to Practise After This Lesson

Write a message to someone you haven't spoken to in a while Use what you learned today. Remember the features of informal writing: contractions, exclamation marks, questions, and everyday vocabulary. Keep it natural and friendly!
