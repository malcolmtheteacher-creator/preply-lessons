Truth and Lies

B2 English Lesson | Expressing Belief, Doubt & Deception

Thinking About Truth & Lies

What would you do in these situations?

Read each scenario and think about how honest you would be:

Scenario 1: Your friend's new flat
Your friend has just moved into a new flat and asks what you think of it. You think it's small, dark, and the location is terrible. What do you say?
Honest: "It's quite small and dark. The location doesn't look ideal."

Softer: "It has potential, but maybe it could use some improvements."

White lie: "It's lovely! I'm sure you'll make it very nice."
Scenario 2: Providing an alibi
A friend asks you to say that he was with you on a certain night. Someone official asks you questions. What do you say?
Honest: "Actually, I can't confirm that. I wasn't with him that night."

Evasive: "I'm not sure I can remember what I was doing that night."

Dishonest: "Yes, he was definitely with me." (lying)
Scenario 3: Declining an invitation
A friend invites you to a party, but you really don't want to go. Do you invent an excuse?
Honest: "Thanks for the invite, but I'm not really in the mood for a party."

White lie: "I'd love to, but I have to work that day." (when you don't)

Diplomatic: "I appreciate it, but I can't make it this time."
Scenario 4: Taking responsibility
Your partner breaks a vase at your mother's house. Your mother asks if you know what happened. What do you say?
Honest: "Yes, my partner broke it by accident. I'm so sorry."

Evasive: "It looks like it broke. These things happen."

Dishonest: "I have no idea how it broke." (lying)

Types of Lies & Deception

English has many words to describe different kinds of dishonesty. Match each expression with its meaning:

A home truth
An unpleasant fact about someone that is difficult for them to hear
A white lie
A small, harmless lie told with good intentions (to avoid hurting someone's feelings)
A tall story
An exciting but unbelievable story; something that sounds exaggerated
The honest truth
Something that is completely true and sincere
A fib
A small, childish lie
A blatant lie
An obvious, shameless lie that is clearly false
A pack of lies
A series of untruths; multiple lies told together
To fool someone
To trick someone or make them believe something false, often as a joke
To make something up
To invent a story or an excuse
To mislead someone
To be dishonest by not telling the whole truth or giving incomplete information

Expressing Belief, Doubt & Disbelief

When someone tells you something, you can respond in different ways depending on whether you believe them or doubt them. Here are useful expressions:

  • It's the absolute truth. — I completely believe it's true.
  • I think it might well be true. — I probably believe it.
  • I think it could be true. — It's possible; I might believe it.
  • It has to be true. — It must be true.
  • It sounds possible. — That seems like it could happen.
  • That sounds reasonable. — That makes sense to me.
  • That sounds a bit unlikely. — That doesn't sound very believable.
  • That can't possibly be true. — I completely disbelieve it.
  • It seems rather improbable. — That doesn't seem likely.
  • This must be a pack of lies. — I believe you're lying.
  • I have my doubts. — I'm not confident it's true.
  • If you ask me, it's a complete lie. — I think you're lying.
  • I'm not at all sure. — I don't know if it's true or not.
  • I really have no idea. — I genuinely don't know.
  • It's difficult to tell one way or the other. — It could be true or false.
  • I'm skeptical about that. — I have some doubts, but I'm not completely sure.
  • That's questionable. — I have some doubts about it.

Practice: Match the Response to the Situation

Your friend says: "I met a famous actor yesterday at the supermarket!"
Possible responses:
• "That sounds unlikely. Which supermarket?"
• "You're joking! Which actor?"
• "If you ask me, that's not true."
• "How interesting! Tell me more."
Your friend says: "I studied for 10 hours yesterday."
Possible responses:
• "I believe you! You're very dedicated."
• "That seems possible. What were you studying?"
• "I have my doubts. You looked like you were on social media all day!"
• "That's quite impressive!"

Play: Truth or Lie

This is a speaking game. You will prepare 5 statements about yourself — some true and some false. Your partner must guess which are true and which are lies.

How to Play

  1. Step 1: Write 5 statements about yourself. They can be all true, all false, or a mixture.
  2. Step 2: Tell your partner one statement.
  3. Step 3: Your partner asks follow-up questions: When? Where? How? Why? What?
  4. Step 4: If you're lying, keep lying and try to be convincing! If you're telling the truth, stick to the facts.
  5. Step 5: Your partner guesses if it's true or false. If they guess correctly, they get a point. If not, you keep the point.
  6. Step 6: Take turns until all 10 statements (5 each) have been told.
  7. Step 7: Whoever has the most points wins!

Preparation: Make Your Statements

Think of 5 statements about yourself. Use the guide below to help you:

Statement 1 (About travel):
Example: "I've been to 12 countries." or "I once stayed in a castle."
Statement 2 (About an unusual experience):
Example: "I've appeared on television." or "I once won a competition."
Statement 3 (About a skill or talent):
Example: "I can speak 5 languages." or "I can juggle."
Statement 4 (About something embarrassing):
Example: "I once fell off a bike in front of my school." or "I forgot my best friend's birthday."
Statement 5 (Your choice — about anything!):
Example: "I have a pet snake." or "I hate chocolate."

During the Game: Tips for Success

If you're telling the truth:
  • Stay consistent with your details.
  • Give specific dates, places, and reasons.
  • Show confidence when answering questions.
If you're lying:
  • Create a convincing story with details.
  • Be consistent with your lie.
  • Act confident, even if you're not sure.
  • Don't make it too outlandish or people will know it's false.

Discussion & Reflection

When is it OK to tell a white lie?
Is it ever acceptable to tell a small lie to avoid hurting someone's feelings? When is it not OK?
Can you always tell when someone is lying?
What body language or signs make you suspicious that someone is not telling the truth?
How do you react when you discover someone has lied to you?
Do you confront them immediately? Do you lose trust? Can you forgive them?
Is honesty always the best policy?
Are there times when being completely honest might cause unnecessary harm?
