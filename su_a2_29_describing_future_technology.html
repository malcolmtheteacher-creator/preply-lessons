How Likely Is It?

When we talk about the future, we often use adverbs of probability to show how sure we are. Look at these adverbs:

Adverbs of Probability (from certain to uncertain)

Definitely, certainly, probably, possibly

Definitely and certainly = I am very sure ✓✓✓

Probably = I think this is likely, but I'm not certain ✓✓

Possibly = It could happen, but I'm not sure ✓

Ways to Give Your Opinion

In the previous section, you made predictions. Now look at different ways to introduce your opinion:

Sort These Expressions

Read each expression and decide: Is it for stating an opinion, giving a reason, or concluding?

Stating Opinion

  • ShowI think ...
  • ShowIn my opinion ...
  • ShowI reckon ...

Giving Reason

  • Show... because ...
  • Show... for a number of reasons ...
  • Show... for one key reason ...

Concluding

  • ShowI'm not sure but maybe ...
  • ShowThat's what I think, anyway.

Example Sentences

See how these expressions work together:

Example 1
I think electric cars will definitely become cheaper. That's what I think, anyway.
Example 2
In my opinion, people will probably work from home more, for one key reason—technology is getting better every year.
Example 3
I reckon Mars holidays will be possible in the future, but for a number of reasons, they will be very expensive.

Useful Sentence Structures

Use these patterns to make your predictions:

[Adverb], [subject] will [verb].

Example: "Definitely, robots will do more jobs in factories."

I think [subject] will [verb] because ...

Example: "I think electric cars will be more common because oil will be expensive."
