Language for Describing Abilities and Skills

When analysing suppliers, use precise language to express what they can and cannot do:

Expressing Competence and Capability

  • Be able to do / use — "They are able to provide custom solutions"
  • Be qualified at — "They are highly qualified at software integration"
  • Have basic skills — "They have basic customer service skills"
  • Do something at a low level — "They operate at a low level in quality control"
  • Be an expert user — "They are expert users of industry standards"
  • Be capable of — "They are capable of handling large-scale orders"
  • Have full mastery — "They have full mastery of their production process"

Pronunciation Focus: Weak Forms

In natural speech, pronouns and small words become weak forms:

  • "There are some differences..." — /ə/ (schwa sound for "are")
  • "They are very good at..." — /ə/ (schwa for "are")
  • "They can offer..." — /kən/ (weak form of "can")
  • "What they do..." — /wət ðə/ (weak forms throughout)

Native speakers often reduce these words to unstressed syllables. Practice listening to and reproducing these weak forms to sound more natural.

Practice: Use appropriate expressions

Scenario 1: Supplier X delivers on time but has limited technical expertise.

"Supplier X is able to meet deadlines, but they have only basic technical skills."

Or: "While they are capable of on-time delivery, they are not qualified at advanced technical problem-solving."

Scenario 2: Supplier Y is an expert in quality but cannot scale production quickly.

"Supplier Y has full mastery of quality control but is unable to scale quickly."

Or: "They are expert users of quality standards, though they operate at a low level of production flexibility."

Comparing Suppliers: Key Considerations

When evaluating suppliers, consider these important dimensions:

Quality & Reliability

Can they consistently deliver to standard?

Cost & Value

Do they offer competitive pricing without sacrificing quality?

Communication

Are they responsive and clear in their dealings?

Technical Expertise

Can they handle your technical requirements?

Scalability

Can they grow with your business needs?

Risk Management

Do they have contingency plans for disruptions?

Analysing Supplier Differences

Compare these two suppliers:

Supplier A: Established, expensive, excellent quality, slow to adapt

Supplier B: New, affordable, good quality, flexible and innovative

Key differences:

  • Supplier A is qualified at quality but operates at a low level of flexibility
  • Supplier B is capable of innovation and adaptation but has less proven track record
  • A offers reliability; B offers agility and cost savings
  • Different suppliers suit different business needs and timelines

Useful Language for Ranking Suppliers

  • "Most of us find that [Supplier X] offers better value."
  • "Only a few of us consider [Supplier Y] to be cost-effective."
  • "None of us prefer suppliers who cannot meet [specific criterion]."
  • "There are some differences in what they can offer us."
  • "They are very good at [specific skill], but limited in [another area]."

Timed Speaking Task

Task: Ask and answer questions about supplier skills and capabilities. Compare what different suppliers can offer.

Time remaining: 10:00

Your task structure (10 minutes):

  1. Opening (1 min): "I work with suppliers in the [industry] sector. Let me describe their key capabilities..."
  2. Supplier 1 (2 mins): Describe their abilities using precise language
  3. Supplier 2 (2 mins): Describe a second supplier's capabilities
  4. Comparison (2 mins): Compare the two suppliers' strengths and weaknesses
  5. Evaluation (2 mins): Explain which supplier you'd choose for different scenarios and why
  6. Closing (1 min): Summarize the key criteria for supplier selection

Useful questions and expressions:

  • "Can you... [base form verb]?" (speak English, use CAD software)
  • "Do you know how to... [base form]?" (handle bulk orders, meet tight deadlines)
  • "Are you good at... [verb+ing]?" (problem-solving, communication)
  • "What are you capable of?" / "What can you offer?"
  • "Are there any differences in what they provide?"
  • "Which supplier is best suited for..."

Preparation notes:

Discussion and Reflection

Question 1: Skills Assessment

How important is technical expertise versus communication skills when selecting a supplier? Which would you prioritize?

Question 2: Risk and Reliability

Would you prefer an established supplier with proven reliability or a new supplier offering innovation at lower cost? What are the trade-offs?

Question 3: Scalability and Growth

Why is scalability important? How should a growing business plan for supplier relationships as their needs increase?

Key Takeaways

  • Use precise language to describe what suppliers can and cannot do
  • No supplier excels in all areas—identify your priorities
  • Weak forms in pronunciation make English sound more natural
  • Good supplier relationships require clear communication about capabilities
  • Linking words naturally ("they can offer...") improves fluency
