Supplier Analysis

Evaluate suppliers and make informed business decisions

Essential Business Vocabulary

When evaluating suppliers, you need to understand key business and procurement terms.

Supplier / Vendor

A company that provides goods or services to another business

Procurement

The process of finding and purchasing goods or services

Tender

A formal request for suppliers to submit bids or proposals

Contract

A legal agreement between two parties (buyer and supplier)

Specification

Detailed description of what you need (quality, quantity, standards)

Compliance

Meeting required standards, laws, and safety regulations

Lead Time

Time between placing an order and receiving delivery

Quality Assurance

Systematic checks to ensure products meet standards

Due Diligence

Thorough investigation of a supplier's financial and operational health

Financial Stability

Ability of a supplier to remain in business and meet commitments

Language for Analysis and Evaluation

Use these phrases when analysing and making recommendations about suppliers.

Introducing Evidence

  • "Based on the data..." / "According to the figures..."
  • "The evidence suggests..." / "The results indicate..."
  • "The data shows that..."

Highlighting Concerns

  • "One significant concern is..."
  • "A notable weakness is..."
  • "However, there is a risk that..."
  • "The main limitation is..."

Making Overall Judgments

  • "Overall, X appears to..." / "On balance, X seems..."
  • "X demonstrates..." / "X shows clear evidence of..."
  • "In summary, X is the strongest candidate because..."

Making Recommendations

  • "It would be advisable to..."
  • "We recommend..." / "It is recommended that..."
  • "We should consider..." / "It would be prudent to..."
  • "Subject to [condition], we suggest..."

Practice Using Analysis Language

Complete this sentence: "Based on the data, Supplier B has the lowest prices, but is a significant concern."

Example answer: "Based on the data, Supplier B has the lowest prices, but their quality rating is a significant concern." or "...their lead time of 12 weeks is a significant concern."

How would you recommend choosing between two suppliers with equal pricing?

Example: "It would be advisable to compare their quality assurance systems and financial stability. Overall, we would recommend Supplier A because they meet minimum requirements and have demonstrated financial stability over five years."

Key Evaluation Criteria

When evaluating suppliers, assess them across multiple dimensions:

1. Price

The cost of goods or services. Consider unit price, volume discounts, payment terms.

2. Quality

Do products meet your specifications? What is their quality track record? What certifications do they hold?

3. Reliability

Can you depend on them? Do they meet deadlines? What is their reputation in the market?

4. Lead Times

How quickly can they deliver? Can they meet your production schedule? Do they offer flexibility?

5. Customer Service

How responsive are they? How do they handle problems? Do they provide technical support?

6. Compliance and Certification

Do they meet industry standards (ISO, safety regulations, environmental standards)? Are they audited?

7. Financial Stability

Will they stay in business? Can they fulfill long-term contracts? What is their financial track record?
