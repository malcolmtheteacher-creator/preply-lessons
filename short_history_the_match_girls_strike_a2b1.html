The Match Girls' Strike

In 1888, more than a thousand women and girls stopped work in a London factory. And they won.

Today: you will tell the true story of some workers who protested against a big company. You will use the past simple to tell it.
Let's see what comes to mind. Say your ideas out loud. There are no wrong answers.
  • What can workers do if their pay is very low?
  • Do you know any story about workers who protested?
  • Can newspapers change what companies do? How?

Eight words for the story

Read each sentence. Say what you think the word means. Then reveal the answer.

Activity 1 · Guess from the sentence

1. Her wages were very low. She worked all week, but she could not buy enough food.
wages (n) — the money you get for your work. Example: Wages in the factory were low.
2. If a worker was late, the boss took money from her pay. This money is called a fine.
a fine (n) — money you must pay because you broke a rule. Example: She paid a fine for being late.
3. The factory was dangerous. The chemicals made many workers ill.
dangerous (adj) — not safe; it can hurt you. Example: Fast cars on small roads are dangerous.
4. A journalist wrote an article about the factory. Thousands of people read it in the newspaper.
an article (n) — a piece of writing in a newspaper or magazine. Example: I read an article about football.

Activity 2 · Which word fits? (first letter given)

5. The boss said, "You can't work here now." The boss s_____ her. (She lost her job.)
to sack (v) — to tell someone to leave their job. Example: The company sacked ten workers.
6. The workers stopped work and left the building together. They w_____ o__.
to walk out (v) — to leave work suddenly as a protest. Example: The workers walked out at noon.

Activity 3 · Choose the meaning

7. The bosses and the workers made a deal.
(a) a fight   (b) an agreement   (c) a holiday
(b) — a deal (n) is an agreement between two sides.
8. The women started a union.
(a) a type of food   (b) a place to work   (c) a group that helps workers
(c) — a union (n) is a group of workers who help each other and ask for better pay and conditions.

Now use them — out loud

Before we read:
  • Use wages, fine and dangerous. What do you think the story is about?
  • Which words are about problems? Which words are about change? Sort them and say why.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory. Then reveal the answers.

Part 1 — The factory

In 1888, a big factory in London made matches. Most of the workers were women and girls. Some of them were only thirteen or fourteen years old. They worked about twelve hours a day, and their wages were very low.

The bosses also took money from their pay for small things. A worker paid a fine if she talked, if she was late, or if she dropped a match.

The work was dangerous, too. The women worked with a chemical called white phosphorus. They breathed it all day. Some of them got a terrible illness called "phossy jaw". It hurt the bones in the face. A few women died.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who worked in the factory?

2. What happened to a worker's pay if she talked or was late?

1. Mostly women and girls. Some were only thirteen or fourteen.

2. The bosses took money from it as a fine.

Discuss:
  • Why did the women keep working in these conditions? What else could they do?

Part 2 — The article and the walkout

In June 1888, a journalist called Annie Besant talked to some of the workers. She wrote an article about the factory. Many people read it and felt angry.

The company was angry too. The bosses told the workers to sign a paper. The paper said, "We are happy here." One woman said no. The company sacked her.

Then about 1,400 women and girls walked out. They had almost no money. But they marched through the streets, and the newspapers wrote about them. People in London sent them money.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did Annie Besant do?

2. Why did the women walk out?

1. She talked to workers and wrote an article about the factory.

2. The company sacked a woman who refused to sign the paper.

Discuss:
  • The women had almost no money, but they walked out. Was that brave? Would you do it?

Part 3 — The deal

After about two weeks, the bosses agreed to talk. They made a deal. The company stopped the fines, and the woman got her job back. The workers got a separate room for their meals. The women also started a union.

The strike did not solve every problem. Phossy jaw continued for many years. Later, Britain banned white phosphorus. But the strike showed something important: poor workers can win.

People still ask one question about this story. Some people say Annie Besant was the hero. Some historians say the women organised the strike themselves, and Besant joined later.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did the workers win?

2. What do some historians say about the strike?

1. No more fines, the sacked woman got her job back, a separate room for meals, and a union.

2. The women organised the strike themselves. Annie Besant joined later.

Discuss:
  • Who do you think is the hero of this story? Why?

Grammar — telling a story in the past

We use the past simple to tell a story about the past. We also use some verbs with to.

Past simple:
• Regular verbs end in -ed: walk → walked, stop → stopped, march → marched.
• Some verbs are irregular: go → went, write → wrote, win → won, make → made, get → got.
• Negative: did not (didn't) + base verb: "They didn't have much money."
Verb + to + base verb:
• "The bosses refused to talk." (They said no.)
• "They agreed to talk." (They said yes.)
• "The women decided to stop work." (They made a choice.)
Join your ideas: because (the reason), so (the result), but (a different idea).

1. Past simple — write the correct form

1. In 1888, the women (walk) out of the factory.

2. A journalist (write) an article.

3. The women (not / have) much money.

4. After two weeks, the workers (win).

1. walked · 2. wrote · 3. did not have (didn't have) · 4. won

2. Which verb? Write refused, agreed or decided

1. At first, the bosses said no. They to talk.

2. After two weeks, the bosses said yes. They to change the rules.

3. The women thought about it and made a choice. They to stop work.

1. refused · 2. agreed · 3. decided

3. Join the ideas — write because, so or but

1. The wages were very low, the women were angry.

2. The women stopped work they wanted fairer rules.

3. They had almost no money, many people helped them.

1. so (result) · 2. because (reason) · 3. but (different idea)

Keep these. You will use them to tell the whole story in the Speak tab.

Put it in order

These six things happened in the story, but they are in the wrong order. Say the correct order out loud. Then check.

A. The bosses agreed to a deal.
B. About 1,400 women and girls walked out.
C. The women started a union.
D. A journalist wrote an article about the factory.
E. The company sacked a woman.
F. People in London sent money to the strikers.
D → E → B → F → A → C
First the article. Then the company sacked a woman. Then the women walked out. Then people sent money. After two weeks there was a deal. Finally, the women started a union.
