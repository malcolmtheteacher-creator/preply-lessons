What is this?

An impossible choice appears. There is no right answer — only arguments. Both of you take a position, attack the other's, then deal with a twist that makes everything harder.

Each dilemma has 4 phases

Phase 1 — Take a position: Both of you read the dilemma. Each person states their position and defends it. (60 seconds each)
Phase 2 — Argue the opposite: Now argue AGAINST your own position. Defend the side you disagree with. (60 seconds each)
Phase 3 — The twist: New information appears that complicates everything. Discuss how it changes your thinking.
Phase 4 — Final verdict: Has your position changed? Why or why not?

Language for arguing both sides

"On one hand... on the other..." "I see the argument for..." "Playing devil's advocate..." "Even though I believe X, I can see why..." "The strongest argument against my position is..." "Having said that..." "This changes things because..."

Reflection

Which dilemma was hardest? Was it because you felt strongly about one side, or because both sides seemed equally valid?
How did it feel to argue against your own position? Did you discover anything? Did it make your original position stronger or weaker?
Did any twist genuinely change your mind? Or did you stick with your original gut feeling despite the new information?
In real life, when was the last time you changed your mind about something important? What convinced you?
