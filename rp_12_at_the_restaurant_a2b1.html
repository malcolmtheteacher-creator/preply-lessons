At the Restaurant

It's your sister's birthday. You have a table at a nice restaurant. Time to order good food, keep her safe, and check the bill — all in simple, polite English.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

It is Saturday evening. It is your sister's birthday, and you want a nice dinner for her. You booked a table at a small Italian restaurant. A table for two, at eight o'clock.

There is one important thing: your sister has a nut allergy. Nuts can make her very ill. So before you order, you must ask the waiter to check the food. This is easy to do — you just ask, politely and clearly.

The waiter is friendly and happy to help. At the end, there is one small problem: the bill is not correct. But that is fine. You can ask, politely, and the waiter will fix it.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like eating in restaurants? What is your favourite food to order?
  2. When your food or your bill is wrong, do you say something, or do you stay quiet? Why?

Who's who

You
The Diner 🍷

You are having dinner for your sister's birthday. Warm and polite. You want good food, safe food for her, and a correct bill.

The other person
The Waiter 🍽️

Kind and helpful. Happy to check the food with the kitchen, happy to help you choose — and happy to fix a mistake, if you ask nicely.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. It is OK to speak slowly. It is OK to ask the waiter to say something again — just say "Sorry, can you say that again, please?"

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the polite words.

WaiterGood evening, and welcome to Da Marco! Do you have a booking?
YouYes, we do. A table for two, under Bello. It's my sister's birthday today!
WaiterHappy birthday! Please, come this way. Here is your table, by the window. Can I get you a drink?
YouSome water for the table, please. And can we have a few more minutes with the menu?
WaiterOf course, take your time. Is there anything I should know?
YouYes, one thing. My sister has a nut allergy. Can you check with the kitchen which dishes are safe?
WaiterThank you for telling me. That is very important. The pesto is not safe, but many other dishes are fine. I will ask the chef.
YouThat's good to know, thank you. I can't decide between the fish and the lamb. What do you recommend?
WaiterThe lamb is very good tonight. I recommend the lamb.
YouPerfect — the lamb for me, and the fish for my sister, please.
Waiter(later) I hope you enjoyed your meal. Here is the bill.
YouIt was delicious, thank you. Excuse me — I think there is a small mistake. We didn't have any wine.
WaiterOh, you are right! I am so sorry. I will fix it now.
YouThank you. No problem at all!

From memory — don't look up!

1. Why does the diner tell the waiter about the nut allergy? And when — before or after ordering?

2. The diner can't choose between two dishes. What does the diner do?

3. There is a mistake on the bill. What is the mistake, and how does the diner say it — angrily or gently?

1. Because nuts can make the sister very ill, so the kitchen must check the food is safe. The diner says it before ordering — that is the safe time, when the food is not cooked yet.
2. The diner asks the waiter for help: "What do you recommend?" The waiter recommends the lamb, and the diner takes the advice.
3. The bill has wine on it, but they did not have any wine. The diner says it gently — "I think there is a small mistake" — not angrily. So the waiter is happy to fix it, and the evening stays nice.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use in any restaurant. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it with a smile. A friendly voice and the words please and thank you do a lot of work in English. You do not need long sentences. Short and polite is perfect. And it is always OK to ask the waiter for help.

🍽️ Booking & arriving

Phrase 1

You are calling the restaurant. You want to reserve a table for two people. Complete it: "I would like to book a ___ for two, please."

"I would like to book a table for two, please."
To "book a table" means to reserve it before you go (you can also say "make a reservation"). "I would like…" is the polite way to say "I want".
Phrase 2

You arrive at the restaurant. You have a booking. Which is the polite, clear way to say it?

A. "Table. Bello. Now."
B. "Hello, we have a table booked for two, under the name Bello."
C. "Where is my table?"
B — "Hello, we have a table booked for two, under the name Bello."
Start with "Hello", say how many people, and give the name — the waiter needs it to find your booking. A and C sound rude, like an order. B is warm and easy.

🧾 The menu & an allergy

Phrase 3

The waiter is ready to take your order, but you need more time to read the menu. What do you say? (Say it out loud first, then check.)

"Can we have a few more minutes, please?"
This asks for more time in a nice way. Other good ones: "We need a little more time, please." / "Can you come back in a minute?" You are not being rude — you just want to choose well.
Phrase 4

Someone with you has a nut allergy. You need the kitchen to check the food is safe. Complete it: "She has a nut ___. Can you ___ with the kitchen?"

"She has a nut allergy. Can you check with the kitchen?"
An "allergy" is when a food can make you ill. "Check with the kitchen" means ask the cooks. Always say this before you order, not after. It keeps the person safe.

🍷 Ordering

Phrase 5

You can't decide what to eat. You want the waiter to help you choose. Which is the best, friendliest way to ask?

A. "What is good? Is everything bad?"
B. "What do you recommend?"
C. "You choose. I don't care."
B — "What do you recommend?"
"What do you recommend?" asks the waiter, kindly, to tell you the best dish. It makes the waiter happy to help. A sounds unsure and rude; C sounds like you don't care about the meal.
Phrase 6

You like two dishes, and you can't choose between them. Complete it: "I can't ___ between the fish and the lamb."

"I can't decide between the fish and the lamb."
"I can't decide" means you like both and can't choose. You can also say "I can't choose between…". This is a nice way to open the door for a recommendation.

🛎️ The bill

Phrase 7

The meal is over. You want to pay. Ask for the bill politely. Put the words in order:

the bill,can we haveplease?
"Can we have the bill, please?"
The "bill" is the paper that says how much to pay. "Can we have …, please?" is a soft, polite way to ask for anything: "Can we have some water, please?", "Can we have the menu, please?"
Phrase 8

The bill is wrong — it has something on it that you did not have. You want it fixed, but you don't want to sound angry. Which line is best?

A. "This is wrong! You want more money from me!"
B. "Excuse me, I think there is a small mistake on the bill."
C. "Why is this so expensive?"
B — "Excuse me, I think there is a small mistake on the bill."
"I think there is a small mistake" is calm and kind. You are not saying the waiter is bad — just that something is wrong. So the waiter can fix it, and nobody is upset. A and C sound angry.

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work in restaurants, cafés, shops — everywhere. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"I would like ___, please." — What do you want to order or ask for? (a coffee, a table by the window, the menu…)

"Can we have ___, please?" — What can you ask for at a table? (some water, the bill, more bread…)

"I think there is a small mistake ___." — Where could there be a mistake? (on the bill, with my order…)

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole evening out loud — book a table → arrive → ask for a minute → the allergy → choose your food → the bill → the small mistake. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your table. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the diner 🍷

The waiter speaks — you answer. Be polite: use please, thank you, and I would like.

WaiterGood evening, welcome to Da Marco! Do you have a booking?
WaiterWonderful — here is your table. Can I get you a drink while you read the menu?
WaiterOf course. Is there anything I should know?
WaiterThank you for telling me. I will check every dish with the chef. Do you know what you want?
WaiterGood choice! (later) I hope you enjoyed your meal. Here is the bill.
WaiterHere you are, take your time.
WaiterOh, you are right! I am so sorry. I will fix it now.

Round 2 — now YOU are the waiter 🍽️

Change places. You work at the restaurant. Be friendly and helpful. Say "Welcome", "Of course", "I recommend…", "I'm sorry".

DinerHello, we have a table booked for two, under the name Alvarez.
DinerThank you. Before we order — my friend has a nut allergy. Can you check the food is safe?
DinerGood to know. I can't decide between the pasta and the lamb. What do you recommend?
DinerSounds lovely. And later, can we have the bill, please?
DinerExcuse me — I think there is a small mistake. We didn't have a dessert.
The Twist

🥜 A problem on the plate

One more round — no help this time. The food arrives. It looks lovely… but then you see nuts on your sister's plate! She has a nut allergy, so she cannot eat this. Nobody has eaten yet. Start the timer and speak: call the waiter calmly, say clearly what is wrong and why it is important, and ask for a new, safe dish. Stay polite and friendly — the waiter will help you if you are kind. What do you say?

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I gave the name and the number of people clearly.
  • I told the waiter about the allergy BEFORE ordering.
  • I asked for help to choose ("What do you recommend?").
  • I said the bill problem gently, not angrily.
  • I stayed polite and friendly the whole time.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

Eating out is not new. Two thousand years ago, in the Roman city of Pompeii, there were many small food shops on the streets. They were called thermopolia. They had hot food in big clay pots — a bit like fast food today.

Many people in Pompeii had no kitchen at home. So they ate on the street, bought from someone who cooked for money. In one old shop, we can still see what was on the menu: duck, pig, fish, and more.

So when you book a table, ask the waiter, and pay the bill, you are doing something very old. The people of Pompeii did the same — and yes, they also argued, politely, about the bill!
