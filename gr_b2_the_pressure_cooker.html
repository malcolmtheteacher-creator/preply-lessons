What is this?

A conversation game. Each round, a random scenario appears on screen — a situation where two people want different things. One of you plays Person A, the other plays Person B. You talk it out.

The catch: Halfway through each conversation, a twist appears — new information that changes everything. You have to adapt on the spot.

How each round works

Every round follows the same rhythm:

Read — A random scenario appears. Both of you read the situation and your roles. (30 seconds)
Talk — Start the conversation in your roles. Argue, negotiate, persuade, complain — whatever the situation needs. (3 minutes)
Twist — At 1:30, new information appears on screen. Both of you see it. React to it. (keep talking)
Debrief — Quick reflection question. Who won? What worked? (1 minute)

Before we start

Quick question: Think of the last time you had to persuade someone in real life — a shop, a landlord, a colleague, anyone. What happened? Who won? (1 minute)

Language you'll need across all rounds

"I understand, but..." "What if we..." "That's not acceptable because..." "I see your point, however..." "The problem with that is..." "Could you at least..." "I'm willing to... if you..." "Let me be honest with you..."

Reflection

Which scenario was the most difficult to handle? Was it the situation itself, or the twist that made it hard?
Did you find it easier to argue FOR something you wanted, or AGAINST something you didn't want? Which role felt more natural — attacking or defending?
In real life, how do you usually handle conflict? Do you avoid it, confront it directly, or try to find a compromise? Did any of these scenarios feel like a real situation you've been in?

Language check

Pick 3 phrases from today that you want to remember. Say them out loud in a new sentence — not from the scenarios, but from your own life.
