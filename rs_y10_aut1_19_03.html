The Problem of Evil and Suffering

What You'll Learn

Explore why evil and suffering exist in a world created by an all-powerful, all-loving God. Examine Christian theodicies and responses.

Recall: Why Does Suffering Exist?

Warm-up: 5 minutes
5:00

One of the biggest challenges to religious faith is the existence of evil and suffering. Let's review the key concepts before exploring Christian responses.

What is a theodicy?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: A theodicy is an explanation of why evil and suffering exist in the world if God is all-powerful and all-loving.

What is the Epicurean paradox?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: The Epicurean paradox states: If God is all-powerful, He can stop evil. If He is all-loving, He wants to. So why does evil exist?

What is "natural evil"?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Natural evil is suffering caused by natural phenomena — earthquakes, diseases, hurricanes — not by human choice.

What is "moral evil"?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Moral evil is suffering caused by human choice and action — war, theft, murder, cruelty.

The Problem of Evil: A Core Challenge to Faith

Christians believe God is:

  • Omnipotent (all-powerful)
  • Omnibenevolent (perfectly good and loving)
  • Omniscient (all-knowing)

Yet the world contains tremendous evil and suffering:

  • Natural suffering: Earthquakes, tsunamis, diseases, accidents — cause innocent pain
  • Moral suffering: War, abuse, injustice, cruelty — caused by human wickedness

The paradox: If God is all-powerful, He CAN stop evil. If God is all-loving, He WANTS to. So why doesn't He?

Two Major Christian Theodicies (Explanations)

1. Augustine's Free Will Defence

Augustine (4th-5th century) argued that God gave humans free will — the genuine ability to choose between good and evil. Free will is a great good because:

  • Love requires choice. Forced goodness isn't real virtue.
  • A world with free beings capable of real good is better than a world of automatons.

The cost: Humans often choose evil, causing moral suffering. God allows this to preserve human freedom.

2. Irenaeus's Soul-Making Theodicy

Irenaeus (2nd century) argued that suffering serves a purpose: it develops human character and moral growth. This is sometimes called the "vale of soul-making."

  • Courage requires danger to face.
  • Compassion requires suffering to witness and relieve.
  • Self-sacrifice requires hard choices.
  • A world of ease and comfort produces shallow characters.

The idea: God allows suffering (including natural suffering) as a means of developing human virtue and spiritual maturity. The goal is not a world without suffering, but a world in which humans grow and achieve moral and spiritual development.

Challenges to Christian Theodicies

Objection to Free Will Defence:

  • Does God's foreknowledge (omniscience) really allow free will? If God knows beforehand what I'll choose, am I truly free?
  • Natural evil (earthquakes, childhood cancer) is not a result of human free will. Why does God permit innocent suffering?

Objection to Soul-Making:

  • Can't God develop character without such extreme suffering? Would a slightly less painful world still develop virtue?
  • Some suffering serves no character-building purpose — a child born with severe genetic disease experiences only pain, not growth.
  • If God is all-powerful, why not use other means to develop character besides suffering?

How Christians Respond to Suffering in Practice

Jesus' Suffering: Christians point to Jesus' suffering and death as central to their faith. Jesus didn't prevent suffering; He endured it and transformed it through redemption. This shows that:

  • God is not distant from suffering; God entered into it.
  • Suffering can be redeemed — given new meaning and purpose through faith.

Prayer and Lament: Many Christians respond to suffering through honest prayer, including lament (crying out to God in pain). The Psalms contain many laments where believers pour out their anguish to God.

Community and Compassion: Rather than trying to "solve" evil theoretically, many Christians focus on responding to suffering with love, service, and justice. Caring for the sick, working against injustice, and supporting the grieving are ways of addressing evil.

Review: The Problem of Evil & Christian Responses

What are the three divine attributes that create the problem of evil?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Omnipotence (all-powerful), omnibenevolence (all-loving), and omniscience (all-knowing).

What is Augustine's theodicy?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Augustine argues that God gave humans free will, which is a great good. Evil results from human misuse of free will.

What is Irenaeus's theodicy?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Irenaeus argues that suffering develops human character and virtue — the "soul-making" theodicy. Moral growth requires overcoming challenges.

What is a major problem with these theodicies?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: They don't fully explain natural evil (earthquakes, disease) that results from no human choice, and some suffering seems to serve no character-building purpose.

How do Christians respond to suffering in practice?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Through trust in Jesus' suffering and resurrection, prayer and lament, community support, and compassionate service to those who suffer.
