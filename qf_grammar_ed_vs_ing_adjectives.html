🎯 -ED vs -ING Adjectives

"I am boring" vs "I am bored" — they mean VERY different things | A2–B1

What's wrong with these sentences?

Read each sentence. Think about what the speaker ACTUALLY means — and what they accidentally said.

🗣️ "The film was very bored. I fell asleep."

What did they mean? What did they actually say?

🗣️ "I am very interesting in history."

This person loves history... but what did they accidentally tell you about themselves?

🗣️ "My teacher is so boring."

(Wait — this one might actually be correct! Is it?)

✓ What's happening

Sentence 1: The film was boring (the film causes the boredom). Saying "the film was bored" means the film felt bored — films don't have feelings!

Sentence 2: I am very interested in history. Saying "I am interesting" means "I am a fascinating person" — not what they meant!

Sentence 3: This IS correct. The teacher causes the boredom → the teacher is boring. (Sorry, teacher.)

📌 The Pattern

-ED = how someone FEELS (the receiver of the feeling)

-ING = what CAUSES the feeling (the source)

📚 The Rule (Dual Coding)

-ED = How you FEEL

😊😱😴

I am excited.

She felt tired.

We were shocked.

He seems confused.

They got annoyed.

The PERSON experiences the feeling.

-ING = What causes it

🎬📚⚡

The film is exciting.

The journey was tiring.

The news was shocking.

The instructions are confusing.

The noise is annoying.

The THING/SITUATION creates the feeling.

💡 The Trap: "I am boring" IS correct English — but it means "I cause boredom in other people." If you mean "I feel bored," you need bored. The same sentence with -ED and -ING describes a completely different reality.

🎯 The Key Question

When you're choosing, ask yourself:

→ Am I describing a FEELING someone has? Use -ED

→ Am I describing something that CREATES a feeling? Use -ING
