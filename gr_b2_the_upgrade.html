Welcome to the Den

You're going to take boring, everyday objects and pitch upgraded versions to a sceptical investor — but this time, you need to know your numbers. Just like in Dragon's Den, the investor will ask: how much do you want, and what percentage are you offering?

💡
The Pitcher
Presents the product, states the financials, and makes the ask: "I'm asking for £X for Y% of my company."
🦈
The Investor
Checks the maths. Challenges the valuation. Can counter-offer with a different deal.
Each round: A product + constraint appear, along with your company's financial information. You have 30 seconds to prepare your pitch and work out your ask, then 90 seconds to pitch and negotiate.

Quick Guide — How Equity Works

When you ask an investor for money, you offer them a percentage of your company in return. That percentage is called equity.

The Key Formula
Company Valuation = Investment ÷ Equity %

Example: "I'm asking for £50,000 for 10%"
→ You're saying your company is worth £50,000 ÷ 0.10 = £500,000

Example: "I'm asking for £50,000 for 25%"
→ You're saying your company is worth £50,000 ÷ 0.25 = £200,000

⚠ The more equity you give away, the lower you're valuing your company.
The investor will be thinking:
"Is this company really worth that much?"
"How long until I get my money back?"
"What if I offered less money for more equity?"

4 pitches total. You'll alternate who pitches and who invests.

Language Debrief

Pitching, negotiating, and talking about money all use very specific language. Here are the key patterns you practised today.

Making the ask:
"I'm looking for [amount] in exchange for [%] of my company."
"Based on our projections, I believe the company is worth..."
"For that investment, you'd see a return within..."
"The numbers speak for themselves — we're already turning over..."
Justifying the valuation:
"We're projecting [amount] in revenue within the first year."
"Our production costs are only [amount], which gives us a healthy margin."
"The market for this is estimated at..."
"We've already sold [number] units at [price] each."
Investor pushback:
"Based on those numbers, you're overvalued."
"I'll offer you the money, but I want [higher %]."
"You're asking me to value this at [amount] — convince me why."
"What happens if sales don't hit your projections?"
"I like the product, but the numbers don't add up."
Negotiating:
"I can't go higher than [%] — that would undervalue the business."
"What if we met in the middle at [%]?"
"I'd consider [%] if you could also bring your expertise in..."
"That's too much equity for that amount of investment."
"I'll shake on [amount] for [%]. Final offer."

Discussion
