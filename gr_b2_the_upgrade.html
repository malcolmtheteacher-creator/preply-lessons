How This Works

You're going to take boring, everyday objects and pitch creative upgrades to a sceptical investor. Think Dragon's Den / Shark Tank — but for things like pillows, shopping trolleys, and park benches.

💡
The Pitcher
Presents the upgraded product. Must make it sound like the most important invention of the century.
🦈
The Investor
Asks tough questions. Looks for weaknesses. Decides: invest, maybe, or walk away.
Each round: A mundane object + a creative constraint appear. The Pitcher has 30 seconds to prepare, then 90 seconds to pitch. The Investor grills them. Then swap roles.

4 pitches total. You'll alternate who pitches and who invests.

Language Debrief

Pitching and persuasion use very specific language patterns. Let's look at the key ones.

Selling the vision:
"Imagine a world where..."
"What if I told you that [everyday problem] could be solved by..."
"This isn't just a [product] — it's a revolution in [area]."
"We're not selling a product. We're selling a lifestyle."
Handling tough questions:
"That's a great question, and here's why..."
"I'm glad you asked that, because..."
"The research actually shows that..."
"I understand your concern, but consider this..."
Investor pushback:
"Where's the evidence for that?"
"Who is actually going to buy this?"
"What's your competitive advantage?"
"That sounds great in theory, but in practice..."
"I'm not convinced. Change my mind."
Qualifying and hedging:
"Early data suggests..."
"Based on our research..."
"While it's still in the early stages..."
"The potential market is estimated at..."
