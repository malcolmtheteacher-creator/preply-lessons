Cheaper and better. In English, a little -er at the end of a word means "comparing two things". ("Better" is a special word — you'll meet it soon.) You hear these words every day: shopping, food, weather, people.

Bigger Than: Comparing Two Things

Two cafés, two phones, two cities — this is how you say which one wins.

Spot the pattern

Three sentences people really say:

  • "My sister is taller than me."
  • "This little café is cheaper than the big one in the centre."
  • "For me, the sea is more beautiful than the mountains."

Look at the bold words. Short words and long words — what is different? Say your idea, then check.

Check the pattern CLICK
Short words take -er: tall → taller, cheap → cheaper. Long words (three parts or more) take more in front: more beautiful. And the word than joins the two things you compare.
short word + er + than  →  old → older than
spelling: big → bigger (double letter) · happy → happier (y → i)
long word: more + word + than  →  more expensive than
✗ more big  →  ✓ bigger   ·   ✗ more cheap  →  ✓ cheaper. Short words never take "more".

Two facts, one sentence

Tea: €1.   Coffee: €3.
→
"Tea is cheaper than coffee."

Two facts become one sentence. The little word "than" joins the two things.

The Best of All: The Number One

One café is cheap. Another café is cheaper. But one café in your town is the cheapest of all — the number one.

Spot the pattern

Three more real sentences:

  • "This is the smallest room in our flat — and it's mine."
  • "Friday is the busiest day of my week."
  • "For me, this is the most interesting street in the city."

Look at the start and the end of each bold part. What do you see? Say it, then check.

Check the pattern CLICK
Short words: the + -est (small → the smallest; busy → the busiest, y changes to i). Long words: the most + word (the most interesting). This is the "number one" form — one thing out of a whole group. And it always starts with the.
the + short word + est  →  the oldest
spelling: happy → the happiest (y → i)
the most + long word  →  the most expensive
Don't forget "the"!   ✗ She is tallest in the class  →  ✓ She is the tallest in the class.

Good, Better, Best — and Bad, Worse, Worst

Two very common words break all the rules. You use them every day.

Spot the pattern

  • "This bakery is good, but the bakery on my street is better."
  • "The weather was bad on Saturday, and on Sunday it was worse."
  • "For me, my grandmother's soup is the best soup in the world."

Can you see "gooder" or "badder" anywhere? No! So what happens instead? Say your idea, then check.

Check your idea CLICK
"Good" and "bad" change completely: good → better → the best and bad → worse → the worst. No -er, no "more". They are new words — you learn them by heart, and you hear them all day long.
good → better → the best
bad  → worse  → the worst
✗ gooder   ✗ goodest   ✗ badder   ✗ more good — these words don't exist.
