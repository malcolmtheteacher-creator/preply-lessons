Stonehenge

Very old stones, and a big mystery.

Want the full version? Read this lesson at B2/C1 level →

Today: tell the story of Stonehenge, a very old place in England, and talk about its big mystery. Grammar: the passive (was/were + verb).

Key words — match them first

Before we read, let's learn the important words. Match each word (1–8) to its meaning (A–H). Say your answers out loud — "I think 1 is D" — then check.

Words

  1. a stone
  2. a circle
  3. ancient
  4. to build
  5. to move
  6. heavy
  7. a mystery
  8. a temple

Meanings

  • A. very, very old
  • B. a hard piece of rock
  • C. a round shape, like the letter O
  • D. to take something from one place to another place
  • E. something we do not understand or know the answer to
  • F. a special building for praying to a god
  • G. to make something (a house, a wall) from stones or bricks
  • H. not light; difficult to lift or carry

1 = B (a stone) · 2 = C (a circle) · 3 = A (ancient) · 4 = G (to build)

5 = D (to move) · 6 = H (heavy) · 7 = E (a mystery) · 8 = F (a temple)

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then try the questions from memory, and talk about the last one.

Part 1 — Very old stones

Stonehenge is a circle of very big stones in England. The stones stand together in a ring. It is very famous.

Stonehenge is very, very old — about 5,000 years old. People built it a long, long time ago. They worked for many years.

There are big stones and smaller stones. The big stones are very heavy. Some of them weigh 25 tonnes — that is like four big elephants! It is amazing that people built this so long ago.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What is Stonehenge, and where is it?

2. How old is it?

1. It is a circle of very big stones in England.

2. It is very old — about 5,000 years old.

Talk about it:
  • 5,000 years is a very long time. What other old places or buildings do you know?

Part 2 — A big mystery

Some of the stones came from a place called Wales. Wales is about 230 kilometres away! That is very far.

The stones are very heavy. But the people did not have machines. They did not have wheels. They did not have metal tools.

So how did they move these heavy stones so far? Nobody knows exactly! It is a big mystery. People have many ideas, but we are not sure.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did some of the stones come from? How far is it?

2. Why is it difficult to understand how the people moved the stones?

1. Some stones came from Wales — about 230 km away.

2. The stones are very heavy, and the people did not have machines, wheels, or metal tools. Nobody knows exactly how they moved them.

Talk about it:
  • How do you think the people moved the heavy stones? What is your idea?

Part 3 — Why did they build it?

We also do not know why the people built Stonehenge. There is no writing from that time. So we can only guess.

Maybe it was a temple, a special place for a god. Maybe it was a big calendar to follow the sun. We are not sure.

But we do know one thing. Some people were buried at Stonehenge. So it was a special place. Today, many tourists visit Stonehenge every year. They come to see the old stones and to think about the mystery.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What are two ideas about why people built Stonehenge?

2. What is one thing we do know about it?

1. Maybe it was a temple. Maybe it was a calendar to follow the sun. (We are not sure.)

2. We know that some people were buried there, so it was a special place. Many tourists visit it today.

Talk about it:
  • Would you like to visit Stonehenge? Why or why not?

Grammar: the passive

This story has a special problem: we often do not know who did something. The passive helps us here.

We use the passive when we don't know who did it, or it is not important.
We make it with was / were + the past participle (the third form of the verb).
Example: "the stones WERE MOVED" — we don't say who moved them.
More examples: "Stonehenge was built." · "Some people were buried there."

1. Was or were?

Put the verb in the passive. Use was for one thing, were for many things.

1. Stonehenge (build) about 5,000 years ago.

2. The big stones (move) a long way.

3. Some stones (bring) from Wales.

4. Some people (bury) at Stonehenge.

1. was built (one place — was)

2. were moved (many stones — were)

3. were brought (many stones — were)

4. were buried (many people — were)

2. Make passive sentences

Change these sentences to the passive. Then read them out loud.

1. People built Stonehenge. → Stonehenge by people.

2. They made the circle from big stones. → The circle from big stones.

3. Many tourists visit Stonehenge. → Stonehenge by many tourists. (Tip: this one is now, so use "is")

1. was built

2. was made

3. is visited (present passive: is/are + past participle)
