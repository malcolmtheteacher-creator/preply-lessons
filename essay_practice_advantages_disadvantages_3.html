Your task — about 40 minutes

In some countries, young people are encouraged to work or travel for a year between finishing high school and starting university studies. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages for young people who decide to do this.

Real past IELTS Task 2 question
One reminder before you start: give both sides genuine depth, explain why each point matters (don't just list), and finish with a clear on-balance view. Plan for ~5 minutes, write for ~30, and check for ~5.
0 words (aim 250+)

Band 8 model answer (~265 words)

In a number of countries, school-leavers are urged to spend a year working or travelling before beginning their university studies. This essay will examine both the benefits and the drawbacks of taking such a gap year.

There are clear advantages to this practice. Firstly, a year away from formal study allows young people to mature and gain independence. Someone who has worked to fund their own travels, or held down a demanding job, returns to education with greater self-discipline and a clearer sense of purpose. Secondly, the experience broadens horizons: travelling exposes young people to different cultures and perspectives, while employment provides practical skills and insight into the working world that no classroom can replicate. Many students consequently begin their degrees more focused and motivated.

Nevertheless, there are notable disadvantages. The most obvious is the loss of academic momentum. After twelve months away from study, some students find it difficult to return to disciplined reading and writing, and a minority never resume their studies at all. There is also a financial dimension: travelling can be expensive, and not every family can afford to support a child who is neither studying nor earning steadily, which risks turning the gap year into a privilege of the wealthy.

In conclusion, a gap year offers valuable opportunities for personal growth and real-world experience, but it also carries risks of lost momentum and cost. On balance, provided it is used purposefully rather than as an extended holiday, the advantages are likely to outweigh the drawbacks for most young people.
