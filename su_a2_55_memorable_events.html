What you'll learn

Let's Get Started

Thinking About Your Childhood

Answer these questions to start thinking about your childhood experiences. We'll use these ideas in our lesson today.

1. Did you use to study hard at primary school?

Personal answer: Yes, I did / No, I didn't. Share an example: "I used to enjoy math, but I didn't use to like language classes."

2. Did you use to enjoy going to secondary school?

Personal answer: Yes, I did / No, I didn't. Example: "I used to love the sports classes, but I didn't use to enjoy the school food!"

3. What did you use to do in your free time when you were a teenager?

Personal answer: Think of 2-3 activities. Example: "I used to play football, read books, and listen to music."

Grammar: "Used To" for Past Habits

Use "used to" to talk about habits or things you did regularly in the past, but don't do now.

Affirmative Sentences

Subject + used to + base verb

I used to play with trains and cars. (in the past, regularly; now I don't)
I used to dream of the end of the school year. (I thought about it often back then)
My sister used to go and just look at dolls in the shop window. (she did this habit regularly)

Negative Sentences

Subject + didn't use to + base verb

We didn't use to have a lot of money. (in the past, we were not rich; possibly we are now)
Children didn't use to have smartphones. (this is a fact about the past)

Complete These Sentences

Finish these sentences about your own life. Think carefully about the verb form.

When I was a child, I used to

Examples: "...play with dolls." "...love cartoons." "...go to the park every day." "...have birthday parties."

When I was a child, I didn't use to

Examples: "...have a mobile phone." "...use the internet." "...play video games." "...go to school on my own."

Important Grammar Note

Pronunciation: We say "used to" like "YOO-stuh" (not "USE-tuh"). The /z/ sound in "used" is soft, like in "lose" not "choose". Listen carefully and repeat!

Weak Forms: Sound Natural

In natural speech, we often say "used to" very quickly, almost like "uhs-tuh". This is called a "weak form".

Listen and Repeat

These sentences use the weak form of "used to". Notice how fast and soft it sounds:

"I used to have car races with my brother."

The "used to" is very fast and soft. It connects to the verb that follows.

"We didn't use to have much money."

In negatives, we say "use to" (not "used to"), but the sound is still quick.

"She used to study hard in school."

The weak "used to" is almost silent at the beginning of a sentence.

Speaking Task: Was Life Different Before?

Think about each statement. Do you agree or disagree? Give examples from your life and explain why.

The Statements

Children used to be more active in the past. Now they just play computer games.

Do you agree? Why or why not? Think of examples from your experience.

Families used to talk together in the past. Now everyone is on social media.

Do you agree? Why or why not? Think of examples from your experience.

People used to stay in their country for holidays. Now people travel all over the world.

Do you agree? Why or why not? Think of examples from your experience.

People used to eat healthier food in the past. Now everybody eats junk food.

Do you agree? Why or why not? Think of examples from your experience.

It used to be easier to get a job 20 years ago. Now lots of people are unemployed.

Do you agree? Why or why not? Think of examples from your experience.

Useful Language

EXPRESSING AGREEMENT
I agree with that. I used to... when I was a child.
Use this to show you agree with a statement and explain with a past habit.
EXPRESSING DISAGREEMENT
I don't really agree. In my experience, I used to...
Politely disagree and give your own example from the past.
GIVING AN EXAMPLE
For example, when I was younger, I used to...
Introduce a personal example to support your point.
