Five Pillars & Daily Practice

The Five Pillars vs Ten Obligatory Acts

Five Pillars (Sunni & Shi'a): Universal Islamic obligations. Foundation of Islamic practice.

1. Shahadah: Profession of faith. 2. Salah: Five daily prayers. 3. Sawm: Ramadan fasting. 4. Zakah: Almsgiving (2.5%). 5. Hajj: Pilgrimage to Mecca (if able).

Ten Obligatory Acts (Shi'a): Five Pillars plus: Wilayah (authority of Imam), Tawallah (love of Imam), Tabarra (disassociation from enemies), Jihad, Amr bil Maruf (commanding good), Nahi an al Munkar (forbidding evil).

Shahadah: The Declaration of Faith

Arabic: "La ilaha illallah, Muhammad rasulallah"

Translation: "There is no god but Allah, Muhammad is His messenger."

Significance: Recited at conversion. Core of Islamic identity. Testifies to monotheism and Muhammad's prophethood.

Usage: Five times daily in Salah. Whispered in newborn's ear. Last words many Muslims speak at death.

Salah: The Five Daily Prayers

Times: Fajr (dawn), Dhuhr (midday), Asr (afternoon), Maghrib (sunset), Isha (night). Prayer times vary by location and season.

Wudu (ritual purification): Washing face, hands, arms, head, feet. Spiritual preparation before prayer.

Rak'ahs (units): Each prayer has set number of bows and prostrations. Fajr (2), Dhuhr (4), Asr (4), Maghrib (3), Isha (4).

Direction: Always toward Ka'bah in Mecca (Qiblah). Unites believers worldwide in prayer.

Community: Jummah (Friday congregational prayer at midday) most important. Men generally attend mosque; women may participate.

Fasting, Almsgiving & Pilgrimage

Sawm: Fasting During Ramadan

What: Abstaining from food, drink, and other physical needs from Fajr (dawn) to Maghrib (sunset) throughout Ramadan (9th Islamic lunar month).

Purpose: Develop self-discipline, spiritual awareness, empathy for poor, obedience to Allah.

Exceptions: Children, elderly, sick, pregnant/nursing women, travelers may defer or skip. Make up fasts later if able.

Laylat al-Qadr (Night of Power): Occurs in last 10 nights of Ramadan. Night Qur'an first revealed. Considered 1,000 nights in blessing. Intense prayer and recitation.

Eid ul-Fitr: Festival breaking fast. Celebration with family, special food, new clothes, giving to poor. Marks Ramadan's end.

Zakah: Obligatory Almsgiving

Amount: 2.5% of accumulated wealth over one year (lunar year).

Nisab (threshold): Minimum wealth required to pay Zakah. Varies by commodity (gold, silver, crops).

Recipients: Poor, needy, debtors, wayfarers, slaves, converts, Islamic workers, those fighting oppression.

Spiritual dimension: "Zakat" means purification. Purifies remaining wealth. Shows social responsibility. Corrects wealth inequality.

Khums (Shi'a): One-fifth (20%) of surplus wealth after expenses. Additional to Zakah. Supports scholars and community needs.

Sadaqah: Voluntary charity beyond Zakah. Encouraged constantly. No minimum amount.

Hajj: The Pilgrimage to Mecca

Requirement: Once in lifetime if physically and financially able. Usually 5 days minimum. About 2 million pilgrims annually.

Sacred state (Ihram): Pilgrims enter state of sanctity. Wear plain white garments. Avoid luxury, violence, sexual activity. Equal before Allah regardless of wealth.

Key rituals: Tawaf (circumambulate Ka'bah seven times). Sa'y (run between hills seven times). Standing at Arafat (central act). Stoning of Jamarat (reject Satan's temptations).

Eid ul-Adha: Festival of Sacrifice. Commemorate Abraham's willingness to sacrifice son. Animal sacrifice; meat distributed to poor.

Jihad, Festivals & Community Life

Jihad: Struggle & Striving

Greater Jihad: Internal spiritual struggle. Controlling desires, pursuing righteousness, personal growth in faith. Personal battle against ego and sin.

Lesser Jihad: Physical struggle or fighting. Defense against oppression. Permitted only under strict conditions in Islamic law. Never aggressive.

Misconceptions: Jihad does not mean terrorism. Jihad does not mean only violent. Most important Jihad is spiritual.

Conditions for Just War: Defensive only, declared by authority, exempt non-combatants, limited means, peaceful settlement preferred.

Eid ul-Fitr (Festival of Breaking Fast)

Celebrates end of Ramadan. Special prayers at dawn. Family gatherings, new clothes, special food. Giving to poor (Zakat al-Fitr). Joyful celebration.

Eid ul-Adha (Festival of Sacrifice)

Commemorates Abraham's sacrifice. Animal slaughter. Meat to family, friends, poor. Occurs during Hajj. Major festival in Islamic calendar.

Ashura & Shi'a Observance

Ashura: 10th day of Muharram (1st Islamic month). Marks martyrdom of Imam Hussein (grandson of Muhammad) at Karbala (680 CE).

Shi'a practice: Mourning rituals, processions, passion plays, chest-beating. Expression of grief and solidarity with Hussein's suffering.

Spiritual significance: Remembrance of sacrifice for faith. Identifies with Hussein's resistance to tyranny.

Islamic Practices Summary

Islamic Practice Summary

Daily: Five Salah prayers, Shahadah recitation, Qur'an reading.

Monthly: Zakah planning and payment.

Yearly: Ramadan fasting, Eid celebrations, potential Hajj.

Lifetime: Hajj pilgrimage, Islamic education, community involvement, moral development.

Guidance

Exam focus: Know Five Pillars in order. Understand wudu, rak'ahs, Qiblah for Salah. Know Ramadan dates vary by lunar calendar. Understand Zakah calculation (nisab, 2.5%). Know Hajj rituals and significance. Distinguish Greater/Lesser Jihad clearly.
