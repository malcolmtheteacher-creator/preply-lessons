Common Adjectives for Technology

These words describe the qualities of new technology:

  • Fast, faster, fastest — quick performance
  • Powerful — strong processing or capability
  • User-friendly — easy to use
  • Intuitive — natural and easy to understand
  • Convenient — practical and helpful
  • Lightweight / Light — not heavy
  • Stylish — attractive design
  • Innovative — new ideas and technology
  • Addictive — so good you want to use it all the time

Sentences About Comparing Technology

Read these sentences. Can you guess which products they describe?

1. It's much faster than the old model.
2. The battery lasts much longer than before.
3. This model has much higher specifications than that one.
4. The screen resolution is much higher.
5. The great thing is it has a more powerful zoom than before.
6. The new model is a lot lighter than the old one.
7. This one uses cutting edge technology and has a lot more features and functions.
8. It's a change for the better because it's more user-friendly and intuitive.

What Do You Notice?

These sentences use: comparatives (faster, longer, lighter), "much" for emphasis, "a lot more/fewer", "better than", phrases for comparing values. All compare a new product with an old one.

Comparative Adjectives Rules

📖 Click to expand grammar rules ▼

One-Syllable Words: Add -er and -est

old → older → oldest

fast → faster → fastest

light → lighter → lightest

Words Ending in -e: Add -r and -st

nice → nicer → nicest

large → larger → largest

Consonant + Vowel + Consonant: Double the Consonant

big → bigger → biggest

hot → hotter → hottest

Ending in Consonant + -y: Change y to i, Add -er and -est

heavy → heavier → heaviest

happy → happier → happiest

stylish → more stylish → most stylish

Two-Syllable Adjectives and Longer: Use "more" and "most"

careful → more careful → most careful

powerful → more powerful → most powerful

user-friendly → more user-friendly → most user-friendly

innovative → more innovative → most innovative

Irregular Comparatives and Superlatives

good → better → best

bad → worse → worst

far → further → furthest

Comparing in Sentences

Sentence patterns for comparing:

Pattern 1: Subject + verb + comparative + than
Examples:

• This phone is faster than my old one.

• The new camera is more powerful than the old model.

• This laptop is lighter than that one.

Pattern 2: Comparative + much / a lot / far / significantly
Examples:

• This phone is much faster than my old one.

• The battery lasts a lot longer than before.

• The new model is far more user-friendly.

Pattern 3: Not as... as (for negative comparisons)
Examples:

• The old model is not as fast as the new one.

• This laptop is not as lightweight as that one.

• The old phone is not as intuitive as the new version.

Practice: Match Comparisons

Which sentences are correct? Click to check:

My new phone is more faster than my old one.
Incorrect! "More fast" is wrong. Use "faster" (simple comparative).
Correct: "My new phone is faster than my old one."
The screen is much higher resolution than before.
Correct! "Much higher" is the right way to say this. You can also say "The screen has much higher resolution."
This model is more powerful than that model is.
Correct! The extra "is" is optional but grammatically fine. You can also say: "This model is more powerful than that one."

Describing Your Product

Choose a technology product you own (phone, laptop, camera, tablet, etc.). Prepare notes to compare it with an older version or a different product.

📝 Make Notes About Your Product

  • What is it? Brand and model name
  • When did you buy it? How long have you had it?
  • Why did you choose it? What made it special?
  • What do you like about it? 3-4 positive features
  • How does it compare? Compare to your old version or a competitor's product
  • Any disadvantages? What could be better?

Comparing with the old version:

What would you change?
