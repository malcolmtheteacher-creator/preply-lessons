Modern Social Justice Issues & Religious Responses

Climate Justice

Pope Francis, Laudato Si' (2015): "Care for our common home." The Pope teaches that environmental destruction harms the poor most. Creation care is a spiritual duty.

Islamic Declaration on Climate Change (2015): Muslim leaders declared climate action an Islamic duty, grounded in stewardship (khalifah) of Earth.

Buddhist perspective: Pratityasamutpada (dependent origination) teaches all life is interconnected. Harming the environment harms ourselves.

The Refugee Crisis

Christian teaching: "Welcome the stranger" (Matthew 25:35). Jesus taught compassion for those fleeing persecution.

Islamic duty: Hijra (migration) is sacred in Islam — Muhammad himself was a refugee. Hospitality is a core virtue. Many Muslim organisations provide aid to refugees.

Sikh response: Khalsa Aid operates in refugee camps globally, providing food, healthcare, education — no questions asked.

Economic Inequality

The problem: The wealth gap is growing. Billionaires exist while children go hungry. Food banks proliferate in wealthy nations.

Faith-based response: Christian Aid, Islamic Relief, Khalsa Aid, Buddhist organisations all campaign for living wages, fair trade, and economic justice.

Humanist response: Evidence-based campaigns for minimum wage, progressive taxation, wealth redistribution.

Fairtrade & Ethical Consumerism

The issue: When you buy coffee, chocolate, or clothes, does the farmer earn enough to feed their family?

Religious response: Stewardship, Zakah, Dasvandh all call us to buy fairly. Many believers choose Fairtrade, ethically-made products.

Humanist response: Using consumer power to reduce suffering is effective altruism — aligning purchases with values.

Investigation: Modern Issues & Responses

Choose ONE modern social justice issue. Explain how at least TWO traditions (or secular approach) would respond:

Choose an issue:

  • Climate change and environmental destruction
  • Refugee crisis and forced migration
  • Food insecurity and hunger
  • Child labour in supply chains
  • Gender inequality and violence
  • Wealth inequality and poverty

Then think about:

  • How would a Christian respond to this issue?
  • How would a Muslim respond?
  • How would a Sikh respond?
  • How would a humanist respond?
  • Would they agree or disagree? Why?

Statement to Evaluate

"Religious organisations do more for social justice than governments."

Do you agree or disagree? Why?

Think about:

  • What can religious organisations do that governments can't?
  • What can governments do that faith groups can't?
  • Is one approach faster, more effective, or more ethical?
  • Should they work together? How?
  • Are there examples of government failure and faith group success (or vice versa)?
