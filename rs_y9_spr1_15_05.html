Modern Justice Campaigns

Climate and Refugee Justice

Climate: harms poorest nations least responsible. Pope Francis Laudato Si calls moral crisis. Islamic scholars: Earth belongs to God; destroying creation betrays trust. Refugees: 100+ million displaced. Christianity: love stranger; kingdom includes vulnerable. Islam: Prophet was refugee; teach sanctuary. Wealthy nations responsibility to provide asylum.

Food Poverty and Religious Organisations

Food poverty: 800+ million hunger, world produces enough. Results from poverty, conflict, inequality, distribution. Christianity: Jesus fed 5,000; feed hungry is core. Islam: best charity is feeding hungry; Ramadan emphasizes sharing. Sikhism: Langar serves free daily; no one sleeps hungry. Organisations: Christian Aid (40+ countries), Islamic Relief (emergencies, development), Khalsa Aid (Sikh relief, emphasizes sewa), World Vision. Why faith: mobilize billions; moral conviction; work where governments fail.

Religious Approach Strengths

Scale: billions in faith communities. Moral motivation: faith provides conviction; people sacrifice. Trust: leaders have credibility. Long-term: institutions persist centuries. Reach: exist in remote poorest areas. Examples: abolitionists ended slavery; Islamic scholars challenge extremism; langar feeds millions.

Critiques and Limits

Corruption: some exploit donations; prosperity gospel contradicts justice. Conservatism: resist change (women leaders, LGBTQ). Patriarchy: women excluded. Selective: active on abortion but silent on racism. Complicity: historically supported oppression. Dependency: charity without addressing structures. Secular: rights-based enforcement more reliable.

Balance

Religious organisations motivated extraordinary work but need accountability. Most effective: combine faith inspiration with secular governance, law, rights frameworks.
