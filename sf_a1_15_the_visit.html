Meanings

  • A. to say hello to a person who arrives
  • B. a person who visits you
  • C. the place where ships come in
  • D. a short walk or drive to see a place
  • E. the person who invites you and looks after you
  • F. happy because of a good thing you did
  • G. to let a person see a thing
  • H. a drink and a few words for a good moment: "To Leo!"

Part 1 — Arrivals

The twelfth of March, half past ten. This time Leo is not the passenger. He is the host. He stands at Arrivals with a coffee — and no umbrella. It's sunny.

Mr Hill: Leo!

Leo: Welcome! How was your flight?

Mr Hill: Early, and no delay. A miracle.

Leo: Do you have a suitcase?

Mr Hill: Only hand luggage. I learn from you.

Leo: The car is outside. It's twenty minutes to the hotel. No traffic.

Mr Hill: No rain, no traffic. Where am I?

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who is the host now?

2. How was Mr Hill's flight? What is the weather like?

1. Leo. Mr Hill is the guest.

2. Early, no delay. It is sunny.

Talk about it:
  • And you? When a guest arrives, what do you say and do first?

Part 2 — The tour

The hotel is small, quiet, near the port. Then the office: forty people, and a cake. Then a tour of the city: the old town, the port, the sea.

Mr Hill: How many ships come here?

Leo: About twenty a day. Big and small.

Mr Hill: And where is your favourite place?

Leo: There. The café on the corner, opposite the old church. I have my coffee there every morning.

Mr Hill: Every morning?

Leo: Every morning at half past six.

Mr Hill: You and your six o'clock!

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How many ships come to the port?

2. What is Leo's favourite place? When does he go there?

1. About twenty a day.

2. The café on the corner, opposite the old church. Every morning at half past six.

Talk about it:
  • And you? Where do you take a guest in your city? What is your favourite place?

Part 3 — Dinner at home

Seven o'clock. Carla's fish. Marco in his football shirt, Sofia in her pink T-shirt. Mr Hill brings the wine.

Mr Hill: Carla, this is delicious.

Carla: Thank you. New recipe. From the internet.

Sofia: Do you like London?

Mr Hill: Very much. But your city is warmer.

Marco: Do you like football?

Mr Hill: I love it.

Leo: Marco — after the plates.

Mr Hill: A toast. To Leo. Three days in London, and now I can't stop him talking.

Leo: In English!

Carla: In English.

Leo looks at his family, his guest, his table. Say hello. Smile. Ask questions. It works.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What does Mr Hill bring? What does Carla cook?

2. What is the toast?

1. Wine. Carla cooks fish, a new recipe.

2. To Leo: three days in London, and now he can't stop talking — in English.

Talk about it:
  • And you? Who comes to dinner at your home? What do you say at the end of a good evening?

Grammar: the ten questions of the story

Fifteen chapters, and the same small questions did all the work. Here they are together. Say each one out loud, and answer it about you.

With be: "Where are you from?" · "How was your flight?" · "Is it far?" · "What time is breakfast?" — answers: "I'm from… / It was… / Yes, it is. / At seven."
With do / does / did: "What do you do?" · "Do you like football?" · "What time does he get up?" · "Did you buy something?" — answers: "Yes, I do. / No, he doesn't. / Yes, I did."
With how: "How many ships?" (a number) · "How much is it?" (money) · "How long are you staying?" (time) · "How old is she?" (age)
Polite: "Can I see your passport?" · "Could we have the bill?" · "Would you like a coffee?" · "Will you come in March?" — "Yes, please. / Yes, I will."
