Phase 1: Gear Up
Every villain needs the right tools
Before you can pull off the heist of the century, you need to prepare your equipment. A true mastermind thinks of everything — not just the obvious tools, but the clever solutions that separate amateurs from legends.
PROCEDURAL WRITING 📝
Good procedural writing uses sequence words (first, then, next, finally) and precise verbs to explain exactly what to do.
EXAMPLE:
"Get the thing and use it to open the door."
"First, attach the magnetic decoder to the keypad. Then, wait for the green light. Finally, enter the override code: 7-4-2-9."
DESCRIPTIVE DETAIL 🎨
Don't just name things — describe them. What does it look like? What makes it special? Why did you choose THIS tool?
EXAMPLE:
"I'll bring a grappling hook."
"I'll bring my custom titanium grappling hook — lightweight, silent, with a matte black finish that won't reflect any light."

Phase 2: Break In
Getting inside is only the beginning
You've arrived at the museum. It's 2:47 AM. The streets are empty. A single security light flickers above the service entrance.

There are three ways in: the rooftop skylight, the basement loading dock, or the side maintenance door. Each has its own challenges. Each requires a different approach.

How do you get in — and more importantly, how do you make sure no one knows you were ever here?
BUILDING TENSION 😰
Use short sentences for tense moments. Describe what you hear, see, and feel. Make the reader hold their breath!
EXAMPLE:
"I went through the door carefully."
"The door creaked. I froze. Footsteps. Getting closer. My heart hammered against my ribs as I pressed myself into the shadows."
SHOWING NOT TELLING 👁️
Don't say "I was scared" — SHOW the fear through actions, sounds, and physical sensations.
EXAMPLE:
"I was nervous."
"Sweat trickled down my neck. My fingers trembled as I reached for the lock."

Phase 3: The Grab
The moment of truth
You've made it to the Cretaceous Wing. The massive skeleton of a T-Rex towers above you in the moonlight streaming through the windows.

And there it is. In a glass case at the center of the room. The Velociraptor claw, curved and deadly, preserved for 75 million years — about to change owners.

But this isn't just any display case. It has pressure sensors, laser tripwires, and a backup alarm that triggers if the glass is touched wrong.

This is the most dangerous part of the heist. One wrong move and it's over.
STEP-BY-STEP PRECISION 🔬
When describing a complex process, break it into tiny, precise steps. Each movement matters. Each action is deliberate.
EXAMPLE:
"I opened the case and took the claw."
"I placed the suction cup exactly three centimetres from the edge. Slowly, I began to cut a perfect circle in the glass, holding my breath with each rotation."
SENSORY DETAILS ✨
Use ALL your senses. What do you see, hear, smell, feel? The hum of electronics? The cold air? The weight of the claw?
EXAMPLE:
"The claw was heavier than I expected — cold, ancient, rough against my gloved fingers. It smelled like old stone and secrets."
