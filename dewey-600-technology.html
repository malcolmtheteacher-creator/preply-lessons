Elaboration: How Technology Builds on Science

Each invention is built on discoveries from Dewey 500 (Science). See the connections:

1440: Printing Press - Used principles of mechanics (Archimedes screws, levers). Made communication technology accessible.
1769: Steam Engine (James Watt) - Applied Newton's Laws of Motion. Converted heat energy into mechanical work. Built on thermodynamics knowledge.
1876: Telephone (Bell) - Used principles of electricity and wave physics. Sound converted to electrical signals, transmitted, reconverted to sound.
1879: Light Bulb (Edison) - Applied electromagnetic principles. Used resistance heating and vacuum properties. Built practical application of electrical theory.
1903: Airplane (Wright Brothers) - Applied aerodynamics (Newton's Laws of Motion). Understood lift, drag, thrust. Experimental approach to physics.
1928: Penicillin (Fleming) - Based on Germ Theory (Pasteur, 1861). Accidental discovery of antibiotic showing how bacteria can kill bacteria. Applied microbiology.
1947: Transistor (Bell Labs) - Based on quantum mechanics and semiconductor physics. Applied Planck's quantum theory to practical computing devices. Made electronics miniaturization possible.
1969: Internet/ARPANET - Applied information theory, electrical engineering, Boolean logic. Built on transistor and computing advances. Connected distributed systems via packet switching.
2007: Smartphone (iPhone) - Integrated ALL previous technologies: transistors (processing), electricity (power), optics (camera), wireless transmission (networks), touchscreen physics. Miniaturized supercomputer in pocket.
2012: CRISPR Gene Editing - Based on DNA structure (Watson & Crick, 1953) and bacterial genetics. Applied molecular biology. Uses precise cutting/editing mechanism. Ultimate precision technology for life itself.

The Great Chain of Technology

Why are these inventions connected? Each solved problems created by the previous generation. Printing press needed distribution → Steam engine provided power → Telephone needed wiring → Light bulb enabled evening work → Airplane enabled travel → Penicillin healed injuries → Transistor enabled computation → Internet connected computers → Smartphone made it portable → CRISPR makes us masters of genetics. This isn't a random list—it's the story of civilization.
