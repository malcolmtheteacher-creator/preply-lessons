The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. While his half-brother was away, Harold acted as regent in England.
a regent (n) — a person who rules on behalf of an absent or young monarch.
2. His enemies said he was illegitimate — not truly Cnut's son.
illegitimate (adj) — (here) not having a lawful claim; born to unmarried parents or of doubtful birth.
3. In 1037 he moved to seize the crown for himself.
to seize (v) — to take something by force.
4. The prince was captured and blinded.
to blind (v) — to make someone unable to see.
5. It was a brutal crime that shocked England.
brutal (adj) — very cruel.
6. His half-brother later ordered his body to be dug up.
to dig up (v) — to remove from the ground.
7. The nickname Harefoot may refer to his swift running.
swift (adj) — fast.
8. Queen Emma was exiled to Flanders.
to exile (v) — to force someone to leave their country.
9. There were three claimants to the throne.
a claimant (n) — a person who claims a right to something.
10. The Encomium Emmae was a work of propaganda for Emma.
propaganda (n) — information used to promote a particular view.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — Who will be king?

When Cnut died in 1035, his chosen heir, Harthacnut — his son with Emma — was in Denmark, busy defending it. Harold, Cnut's son by his first partner Ælfgifu of Northampton, was in England. A council agreed that Harold would act as regent.

Emma's supporters claimed that Harold was not even Cnut's son. In 1037, with Harthacnut still away, Harold was accepted as king, and Emma was exiled to Flanders.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why wasn't Harthacnut crowned at once?

2. What did Emma's supporters claim about Harold?

1. He was in Denmark, defending it.

2. That he was not really Cnut's son.

Discuss:
  • Is it fair to take power while your rival is away?

Part 2 — The crime of 1036

In 1036 Emma's sons from her first marriage, Alfred and Edward, came from Normandy. Alfred landed and was met by Earl Godwin. His men were seized; many were killed, and Alfred was blinded and died at Ely. Harold's men were blamed, and many suspected Godwin of betraying him — a charge he denied.

This crime would haunt English politics for years.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What happened to Prince Alfred?

2. Who was suspected?

1. He was captured, blinded and died at Ely.

2. Harold's men, and Earl Godwin, who denied it.

Discuss:
  • Why might Godwin have been suspected?

Part 3 — A short reign

Harold's reign was short. He died at Oxford in March 1040, just as Harthacnut was preparing to invade. Harthacnut had his body dug up and thrown into a marsh or the Thames. Later it was reburied, possibly at St Clement Danes in London.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How did Harold's reign end?

2. What did Harthacnut do to his body?

1. He died at Oxford in 1040, before Harthacnut's invasion.

2. Had it dug up and thrown into a marsh or the Thames.

Discuss:
  • What does Harthacnut's revenge tell us about the family?

Grammar — The Passive Voice

Historians often care more about what was done than who did it — or they simply do not know who did it. That is the job of the passive.

How it works:
• Form: be + past participle: "The king was crowned at Westminster."
• Add by only when the doer matters: "The castle was built by Norman masons."
• Reporting passives hedge a legend: "He is said to have…", "It was believed that…"

1. Make it passive

Complete each sentence about Harold Harefoot. Use the word in brackets.

1. Harold (accept) as king in 1037.

2. Emma (exile) to Flanders.

3. Prince Alfred (capture) and (blind).

1. was accepted

2. was exiled

3. was captured … blinded

2. Choose the right passive form

1. Godwin (suspect) of betrayal.

2. The body (throw) into the Thames.

3. The Encomium (write) to support Emma.

1. was suspected

2. was thrown

3. was written

Going deeper

The 1030s show how fragile Cnut's empire was without him. Rival mothers — Ælfgifu and Emma — and rival sons competed. Much of our information comes from the Encomium Emmae Reginae, written for Emma, so the case against Harold may be biased.

Worth noticing: the passive lets us describe the crime against Alfred without naming the doer — which is exactly how many medieval sources handle it.
Learning from the past — for today: Absence creates vacuums. If you leave a role or project unattended, someone will fill it. Delegate clearly before you go.
Dig into it — talk these through:

  • Why are crimes against princes remembered so long?
  • Who fills the gap when you're away?
