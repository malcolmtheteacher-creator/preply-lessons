Dealing with Problems

Explaining and Resolving Issues Using Reported Speech

Time: 3:00

Quick Problem-Solving

How would you resolve these situations? Think about what you'd say to explain the problem.

Scenario 1: Travel Day Trouble

You were supposed to pick up your friend from the airport at 10 am, but they never appeared. Later, you discover you had the wrong date. Your friend was furious.

What would you do first? How would you explain what happened?

Scenario 2: Dress Code Disaster

Your friend invited you to what you thought was a casual barbecue. You turned up in jeans and a t-shirt. When you arrived, everyone else was in formal business attire for a charity dinner.

What's the best way to handle this? What would you say?

Scenario 3: Payment Problem

You were at the supermarket checkout with a full trolley. Your card was declined. You left everything there and felt embarrassed.

How would you explain this to the staff? What could you do to resolve it?

Reported Speech: Key Transformations

When we report what someone said, we change tenses:

Direct: "I've booked the biggest room"

Reported: He said he had booked the biggest room / He said he'd booked the biggest room

Direct: "I'll bring the passports"

Reported: She said she would bring the passports / She said she'd bring the passports

Direct: "I don't want you to buy me a present"

Reported: I thought you didn't want me to buy you a present

Practice: Convert to Reported Speech

1. Direct: "I've booked the biggest room"
You said you ____ booked the biggest room.
You said you had booked the biggest room. (Or: you'd booked)
2. Direct: "I'll bring the passports"
I thought you said you ____ bring the passports.
I thought you said you would bring the passports. (Or: you'd bring)
3. Direct: "The group from Wales has dropped out"
I thought you told me the group from Wales ____ dropped out.
I thought you told me the group from Wales had dropped out. (Or: they'd dropped out)
4. Direct: "I don't want you to buy me a present"
I thought you ____ want me to buy you a present.
I thought you didn't want me to buy you a present.
5. Direct: "I'm going to apologise in person"
She said she ____ going to apologise in person.
She said she was going to apologise in person. (Or: she'd go)

Role-Play Scenarios

For each scenario, decide how you would explain and resolve the problem. Use reported speech in your explanation.

Scenario 1: Cinema Mix-Up

Situation:
You and a friend agreed to meet at 7 pm for a film. You waited until 7:30 pm, but your friend didn't arrive. Later, you found out they thought you said 8 pm.
Your task:
How would you explain this to them? Use reported speech to clarify what was said.

Scenario 2: Birthday Party Confusion

Situation:
Your colleague thought their birthday celebration was on Friday evening. You had arranged it for Saturday afternoon. They missed it.
Your task:
How would you resolve this? What would you say about who said what?

Scenario 3: Lost Phone and Missed Messages

Situation:
Your friend lost their phone. You texted to reschedule plans, but they never saw the message. They showed up at the original time and found you weren't there.
Your task:
How would you explain what happened using reported speech? How would you make up for the confusion?

Scenario 4: Gift Misunderstanding

Situation:
Your partner said they didn't want an expensive gift for their birthday. You didn't buy anything special, thinking they'd prefer to choose their own. They felt disappointed and unappreciated.
Your task:
How would you clarify what was said? How would you resolve this misunderstanding?

Resolution Phrases

Use these to explain how you'd solve the problem:

  • "We decided the most sensible thing was to..."
  • "We thought it would be best to..."
  • "We agreed that the fairest solution was to..."

Reflection & Discussion

Take a moment to think about each question. You can write notes or simply reflect.

1. Communication Breakdown

What causes most misunderstandings in your daily life? Is it usually about what was said, or about different expectations?

2. Different Points of View

Have you experienced a situation where you and another person remembered the same conversation completely differently? What happened?

3. Blame vs. Responsibility

When you resolve a problem, is it important to know whose fault it was? Or is it more important just to fix the situation?

4. Apologies and Making Amends

What makes a good apology? Do you always need to admit fault, or is it enough to acknowledge the other person's disappointment?

5. Prevention

How could you prevent these kinds of misunderstandings in future? What communication strategies work best for you?
