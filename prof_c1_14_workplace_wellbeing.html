5 Wellbeing Check-In Moves

Click each card to see the move.

1. The Honest Answer

How to say "I'm struggling" professionally.

Weak: "I'm fine." (but you're not)

Strong: "I want to be transparent — my workload is at a point where something needs to give. I can keep pushing, but something will break."

Why it works: You're stating a fact, not asking for pity. You're showing professional awareness of your own limits. That's mature. That's trustworthy.

2. The Specific Request

Don't complain. Make asks.

Complaint: "I'm so busy. Everything is urgent. There's too much."

Request: "What I actually need right now is to deprioritise the monthly reporting. It's taking 15 hours a week and it's the lowest ROI thing I'm doing. Can that move to next quarter?"

Why it works: You're giving your manager something to work with. They can say yes or no. They can negotiate. But they understand what you need.

3. The Boundary Statement

How to protect capacity without sounding uncommitted.

Weak: "I can't work weekends. That's my personal time."

Strong: "I can take this on if we agree to deprioritise X. If I do both, my quality drops. I work best when I have focused time, not fragmented across too many things."

Why it works: You're saying "I care about quality." Not "I don't want to work." Your boundary is professional, not personal.

4. The Wellbeing Check-In as a Manager

How to actually check in, not just ask.

Weak: "How are you?" → "Fine." → End of conversation.

Strong: "I've noticed you seem stretched lately. Is that accurate? What would help?"

Why it works: You're showing you've observed. You're naming the thing. You're asking what they need, not offering unsolicited solutions. That creates safety to answer honestly.

5. Sustainable Performance Language

Reframe rest and boundaries as professional, not personal.

Wrong frame: "I need time off because I'm tired." (sounds weak)

Right frame: "I work best when I have recovery time. Taking Friday off this week means I show up more focused and creative next week. I maintain quality over time."

Other examples: "I don't email after 6 because my best thinking happens when I'm rested." "I need blocking time to concentrate. That's when I do my best work."

Why it works: You're showing that wellbeing is about performance, not weakness. High performers need maintenance.
