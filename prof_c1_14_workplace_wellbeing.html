Workplace Wellbeing

C1 Level · Professional Life

Key Vocabulary

Click each card to reveal the definition.

Burnout Physical and emotional exhaustion caused by prolonged workplace stress, resulting in reduced performance and cynicism.
Resilience The ability to recover from difficulties, adapt to stress, and maintain wellbeing despite challenges.
Work-Life Balance The equilibrium between time and energy devoted to work and time devoted to personal, family, and leisure activities.
Presenteeism Showing up to work physically but being unproductive due to illness, stress, or lack of engagement; "present but absent."
Absenteeism Frequent absence from work, often caused by illness, disengagement, or poor working conditions.
Psychological Safety The perception that you can express concerns, ask for help, or admit mistakes without fear of punishment or humiliation.
Autonomy The freedom to make decisions about how, when, and where you work; control over your own tasks and time.
Recognition Acknowledgment and appreciation of an individual's contributions, effort, or achievements by management and peers.
Boundary A limit or expectation, such as not working after hours or taking weekends off, to protect personal time and wellbeing.
Wellbeing Strategy A planned approach or initiative designed to improve employee mental, physical, and emotional health at work.

Stress vs. Burnout: What's the Difference?

Understanding this distinction is critical for recognizing wellbeing problems early.

Stress

  • Short-term response to pressure or challenge
  • Increases energy and focus (can be positive)
  • Resolved when the stressor is removed or managed
  • Example: Preparing for an important presentation
  • Normal and occasional—everyone experiences it

Burnout

  • Long-term state of physical and emotional exhaustion
  • Drains energy and reduces motivation
  • Persists even when stressors are removed (deeper issue)
  • Example: Chronic overwork, lack of support, no progress or recognition
  • More serious—requires intervention and recovery time

Signs of Burnout in the Workplace

Individual Signs:

  • Chronic fatigue or insomnia
  • Detachment from work ("I don't care anymore")
  • Reduced performance despite effort
  • Cynicism or negativity
  • Frequent illness (weakened immune system)
  • Increased irritability or anxiety

Team/Organizational Signs:

  • High turnover, especially among good performers
  • Increased absenteeism and presenteeism
  • Declining productivity and quality
  • Interpersonal conflicts within the team
  • Low morale and engagement scores

Contributing Factors

Workload: Overwhelming demands, unrealistic deadlines, insufficient resources.

Lack of Control: No autonomy, micromanagement, decisions made without input.

Insufficient Support: Poor management, lack of mentoring, inadequate training or tools.

Poor Relationships: Conflict with colleagues, isolation, lack of psychological safety.

No Recognition: Efforts go unacknowledged; no path for advancement or growth.

Values Misalignment: Personal values don't match organizational goals or practices.

Language for Discussing Mental Health at Work Diplomatically

These conversations require sensitivity, confidentiality, and respect.

When You Notice Someone Struggling:

"I've noticed you seem different lately. Is everything okay? I'm here if you want to talk."

"I'm concerned about you. How are you really doing?"

"No pressure to share, but if there's anything I can do to support you, please let me know."

When Someone Discloses a Mental Health Issue:

"Thank you for trusting me with this. Your wellbeing is important."

"How can I support you? What do you need?"

"This is confidential. Let's talk about what accommodations or adjustments might help."

Avoid: "Just think positive" or "You just need a vacation."

Starting the Conversation About Boundaries:

"I'm noticing we're all working late. I want to make sure this is sustainable. Let's talk about what's driving this."

"I encourage everyone to protect their evenings and weekends. Burnout isn't a badge of honor."

"If you're struggling to manage your workload, let's figure out how to adjust priorities."

Advocating for Wellness Programs:

"Employee wellbeing directly impacts productivity and retention. We should invest in mental health support."

"What if we offered flexible work arrangements, wellness stipends, or access to counseling?"

"Research shows that companies prioritizing wellbeing have lower turnover and higher engagement."

How to Raise Wellbeing Concerns Professionally

To Your Manager:

"I want to discuss something that's affecting my performance. I've been managing [workload/conflict/lack of support], and I'm concerned about burnout. Can we talk about how to address this?"

Be specific. Provide examples. Propose solutions. This isn't complaining—it's problem-solving.

To HR or a Wellbeing Committee:

"I'd like to suggest an initiative. We could [flexible work, mental health training, stress management workshops]. This would benefit both employees and the organization."

To a Team Member (Peer Support):

"I care about you and I've noticed [specific observation]. I want to help. Let's figure out what you need."

Discussion: Creating a Healthy Workplace Culture

Reflection Questions:

  • What does wellbeing look like in your current workplace?
  • What barriers prevent people from prioritizing their health?
  • How much responsibility should organizations take vs. individuals?
  • What small changes could make a big difference?
  • How do you model healthy boundaries?

Your Speaking Task

Prepare a 4-5 minute presentation of a 3-point wellbeing initiative for a company. You can choose a real company or invent one.

Structure Your Presentation

Opening (30 seconds)

Introduce the company and the problem. Set the stage: "Research shows that X% of employees are experiencing burnout. We need to act."

The Case (1 minute)

Make the business case. Why does wellbeing matter? What are the costs of ignoring it? (Higher turnover, lower productivity, talent loss, poor reputation, health insurance costs.)

Initiative 1 (1 minute)

Present your first initiative. What is it? How will it work? What resources does it need? What's the expected impact?

Examples: Flexible work arrangements, mental health stipends, wellness programs, stress management training, manager training on wellbeing conversations.

Initiative 2 (1 minute)

Your second initiative. Follow the same structure.

Initiative 3 (1 minute)

Your third initiative. Follow the same structure.

Closing (30 seconds)

Summarize the impact: "These three initiatives will reduce burnout, improve retention, and create a healthier, more productive workplace. I recommend we pilot this starting [date]."

Key Language for Your Presentation

Use this vocabulary and phrasing:

  • "The issue is..." (state the problem)
  • "Research shows that..." (support with data)
  • "If we don't act, we risk..." (set stakes)
  • "My recommendation is..." (propose solution)
  • "This would enable employees to..." (benefit)
  • "The expected outcome is..." (measurable result)
  • "The first steps would be..." (actionable next steps)

Prepare Your Notes

Outline your three initiatives. Include specific details and expected outcomes.
