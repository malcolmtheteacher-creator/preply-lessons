Data Analytics Vocabulary

Click each card to reveal the definition.

KPI Key Performance Indicator; a measurable value showing how effectively something is being achieved
Benchmark A standard or reference point for comparison; usually from competitors or industry best practices
Correlation A statistical relationship between two variables; how closely they move together
Trend A general direction or tendency in data over time (upward, downward, or stable)
Outlier A data point that differs significantly from other observations in a dataset
Sample Size The number of observations or respondents in a study; larger samples are generally more reliable
Variance The spread or dispersion of data points from the average; indicates data consistency
Dashboard A visual interface displaying key metrics and performance data in real time
Insight A meaningful observation or understanding derived from data analysis
Metric A system of measurement or a quantifiable indicator used to track performance

Language for Describing Data

Describing Trends and Movements

When values increase:

  • "There is a significant upward trend in..."
  • "The data shows a sharp increase in..."
  • "We have experienced consistent growth in..."
  • "The metric has risen steadily from X to Y"

When values decrease:

  • "There is a marked decline in..."
  • "The data reveals a sharp drop in..."
  • "We have seen a consistent downward trend in..."
  • "The metric has fallen from X to Y"

When suggesting relationships:

  • "The data suggests a correlation between..."
  • "This is consistent with..."
  • "We can observe a pattern where..."
  • "The evidence indicates that..."

When drawing conclusions:

  • "Based on these findings, we can infer that..."
  • "The data implies that..."
  • "If this trend continues, we should expect..."
  • "It is reasonable to conclude that..."

Expressing Confidence and Uncertainty

High confidence:

  • "The data clearly shows..."
  • "It is evident that..."
  • "There is strong evidence that..."

Moderate confidence:

  • "The data appears to show..."
  • "There is evidence suggesting that..."
  • "It seems that..."

Low confidence or caution:

  • "The data could indicate that..."
  • "We should be cautious about concluding that..."
  • "More data is needed to confirm that..."

Practice Describing Data

Sample Dataset: Monthly Customer Acquisition

Scenario: A software company has tracked customer acquisition over 6 months. Here are the numbers:

  • January: 145 new customers
  • February: 168 new customers
  • March: 152 new customers
  • April: 203 new customers (spring marketing campaign launched)
  • May: 245 new customers
  • June: 287 new customers

Note: Industry benchmark for similar companies is 180 customers/month.

Question 1: Describe the Trend

Write 2-3 sentences describing what the data shows. Use language from the "Interpreting Data" section.

"The data shows a significant upward trend in customer acquisition over the six-month period. Starting from 145 customers in January, the company experienced steady growth, with a marked acceleration following the spring marketing campaign launch in April. By June, acquisitions had increased to 287 customers, representing a 98% increase from the baseline."

Question 2: Compare to Benchmark

The industry benchmark is 180 customers/month. How does this company compare? Write 1-2 sentences.

"The company performed below benchmark in January through March, averaging 155 customers compared to the 180-customer benchmark. However, from April onwards, the company has consistently exceeded the benchmark, suggesting the marketing campaign was highly effective and has established a new competitive advantage."

Question 3: Identify Patterns

What insights does this data provide? What patterns do you notice? What might be happening?

"The data suggests a strong correlation between the marketing campaign and customer acquisition rates. The turning point in April indicates that the investment in marketing yielded measurable results. The continued acceleration in May and June could indicate growing word-of-mouth momentum or sustained campaign effectiveness. The absence of any decline suggests customer retention is supporting ongoing growth."

Discussion: When Data Conflicts with Instinct

Time Remaining: 10:00

The Scenario

Your company has sold products in three regions for five years. Regional managers have strong instincts about their markets and believe Region A is most profitable. However, the data analytics team has just released a comprehensive audit. Their findings show:

  • Region A (manager's favourite): High revenue but high costs; actual profit margin is 12%
  • Region B: Moderate revenue; profit margin is 31% (highest)
  • Region C: Low revenue; profit margin is 24%

The regional managers argue: "We know our markets. Revenue is what matters. The numbers might be wrong."

Discussion Questions

1. Why might the data show something different from the managers' instincts?

Consider: What are they measuring? What might they be missing? How do revenue and profit differ?

2. How would you respond to "The numbers might be wrong"?

What questions would you ask to verify the data? How would you build confidence in the findings?

3. What decision should the company make?

Should they trust the data or the managers' instinct? How could they combine both sources of information?

4. Speaking Challenge

Prepare a 3-minute explanation to the management team on why this data matters. Use language like "The data suggests...", "This is consistent with...", "We can infer that..."
