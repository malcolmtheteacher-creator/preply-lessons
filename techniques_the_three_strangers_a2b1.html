The Three Strangers

Hardy's story of a party on a rainy night, three knocks at the door, and a very strange song.

Today: you'll read about three strangers who come to a party on a rainy night — and you'll try to guess who they are before the guests do. You'll also practise the little questions at the end of sentences: It's cold, isn't it?
Warm up — before we read. Talk about these questions out loud. There are no wrong answers.
Discuss:
  • It's night and it's raining. A stranger knocks on your door. Do you open it? What do you say?
  • What questions do you ask when you meet a new person at a party?
  • Do you like stories with a mystery? Are you good at guessing the ending?

Now — the words for today's story

Ten words open the story. Do the four short activities. Say your answer out loud first, then click Reveal.

A · What does the word mean here?

1. Twenty people were in the little house. They danced, sang and ate. It was a happy party for a new baby — a christening.
a christening (n) — a church ceremony where a baby gets its name. People often have a party after it.
2. He walked for three hours in the rain with no coat. When he arrived, he was soaking wet.
soaking wet (adj) — very, very wet. You can also say soaked.
3. Nobody in the house knew the man. They never saw him before. He was a stranger.
a stranger (n) — a person you don't know. Careful: it is not the same as strange (adj) = unusual. But a stranger can be strange!

B · Which word means…?

Three words: prison · escape · hangman. Say the right word, then reveal.

1. Which word means a building where criminals must stay?
a prison (n) — "He was in prison." Old British word: gaol (say it like jail).
2. Which word means to get out of a place when people want to keep you there?
to escape (v) — "The prisoner escaped from prison." Also a noun: an escape.
3. Which word means a man whose job was to kill criminals with a rope?
a hangman (n) — in the past, criminals were hanged. You may know the word game "Hangman"!

C · Choose the meaning — a / b / c

1. When the man saw the hangman, his face went white and he ran away. To run away is to…
  (a) run in a race   (b) run to leave a place quickly, because you are afraid   (c) run home for dinner
(b) — to run away (from) = to escape from a person or place. Past: ran away.
2. The men went out into the night to search for the prisoner. To search for is to…
  (a) look carefully for   (b) shout at   (c) sing to
(a) — search for = look for, carefully. Also a noun: a search. Think of a search engine!

D · Word families & word partners

1. Complete: a man who is in prison is a ___. A man who steals is a ___.
a prisoner · a thief (plural: thieves). The verb: steal, stole, stolen. "He stole a sheep."
2. Complete with knock: "Someone ___ on the door." · "There was a ___ at the door."
Someone knocked on the door (verb) · There was a knock at the door (noun). In this story there are three knocks!

The story, in three parts

This is a famous English story, told here in simple English. Read each part. Then answer the questions from memory, and talk about one question.

Part 1 — The first knock

About two hundred years ago, there was a little house on a hill in the south-west of England. It was three miles from the town of Casterbridge. One night in March, it rained very hard. But inside the house, Shepherd Fennel and his wife were having a party. Their new baby had her christening that day. Twenty people were there. They danced, ate and drank by a big, warm fire, and a boy played the violin.

At eight o'clock, someone knocked on the door. A tall, thin man was outside. He was soaking wet. "Can I come in for a little while?" he asked. "Of course!" said the shepherd. The stranger sat in the warmest place, next to the fire. He was friendly, but he didn't talk much about himself. He took out a pipe. "Has anyone got some tobacco?" he asked. "I lost mine on the road." The guests thought it was a little strange: a pipe, but no tobacco.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why are the Fennels having a party?

2. Where does the first stranger sit, and what does he ask for?

1. It is the christening of their new baby.

2. Next to the fire, in the warmest place. He asks for some tobacco for his pipe.

Discuss:
  • The shepherd lets a stranger into his house at night. Is that kind, or not very clever?

Part 2 — The second knock

Soon there was a second knock. This man was short, fat and loud. He sat down next to the first stranger and drank a lot. Mrs Fennel wasn't happy about that! "What's your job?" someone asked him. He didn't answer. He sang a song: "My job is very rare… I tie my customers and take them up high…" Only one person sang the song with him: the first stranger, in a loud voice.

Then everyone understood. The second stranger was the hangman! Tomorrow, in Casterbridge prison, he was going to hang a man. The prisoner was a poor watchmaker. His children were hungry, so he stole a sheep. Everyone at the party thought it was a terrible punishment.

Then there was a third knock. A small man opened the door and asked for directions. But when he saw the two men next to the fire, his face went white. He looked at them for a second. Then he turned and ran away into the dark.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What is the second stranger's job? How do the guests find out?

2. What does the third stranger do?

1. He is the hangman. He sings a song about his job.

2. He sees the two men next to the fire, goes white, and runs away.

Discuss:
  • Why do you think the third man ran away? Who do you think he is?

Part 3 — The wrong man

Then they heard a gun from the town. "That's the prison gun!" said the hangman. "A prisoner has escaped!" Everyone thought: the third stranger! That's why he ran! The men ran out into the rain to search for him. They found him under a tree and brought him back to the house. But two officers from the prison said, "That isn't the prisoner. He looks completely different."

"Then why did you run away?" they asked the little man. He explained. The prisoner was his brother. He came to say goodbye to him before he died. But when he opened the door, he saw his brother — next to the fire, sitting next to the hangman, and singing the hangman's song! His brother looked at him, and the look said: "Don't say anything!" So he ran away.

The first stranger was the prisoner! But now he was gone. The next day, people searched for him — but not very hard. They felt sorry for him. Nobody ever found him.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who is the third stranger really?

2. Who is the first stranger? Why don't people search very hard for him?

1. The prisoner's brother. He came to say goodbye.

2. The escaped prisoner. People feel sorry for him — he only stole a sheep because his children were hungry.

Discuss:
  • Did you guess the ending? What clues did the writer give us about the first stranger?

Grammar — easy question tags: isn't it? didn't he?

At the party, people want to know about the strangers. But it's not polite to ask "Who are you?" So English speakers say a sentence and add a little question at the end: "You're not from here, are you?" This little question is a question tag.

How to make them:
• Positive sentence → negative tag: "It's cold, isn't it?" "You're tired, aren't you?"
• Negative sentence → positive tag: "You aren't from Casterbridge, are you?"
• Use the same verb: is → isn't, can → can't, was → wasn't.
• No helping verb? Use do / does / did: "He sings well, doesn't he?" "He ran away, didn't he?"
Why do we use them? To check something, or to start a friendly conversation: "Lovely party, isn't it?"
• Voice goes down ↘ = "I'm sure. Please agree."  ·  Voice goes up ↗ = "I'm not sure. Please tell me."
• Answer with a short answer: "You're a stranger here, aren't you?" — "Yes, I am." / "No, I'm not."

1. Positive or negative?

Write the tag.

1. It's raining hard, ?

2. You aren't hungry, ?

3. The baby is beautiful, ?

1. isn't it   2. are you   3. isn't she

2. do, does or did?

Going deeper

Thomas Hardy (1840–1928) is a famous English writer. He wrote many novels and stories about the countryside in the south-west of England. He called this place "Wessex". "Casterbridge" is the real town of Dorchester. He wrote "The Three Strangers" in 1883. In the story's time, in England, a person could die for stealing a sheep.

The heart of it. There are three knocks and three strangers. In stories, number three is usually the most important — so we watch the third stranger. But the important man was next to the fire from the start! The writer gives us clues: the pipe with no tobacco, the short answers, and the loud singing. The best moment is the song: the hangman and the prisoner sit side by side and sing about hanging. It is funny and terrible at the same time. And at the end, the people don't search very hard. That is their way of saying: this law is wrong.
Talk it through:
  • Why does the first stranger sing the hangman's song so loudly?
  • The hangman is friendly and funny. Do you like him? Why or why not?
  • The people don't search very hard. Are they right?
  • Do you know other stories with three — three brothers, three wishes, three pigs?
