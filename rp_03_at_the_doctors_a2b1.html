At the Doctor's

You are not well. Today you see the doctor. You will say how you feel, listen, and ask a few simple questions.

This is the easier version. Want the harder one? → Full B2/C1 version →

The Scene 🎬

For about a week, you have not felt well. You have a cough, and you feel very tired. At night, the cough is worse, so you don't sleep well.

Today you have an appointment at the doctor's. You are a little nervous — you don't know all the words in English. But that is OK. You only need to do three simple things: say how you feel, listen to the doctor, and ask when you don't understand.

The doctor is kind and calm. They will ask you some questions, listen to your chest, and tell you what to do.

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. When you are ill, do you go to the doctor quickly, or do you wait? Why?
  2. What is difficult about seeing a doctor in another language? What helps you?

Who's who

You
The Patient 🤒

Tired and not well. You want to explain how you feel, and understand what the doctor says.

The other person
The Doctor 🩺

Kind and calm. They ask questions, listen, and give you clear, simple advice.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. It is OK to speak slowly. And it is always OK to say: "Sorry, I don't understand. Can you say it again, please?"

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for how the patient says how they feel.

DoctorGood morning. Come in, sit down. How can I help you today?
YouGood morning, doctor. I don't feel well. I have a cough, and I feel very tired.
DoctorI'm sorry to hear that. How long have you had the cough?
YouFor about a week. It's worse at night, so I can't sleep well.
DoctorI see. Do you have a temperature? Any pain?
YouA little. My chest hurts a bit when I cough.
DoctorOK. Let me listen to your chest. Breathe in… and out. Good. It's just a chest infection — nothing serious.
YouOh, good. I was a bit worried.
DoctorThat's normal. I'll give you some medicine. Take it two times a day, after food.
YouSorry, how long do I take it for?
DoctorFor one week. And please drink lots of water and rest.
YouOK. So, two times a day, after food, for one week?
DoctorThat's right. Come back only if you don't feel better in a week.
YouThank you, doctor. Thank you very much.

From memory — don't look up!

1. How does the patient feel? Say two things.

2. How long has the patient had the cough, and when is it worse?

3. The doctor gives three pieces of advice. What are they?

1. The patient has a cough and feels very tired. (Their chest also hurts a little when they cough.)
2. For about a week, and it is worse at night.
3. Take the medicine two times a day, after food, for one week; drink lots of water; and rest.

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use at any doctor's. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Speak slowly — and it's OK to ask. You do not need difficult words. Short and clear is best. If you don't understand the doctor, just say: "Sorry, can you say that again, please?" A good doctor is always happy to help.

🤒 Saying how you feel

Phrase 1

You sit down. The doctor asks how they can help. What do you say first? Which one is right?

A. "I am fine, thank you."
B. "I don't feel well."
C. "Nice to meet you."
B — "I don't feel well."
This is the simple, clear way to start. A means you are healthy (so why are you here?), and C is just a hello. "I don't feel well" tells the doctor there is a problem.
Phrase 2

Now say your two problems: a cough, and being tired. Complete it: "I have a ___, and I feel very ___."

"I have a cough, and I feel very tired."
Use "I have a…" for things you can name (a cough, a cold, a headache) and "I feel…" for how your body feels (tired, hot, dizzy, sick).

🕐 Giving details

Phrase 3

The doctor asks: "How long have you had the cough?" You started about a week ago. Complete it: "I've had it ___ about a week."

"I've had it for about a week."
Use "for" with a length of time: for a week, for three days, for two months. "About" means "more or less" — you don't need to be exact.
Phrase 4

The cough is bad in the evening and night. Put the words in order:

worseit'sat night
"It's worse at night."
"Worse" is the opposite of "better". You can also say "It's worse in the morning", "It's worse when I walk".

💊 Understanding the advice

Phrase 5

The doctor gives you medicine. You want to know how many days. Put the words in order:

do Ihow longtake it for?
"How long do I take it for?"
A very useful question about medicine. You can also ask: "How many times a day?", "Do I take it with food?"
Phrase 6

You want to check you understood: two times a day, after food. Start with the small word that means "let me check". Complete it: "___, two times a day, after food?"

"So, two times a day, after food?"
Start with "So,…" and repeat the important information back. This is a great way to check you understood — and the doctor can say "Yes, that's right" or fix a mistake.

💛 Feelings & thank you

Phrase 7

The doctor says it's nothing serious. You feel better because before, you were a little afraid. Which sentence says this?

A. "I was a bit worried."
B. "I am very happy today."
C. "I don't like doctors."
A — "I was a bit worried."
"A bit" means "a little". "Worried" means you were thinking something bad might be wrong. It's a normal, honest thing to say to a doctor.
Phrase 8

At the end, you want to thank the doctor warmly. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you, doctor. Thank you very much."
Saying "doctor" and adding "very much" makes your thank you warm and polite. A smile helps too.

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work every time you are not well. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"I have a ___, and I feel ___." — Imagine you are ill. What is wrong? (a cold, a headache, a sore throat…)

"I've had it for ___." — How long? (a few days, a week, since Monday…)

A question for the doctor about your medicine — write one.

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — say how you feel → give details → ask about the medicine → check you understood → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your appointment. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the patient 🤒

The doctor speaks — you answer. Say how you feel, give details, and ask when you need to.

DoctorGood morning. Sit down. How can I help you today?
DoctorI'm sorry to hear that. How long have you had it?
DoctorLet me have a look. … OK, it's nothing serious. I'll give you some medicine.
DoctorTake it two times a day, after food, for one week.
DoctorThat's right. Get lots of rest. Take care!

Round 2 — now YOU are the doctor 🩺

Change places. You are the doctor. Be kind and calm. Ask simple questions and give simple advice.

PatientGood morning, doctor. I don't feel well. I have a headache and I feel hot.
PatientFor two days. It's worse in the morning.
PatientOK. What should I do?
PatientThank you, doctor.
The Twist

❓ A word you don't know

One more time. The doctor uses a word you don't know, and speaks a little fast: "I think you have a mild viral infection." Don't panic! Don't say "yes" if you didn't understand. What do you say? Ask them to help you: "Sorry, I don't understand. Can you say it in an easy way?" or "Can you say that again, slowly, please?" Practise asking for help — it is one of the most useful things you can do in English. Start the timer and try!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I said how I feel with "I have a…" and "I feel…".
  • I said how long, using "for".
  • I asked at least one question about the medicine.
  • I checked I understood, by saying it back ("So, …?").
  • I said "thank you" warmly, and I asked for help if I didn't understand.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

Today, when you are ill, you go to the doctor. They listen, they know what it is, and they give you medicine. In a week, you feel better.

A hundred years ago, in 1918, there was a terrible illness called the Spanish Flu. It went all around the world. There was no good medicine, and doctors could not stop it. Millions of people died.

So a cough and a week of medicine is a small thing. We are very lucky to have doctors and medicine today.
