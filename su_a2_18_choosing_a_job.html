Choosing a Job

A2 Level English Lesson

Context

What makes a good job? Let's think about different types of jobs and what skills they need.

Activity: Order These Jobs

Look at these six jobs and order them from 1 (you're best at) to 6 (you're worst at):

  • Builder
  • Teacher
  • Researcher
  • CEO (Chief Executive Officer)
  • Artist
  • Sales Person
Why did you choose this order?

• Which job needs the most physical skill?
• Which job is the most creative?
• Which job needs the best communication skills?
• Which job pays the most?
• Which job would make you happiest?

Job Types Quiz

Below are six categories of job activities. For each activity, choose if it's interesting (👍) or not interesting (👎) to you.

A: PRACTICAL JOBS

Activities with hands, body, and things
Builder, farmer, gardener, mechanic, electrician, plumber, carpenter, athlete, outdoor guide

B: SUPPORTIVE JOBS

Working with people and helping them
Teacher, nurse, counselor, coach, social worker, HR manager, therapist, trainer, mentor

C: RESEARCH JOBS

Working with information and ideas
Scientist, researcher, data analyst, librarian, journalist, engineer, accountant, economist

D: MANAGEMENT JOBS

Planning, organizing, and leading
Manager, director, project manager, CEO, supervisor, team leader, administrator, organizer

E: CREATIVE JOBS

Using imagination and thinking of new ideas
Designer, artist, musician, writer, inventor, architect, creative director, entrepreneur, actor

F: MARKETING / COMMUNICATIONS JOBS

Working with people and changing opinions
Salesperson, marketing manager, public relations specialist, advertiser, business development, customer service manager

What did you discover? Which categories interested you most? Those are the job types that might be best for you!

Language Focus: Verb-Noun Collocations

A collocation is when two or three words often appear together. For example, we say "have breakfast" not "eat breakfast" (in some languages).

Activity: Fill in the Blanks

Click on each blank space to reveal the answer. The verb + noun collocation completes the sentence.

1. People in supportive jobs often ______ other people.

2. Research jobs sometimes ______ information.

3. Managers need to ______ people.

4. In many jobs, you ______ the skills from university.

5. Creative people find it easy to ______ of solutions.

6. When things don't work, somebody has to ______ them.

7. Journalists ______ a lot of research.

8. Parents ______ their children.

9. It's important to ______ relationships with customers.

10. Effective communications can ______ people's opinions.

What did you notice?

• Some verbs are very common: advise, manage, lead, use, think, fix, do, encourage, build, change
• These verbs are used for MANY different jobs
• Learning collocations helps you speak more naturally and sound more like a native speaker
• When you learn a new noun, learn it with its common verb

Your Turn

Think of a job you know about (your job, a family member's job, or any job). Describe it without saying the name. Use the language frame below. You will have 3 minutes.

"This is a _____ job (Practical / Supportive / Research / Management / Creative / Marketing).

In this job you need to _______ every day.

It's very important to _______ / not to _______.

This job can be challenging because _______.

It's a good job for _______ (adjectives: organized, creative, friendly, patient, etc.) people.

What do you think this job is?"

Write or Speak Your Answer

If you want to practice writing, type your answer below. Then read it aloud!

• Think about the job first — what does this person do every day?
• What skills does this person need?
• What makes this job difficult?
• Who is good at this job? What kind of person?
• Practice saying it once or twice before you do it for real

Wrap-Up: Discussion

These questions help you think about your future and what makes a good job for you.

Discussion Questions

  1. If you could choose your job again, would you choose the same one? Why or why not?
  2. Is it more important to make money or to enjoy your job? Can you have both?
  3. Would you move to another country for better job opportunities? What would make you say yes?
