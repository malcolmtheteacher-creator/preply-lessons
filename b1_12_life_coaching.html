Linking Phrases for Contrast and Consequence

These phrases help you show relationships between ideas. Use them when discussing problems and solutions.

Phrases Meaning "So" or "Therefore"

Therefore…

Overworking is bad for your health, therefore you should reduce your hours.

As a result…

Many people consume too much sugar, as a result they develop health problems.

Consequently…

She drinks three cups of coffee daily, consequently she cannot sleep well.

Phrases Meaning "But" or "However"

However…

Everyone knows exercise is important, however many people do not exercise regularly.

Nevertheless…

The job pays well, nevertheless it makes him unhappy.

On the other hand…

Working full-time gives you money, on the other hand it leaves little time for family.

Phrases for Giving Strong Advice

Click on each phrase to see how it is used. Notice the strong language to emphasise how important the advice is.

STRONG RECOMMENDATION
I highly recommend gaining more life experience.
Formal, strong advice showing you believe this is very good for the person
STRONG CAUTION
I strongly caution against consuming junk food.
Formal warning that something is bad or dangerous
ABSOLUTE ENDORSEMENT
I can wholeheartedly recommend breaking bad habits.
Very enthusiastic support for an action that improves wellbeing
PRACTICAL SUGGESTION
When exercising, it is definitely a good idea to begin slowly.
Practical advice that is clearly sensible and safe
STRICT PROHIBITION
You absolutely mustn't work long hours.
Very strong warning that something is dangerous or harmful
GENTLE BUT FIRM
To avoid depression, you really should reduce your hours.
Friendly but clear advice about something important

Real Life Problems for Coaching

Read each person"s problem. What advice would you give? What consequences might result from not changing?

Alex: Career Stuck

"I have an amazing job, but every day I ask myself: am I really helping anyone? The work is good, but I feel like I am not doing anything meaningful with my life. My salary is high, however I feel empty."

Jordan: Work-Life Balance

"I work 12 hours every day. I know overworking is bad for my health, but I cannot stop. I do not exercise. I drink too much coffee and skip meals. I feel tired all the time, nevertheless I continue the same routine."

Sam: Lifestyle Habits

"I want to be healthier. I know that fast food is bad for me, but it is quick and easy. I do not have time to cook. I am sedentary because my job requires sitting all day. As a result, I have gained weight and feel unhappy."

Casey: Feeling Lost

"I feel like I need more life experience. Everyone else seems to know what they want, but I am confused about my future. I have not travelled or tried new things. I am afraid of making bad decisions."

Life Coaching Roleplay

Now you are a life coach. A client is going to tell you about their problem. Your job is to listen and give advice.

As a life coach, you should:

  • Ask questions to understand the problem clearly
  • Show empathy and listen carefully
  • Give strong, practical advice using the phrases you learned
  • Explain the consequences of change and of not changing
  • Use linking phrases (therefore, however, as a result, etc.)

Remember to use:

  • Linking phrases: "Therefore…", "As a result…", "However…", "On the other hand…"
  • Advice phrases: "I highly recommend…", "You absolutely mustn't…", "I strongly caution against…"
  • Expressions: "Consequently…", "Nevertheless…", "Definitely a good idea…"
