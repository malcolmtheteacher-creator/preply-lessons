The Good News

Small talk is a skill, not a talent. It can be learned. Today you'll learn 5 professional phrases that work in any business meeting - whether you're meeting distributors, investors, or new partners for your company.

Key point: These phrases will help you build trust quickly - before you talk about business, contracts, or negotiations.

Language Focus A: Starting Conversations

These expressions help you START a conversation professionally. Click each card to learn more.

"How was your journey?" Click to open
Meaning
Ask about their travel to the meeting. Shows you care about them as a person.
Form
How was your + [noun]?
(journey / flight / trip)
Register
Professional and warm. Good for first meetings.
Example in a meeting:

"Good morning! Please, sit down. How was your journey? Did you find the office easily?"

NOW YOU TRY:

You're meeting two people today: a distributor who flew 6 hours from Stockholm, and a supplier who drove 30 minutes from a nearby city. How does your opening small talk DIFFER for each?

Language bank:
Long journey: "You must be tired / That's a long flight / Did you manage to get any sleep? / Can I get you a coffee first?"
Short journey: "Easy drive? / Not too much traffic? / At least you didn't have far to come"
"Is this your first time in [city/country]?" Click to open
Meaning
Find out if they know the place. Opens conversation about the location.
Form
Is this your first time in + [place]?
Register
Friendly. Easy to answer yes or no. Good conversation starter.
Example in a meeting:

"Welcome to Barcelona! Is this your first time in Spain?"

If yes: "Oh wonderful! You must try the local food while you're here."

If no: "Ah, so you know the city already. What's your favourite part?"

NOW YOU TRY:

This question has two possible answers. Prepare responses for BOTH scenarios:

If YES (first visit): "Oh wonderful! You should try... / Let me recommend... / The old town is worth seeing / I'd be happy to suggest some restaurants"

If NO (been before): "Ah, so you know the city! What's your favourite...? / Where do you usually stay? / Do you have a favourite restaurant here?"
"I've heard great things about [company/product]." Click to open
Meaning
You did research. You know about them. This is a compliment.
Form
I've heard great things about + [noun]
Register
Professional compliment. Makes people feel respected.
Example in a meeting:

"Before we start, I just want to say - I've heard great things about your distribution network. Your reputation in the market is excellent."

Tip: Be specific! "Great things about your company" is okay. "Great things about your organic product line" is better.

NOW YOU TRY:

You have a meeting with a potential partner tomorrow. Compare these two versions:

WEAK: "I've heard great things about your company."

STRONG: "I've heard great things about your organic range - especially your expansion into the premium segment."

Build your own STRONG version: What would you research? Their market share? Recent news? A specific product line? New store openings?

Language Focus B: Keeping the Conversation Going

These expressions help you CONTINUE a conversation after the first questions. Click each card to learn more.

"That sounds interesting. Tell me more about..." Click to open
Meaning
Show interest. Invite them to speak more. People love to talk about themselves.
Form
That sounds interesting. Tell me more about + [topic]
Register
Warm and engaged. Shows you're listening, not just waiting to speak.
Example in a meeting:

Partner: "We've been expanding into the Scandinavian market recently."

"That sounds interesting. Tell me more about how the Scandinavian market is different."

NOW YOU TRY:

Someone says: "It's been a challenging year - supply chain problems, but we're also expanding into three new markets."

Two threads - which do you pick?

Option A (challenges): "That sounds difficult. Tell me more about the supply chain issues - are things improving now?"

Option B (growth): "Three new markets! That sounds exciting. Tell me more about where you're expanding."

Strategic choice: In small talk, do you start with problems or positives? Why?
"Actually, we have something similar. In our case..." Click to open
Meaning
Connect their experience to yours. Create common ground. Build relationship.
Form
Actually, we have something similar. In our case, + [your example]
Register
Collaborative. Shows you understand them. Builds trust.
Example in a meeting:

Partner: "Finding good suppliers is always difficult in this industry."

"Actually, we have something similar. In our case, we spent two years finding the right reliable suppliers. Quality control was the biggest challenge."

Why this works: You're not competing. You're saying "I understand your situation because I have the same experience." This builds trust.

NOW YOU TRY:

They said: "Finding reliable suppliers is always the hardest part." You want to connect, but not make it all about you.

The pattern: Connect → Share briefly → Return to them

"Actually, we have something similar. In our case, [YOUR 1-2 sentence example]. How did you eventually solve it? / What's worked for you?"

Practice: Fill in your real example, then choose your "return question."
