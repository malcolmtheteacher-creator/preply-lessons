Practice: Give Advice
Choose a problem scenario below. Read the situation and practise giving advice using the suggestion phrases you learned.
Scenario 1: Not Enough Money for Food
A friend tells you: "I am struggling financially. I don't have enough money to buy food this month. My family can't help me either."
Possible responses:
• "Why not try cutting back on other expenses? That could free up some money."
• "One option would be getting a part-time job to earn extra money."
• "Have you looked into food banks or student support services? They might be able to help."
• "You could also speak to your university about emergency funding."
Scenario 2: Struggling with Workload
A classmate says: "I have too much coursework. Three assignments are due next week and I don't know how to manage everything."
Possible responses:
• "Have you tried booking a tutorial? Your teacher might help you understand the work better."
• "You could also start a study group with classmates. Working together might be faster."
• "Why don't you speak to your tutor about an extension? They might be able to help."
• "One option would be breaking the work into smaller tasks each day."
Scenario 3: Feeling Lonely
Your friend is quiet and says: "I've been here for three months but I don't have many friends. I feel lonely most of the time."
Possible responses:
• "Why don't you join a club or sports group? That's a great way to meet people with similar interests."
• "You could also talk to someone at the student support centre. They can give you advice."
• "Have you tried inviting a classmate for coffee? Small steps help."
• "One option would be attending social events. There are usually many activities on campus."
Scenario 4: Difficult Course
A peer confides: "My course is much harder than I expected. I'm not sure I can pass my exams."
Possible responses:
• "Have you tried speaking to your instructor? They might offer extra support or resources."
• "You could also form a study group with other students taking the same course."
• "Why don't you look into peer tutoring or online resources? Extra help exists."
• "One option would be meeting with an academic advisor to discuss strategies."
Scenario 5: Money Problems Affecting Grades
A friend mentions: "I need to work extra hours to pay bills. This means I have less time for studying and my grades are dropping."
Possible responses:
• "Have you spoken to your school about financial support? Many institutions offer grants."
• "You could also speak to your teachers about your situation. They may understand and offer flexibility."
• "Why don't you set up a meeting with your academic advisor? They can suggest solutions."
• "One option would be adjusting your work hours temporarily to focus on your studies."
Reflection and Discussion

1. When you give advice, what is most important to do?

Think about listening, asking questions, offering options, or being patient. What makes advice helpful?

2. Is it better to give one solution or many options?

Consider: Do people like having choices? Or is it confusing? What works best?

3. What do you do when someone asks for your advice?

How do you respond? Do you use suggestion phrases? Are there phrases in your first language that are similar?

4. Which type of problem is hardest to give advice about?

Financial problems, relationship issues, work stress, or health concerns? Why is it difficult?

5. How do you ask for help when you have a problem?

Do you talk to friends, family, or professionals? What phrases do you use?
