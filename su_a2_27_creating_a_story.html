To start with, I moved into this cottage in the country. One day, I started hearing strange noises. A few days later, I found out what was making the sounds. Finally, I felt safe again.

Notice: We use these linkers to create a logical sequence. The listener follows the story because they understand the order of events.

Past Simple and Continuous

These two tenses work together to tell stories clearly.

Past Continuous = The Background

Form: was/were + verb-ing
Use: Describes what was happening (the situation or background)
"I was walking in the park..." (the background situation)

Past Simple = The Main Event

Form: verb-ed (regular) or irregular forms (went, saw, etc.)
Use: Describes what happened (the main story action)
"I heard a noise." (the main event that happened)

How They Work Together

"I was living in this cottage [background] when I started to hear things [main event]."
Background: "It was a beautiful day."
Main Event: "I lost my keys."
Together: "It was a beautiful day when I lost my keys."
Background: "I was driving home from work."
Main Event: "My car broke down."
Together: "I was driving home from work when my car broke down."
Background: "We were watching a film."
Main Event: "The lights went out."
Together: "We were watching a film when the lights went out."

Practice: Identify the Tenses

"I moved into this place in the country a few years ago. I wanted to live somewhere peaceful. It was an old cottage that was built about 200 years ago. I loved the open spaces and fresh air. Well, I did for the first few weeks at least. Then, one night, I started to hear things. It started with the floorboards. I heard creaking, like there was someone walking outside."
  • Past Simple: moved, wanted, loved, started, heard
  • Past Continuous: was (setting), were (describing the quality)
  • Key structure: "I started to hear" (past simple) + "there was someone walking" (past continuous describing background)

Pausing to Create Drama

One of the most powerful tools in storytelling is the pause. Strategic pauses keep listeners interested and build suspense.

Sentences with Drama

Look at these example sentences. The slashes (/) show where to pause:

"Then, // at night // I started to hear things."
"Eventually, // I found the courage // to go out."
"So anyway // I crept to the door // and opened it slightly."

Why Pausing Works

Pause 1 - After a time linker: "Then, // I heard a noise." — The listener prepares for what's coming
Pause 2 - In the middle of action: "I walked to the door // and opened it." — Creates suspense and drama
Pause 3 - Before important details: "I saw something unusual. // A strange object was sitting on the floor." — Makes people pay attention

Practice: Where Would You Pause?

Story fragment:

"One day I was sitting at my desk working when I heard a loud bang from outside. I stood up and looked out the window. There was nothing unusual. I sat back down. Then I heard it again. This time it was different. I got up quickly and ran to the window."

"One day // I was sitting at my desk // when I heard // a loud bang from outside. I stood up // and looked out the window. Nothing... [long pause] ...unusual. I sat back down. Then // I heard it again. This time // it was different. I got up quickly // and ran to the window."

Why these pauses? They create suspense, let information sink in, and build drama. The long pause before "unusual" makes listeners think something WAS unusual!

When you tell your story: speak clearly, pause at natural breaks, and let the drama build!

Key Takeaways

To tell a great story:
✓ Use time linkers to show the order of events
✓ Mix Past Simple (main events) with Past Continuous (background)
✓ Add pauses to create drama and hold attention
✓ Speak clearly and with confidence
✓ Choose interesting details that help listeners picture the story
