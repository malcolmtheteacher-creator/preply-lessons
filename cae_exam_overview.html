Cambridge English · C1 Advanced (CAE)

The Full Exam Landscape

Four papers, one integrated certificate. This course focuses on Writing Part 1 — but every candidate is examined on all four components below.

CEFR Level

C1 — Effective Operational Proficiency

Accepted by thousands of universities and employers worldwide as proof you can study or work in a demanding English-speaking environment. A top-grade (A) pass is certified at C2.

Exam Shape

4 papers · ~4 hours 10 minutes total

Reading & Use of English + Writing + Listening taken on the same day (3 h 55 min). Speaking is a separate 15-minute paired session with another candidate and two examiners.

The Four Papers

Cambridge weights each paper equally — 20% each — for a total of 100%. The Reading & Use of English paper is one paper, worth 40% combined.

How the four papers add up to the final score

All four papers carry equal weight (20% each) toward the overall Cambridge English Scale score. Reading and Use of English are reported separately on the Statement of Results, but together they form one paper — so that paper is effectively worth 40%.

Final score composition
Reading · 20%
Use of English · 20%
Writing · 20%
Listening · 20%
Speaking · 20%

The Cambridge English Scale — what score earns what grade

C1 Advanced is reported on the Cambridge English Scale from 160 to 210. Your overall score is the mean of the five skill scores. Grade boundaries are fixed by Cambridge and the same in every session.

B2 Level 160-179
Grade C (C1) 180-192
Grade B (C1) 193-199
Grade A (C2) 200-210
160-179 · Level B2 Below the pass mark for CAE. Candidates receive a B2 Level certificate on the Cambridge English Scale.
180-192 · Grade C Pass. Certified at C1 on the CEFR.
193-199 · Grade B Pass. Certified at C1 on the CEFR.
200-210 · Grade A Top grade. Certified at C2 on the CEFR — the level above what the exam nominally tests.

Why Writing Part 1 is where this course invests. The Writing paper is worth 20% of the final grade — the same as Listening or Speaking. But it's the paper with the biggest margin between an average candidate and a top one: Cambridge's own examiner reports consistently flag poor paragraph control, weak verdicts and flat vocabulary as the most common reasons candidates stall at Grade C. Mastering the Part 1 discursive essay first gives you transferable architecture (topic sentences, reasoned verdicts, semi-formal register, C1 grammar showcase) that carries straight into every Part 2 task type.
