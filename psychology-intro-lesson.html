Real Stories from the Field

Phineas Gage (1848) — A railway worker survived an iron rod passing through his skull. He lived, but his personality changed completely. This case revolutionised our understanding of how brain regions affect personality and behaviour.

Little Albert (1920) — Psychologist John Watson conditioned a baby to fear white rats. This controversial experiment demonstrated that emotional responses could be learned — launching decades of research into conditioning and phobias.

Patient H.M. (1953) — Henry Molaison had brain surgery for epilepsy and lost the ability to form new memories. He could remember his childhood but not what happened five minutes ago. His case taught us that memory isn't one thing — it's many systems working together.

1. Cognitive Bias

+
Meaning

A systematic error in thinking that affects decisions and judgments. Our brains take mental shortcuts that sometimes lead us astray.

Form & Usage

cognitive bias (noun, countable) — plural: cognitive biases

Common collocations: suffer from a cognitive bias, fall prey to cognitive biases, overcome cognitive biases

Examples

"Confirmation bias leads people to seek information that supports their existing beliefs."

"The researchers identified several cognitive biases affecting jury decision-making."

🎯 NOW YOU TRY

Describe a cognitive bias you've noticed in yourself or others. Use the term in a complete sentence.

2. Conditioning

+
Meaning

A learning process where behaviour is modified through associations (classical conditioning) or consequences (operant conditioning).

Form & Usage

conditioning (noun, uncountable) | to condition (verb)

Types: classical conditioning, operant conditioning, social conditioning

Examples

"Pavlov's dogs demonstrated classical conditioning — they learned to salivate at the sound of a bell."

"Advertisers use conditioning to create positive associations with their brands."

🎯 NOW YOU TRY

3. Nature vs. Nurture

+
Meaning

The ongoing debate about whether genetics (nature) or environment (nurture) plays a greater role in shaping human behaviour and development.

Form & Usage

nature vs. nurture (noun phrase) — also: nature versus nurture, the nature-nurture debate

Related terms: hereditary, innate, environmental factors, socialisation

Examples

"The nature vs. nurture debate has evolved — most psychologists now see it as an interaction."

"Twin studies have been crucial for understanding how nature and nurture contribute to personality."

Key Academic Terms (Part B)

4. Correlation vs. Causation

+
Meaning

Correlation: Two things are related or occur together.
Causation: One thing directly causes the other.
The key insight: correlation does NOT prove causation.

Form & Usage

correlation (noun) | to correlate with (verb)

causation (noun) | to cause (verb)

Key phrase: "Correlation does not imply causation"

Examples

"Ice cream sales correlate with drowning deaths, but ice cream doesn't cause drowning — both increase in summer."

"The study found a strong correlation, but couldn't establish causation."

🎯 NOW YOU TRY

Find a headline or claim that confuses correlation with causation. Explain why it's misleading.

5. Placebo Effect

+
Meaning

A measurable improvement in health or behaviour that cannot be attributed to treatment itself — it occurs because the person believes they are receiving treatment.

Form & Usage

placebo effect (noun) | placebo (noun — a fake treatment)

Collocations: control for the placebo effect, placebo-controlled trial, experience a placebo effect

Examples

"In the study, 30% of participants given a sugar pill reported reduced pain — a classic placebo effect."

"Double-blind trials are designed to control for the placebo effect."

🎯 NOW YOU TRY

Have you ever experienced something that might have been a placebo effect? Describe the situation using proper terminology.

Wrap-Up: Key Takeaways

Today's Key Terms

  • Cognitive bias — Systematic errors in thinking that affect our judgments and decisions
  • Conditioning — Learning through association (classical) or consequences (operant)
  • Nature vs. nurture — The debate about genetics versus environment in shaping behaviour
  • Correlation vs. causation — The crucial distinction between things that occur together and things that cause each other
  • Placebo effect — Improvement that occurs because someone believes they're being treated
