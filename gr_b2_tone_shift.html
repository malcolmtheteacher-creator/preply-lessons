It's not what you say — it's how you say it

"That's great" can mean genuine excitement, biting sarcasm, total boredom, or surprised disbelief — depending entirely on your tone of voice. This game trains that skill.

How it works: A phrase appears. One person secretly picks a tone/emotion from the options, then says the phrase in that tone. The other person has to identify which tone they used. Then swap.

Warm-up

Say "Oh, you're here" in these 4 ways:
1. You're happy to see them
2. You're annoyed they showed up
3. You're surprised — you thought they left
4. You're terrified of them

What changed? The words are identical — what did your voice do differently each time?

Reflection

Which tone was hardest to produce? Sarcasm? Disappointment? Boredom? Why?
Which tone was hardest to detect? Were there tones that sounded similar to each other?
In your language, do the same tones work? Is sarcasm expressed the same way? Are there tones in your language that don't exist in English?
Think about real misunderstandings. Has your tone ever been misread in English? A message that was meant as a joke but taken seriously? What happened?
