The Last Class

Alphonse Daudet's story of a boy who did not want to study — until the last lesson.

Want the fuller version? Read this story at B2/C1 level →

Today: you'll tell the story of a boy's last French lesson — and you'll practise saying what is right and what was a mistake: you should study / I should have studied.
Warm up — before we read. Talk about these questions out loud. There are no wrong answers.
Discuss:
  • Is there something you want to learn but always say "next month, next year"?
  • How many languages do people speak in your country or region?
  • Why do people study a language? Give three reasons.

Now — the words for today's story

Ten words open the story. Do the four short activities. Say your answer out loud first, then click Reveal.

A · What does the word mean here?

1. Usually the classroom was noisy: desks, voices, the teacher's ruler on the table. That morning the room was completely silent.
silent (adj) — with no sound at all. Noun: silence.
2. On the wall of the town hall there was a paper with an official announcement. People stood and read it. It was a notice.
a notice (n) — an official message on a wall or board for everybody to read.
3. Franz did not study at home. He played by the river and looked for birds' nests. He wasted a lot of mornings.
to waste (v) — to use time or money badly, for nothing. "Don't waste time." Noun: a waste of time.

B · Which word means…?

Three words: lesson · village · mother tongue. Say the right word, then reveal.

1. Which word means a period of time when you learn something at school?
a lesson (n) — "We have four lessons this morning." Also: to learn a lesson = to learn from a mistake.
2. Which word means a very small town in the countryside?
a village (n) — smaller than a town. "Everyone in the village knew him."
3. Which word means the language you learned first, at home, as a small child?
your mother tongue — also called your first language or native language.

C · Choose the meaning — a / b / c

1. Franz was ashamed because he could not say his lesson. Ashamed means…
  (a) very angry   (b) feeling bad about yourself   (c) very cold
(b) — ashamed (adj) = you feel bad about something you did or did not do. "I was ashamed of my work."
2. The old men came to the school to say thank you and goodbye to the teacher. They wanted to show respect. Respect means…
  (a) a good opinion of someone, and polite behaviour to them   (b) money   (c) fear
(a) — respect (n/v) — "I have great respect for her." Adjective: respectful.

D · Word families & word partners

1. You know to learn. Complete: to learn a language · to learn something ___ heart (= perfectly, from memory). And what do we call the person who learns?
to learn something by heart · the person: a learner (or a student, a pupil).
2. One phrasal verb fits all three: to put ___ your homework until tomorrow · to put ___ the meeting until Friday · Stop putting it ___! What does it mean?
put off = to do something later, not now. "I always put off my homework."

Now use them — out loud

Talk before we read:
  • Use notice, silent, village and put off: why do you think the whole village comes to a children's classroom?
  • Which words are about school? Which are about feelings? Say them in two groups.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. Then answer the questions from memory, and talk about one question.

Part 1 — Late for school

Franz was late. He was also afraid, because the teacher, Monsieur Hamel, said he would ask questions about grammar that morning — and Franz did not study. It was a beautiful warm day. He could hear birds in the trees and soldiers in the field. "Maybe I can stay outside today," he thought. But he ran to school.

Near the town hall he saw some people in front of the notice board. For two years all the bad news came from that board. Franz did not stop.

Usually, at the beginning of school, there was a lot of noise: desks, voices, the teacher's ruler on the table. But that morning the school was silent — silent like a Sunday. Through the window Franz saw all the children in their seats. He opened the door. His face was red. But Monsieur Hamel was not angry. "Go to your place quickly, little Franz," he said gently. "We are starting without you."

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Why is Franz afraid that morning?

2. What is strange when he arrives at school? Give two things.

1. He is late, and the teacher will ask questions about grammar, but Franz did not study.

2. The school is completely silent, the children are already in their seats, and the teacher is not angry with him.

Discuss:
  • Were you ever late for school or work? How did you feel?

Part 2 — The last lesson

Franz saw two more strange things. Monsieur Hamel was wearing his beautiful green coat and his black hat — clothes for special days only. And at the back of the room, on the empty seats, the old people of the village were sitting: old Hauser with a very old book, the old mayor, the postman. They all looked sad.

Then Monsieur Hamel spoke. "My children, this is our last French lesson. There is an order from Berlin: from tomorrow, the schools here will teach only German. The new teacher comes tomorrow. Please listen carefully today."

Franz could not believe it. So that was the news on the notice board! His last French lesson — and he could hardly write! He thought about all the mornings by the river, all the days he did not study. Suddenly his books were not boring. They were like old friends. Then Monsieur Hamel called his name for the grammar. Franz stood up. He could not say one line. He looked at the floor and his heart was beating fast.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who is at the back of the classroom, and why?

2. What is the news, and how does Franz feel?

1. The old people of the village — Hauser, the old mayor, the postman. They came for the last French lesson.

2. From tomorrow the school will teach only German. Franz feels terrible: he wasted his time and now it is finished.

Discuss:
  • Franz's books were boring, and then suddenly they were important. What changed — the books, or Franz?

Part 3 — Two words on the blackboard

Monsieur Hamel was not angry with Franz. "You are punished enough," he said. "We all say the same thing: 'There is time. I will learn tomorrow.' And then it is too late." Then he said something surprising. "It is my fault too. I sent you to water my garden. I gave you a holiday when I wanted to go fishing."

He talked about the French language. "It is the most beautiful language in the world," he said. "Keep it. Never forget it. When people lose their freedom, their language is the key to the prison." Then he read from the grammar book, and Franz understood everything — it was easy today! After that they wrote. On every page were the words of their country, again and again, in beautiful writing. Everybody worked in silence. At the back, old Hauser was writing with the small children, and his voice was shaking.

At twelve o'clock the church bell rang. Outside, the soldiers' trumpets played. Monsieur Hamel stood up. His face was very white. "My friends," he said, "I —" But he could not finish. He turned to the blackboard, took the chalk, and wrote two words in very big letters: the name of his country. Then he put his head against the wall. He did not speak. He made a small sign with his hand: that is all — you can go.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Who does Monsieur Hamel blame?

2. What happens at twelve o'clock?

1. Everybody — the parents, the children, and also himself, because he sent pupils to work in his garden and gave holidays when he wanted to fish.

2. The church bell rings and the soldiers play their trumpets. The teacher cannot speak, so he writes two big words on the blackboard and sends the class home with his hand.

Discuss:
  • The teacher says a language is "the key to the prison". What do you think he means?

Grammar — should and should have

This story is full of one feeling: "I did the wrong thing, and now it is too late." English has an exact structure for that.

should / shouldn't + verb = advice about now or the future. It is a good idea, or a bad idea.
• You should study tonight. (good idea)
• You shouldn't waste your time. (bad idea)
No to and no -s: ✓ He should study. ✗ He should to study. ✗ He shoulds study.
should have + past participle = about the PAST. You did the wrong thing, and you cannot change it now.
• I should have studied. (I didn't study — and now I am sorry)
• He shouldn't have waited. (He waited — and it was a mistake)

In fast speech: should've /ˈʃʊdəv/ · shouldn't have /ˈʃʊdəntəv/. Listen for it — it sounds like one word.
Careful: ✗ I should studied. ✓ I should have studied.

1. should or shouldn't?

Write should or shouldn't in each gap.

1. Franz is late again. He get up earlier.

2. The children talk during the last lesson.

3. If you want to learn a language, you practise a little every day.

1. should   2. shouldn't   3. should

2. Now the past — should have / shouldn't have

Write three words in each gap.

1. Franz did not study his grammar. He (study) it.

2. He played by the river every morning. He (play) so much.

3. The village did not learn to read. They (learn) years before.

1. should have studied   2. shouldn't have played   3. should have learned

Always three parts: should + have + past participle.

3. Looking back — complete the paragraph

Write the correct form in each gap. Then read the paragraph aloud.

Franz is very sorry today. He (listen) to his teacher. He (waste) so many mornings by the river. The parents (send) their children to school more often. Monsieur Hamel says that he (give) so many holidays, and that he (be) stricter. Now it is too late. But from tomorrow, Franz (study) every day — that is his decision.

should have listened · shouldn't have wasted · should have sent · shouldn't have given · should have been · should study

Look at the last one: it is about tomorrow, so we use should + verb, with no "have".

Keep this structure ready — in the Speak tab you will talk about your own learning.

Going deeper

Daudet wrote this story in 1873, after a war between France and Prussia. The region of Alsace became German, and the schools had to teach in German. French children read this story in school for a hundred years. Remember: it tells one side of the story. In that region, many families spoke German or Alsatian at home too.

The heart of it. There is no battle in this story. There are no politicians. There is a boy who did not do his homework, a teacher in his best coat, some old men at the back of the room, and a lesson that finishes at twelve o'clock. The big event stays outside: a notice on a wall, a trumpet in the street. And the teacher does something surprising — he does not only blame the soldiers. He blames the parents, the children, and himself. At the end he cannot speak. So he writes two words on the blackboard, as big as he can, and sends the children home with his hand.

Talk it through:
  • Why does the teacher blame himself too? Is that fair?
  • Franz understands the value of school on the last day. Is that normal for people?
  • Why is the story stronger with a small classroom instead of a big battle?
  • You are learning a language now, and nobody is stopping you. Does this story change how you feel about that?

Task B · Whose fault is it? (3–4 minutes)

Say what you think and give three reasons. Franz did not study. But his parents sent him to work, and his teacher gave many holidays. Who should we blame? What should each person have done?

Talk about studying, mistakes and languages.
