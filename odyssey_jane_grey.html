The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. Jane was a gifted scholar.
a scholar (n) — a person who studies.
2. She was proclaimed queen.
to proclaim (v) — to announce officially.
3. She was reluctant to accept the crown.
reluctant (adj) — unwilling.
4. Her supporters began to desert her.
to desert (v) — to leave.
5. She was condemned as a traitor.
a traitor (n) — someone who betrays their country.
6. Mary at first wished to spare her.
to spare (v) — to not kill.
7. Wyatt's rebellion sealed her fate.
a rebellion (n) — a revolt.
8. She spoke on the scaffold.
a scaffold (n) — the platform for executions.
9. Jane was a pawn.
a pawn (n) — someone used by others.
10. The court chose to condemn her.
to condemn (v) — to sentence to punishment.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — The scholar

Lady Jane Grey, great-granddaughter of Henry VII, was highly educated in Latin, Greek and Hebrew. In 1553 she married Guildford Dudley, son of the Duke of Northumberland.

When Edward VI died, she was proclaimed queen on 10 July 1553. Sources suggest she was reluctant.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What did Jane study?

2. Who was her father-in-law?

1. Latin, Greek and Hebrew.

2. The Duke of Northumberland.

Discuss:
  • Why would a scholar be chosen as queen?

Part 2 — Nine days

Mary, Henry VIII's daughter, gathered support in East Anglia. Councillors and soldiers deserted Jane. On 19 July Mary was proclaimed queen in London; Jane's reign lasted about nine days (thirteen by some counts).

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. How long did Jane's reign last?

2. Why did it fail?

1. About nine days.

2. Mary gained support and Jane's supporters deserted.

Discuss:
  • Why did people prefer Mary?

Part 3 — The scaffold

Jane was imprisoned in the Tower. Mary was at first reluctant to execute her, but after Wyatt's Rebellion in early 1554, which included Jane's father, she decided Jane was too dangerous. Jane was executed on 12 February 1554. According to accounts, she said on the scaffold that she had not wanted the crown.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. What sealed Jane's fate?

2. What did she say on the scaffold?

1. Wyatt's Rebellion, which involved her father.

2. That she had not wanted the crown.

Discuss:
  • Was Mary right to execute Jane?

Grammar — Reported Speech

Much of what we know about the past comes from chroniclers reporting what people said. Reported speech moves the tense back and changes the pronouns.

How it works:
• "I will return," he said → He said that he would return.
• "We have lost," she said → She said that they had lost.
• Reporting verbs: claimed, promised, warned, insisted, denied.

1. Report the words

Complete each sentence about Lady Jane Grey. Use the word in brackets.

1. Jane said, "I did not want the crown." → She said that she the crown.

2. Mary said, "I will spare her." → Mary said that she her.

3. Councillors said, "We support Jane." → They said that they Jane.

1. had not wanted

2. would spare

3. supported

2. Choose the reporting verb

1. Northumberland that Jane was the rightful queen. (insisted / denied)

2. Her supporters to defend her, but deserted. (promised / claimed)

3. Jane that she had acted willingly. (denied / promised)

1. insisted

2. promised

3. denied

Going deeper

Jane is often seen as a tragic victim of adult ambition. Paintings like Paul Delaroche's The Execution of Lady Jane Grey (1833) helped make her a romantic figure.

Worth noticing: some historians argue she was more strong-minded than the "innocent victim" image suggests.
Learning from the past — for today: Know whose plan you are in. Before accepting a role or promotion, ask who benefits and what risks you carry.
Dig into it — talk these through:
