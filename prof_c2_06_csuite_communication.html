C-Suite Communication Vocabulary

Executive Summary A concise, high-level overview of key findings, recommendations, and implications, typically one page or two slides.
Bottom Line The core message or decision point; what matters most to the business. Always lead with this in executive communication.
ROI Return on Investment; the profit generated for each unit of capital deployed. Executives think in ROI terms.
Risk Appetite The level and type of risk the company is willing to accept to achieve strategic objectives.
Strategic Alignment How a proposal or initiative supports the company's overarching strategic goals and priorities.
Scalability The ability of a solution or business to grow efficiently without proportional increases in cost or complexity.
Bandwidth The capacity of a team or organisation to take on additional projects or responsibilities.
Value Creation The increased economic worth generated for shareholders and stakeholders through strategy execution and operations.
Decision Rights Clarity about who has authority to make what decisions, preventing bottlenecks and political battles.
Accountability Clear ownership of outcomes; leaders must be able to articulate what they are responsible for and why.

What CEOs, CFOs, and COOs Want to Hear

Understanding executive priorities transforms how you communicate.

The CEO Perspective

Priorities: Strategic direction, competitive positioning, shareholder value, stakeholder confidence.

What they want to hear:

  • "This initiative supports our stated strategy of [strategic priority]."
  • "Here's how we win against our three main competitors on this dimension."
  • "This positions us for the next market cycle."
  • "Our stakeholders care about this: investors want [benefit], customers want [benefit], employees want [benefit]."

The CFO Perspective

Priorities: Capital efficiency, risk management, cash flow, compliance, returns on invested capital.

What they want to hear:

  • "This investment requires £X capex and will return Y% ROI over Z years."
  • "We've stress-tested this against three scenarios; here's our downside protection."
  • "This improves our capital efficiency and strengthens our balance sheet."
  • "Risk has been quantified and mitigated."

The COO Perspective

Priorities: Execution excellence, operational efficiency, team capability, process improvement, scalability.

What they want to hear:

  • "We have the bandwidth to execute this without compromising core operations."
  • "Here's our implementation plan with clear milestones and decision gates."
  • "Our teams have the capability, or we have a clear plan to build it."
  • "This scales efficiently; we've planned for 2x and 5x growth scenarios."

The Universal Executive Expectation

Every executive wants to hear three things:

1. You know what you're talking about. Be precise, factual, and speak with authority.

2. You've thought about it deeply. Show evidence of rigorous analysis, scenario testing, and risk mitigation.

3. You're accountable. Own your recommendation and be clear about what could go wrong.

Critical insight: Executives are drowning in information. Your job is not to provide more data—it's to synthesise, prioritise, and present what matters most in the clearest possible way.

Precision Language for Complex Ideas

At C2 level, expressing sophisticated business concepts with clarity and concision is essential.

The Art of the Executive Summary Opening

Weak opening:

"This is a complicated project we've been working on. There are many factors to consider, and we wanted to get your input on how to proceed."

Strong opening:

"We recommend approval of the £15M investment in Platform X. It delivers 28% ROI over four years and positions us as the market leader in the fastest-growing segment. We've stress-tested against downside scenarios; we break even at 60% of our conservative forecast."

Translating Complexity into Clarity

Weak (unclear):

"Our supply chain optimisation initiative has shown promising KPIs across multiple operational vectors, with some positive indicators on cost reduction and customer satisfaction metrics."

Strong (clear):

"Our supply chain redesign reduced costs by 8% while improving on-time delivery from 92% to 97%. We've identified an additional 3% cost reduction opportunity if we consolidate our supplier base from 47 to 28 vendors."

Handling Uncertainty with Precision

Weak (vague):

"We think this could work pretty well, but it's hard to say for sure."

Strong (precise about uncertainty):

"Our base case forecasts £8M in annual savings. The upside case, if adoption exceeds 80% of our sales force, reaches £12M. The downside, if adoption is only 40%, is £4M. We're factoring in £1M for implementation risk and expect payback in 18 months."

Connecting to Strategic Priorities

Weak (disconnected):

"This is a good idea that will help us grow."

Strong (aligned to strategy):

"This directly addresses our strategic priority of becoming the low-cost operator in our sector. It improves gross margins from 42% to 47%, bringing us to parity with competitor X and ahead of competitor Y."

The Language of Accountability

Weak (diffuse responsibility):

"There are some risks, but we'll monitor them as we go."

Strong (clear ownership):

"I take full accountability for delivering £X EBITDA by year-end. Here are the key risks: technology adoption, competitive response, and macro slowdown. I have mitigation plans for each. I'll report monthly to the CFO."

Speaking Task: Investment Case Pitch

You have three minutes to pitch a major investment to the CEO and CFO. Mastery is demonstrated through clarity, conviction, and strategic alignment.

Your Scenario

You lead Product Development. You're proposing a £25 million investment to build a new AI-powered analytics platform. The company's strategy emphasises moving upmarket and creating recurring revenue through software offerings. You have existing customers in the enterprise segment and early positive feedback from a pilot.

  • Capex: £25M (year 1); £10M (year 2)
  • Timeline to market: 18 months
  • Revenue forecast: £8M (Y3), £22M (Y4), £35M (Y5)
  • Target market: £2 billion addressable, 12% annual growth
  • Key risk: Development timeline slippage; competitive response
10:00

Your Task: Write Your Three-Minute Pitch (10 minutes to prepare)

Structure your pitch to include:

1. The Strategic Case (30 seconds)

How does this align with company strategy? Why now? What's the competitive urgency?

2. The Market Opportunity (30 seconds)

Size, growth, competitive positioning. Why will customers buy this?

3. The Financial Case (45 seconds)

Capex, payback period, ROI. Show you've done the math and stress-tested the assumptions.

4. The Risks and Mitigation (30 seconds)

What can go wrong? How are you protecting downside?

5. The Call to Action (15 seconds)

What decision do you need? What happens next?

Delivery challenge: Record yourself delivering this pitch aloud. Listen for clarity, pacing, and conviction. Can you deliver 300 words per minute while remaining intelligible? Do you sound like you believe in what you're saying, or defensive? Confidence is built through preparation.
