Skills Focus 1: Recognising Uncomfortable Situations

Understanding what makes a situation uncomfortable is the first step to handling it well.

Case Study: A Performance Review Misunderstanding

What happened: Izzy and Miho had a performance review. Miho has had some performance issues. Izzy clearly states what needs improving. Miho prefers to read between the lines and contemplate things carefully.

Izzy's perspective: "Miho agreed with all the things I told her to do. I was happy at how receptive she was. But she seemed a little serious and quiet. I made some light-hearted comments about the improvements she needed to make."

Miho's perspective: "Izzy seemed to be making a joke about some really serious things. She just kept talking about how I need to improve. I was shocked and really upset when I got home. I can't believe I made all those mistakes, and she's only just told me. There must be something wrong with me. I'm not looking forward to Monday."

What made this situation uncomfortable?

  • Izzy communicated directly; Miho preferred indirect feedback
  • Izzy used humour to soften criticism; Miho felt jokes made it less serious
  • Cultural communication styles did not match
  • Neither person understood the other's perspective

Potential Sources of Intercultural Discomfort

Common sources include:

  • Direct vs. indirect communication: Some cultures value bluntness; others prefer hints and implication
  • Personal space and touch: Comfort levels vary widely across cultures
  • Eye contact: In some cultures, direct eye contact is respectful; in others, it's disrespectful
  • Humour: What is funny in one culture may offend in another
  • Time orientation: Some cultures are punctual; others are more flexible
  • Formality levels: Expectations for formal vs. casual interaction differ
  • Conflict style: Some cultures address conflict directly; others avoid confrontation
  • Gender roles: Expectations about how genders interact vary
  • Religious or political sensitivities: Topics that are taboo or require careful handling

Skills Focus 2: Response Strategies

When you encounter an uncomfortable intercultural situation, you have several strategies to choose from.

Key Response Strategies

Strategy 1: Clarify and Ask Questions

What to do: Ask the other person to explain. Don't assume you understand.
Example: "I want to make sure I understood correctly. Can you explain what you meant?"

Strategy 2: Explain Your Perspective

What to do: Share your background or cultural context to help them understand you.
Example: "In my culture, direct feedback is seen as professional. I didn't mean to be harsh."

Strategy 3: Acknowledge Differences

What to do: Recognise that both approaches are valid in their own contexts.
Example: "I see now that we communicate differently. Both ways are normal in our cultures."

Strategy 4: Adapt Your Behaviour

What to do: Adjust your communication style when you know the cultural preference.
Example: Use more indirect language if you know the person prefers it. Be more formal if that's expected.

Remember: Your goal is understanding, not winning the argument. Staying curious about the other person's perspective is more effective than defending your own.

Task: Confidence-Building Strategies

Here are three proven strategies to help you feel more confident in intercultural situations. Read each one and reflect on how it might help you.

Strategy 1: Visualise Success

How it works: Imagining uncomfortable intercultural situations ahead of time helps reduce anxiety and increases performance.

1. Think about the next intercultural situation you will encounter.
2. Find out as much information as you can about it (who, where, context).
3. Imagine the scene in detail: What are you wearing? Who are you with? What can you hear?
4. Engage all your five senses to create a vivid mental picture.
5. Sit with your eyes closed for 3-5 minutes imagining the situation.
6. Visualise how it will go right. How could things go wrong, and how would you handle it?

Reflection: Do you think visualising a positive or negative outcome is more useful? How might this visualisation activity help you in a future intercultural situation?

Strategy 2: Keep a Journal

How it works: Writing about uncomfortable experiences helps you view them objectively and learn from them.

Why journalling helps:

  • View experiences objectively and clear your mind of stress
  • Recognise which cultural preferences trigger emotional reactions in you
  • Learn and try out new ways of responding to culturally different situations

How to journal:

Write about the situation factually: when, where, who was present
Describe your emotions and what you felt physically
Write about the cultural differences between you and the other person
List the assumptions or stereotypes you made
Explain why you think the situation happened

Reflection: What could be the long-term benefits of keeping a journal like this?

Strategy 3: Build Diverse Relationships

How it works: The more diverse relationships you have, the stronger your confidence in intercultural situations.

Steps to build diversity:

1. List the people at work you have the most trustful relationships with
2. Categorise them: age, gender, ethnic background, nationality, ability, etc.
3. Ask: Are there groups missing from my list?
4. Find ways to connect with people in those groups
5. Seek new intercultural experiences: try new cuisines, join new social groups, learn a language

Reflection: What are the long-term benefits of having a larger and more diverse network? How might increasing your intercultural experiences help you feel more confident?
