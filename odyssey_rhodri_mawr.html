The words for today's story

Each new word is hidden in a sentence. Read it aloud and say what you think the bold word means from the rest of the sentence — then reveal it and see how close you were.

1. Rhodri inherited Gwynedd in 844.
to inherit (v) — to receive from a parent who has died.
2. Wales had several small kingdoms.
a kingdom (n) — a country ruled by a king.
3. He united much of Wales.
to unite (v) — to join together.
4. Viking raiders attacked the coast.
a raider (n) — a person who attacks quickly and steals.
5. He drove off the Danes.
to drive off (phr v) — to force to leave.
6. He spent time in exile in Ireland.
an exile (n) — living away from your country.
7. He was an ancestor of later princes.
an ancestor (n) — a person in your family long ago.
8. Mawr means "great" in Welsh.
mawr (adj) — (Welsh) great, big.

The story, in three parts

Read each part. After it, there's a quick comprehension check (try the questions from memory first), then one thing to discuss.

Part 1 — Gwynedd and beyond

Rhodri ap Merfyn became king of Gwynedd in north-west Wales in 844. In 855 he gained Powys, his mother's kingdom, and around 872 Seisyllwg in the south-west through his wife. By then he had become the most powerful ruler in Wales, though not of all Wales.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Which kingdoms did Rhodri gain?

2. Did he rule all Wales?

1. Powys and Seisyllwg.

2. No — much of it, but not all.

Discuss:
  • Is it fair to gain kingdoms through marriage and inheritance?

Part 2 — Vikings

Danish Vikings had raided the British coasts for decades. In 856 Rhodri defeated a Danish force on Anglesey and killed its leader, Horm (or Gorm). The news was celebrated even in the court of Charles the Bald in France.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did Rhodri beat the Danes?

2. Who was killed?

1. On Anglesey.

2. Their leader, Horm (Gorm).

Discuss:
  • Why would a victory in Wales be news in France?

Part 3 — Exile and death

In 877 Rhodri was defeated by the Vikings and fled to Ireland. He returned, but in 878 he was killed fighting the English of Mercia. His sons and grandsons, including Hywel Dda, ruled much of Wales for generations.

Quick check — answer from memory, then reveal:

1. Where did he go in 877?

2. How did he die?

1. To Ireland.

2. Killed fighting the Mercians in 878.

Discuss:
  • Is a leader judged by his life or his legacy?

Grammar — Past Perfect & Sequencing

History is a chain of causes. To show that one thing happened before another in the past, English uses the past perfect.

How it works:
• Past simple tells the main line: "The Danes attacked at Christmas."
• Past perfect (had + past participle) reaches further back: "By then, Alfred had already escaped into the marshes."
• Signal words: by the time, already, before, after, when, until.

1. Past simple or past perfect?

Complete each sentence about Rhodri Mawr. Use the word in brackets.

1. By 872, Rhodri (gain) three kingdoms.

2. The Vikings (raid) for decades before 856.

3. After he (lose) in 877, he fled.

1. had gained

2. had raided

3. had lost

2. Put the earlier event in the past perfect

1. His sons ruled after he (die).

2. By 855, he (rule) Gwynedd for eleven years.

3. He returned after he (spend) time in Ireland.

1. had died

2. had ruled

3. had spent

Going deeper

Rhodri's unions were personal: they depended on him. After his death the kingdoms divided again among heirs — a pattern repeated in Welsh history.

Worth noticing: Welsh naming used "ap", meaning "son of": Rhodri ap Merfyn = Rhodri son of Merfyn.
Learning from the past — for today: Build systems, not just success. Rhodri's power depended on him personally. If your project only works because of you, it will fall apart when you leave — write things down and share them.
Dig into it — talk these through:
  • Why didn't Rhodri's unity last?
  • How do small nations survive?
  • What would fall apart if you left your job?
