Have You Ever...? Talking About Experience

For things that happened at some point in your life — you don't say when.

Spot the pattern

Three everyday questions and answers about experience:

  • "Have you ever ridden a horse?"
  • "I've never broken a bone."
  • "She's travelled to over ten countries by herself."

Before you check: what comes right after "have" or "has" every time?

Check the pattern CLICK
have/has + past participle (ridden, broken, travelled) — never the base verb, never the past simple form. "Ever" and "never" just add the meaning "at any time" / "at no time".

I've Just...: Something That Happened Seconds Ago

For something that finished a very short time ago — right now, this minute.

Spot the pattern

Three things people say right after something happens:

  • "I've just watered the plants — that's why the floor's wet."
  • "She's just woken up — give her a minute."
  • "We've just arrived — give us a minute!"

Where exactly does the word "just" sit? Say it before you check.

Check the pattern CLICK
have/has + just + past participle. "Just" always sits between the helper (have/has) and the participle — never before "have", never after the participle.

Ever or Yesterday? One Big Difference

Present perfect = a life experience, no exact time. Simple past = one exact moment.

Spot the pattern

  • "I've been to Rome." (experience — we don't know or care when)
  • "I went to Rome last year." (one trip — an exact time: last year)
  • "She's never been to Spain, but she went to Portugal in 2019."

What tells you which tense to use — "I've been" or "I went"?

Check the pattern CLICK
A word that names an exact, finished time — yesterday, last week, last year, in 2019, when I was a child — always means simple past. No time word, just a life experience? Use present perfect.
Careful: sentences like "I've bought a new phone yesterday" are a very common mistake. "Yesterday" is an exact finished time, so it needs the simple past: "I bought a new phone yesterday."
No exact time → have/has + past participle
yesterday / last year / in 2019 → simple past
