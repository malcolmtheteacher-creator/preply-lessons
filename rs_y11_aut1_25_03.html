What does the Shahadah declare?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: It declares: 'There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the messenger of Allah.' It affirms Tawhid (oneness of God) and Risalah (prophethood of Muhammad).

Why is the Shahadah considered the most important pillar?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: It is the foundation of all other practices. Without believing in Tawhid and Risalah, no other pillar has meaning. It is the act of conversion, the first words a baby hears, and ideally the last words before death.

What is shirk?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Shirk is associating partners with Allah — it is the greatest sin in Islam. The Shahadah directly rejects shirk by declaring there is no god but Allah.

What Qur'anic evidence supports the Shahadah?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Surah 112:1-4: 'Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the eternal. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there any equivalent to Him.' Also Surah 47:19: 'Know that there is no god but Allah.'

How does a person convert to Islam?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: By sincerely reciting the Shahadah before two Muslim witnesses. Niyyah (sincere intention) is essential — the words must come from genuine belief.

Salah: The Five Daily Prayers

Salah is the second pillar of Islam — the five compulsory daily prayers performed at set times. It is the most regular act of worship in a Muslim's life and the primary way of maintaining a direct connection with Allah.

The Qur'an commands: 'Establish prayer for My remembrance' (Surah 20:14). Prayer is not optional — it is a direct command from Allah, established during the Prophet Muhammad's Night Journey (Isra and Mi'raj) when Allah initially commanded 50 prayers, reduced to 5 through Moses's intercession.

The Five Daily Prayers

Fajr — Dawn prayer (2 rak'ahs)
Dhuhr — Midday prayer (4 rak'ahs)
Asr — Afternoon prayer (4 rak'ahs)
Maghrib — Sunset prayer (3 rak'ahs)
Isha — Night prayer (4 rak'ahs)

A rak'ah is one cycle of standing, bowing, and prostrating. The number varies by prayer.

Preparation: Wudu

Wudu (ritual washing) must be performed before salah. It involves washing the hands, mouth, nose, face, arms to the elbows, wiping the head, and washing the feet. Wudu symbolises spiritual purification — preparing to stand before Allah. The Qur'an commands: 'O you who believe, when you rise for prayer, wash your faces and your hands to the elbows' (Surah 5:6).

How Salah Is Performed

Muslims face the qiblah (direction of the Ka'bah in Makkah) for every prayer. The prayer begins with 'Allahu Akbar' (God is the greatest) and includes recitation of Surah Al-Fatiha (the opening chapter of the Qur'an), followed by another surah or verses.

Each rak'ah involves:

Qiyam — standing, reciting from the Qur'an
Ruku — bowing, glorifying Allah
Sujud — prostration, the closest position to Allah
Sitting — between prostrations, asking for forgiveness

The prayer ends with the Taslim — turning the head right and left saying 'As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah' (Peace and mercy of Allah be upon you), acknowledging the angels and fellow Muslims.

Jummah — Friday Prayer

Jummah (Friday prayer) replaces the Dhuhr prayer and is performed in congregation at the mosque. It is obligatory for adult Muslim men. It includes a khutbah (sermon) delivered by the imam, addressing spiritual, moral, and sometimes social issues.

The Qur'an commands: 'O you who believe, when the call to prayer is made on Friday, hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade' (Surah 62:9).

Jummah is significant because it brings the community together weekly, reinforces the ummah, and provides guidance through the khutbah.

Sunni and Shi'a Differences in Salah

While both Sunni and Shi'a Muslims perform five daily prayers, there are some differences:

Sunni Practice

Sunni Muslims pray five separate times daily at the five set times. They pray with arms folded across the chest or stomach. They prostrate on the prayer mat or floor directly. Additional voluntary prayers (Sunnah prayers) are encouraged before and after the compulsory ones.

Shi'a Practice

Shi'a Muslims may combine Dhuhr with Asr, and Maghrib with Isha, praying three times daily instead of five (though still performing five prayers). They pray with arms by their sides. They prostrate on a turbah (clay tablet, often from Karbala). They may add 'Ali is the friend of God' to the Adhan.

Why Does Salah Matter?

Salah is often called the 'backbone of Islam.' The Prophet Muhammad said: 'The first thing a person will be asked about on the Day of Judgement is their prayer. If it is sound, all their other deeds will be sound' (Hadith).

Salah matters because it:

• Maintains a direct connection between the Muslim and Allah — no priest needed
• Creates discipline and structure — five times daily, every day
• Demonstrates submission (sujud/prostration is physically lowering yourself before God)
• Unites the ummah — millions praying in the same direction at the same times
• Provides spiritual comfort and guidance in daily life

Key Terms

Salah: The five compulsory daily prayers — the second pillar of Islam

Rak'ah: One complete cycle of standing, bowing, and prostrating in prayer

Wudu: Ritual washing performed before prayer

Qiblah: The direction of the Ka'bah in Makkah, faced during prayer

Jummah: Friday congregational prayer with khutbah (sermon)

Sujud: Prostration — the closest physical position to Allah

Turbah: Clay tablet used by Shi'a Muslims during prostration

Khutbah: Sermon delivered during Jummah prayer
