Reading-Related Vocabulary

Click each card to reveal the definition.

soft spot for A particular liking or affection for something
into Interested in or enjoying something
put off To discourage someone; to make someone lose interest
gone off To lose interest in something you previously enjoyed
flip through To look through a book quickly, checking pages without reading thoroughly
skip bits To omit or avoid reading certain sections of a text
nose in a book To be completely absorbed in reading; focused on a book
skim To read quickly to find the main ideas without reading every word
read up on To research or learn about a topic by reading about it
couldn't put down A book so interesting that you can't stop reading it
cover to cover Reading a book from beginning to end completely
all-time faves Your favorite books or works that you return to repeatedly

Expressions for Opinions About Reading

Positive: "I've got a soft spot for anything funny." "I've never really been into newspapers, but..." "Then I got into English Literature at school."

Negative: "I think I got put off reading at school." "I'm not a big fan of reading." "I've gone off reading fiction these days."

Lead-In Discussion

Think about each of these statements before discussing with a partner or group:

Is reading important for communication?

Do you think reading makes you a better communicator? Why or why not?

Reading in the Modern Age

Do you think people nowadays read less than they used to? Is online reading replacing print?

The Benefits of Reading

What are the benefits of reading? Does reading reduce stress? Can it improve intelligence?

Reading Challenges

Do you think there's too much to read nowadays? How do you choose what to read?

Personal Reading Stories

Here are some real reading experiences. Which resonate with you?

"I've got a soft spot for anything funny. I really like Bridget Jones's Diary for example and Gentlemen Prefer Blondes is one of my all-time faves."

Opinion: Positive view of humorous fiction.

"I've never really been into newspapers, but I buy The Sunday Times and read that during the week in the mornings."

Opinion: Selective reader; prefers certain publications.

"Then I got into English Lit at school – Charles Dickens, Thomas Hardy, The Bronte sisters."

Opinion: Developed interest in classic literature through school.

"I think I got put off reading at school – we used to have to read aloud and if you made a mistake everybody laughed. And the set books that we were forced to read were so boring."

Opinion: Negative school experience damaged interest in reading.

"I'm not a big fan of reading, but if I have to read I prefer print to online."

Opinion: Reluctant reader; shows medium preference.

"I've gone off reading fiction these days – I read maybe one or two books a year that my wife buys me."

Opinion: Lost interest in fiction; reads only occasionally now.

Reading Behaviors & Strategies

Match these reading habits with their explanations:

1. Do you ever flip through cookery books looking for recipes?
Show Answer
This means browsing quickly, looking for specific information rather than reading completely.
2. Is it effective to skip bits in a book or read it cover to cover?
Show Answer
This question asks about your reading approach: selective reading (skipping) vs. complete reading (cover to cover).
3. Do you know someone who always has their nose in a book?
Show Answer
This idiom describes someone who is constantly reading, absorbed in books.
4. Do you like to skim articles in the newspaper?
Show Answer
To skim means to read quickly for the main ideas, checking headlines and summaries.
5. What was the last thing you had to read up on?
Show Answer
To read up on something means to research or study a topic in depth.
6. What was the last book you couldn't put down?
Show Answer
A book you couldn't put down is one so engaging and interesting that you keep reading and can't stop.

Phrases for Introducing a New Topic

When talking about your reading habits, use these phrases to introduce new ideas:

"As a child, I..."

Introduce experiences from your childhood reading.

"What really changed for me was..."

Explain a turning point in your reading habits.

"Looking back, I realize..."

Reflect on how your perspective has changed.

"These days, I mainly read..."

Describe your current reading preferences.

Speaking Task: Your Reading Story

You'll give a short talk about your reading habits and experiences. Use the topics below to help you prepare. Choose the areas that interest you most—this isn't an interview, so select what you want to discuss.

Talk Topics

Childhood Reading

Your relationship with reading as a child. Were you read to? What were your first books or comics?

Learning to Read

When and how did you learn to read? Do you have memories of this time?

Family Influence

Did your family read? What did they read? Did this influence your own reading?

School Reading

Books you had to read at school. Did you enjoy them? Were any forced on you?

Reading Barriers

What might have put you off reading? Has anything stopped you from reading more?

Current Preferences

What's currently on your bedside table? Do you prefer online or print? Do you read in other languages?

Reading for Purpose

Do you read for work, study, or pleasure? How much time do you spend reading weekly?

Favorites & Changes

All-time favorite books you return to. How have your reading habits changed over the years?

Structure Your Talk

A strong talk follows this structure:

Introduction (30 seconds)

Start with: "I'd like to tell you about my reading habits..." or "As someone who loves/doesn't love reading..."

Your Story (2-3 minutes)

Cover 3-4 topics from the list above. Use transition phrases: "As a child...", "What really changed for me...", "These days..." Include specific examples (book titles, authors, memories).

Conclusion (30 seconds)

Finish with: "In summary...", "Overall, I think...", or "Reading has taught me..."

Be Ready for Questions

Others might ask: "What's your favorite author?" or "Do you have a current read?" Be prepared with specific answers.

Useful Phrases While Speaking

Use these to stay fluent:

  • "I've got a soft spot for..."
  • "I was really into... at that time"
  • "That got me interested in..."
  • "I was put off by..."
  • "I've gone off... these days"
  • "What really appeals to me is..."
  • "The most memorable book was..."
  • "I couldn't put down..."

After Your Talk

Be prepared to:

  • Answer questions about books or authors you mentioned
  • Explain why certain books were important to you
  • Give recommendations based on your reading
  • Compare your reading habits with other speakers
