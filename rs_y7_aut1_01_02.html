Meet the Major Worldviews

Did you know? About 84% of the world's population — roughly 6.7 billion people — identify with a religion. The remaining 16% include atheists, agnostics, and people who say "spiritual but not religious."

The Abrahamic Faiths

Three of the six trace their roots to the same person: Abraham. They're called the "Abrahamic faiths."

Christianity

Founded: ~30 CE, based on the life and teachings of Jesus of Nazareth
Core belief: Jesus is the Son of God who died and rose again to save humanity from sin
Sacred text: The Bible (Old Testament + New Testament)
Key practice: Worship, prayer, sacraments (baptism, communion)
God: One God in three persons (the Trinity — Father, Son, Holy Spirit)

2.4 billion followers · Largest religion worldwide · Majority religion in the UK

Islam

Founded: 610 CE, when the Prophet Muhammad received revelations from God (Allah)
Core belief: There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is His messenger (Shahadah)
Sacred text: The Qur'an (believed to be the direct word of God in Arabic)
Key practice: The Five Pillars — declaration of faith, prayer, charity, fasting, pilgrimage
God: One God (Allah) — absolutely one, no partners, no Trinity

1.9 billion followers · Fastest-growing religion · Second-largest in the UK

Judaism

Founded: ~2000 BCE, rooted in God's covenant with Abraham and the giving of the Torah to Moses
Core belief: There is one God who made a special covenant (agreement) with the Jewish people
Sacred text: The Torah (first five books), plus the wider Tanakh and Talmud
Key practice: Shabbat (Sabbath), dietary laws (kashrut), festivals, prayer
God: One God — the oldest monotheistic tradition still practised

15 million followers · Small but hugely influential · Oldest Abrahamic faith

The Dharmic Traditions

Two more originated in India and share the concept of dharma (cosmic law, duty, or the right way of living).

Hinduism

Founded: No single founder — developed over 4,000+ years in the Indian subcontinent
Core belief: Brahman (ultimate reality) can be worshipped through many gods and goddesses; the soul (atman) is reborn through reincarnation until it achieves moksha (liberation)
Sacred texts: The Vedas, Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, and many more
Key practice: Puja (worship), meditation, yoga, festivals, pilgrimage
God: One ultimate reality (Brahman) expressed through many deities (Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva)

1.2 billion followers · Third-largest religion · No single founder or text

Buddhism

Founded: ~5th century BCE by Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha, "the awakened one")
Core belief: Suffering exists, but the Noble Eightfold Path and meditation can lead to Enlightenment (Nirvana)
Sacred texts: The Tripitaka (Three Baskets)
Key practice: Meditation, ethical conduct, the Middle Way
God: No creator God — the Buddha was a teacher, not a divine being

500 million followers · Varies by country · Originated in India but thrives in East Asia

Sikhism

Founded: 1500 CE by Guru Nanak in Punjab, India
Core belief: One God (Ek Onkar); no caste system; all humans are equal
Sacred text: The Guru Granth Sahib (the "living guru" — treated with reverence)
Key practice: Langar (free communal kitchen), meditation, seva (service to others)
God: One God — beyond gender, form, and all human understanding

30 million followers · Originated in Punjab, India · Strong emphasis on social justice

Not a Religion, But a Worldview

Humanism

Not founded by a person: Emerges from the Enlightenment and rational thought
Core belief: Human reason, compassion, and the natural world are the focus. No God needed. Meaning comes from helping others and improving the world.
Sacred text: None — humanists draw on reason, science, and ethics
Key practice: Charity, volunteering, evidence-based thinking, the Golden Rule (treat others as you'd like to be treated)
God: No belief in God — but deeply ethical and values-driven

50–100 million humanists globally · Growing in secular societies · Values compassion without supernatural belief

Deep-Dive: The Abrahamic vs Dharmic Divide

Abrahamic faiths (Christianity, Islam, Judaism) share:

  • A historical founder (Abraham)
  • A linear view of time (creation → judgement day)
  • A sacred book revealed by God
  • Emphasis on faith in God's word

Dharmic traditions (Hinduism, Buddhism) share:

  • No single founder; philosophical origins
  • A cyclical view of time (rebirth, eternal return)
  • Emphasis on personal practice (meditation, yoga, dharma)
  • Multiple sacred texts or oral traditions

Quick check: What's the key difference?

🔒Think first!

Abrahamic faiths believe in a personal God who revealed Himself through a prophet and a book. Dharmic traditions focus on universal principles (dharma) and personal practice (like meditation) to achieve liberation or enlightenment.

Why is Buddhism placed differently?

🔒Guess first!

Buddhism has no creator God, but some forms venerate the Buddha and bodhisattvas (enlightened beings). Humanism explicitly rejects God entirely. So Buddhism sits between theistic religions and pure atheism/humanism.

Quickfire: True or False?

1. All Abrahamic faiths believe in the same God.

True. Christians, Muslims, and Jews all worship the God of Abraham. Differences lie in belief about Jesus and the Prophet Muhammad.

2. Hinduism is a polytheistic religion.

True (mostly). Hindus worship many gods and goddesses, but many Hindu philosophers argue all are expressions of one ultimate reality (Brahman).

3. Buddhism requires belief in God.

False. The Buddha taught that belief in a creator God is not necessary for enlightenment. The focus is on suffering and the path to Nirvana.

4. Sikhism blends Islam and Hinduism.

Partially true. Guru Nanak (founder) lived in Punjab where both traditions were present, but Sikhism is a distinct worldview, not a blend.

5. Humanism is a religion.

False. Humanism is a worldview based on reason and compassion, not faith in God or supernatural claims. It's non-religious.

What makes something a "worldview"? (Retrieval from Lesson 1)

🔒Recall first!

A worldview is a set of beliefs and values that shape how you see the world, make decisions, and find meaning. It answers big questions: Is there a God? What is right and wrong? What happens after death?

Key Terms

Abrahamic
Religions tracing their roots to Abraham: Christianity, Islam, Judaism.
Dharmic
Traditions originating in India sharing the concept of dharma: Hinduism, Buddhism.
Monotheism
Belief in one God (Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Sikhism).
Trinity
Christian belief that God exists as three persons: Father, Son, Holy Spirit.
Tawhid
The Islamic belief in the absolute oneness of God (Allah).
Brahman
In Hinduism, the ultimate reality that underlies all existence.
Dukkha
Buddhist concept — suffering or dissatisfaction is part of all existence.
Langar
Free communal kitchen in Sikh gurdwaras, open to all regardless of faith, caste, or background.
