2. Encounter

There are thousands of religions in the world. In the UK, Religious Studies focuses on the six largest, plus the most significant non-religious worldview. Let's meet them.

About 84% of the world's population — roughly 6.7 billion people — identify with a religion. The remaining 16% include atheists, agnostics, and people who say "spiritual but not religious."

The Abrahamic Faiths

Three of the six trace their roots to the same person: Abraham, a figure revered in all three traditions. They're called the "Abrahamic faiths."

╋

Christianity

Founded: ~30 CE, based on the life and teachings of Jesus of Nazareth
Core belief: Jesus is the Son of God who died and rose again to save humanity from sin
Sacred text: The Bible (Old Testament + New Testament)
Key practice: Worship, prayer, sacraments (baptism, communion)
God: One God in three persons (the Trinity — Father, Son, Holy Spirit)

2.4 billion followers · Largest religion worldwide · Majority religion in the UK

☮

Islam

Founded: 610 CE, when the Prophet Muhammad received revelations from God (Allah)
Core belief: There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is His messenger (Shahadah)
Sacred text: The Qur'an (believed to be the direct word of God in Arabic)
Key practice: The Five Pillars — declaration of faith, prayer, charity, fasting, pilgrimage
God: One God (Allah) — absolutely one, no partners, no Trinity

1.9 billion followers · Fastest-growing religion · Second-largest in the UK

✡

Judaism

Founded: ~2000 BCE, rooted in God's covenant with Abraham and the giving of the Torah to Moses
Core belief: There is one God who made a special covenant (agreement) with the Jewish people
Sacred text: The Torah (first five books), plus the wider Tanakh and Talmud
Key practice: Shabbat (Sabbath), dietary laws (kashrut), festivals, prayer
God: One God — the oldest monotheistic tradition still practised

15 million followers · Small but hugely influential · Oldest Abrahamic faith

• • •

The Dharmic Traditions

Two more of the six originated in India and share the concept of dharma (cosmic law, duty, or the right way of living).

🕉

Hinduism

Founded: No single founder — developed over 4,000+ years in the Indian subcontinent
Core belief: Brahman (ultimate reality) can be worshipped through many gods and goddesses; the soul (atman) is reborn through reincarnation until it achieves moksha (liberation)
Sacred texts: The Vedas, Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, and many more
Key practice: Puja (worship), meditation, yoga, festivals, pilgrimage
God: Complex — one ultimate reality (Brahman) expressed through many deities (Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva)

1.2 billion followers · Third-largest religion · No single founder or single text

☙

Buddhism

Founded: ~5th century BCE by Siddhartha Gautama (the Buddha, "the awakened one") in what is now Nepal
Core belief: Life involves suffering (dukkha); suffering is caused by craving; suffering can end by following the Eightfold Path
Sacred texts: The Pali Canon (Tripitaka) and many Mahayana sutras
Key practice: Meditation, mindfulness, following the Five Precepts (moral guidelines)
God: No creator God — the Buddha was a teacher, not a deity

500 million followers · "Is Buddhism a religion or a philosophy?" — a genuinely debated question

• • •

The Youngest Religion — and a Non-Religious Worldview

☮

Sikhism

Founded: 1499 CE by Guru Nanak in the Punjab region of South Asia
Core belief: One God (Ik Onkar); all people are equal before God regardless of caste, gender, or religion; live honestly, work hard, share with others
Sacred text: Guru Granth Sahib (treated as a living guru, not just a book)
Key practice: Prayer, langar (free communal kitchen open to all), wearing the 5 Ks
God: One formless God known by many names

30 million followers · Founded just 527 years ago · Strong emphasis on equality and service

△

Humanism

Founded: No single founder — roots in ancient Greek philosophy; formalised in the 20th century
Core belief: Humans can live good, ethical, meaningful lives using reason, empathy, and evidence — without belief in God or the supernatural
Key text: No sacred text — draws on science, philosophy, and human experience
Key practice: Rational inquiry, the Golden Rule ("treat others as you'd want to be treated"), humanist ceremonies (weddings, funerals)
God: No belief in God or gods — this is a non-religious worldview

Growing fast in the UK · ~25% of UK population has no religion · Humanists UK campaigns for secular policies

Abrahamic shared features: Monotheism (one God), Abraham as a key figure, belief in prophets, belief in an afterlife, shared stories (Adam, Noah, Moses), a sacred text, belief that God communicates with humanity.

What all seven agree on: This is the hard one. Arguably: that you should treat others well / that compassion matters / that how you live your life matters / that there are right and wrong ways to act. Even Humanism — with no God — shares a commitment to ethics and human flourishing. The details differ enormously, but the impulse toward moral living is remarkably universal.

Compare your answer. Did you find a genuine shared element? The ability to see both difference and commonality is a core skill in Religious Studies.

4. Apply

The God Spectrum

Not all worldviews agree on God. Some say one God, some say many, some say none. Where do the seven sit on a spectrum?

Your task: For each worldview below, decide where it fits. Write a number from 1 to 5 in each box.

1 = Definitely no God    2 = Probably no God    3 = It's complicated    4 = One God    5 = One God, absolutely central

Christianity

Islam

Judaism

Hinduism

Buddhism

Sikhism

Humanism

Suggested positions:
Islam (5): Tawhid — the absolute oneness of God — is THE central belief.
Judaism (5): "Hear O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one" — monotheism is foundational.
Christianity (4-5): One God, but the Trinity makes it complicated. Still absolutely central.
Sikhism (5): Ik Onkar — there is one God.
Hinduism (3): It's genuinely complicated. One ultimate reality (Brahman), but worshipped through many gods. Some Hindus are monotheist, some polytheist, some neither.
Buddhism (2): No creator God. But some forms of Buddhism include spiritual beings. The Buddha was not a god.
Humanism (1): No belief in God or the supernatural.

Did any surprise you? The point is that "Do you believe in God?" gets very different answers — and some answers resist simple yes/no.

Interleaved Quick-Fire (Lessons 1 & 2)

These questions mix today's content with Lesson 1. No looking back.

1. What does "Abrahamic" mean? Which three religions are Abrahamic?

2. (From Lesson 1) — What is the difference between a theist and an agnostic?

3. Which religion has no creator God? And which non-religious worldview also has no God?

4. (From Lesson 1) — Name four of Ninian Smart's seven dimensions of religion.

5. What is the Sikh sacred text called, and what makes it unusual?

1. "Abrahamic" means tracing roots to Abraham. The three are Christianity, Islam, and Judaism.

2. A theist believes in God. An agnostic says we can't know whether God exists. (One is a belief, the other is about knowledge.)

3. Buddhism has no creator God. Humanism also has no God.

4. Any four from: Narrative, Doctrinal, Ethical, Ritual, Experiential, Institutional, Material.

5. The Guru Granth Sahib. It's unusual because Sikhs treat it as a living guru — it's given its own room, put to bed at night, and consulted for guidance. It's not just a book to read; it has the status of a teacher.
