IB Poetry: Six Lessons

IB English · Unseen poem analysis · builds on the PACER course

Six 50-minute lessons that move from reading an unseen poem to a full timed commentary. Each lesson has a different poem, a different skill and a different kind of student output. Every lesson gives the full text of the poem (all six are in the public domain) and includes spoken and written tasks.

The lessons

Lesson 1

Reading an Unseen Poem

Dickinson, “Because I could not stop for Death”

The three-pass method and four lenses (voice, image, sound, shape). Turn notes into a one-sentence interpretation.

MethodVoiceShift
 Lesson 2

Imagery and Symbol

Blake, “The Tyger”

Sort images into clusters, explain what the pattern implies, and spot the one-word change that shifts the meaning.

Image clustersSymbol
 Lesson 3

Sound and Rhythm

Owen, “Anthem for Doomed Youth”

Name a sound, say what it imitates, and link it to meaning. Read aloud and track the turn from battlefield to home.

SoundOctave and sestet
 Lesson 4

Structure and Form

Shakespeare, Sonnet 130

What the form promises, where the poem keeps and breaks it, and how the volta and couplet carry the argument.

FormVoltaConvention
 Lesson 5

Writing the Poetry Paragraph

Hopkins, “Spring and Fall”

Apply PACER to a poem. Turn a guiding question into an arguable Position, repair a weak paragraph, write your own.

PACERGuiding question
 Lesson 6

Timed Poem Commentary

Yeats, “The Second Coming”

Ten-minute plan, thirty-minute write, ten-minute self-mark against four criteria. The whole course in one sitting.

Exam practiceSelf-marking

Link to the rest of the course

These lessons reuse the habits from the Paper 1 prose course: the ladder of analysis (identify, describe, analyse), precise effect verbs, and the PACER paragraph. Spaced recall in each lesson draws on both the earlier poetry lessons and the prose lessons.
