🏔️ The Olympics Nobody Believes In

January 2026: The Winter Olympics begin in less than a month. The main ice hockey arena still isn't finished. Prosecutors are investigating corruption. Snow machines are working overtime. And the NHL is furious.

What's Actually Happening?

The Hockey Arena Disaster: The main ice hockey venue in Milan — the Milano Santagiulia Ice Hockey Arena — is still under construction. With the Games starting February 6th, International Ice Hockey Federation president Luc Tardif admitted this week that the stands won't be completed on time. Capacity has been reduced from 16,000 to 11,800 seats.

Even worse: the rink was built three feet shorter than the NHL agreed to. Players will use mobile trailer changing rooms. NHL Commissioner Gary Bettman called it "disappointing."

"I don't give a s***, I'm going over there. Even if it's not safe, I still want to play."
— Matthew Tkachuk, Team USA forward

The Corruption Scandal: In May 2024, police raided the offices of the Milano Cortina Foundation. Last week, prosecutors arrested six people and found €120,000 in cash at a construction entrepreneur's home. The investigation covers alleged bid-rigging for the Olympic Village construction.

The Italian government's response? They passed an "Olympics rescue decree" to classify the organising foundation as a private entity — effectively shielding it from certain investigations. The Milan Court has now referred this decree to Italy's Constitutional Court.

🤔 Think about it: If your country was hosting the Olympics and there were corruption allegations, should the government protect the organisers from investigation? Or would that make things worse?

The "Distributed Olympics" Experiment

Milan-Cortina 2026 is spread across three different regions — Lombardy, Trentino-Alto Adige, and Veneto. The distance between Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo? Over 250 miles.

New IOC President Kirsty Coventry was asked if she'd use a helicopter to travel between venues. She laughed and said she'd take trains "like everyone else."

The IOC is calling this the future of the Olympics — a "distributed" model that uses existing venues and reduces construction costs. But critics say it's a logistical nightmare. Mountain roads are narrow. Winter weather is unpredictable. And athletes will spend hours travelling between sites.

For comparison: Sochi 2014 cost over €40 BILLION — four times the original estimate. The IOC is desperate to avoid another Sochi.

Expressions from the Story

1. "Race against time" / "Race against the clock"
Neutral — works in any context
Meaning: To hurry to do something before a deadline; to work urgently because time is running out.
📰 From this story:
"Italian organisers are in a race against time to finish the hockey arena before the Opening Ceremony."
💼 General usage:
"The doctors were racing against time to find a donor."
"We're racing against the clock to submit the proposal before 5pm."
🎯 NOW YOU TRY: Think of a time when you (or someone you know) was racing against time. What was the deadline? Did you make it?
2. "Cut corners"
Neutral — slightly negative connotation
Meaning: To do something in the quickest, cheapest, or easiest way, often by not following proper procedures or standards. Usually implies lower quality or potential problems.
📰 From this story:
"Critics worry the organisers have been cutting corners on construction to meet the deadline — like building the rink three feet shorter than agreed."
💼 General usage:
"The builder cut corners on the foundations, and now the house has structural problems."
"I know we're behind schedule, but we can't afford to cut corners on safety."
🎯 NOW YOU TRY: Have you ever seen a company or person cut corners? What happened as a result?

More Expressions

3. "Sweep something under the carpet/rug"
Neutral — often used critically
Meaning: To try to hide a problem or keep something secret instead of dealing with it properly. Often used when organisations or governments cover up mistakes or scandals.
📰 From this story:
"Critics accuse the Italian government of trying to sweep the corruption allegations under the carpet with their 'rescue decree.'"
💼 General usage:
"The company tried to sweep the safety violations under the rug, but whistleblowers went to the press."
"You can't just sweep this problem under the carpet — we need to address it directly."
🎯 NOW YOU TRY: Can you think of a famous case where a company or government tried to sweep something under the carpet? Did it work?
4. "Be under pressure"
Neutral — very common
Meaning: To be in a stressful situation where you're being pushed to do something or facing difficult expectations. Can refer to time pressure, performance pressure, or external criticism.
📰 From this story:
"The Milano Cortina organisers are under enormous pressure from the IOC, the NHL, and the Italian public to deliver a successful Games."
💼 General usage:
"She's been under a lot of pressure at work since the restructuring."
"The government is under pressure to reduce inflation before the election."
🎯 NOW YOU TRY: When was the last time you were really under pressure? How did you handle it?
5. "Set a precedent"
Slightly formal — common in news/business
Meaning: To establish a pattern or standard that will influence how similar situations are handled in the future. Can be positive or negative depending on context.
📰 From this story:
"If the 'distributed Olympics' model works, Milan-Cortina will set a precedent for future Winter Games — potentially ending the era of massive, single-city construction projects."
💼 General usage:
"This court ruling sets a dangerous precedent for freedom of speech."
"We don't want to set a precedent of accepting late submissions."
🎯 NOW YOU TRY: Can you think of a decision (in your company, country, or life) that set an important precedent?

📋 Quick Review: All 5 Expressions

3. Sweep under the carpet

Hide or ignore a problem

4. Be under pressure

5. Set a precedent

Establish a pattern for the future
