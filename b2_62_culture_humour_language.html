Reporting on Survey Results

These phrases help you comment on and interpret survey findings naturally.

Phrases for Commenting on Findings

Positive/Expected Results:

  • I'm glad to say that... (positive result)
  • As expected, ... (expected result)
  • Predictably, ... (foreseeable result)

Unexpected/Surprising Results:

  • It came as no surprise that... (unsurprising)
  • Amazingly enough, ... (surprising)
  • Somewhat unexpectedly, ... (surprising)
  • Funnily enough, ... (amusing surprise)
  • Interestingly enough, ... (noteworthy)

Negative Results:

  • I was sad to see that... (negative result)

Practice: Match Phrases to Results

Which phrase would you use?

Situation 1: 92% of survey respondents say family is the most important factor in happiness.

"As expected, family emerged as the most important factor in happiness for the vast majority of respondents."

Situation 2: Only 23% of respondents report being very satisfied with their work.

"I was sad to see that only a small minority of respondents are very satisfied with their work."

Situation 3: Interestingly, younger people reported being happier than older people in the survey.

"Interestingly enough, younger respondents reported higher levels of happiness compared to older respondents."

Expressions for Reporting Quantities

Use these phrases to describe percentages and proportions when reporting survey results.

The vast majority More than about 80-90%; almost everyone
Almost all of us A very large proportion; around 85-95%
Most people More than 50%, typically 60-75%
A substantial portion A significant amount; often 40-60%
Just under half Slightly less than 50%; around 45-49%
A little over half Slightly more than 50%; around 51-55%
About three quarters Approximately 75% of the total
Only a small minority A very small percentage; usually 5-15%
Only a very small fraction An extremely small percentage; less than 5%
Nearly everyone questioned Almost all survey respondents; 85-95%

Discussion Questions

What makes you happy? Think about the various factors—relationships, work, leisure, health, or something else.

Think of someone you know who you would describe as a "happy" person. How do they demonstrate their happiness? What do you think makes them happy?

Do you think money is the most important factor in being happy? Why or why not? What other factors might be more important?

How happy do you think people in your country are compared to other countries? What factors might influence national happiness levels?

What do you think is the most important aspect of being happy? Is it family, health, work satisfaction, or something else?

In the future, what will be the most important factor for happiness: money, power, or family? How might priorities change as society evolves?

If you could change one thing to make yourself happier, what would it be and why? What steps might you take to achieve this change?
