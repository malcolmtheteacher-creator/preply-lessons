Today's aim: Today we're learning to explain how sound travels, so you can say what you hear and why.

Words you'll need

sound (n) — what you hear. Example: "I heard a strange sound."
loud (adj) — with a lot of sound. Example: "The music is too loud."
quiet (adj) — with little or no sound. Example: "The library is very quiet."
high (adj) — at the top of the range, like a whistle. Example: "A bird makes a high sound."
low (adj) — at the bottom of the range, like a drum. Example: "A bear makes a low sound."
echo (n) — a sound that hits a wall and comes back. Example: "I heard an echo in the cave."
vibrate (v) — to move back and forth very fast. Example: "A guitar string vibrates."
thunder (n) — the loud noise after lightning. Example: "The thunder was very loud."
lightning (n) — a bright flash of light in a storm. Example: "We saw lightning in the sky."
hear (v) — to receive sound with your ears. Example: "I can hear the rain."

A question to start

In a storm you see a flash of lightning. A few seconds later you hear the thunder.

Say it aloud: Why do you see the lightning before you hear the thunder? What do you think?

Light travels much faster than sound. Light is almost instant. Sound takes about three seconds to travel one kilometre. So the longer you wait for the thunder, the further away the storm is.

Vibrations, loud and quiet, echoes

🔔 Sound is a vibration

Sound starts when something vibrates (moves back and forth very fast). A guitar string vibrates and we hear a sound.

📈 High and low, loud and quiet

A fast vibration makes a high sound. A big vibration makes a loud sound.

🏔️ Echo

Sound hits a hard wall and comes back. This is an echo.

What you just saw

1 · Light is faster
We see the lightning almost at once, because light is very fast.
2 · Sound is slower
Sound needs about three seconds for each kilometre.
3 · Far means a longer wait
The further away the storm is, the longer we wait for the thunder.

Count the seconds between the flash and the thunder, then divide by three. That is how many kilometres away the storm is.

Language for explaining

Facts: the present simple

Sound travels through air.
A guitar string vibrates.

Giving a reason: because and so

I can hear the music because the window is open.
The window is open, so I can hear the music.

Comparing: louder, quieter, higher, lower

A drum is louder than a flute.
A whistle is higher than a drum.

Practice 1: because or so?

Practice 2: choose the best word

Light travels than sound.

How do bats find insects in the dark?

Bats make very high sounds that people cannot hear. The sound hits an insect and comes back as an echo. The bat hears the echo, so it knows where the insect is.

1 · High sound 🦇
The bat makes a very high sound.
2 · Echo 🪰
The sound hits an insect and comes back.
3 · Catch 😋
The bat hears the echo and finds the insect.
