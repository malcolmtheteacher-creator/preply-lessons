Speaking for work

Present with Impact

Structure it, signpost it, and deliver it so the room stays with you to the last word.

1 · Start with a 60-second pitch

Before any theory: pitch something you actually present at work — a project, an idea, a result. Don't prepare. Just talk for one minute.

Time: 1:00

Afterwards, hold one question in mind: did it have a clear beginning, middle and end — or did it just… stop?

2 · The shape of a talk that lands

Five moves. Every strong presentation, from a two-minute update to a keynote, runs on them.

1
Hook — open with a question, a number, or a bold line.
"Three in four of our projects miss their deadline. Today, that changes."
2
Roadmap — tell them where you're taking them.
"I'll cover three things: the problem, the plan, and what I need from you."
3
Three points — one idea each, clearly signposted.
"First… Second… And finally…"
4
Recap — say it again, shorter.
"So: the problem is X, the plan is Y, the ask is Z."
5
Call to action — what you want them to do, decide, or feel.
"If you take one thing away, let it be this…"

3 · Signpost so they never get lost

Fluent speakers still lose the room by rambling. These phrases are the road signs. Steal them.

Open
  • "Let me start with…"
  • "There are three things I want to cover today…"
Move on
  • "That brings me to…"
  • "Now, let's turn to…"
  • "Moving on…"
Emphasise
  • "The key point here is…"
  • "If you remember one thing, remember this."
  • "This is the part that really matters."
Handle a question
  • "Great question — let me come back to that at the end."
  • "So what you're really asking is…"
Wrap up
  • "To wrap up…"
  • "So, where does that leave us?"
  • "Let me leave you with this."

4 · Two habits that change everything

Pause, and stress the word that matters

Speed and a flat voice make even great content forgettable. Punch the key word — then pause.

Flat: "we need to cut costs by twenty percent this quarter."
Landed: "we need to cut costs by twenty percent  …  this quarter."

One idea per sentence

Long, layered sentences read well but speak badly. Break them up. Short sentences sound confident.

Tangled: "Although the data suggests, given the trends we've been seeing accelerate, that we might want to consider acting…"
Clean: "The trend is accelerating. We need to act. Here's how."

5 · Now do it again

Same 60-second pitch — but build it on the five moves, signpost as you go, and pause on your key word.

Time: 2:00

Then run it once more with nothing written down — just the shape in your head.

To finish — talk it through

  • Which presentation do you give most often at work? How would the five-move shape sharpen it?
  • Which signpost phrases will you use in your very next meeting?
  • Where do you tend to speed up or ramble — and what will you do instead?
  • A confident presenter and a nervous one might use the same words. So what actually makes the difference?
