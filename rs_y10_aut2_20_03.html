Last lesson you learned about Jesus's resurrection and return to heaven. Here are 4 questions to refresh your memory.

What does the Resurrection prove about Jesus?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: It proves Jesus is God's son, the saviour, and has power over death. It gives Christians hope for eternal life.

What is the Ascension?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Jesus's return to heaven 40 days after resurrection. He ascended before the disciples' eyes and now sits at God's right hand.

What are two views of Jesus's resurrection?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Physical (body literally rose, tomb empty, could be touched) vs Spiritual (spirit survived, disciples had visions).

Why were the disciples transformed by the resurrection?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: They were frightened and scattered after crucifixion, but the resurrection gave them hope and power to boldly proclaim Jesus.

Heaven: God's Kingdom

What heaven is: Christians believe heaven is the eternal home of God and the blessed. It is not a place "up there" but a spiritual reality—God's presence and kingdom.

Key scripture: "In my Father's house are many rooms; if it were not so, I would have told you. I am going there to prepare a place for you" (John 14:2). Jesus promises believers will live with him forever.

What heaven is like: The Bible uses imagery: streets of gold, a great wedding feast, eternal peace, reunion with loved ones. Most theologians say these are symbols of joy and God's presence, not literal descriptions.

Who goes to heaven? This varies by denomination:

  • Traditional view: Those who believe in Jesus and receive salvation
  • Catholic view: Believers, plus those in purgatory who are purified and then enter heaven
  • Universalist view: All people eventually enter heaven (God's love is universal)

Hell: Separation from God

What hell is: Christians believe hell is eternal separation from God—a state of spiritual darkness and loss. It is not necessarily a place of fire and torture, though medieval tradition imagined it so.

Key scripture: In the Parable of the Rich Man and Lazarus (Luke 16), the rich man dies and is in torment, while poor Lazarus is comforted by Abraham. There is a great chasm separating them—hell is distance from God.

Who goes to hell? Traditionally, those who reject God and sin without repentance. But modern Christians debate this heavily.

Modern debate:

  • Traditional: Hell is real, eternal, and for those who refuse God's grace
  • Metaphorical: Hell is a symbol of spiritual death or separation, not literal fire
  • Annihilationism: The wicked are destroyed, not tormented eternally
  • Universalism: Everyone is eventually saved; God's love is universal

The Last Judgement (Matthew 25:31–46)

What is the Last Judgement? Jesus taught that at the end of time, all people will be judged by God. The righteous will enter eternal life; the wicked will face punishment.

Key scripture: "When the Son of Man comes in his glory, and all the angels with him, he will sit on his glorious throne. All the nations will be gathered before him, and he will separate the people one from another as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats... Then he will say to those on his right, 'Come, you who are blessed by my Father; take your inheritance, the kingdom prepared for you since the creation of the world'" (Matthew 25:31–34).

The Sheep and the Goats: Jesus judges based on how people treated others—feeding the hungry, clothing the poor, visiting prisoners. Righteous people (sheep) enter eternal life; the unrighteous (goats) face eternal punishment.

When does it happen? At the end of time, when Jesus returns. Exact date is unknown ("no one knows the day or hour").

Purgatory (Catholic Doctrine)

The Roman Catholic Church teaches a third place: purgatory. This is where souls are purified after death before entering heaven.

Why? If someone dies with unforgiven venial (minor) sins, they aren't fit for God's presence yet. Purgatory is a temporary state of purification through suffering.

Evidence: Catholics point to 2 Maccabees 12:45–46 and 1 Corinthians 3:15. They also teach that prayers and Masses for the dead can help souls in purgatory.

Protestant rejection: Most Protestants reject purgatory. Martin Luther saw it as unbiblical and a way the Church made money (selling indulgences promised to reduce purgatory time). Protestants believe salvation is instant—you're either saved or not.

Different Christian Views on the Afterlife

Traditional Orthodox View

Heaven and hell are real and eternal. Theosis (union with God) is the Christian goal. Hell is eternal separation from God. The Last Judgement is literal. Icons and prayers help the dead.

Catholic View

Heaven (eternal with God), purgatory (purification), and hell (eternal separation) are all real. Purgatory is temporary. Prayers and Masses help souls progress. Mary and saints intercede for believers.

Evangelical Protestant View

Strong belief in literal heaven and hell. The Last Judgement is real. Salvation through faith in Jesus is the way to heaven. Hell is eternal separation from God for those who reject Christ.

Liberal/Mystical View

Heaven and hell are often metaphorical—spiritual states, not literal places. God is too loving to torture people eternally. Some embrace universalism: all souls eventually find redemption.

Symbolism vs Literal Interpretation

Are heaven and hell real places?

Literal interpretation: Heaven is a real place where God dwells and believers will live forever. Hell is a real place of torment. The Bible describes them literally: streets of gold, fire, darkness.

Symbolic interpretation: Heaven is a symbol of God's presence and eternal joy. Hell is a symbol of spiritual death and separation from God. The Bible uses vivid imagery but these aren't literal descriptions.

Scholarly debate: Most modern theologians and scholars lean towards symbolic interpretation. Ancient texts use poetic language. But many traditional and evangelical Christians hold literal views.

Review: The Afterlife

What is heaven according to Christian belief?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Heaven is eternal life in God's presence. Jesus said, 'In my Father's house are many rooms.' It is where God dwells and believers will live forever.

What is hell?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Eternal separation from God. It is described variously as literal torment, spiritual darkness, or metaphorical separation.

What is the Last Judgement?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: When Jesus returns, all people are judged by God. The righteous enter eternal life; the unrighteous face punishment. Judgment is based on how people treated others.

What is purgatory, and which church teaches it?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: A temporary state after death where souls are purified before entering heaven. The Roman Catholic Church teaches this. Most Protestants reject it.

How do modern Christians interpret heaven and hell differently from the past?

🔒Reveal answer

Answer: Many see heaven and hell as metaphorical spiritual states rather than literal places. Universalists believe all people are eventually saved.
