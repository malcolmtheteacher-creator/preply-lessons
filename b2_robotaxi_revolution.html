🚖 The Robotaxi Revolution

Will self-driving cars replace human drivers? · B1/B2 · 60-min speaking lesson

Goal: Talk about one of the most exciting tech stories of 2026 — driverless taxis. By the end of this lesson you'll have around 12 high-frequency words for tech, transport and the gig economy, and you'll be able to argue both sides of a real city-council debate.

robotaxi
noun
A self-driving taxi — no human driver.
"The first robotaxi service launched in Phoenix in 2020."
autonomous
adjective
Working by itself, without human control.
"Fully autonomous vehicles can drive themselves in any weather."
a fleet
noun
A group of vehicles owned and managed by one company.
"The company added 500 cars to its fleet this year."
an algorithm
noun
A set of rules a computer follows to make a decision.
"The algorithm decides which driver gets the next trip."
surge pricing
noun
When prices automatically rise because lots of people want a service at the same time.
"On New Year's Eve, surge pricing pushed fares to four times the normal rate."
the gig economy
noun
Short, flexible jobs that people get through apps (driving, delivery, freelance work).
"Around 15% of UK workers now earn money in the gig economy."
to dispatch
verb
To send a vehicle (or person) to a specific place.
"The system dispatched the nearest car within 30 seconds."
to scale (up)
verb
To grow a service from small to very large.
"It's easy to test in one city — much harder to scale to a hundred."
latency
noun
Delay in a system — the time between an action and the response.
"Low latency is critical for self-driving cars — milliseconds matter."
infrastructure
noun (uncountable)
The basic systems a service depends on — roads, sensors, networks, charging stations.
"You can't run electric robotaxis without charging infrastructure."
regulation
noun
Official rules made by a government.
"European regulation on self-driving cars is much stricter than in California."
to roll out
verb (phrasal)
To launch a new product or service to lots of users.
"The company plans to roll out the service in five new cities by 2027."

What's actually happening — read this

Short article (around 230 words). Read once for the big picture, then again for detail. Watch out for the vocab from Tab 2.

The robotaxi rollout is faster than anyone predicted

Tech & Mobility · April 2026

Three years ago, fully driverless taxis were a science-fiction story. In 2026, they are an ordinary morning commute in a growing list of cities. Waymo, the American leader, now operates in more than ten US cities, and its fleet of autonomous vehicles completes over a million paid trips a week. In China, three rivals — Baidu, Pony.ai and WeRide — are scaling even faster, helped by friendlier regulation and government investment in smart-road infrastructure.

The big shift is that the technology now dispatches the right car for the right job in real time, with millisecond latency. Customers no longer wait nervously: an app sends a notification, a clean white car turns the corner, and the doors unlock. There is no driver to tip, no awkward small talk, and — for now — no surge pricing at peak hours either.

But there is a human cost. Across the world, around five million people earn money driving cars in the gig economy. As the robotaxi industry rolls out in more cities, those drivers face a difficult choice: retrain, switch platforms, or accept lower pay. Cities are split. Some welcome cleaner, safer streets; others, including most of Europe, are slowing down approvals to protect drivers and study the data.

The race is no longer about whether the technology works. It's about who gets the rules right first.

Six hot takes — pick your side

Don't sit on the fence. For each one, give a clear opinion in 30–60 seconds, then explain WHY. Use vocab from Tab 2 wherever you can.

01

Should governments slow down the rollout of robotaxis to protect human drivers?

Useful frames: "I think we should…" / "On the one hand… but on the other…" / "It depends on whether…"
02

Would you trust a fully autonomous car at 2 a.m. in heavy rain, on an unfamiliar road?

Useful frames: "Honestly, I would / wouldn't…" / "It would depend on…" / "I'd be more worried about…"
03

Surge pricing — clever algorithm, or unfair price-gouging?

Useful frames: "From the company's point of view…" / "But from the customer's side…" / "I'd argue that…"
04

If robotaxis take over your city, what happens to the five million gig-economy drivers? Whose responsibility is it to help them?

Useful frames: "I think the responsibility lies with…" / "The companies should…" / "The government has to…"
05

In ten years, will you OWN a car at all? Or will you just subscribe to a "mobility service"?

Useful frames: "I'm pretty sure / I doubt I will…" / "It depends where I live…" / "If costs come down enough…"
06

Some cities give robotaxis their OWN lanes. Is that fair to cyclists, buses and regular drivers?

Useful frames: "It's a question of priority…" / "I can see the logic, but…" / "I'd argue cyclists should come first because…"

The City Council Pitch — 90 seconds

🎤 Role-play scenario

The setup: Your city council is meeting tonight. They are deciding whether to allow robotaxis to operate from next month. Local taxi unions are protesting outside. You have been given 90 seconds to make your case.

You will make TWO speeches:

  • Speech 1: FOR the robotaxis. Argue why the city should allow them.
  • Speech 2: AGAINST the robotaxis. Argue why the city should block or delay them.

Rules:

  • Use at least 5 vocabulary words from Tab 2 in each speech.
  • Open with a strong opening line. Close with a clear call to action.
  • You cannot say "errm" — pause silently if you need to think.
Time: 1:30

Checklist — for each speech, did you…

Open with a strong line (a fact, a question, or a bold claim)?
Use at least 5 words from the Tab 2 vocab list?
Acknowledge the OTHER side at least once ("I understand the concern about…")?
Finish with a clear "we should / we must / I urge you to…" line?
Stay within the 90 seconds?
