What you'll learn in this lesson

Today you'll explore how people prefer to communicate. You'll recognise your own communication style, learn to spot preferences in others, and practise flexing your communication to work better with different people.

You'll work through the story of three colleagues—Anshul, Bella, and Cho—who communicate very differently. By understanding their styles, you'll see how to adapt your own style for better workplace relationships.

Communication Concepts

Click each card to reveal the definition.

Flex To change your communication behaviour or style to adapt to a situation or person.
Direct Saying exactly what you mean without hiding anything; straightforward.
Indirect Suggesting something without saying it clearly; being tactful or careful.
Measured Careful, deliberate, and thoughtful; not rushed or hasty.
Task-focused Prioritising what needs to be done; concentrating on results and goals.
Relationship-focused Prioritising how people feel; building trust and connection first.

Meet the Team

Here are three team members with different communication styles.

Anshul — Direct, Fast-paced, Task-focused

Communication style:
"Let's get this done. We need results by Friday. I don't need details—just tell me the deadline and the deliverables."
In meetings:
Speaks quickly, interrupts to stay on schedule, moves immediately to action items, wants facts not feelings.

Bella — Indirect, Measured, Relationship-focused

Communication style:
"Before we decide, I want to know how everyone feels. Is everyone comfortable with this? Let's build trust first."
In meetings:
Listens carefully, asks how others are doing, suggests decisions gently, takes time to build consensus.

Cho — Indirect, Measured, Balanced

Communication style:
"Let me think about this carefully. I want to understand all the options before I share my thoughts."
In meetings:
Listens more than he speaks, asks thoughtful questions, needs time to process, is diplomatic.

How to Recognise Communication Preferences

You can spot someone's communication preferences by watching their behaviour, listening to their language, and noticing their vocal cues.

Behaviour Cues
How someone acts in conversations and meetings:
Direct: Interrupts, gets to the point quickly, minimal small talk.
Indirect: Waits for others to speak, builds in context, asks permission.
Fast-paced: Moves between topics quickly, makes rapid decisions.
Measured: Takes pauses, asks clarifying questions, writes things down.
Task-focused: Checks progress, reviews timelines, focuses on outcomes.
Relationship-focused: Remembers personal details, asks how you are, gives praise.
Language Cues
The words and phrases they use:
Direct: "I think...", "Here's the issue...", "No" (without softeners).
Indirect: "Could we...?", "What do you think...?", "Maybe we...", "I wonder if..."
Task-focused: "What's the deadline?", "What are the deliverables?", focus on metrics.
Relationship-focused: "How are you?", "I feel...", emphasis on team, "we" language.
Vocal Cues
How they use their voice:
Direct: Loud, fast-paced, energetic, assertive tone.
Indirect: Softer, more measured, asks questions with rising intonation.
Fast-paced: Rapid speech, little silence, high energy.
Measured: Slower pace, thoughtful pauses, deliberate emphasis.

Apply this to Anshul, Bella, and Cho

Think about what you'd observe in a team meeting:

  • Anshul: Sits forward, speaks first, uses facts, interrupts, moves fast
  • Bella: Listens carefully, uses "we" language, asks others' opinions, softer voice
  • Cho: Takes notes, pauses before speaking, asks thoughtful questions, waits his turn

The Task: Role-Play the Meeting

Imagine Anshul, Bella, and Cho are in a team meeting about a new project. The team has different views on how to approach it.

Your Role

Choose one character to play: Anshul, Bella, or Cho.

Think about what small adjustments your character could make to flex towards their colleagues' preferences. What would they need to do differently to communicate better?

Role-Play Checklist

What each character might flex towards

Anshul could slow down and ask for others' input before deciding.
Bella could be more direct about problems and respect others' need for efficiency.
Cho could prepare ideas in advance and speak up earlier in the meeting.

Reflection Questions

Think about flexible communication in your own workplace:

Why do we need to flex our workplace communication?

Think about what happens when communication styles clash. What problems could occur? How does flexing solve them?

Who needs to flex?

Is it only the person with the "extreme" style (like Anshul) who should flex? Or does everyone share responsibility for adapting to each other?

What if the other person is unable or unwilling to flex?

What can you do if someone refuses to adapt their communication? How do you protect the team relationship?
