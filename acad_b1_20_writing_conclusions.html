Writing Strong Conclusions

B1 Intermediate Academic English

⏱️ Time: 5:00

Elements of a Strong Conclusion

A conclusion ties your essay together and leaves a lasting impression. It has three main parts.

1. Restate Your Thesis Differently

Return to your main argument, but use different words. Show how you've proven or supported it through your essay.

2. Summarise Main Points

Briefly review the key evidence or arguments from your essay. Don't repeat word-for-word; synthesise the ideas.

3. Final Thought

End with significance, implications, or broader meaning. What does this mean? Why should the reader care?

Example Conclusion

Restatement: "Supporting students' mental health and academic success requires more than traditional classroom instruction; universities must provide integrated, multi-faceted support systems."

Summary: "Evidence shows that tutoring programs reduce academic stress, mental health services prevent dropout rates, and peer mentoring improves social integration. Together, these three approaches address the primary challenges first-year students face."

Final Thought: "As universities continue to grow and become more competitive, investing in student support is not optional—it is essential for producing graduates who are both academically competent and emotionally resilient."

Critical: What NOT to Do in Conclusions

Common Mistakes

  • ❌ Introduce new ideas: Never present new evidence or arguments in your conclusion. Everything should already be in your essay.
  • ❌ Use weak phrases: Avoid "In conclusion, I have discussed...", "As I said before...", or "To conclude..."
  • ❌ Copy the introduction: Don't simply repeat your introduction word-for-word.
  • ❌ Make it too long: Conclusions should be roughly 10% of your essay length, not 30%.
  • ❌ Apologise: Never say "I'm not an expert, but..." or "Although I didn't have time to research..."
  • ❌ Become too general: Don't suddenly make huge claims beyond your essay's scope.

Strong Conclusion Phrases

  • ✓ "This analysis reveals that..."
  • ✓ "The evidence demonstrates that..."
  • ✓ "Therefore, it is clear that..."
  • ✓ "These findings suggest that..."
  • ✓ "Ultimately, understanding [topic] is crucial because..."

Evaluating Conclusions

Read each conclusion and identify its strengths and weaknesses.

Example 1

"In conclusion, social media is important. I discussed Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok. Social media will probably be more important in the future. This is why people should use social media carefully."

What are the problems with this conclusion?

Many problems: weak opening phrase ("In conclusion"), doesn't restate thesis properly, summary is vague, the final thought is too general and doesn't follow from the essay. It introduces a new idea (careful use) without support.

Example 2

"These findings demonstrate that regular exercise reduces anxiety and improves academic performance. The three studies reviewed showed consistent improvements in mental health and grades. Therefore, universities should encourage students to participate in physical activity programs. This investment in student wellbeing will ultimately create more successful and healthier graduates."

What makes this conclusion effective?

Strong restatement with "demonstrate", brief but clear summary of the three studies, logical transition with "Therefore", and final thought that connects to broader implications (successful graduates). No new ideas introduced.

Example 3

"As I have shown in this essay, climate change is caused by human activities and will destroy the planet. We must stop climate change immediately. Additionally, we should also solve poverty and stop wars. In summary, many global problems need solving."

Identify the main errors.

Weak opening ("As I have shown"), introduces new topics (poverty, wars) not discussed in essay, becomes too general, summary is vague and doesn't reflect the essay's actual content. Final thought adds scope that hasn't been addressed.

Example 4

"The evidence presented reveals that implementing inclusive design principles improves both accessibility and usability for all users, not just those with disabilities. We examined three case studies showing improved user satisfaction scores and reduced support costs. The significance of this research extends beyond individual companies; it suggests that inclusive design is ultimately a business imperative that benefits everyone."

Evaluate this conclusion's effectiveness.

Excellent conclusion. Strong restatement, specific summary of case studies, logical connection between evidence and broader implication, uses powerful transition "Ultimately", final thought ties back to essay theme without introducing new ideas.
