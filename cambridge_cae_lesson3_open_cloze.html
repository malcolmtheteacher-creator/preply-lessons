What is Open Cloze?

Part 2 gives you a text with 8 gaps. For each gap, you write ONE word. No options are given - you must think of the word yourself.

Part 2 vs Part 1

Part 1 (Multiple Choice Cloze): Tests vocabulary - you choose from 4 options (A, B, C, D)

Part 2 (Open Cloze): Tests grammar - you write the word yourself with NO options

What Kind of Words?

Open Cloze mostly tests grammar words, not vocabulary. The missing words are usually:

Articles & Determiners
  • a, an, the
  • this, that, these, those
  • some, any, no, every
Prepositions
  • in, on, at, by, for
  • with, from, to, of
  • about, during, since
Pronouns
  • it, they, them, one
  • who, which, that, whose
  • what, where, when
Auxiliaries & Modals
  • be, have, do
  • can, could, would, should
  • may, might, must

The 3 Decisions for Open Cloze

  1. Decision 1: What CATEGORY of word is needed? (grammar word? which type?)
  2. Decision 2: Read AROUND the gap - what comes before AND after?
  3. Decision 3: Is this a fixed phrase or collocation I should know?

Decision 1: Identify the Category

The Strategy

Before guessing randomly, ask: "What TYPE of word is missing?" This narrows your options dramatically.

Category Clues

If you see... You probably need... Examples
___ + noun Article / Determiner a, the, this, some, any
verb + ___ + noun Preposition in, on, at, for, with
___ + verb Pronoun / Subject it, they, who, which
have/has + ___ Past participle been, done, made
___ + verb (base) Modal / Auxiliary can, will, should, do
adjective + ___ + adjective Conjunction / Linker and, but, or, as

What category of word is missing?

Decision 2: Read Around the Gap

The Strategy

Never look at JUST the gap. Look at 3-5 words BEFORE and AFTER. The answer often depends on what surrounds it.

Why This Matters

Same gap, different answers:

A: "I am interested ___ learning Japanese."

Answer: IN (interested IN + -ing)

B: "I am good ___ learning languages."

Answer: AT (good AT + -ing)

C: "I am capable ___ learning quickly."

Answer: OF (capable OF + -ing)

The Trap

Students often see "I am ___" and immediately think of common patterns. But the word AFTER the gap changes everything!

Practice: Read the Full Context

Fill each gap - pay attention to what comes AFTER

1. "She insisted ___ paying for dinner."

ON

insist ON + -ing (NOT insist to pay)

2. "He succeeded ___ passing the exam."

IN

succeed IN + -ing (NOT succeed to pass)

3. "They prevented him ___ leaving."

FROM

prevent someone FROM + -ing

4. "I apologized ___ being late."

FOR

apologize FOR + -ing

5. "We are looking forward ___ meeting you."

TO

look forward TO + -ing (NOT look forward for)

Key Insight

Many Open Cloze answers are dependent prepositions - prepositions that MUST follow certain verbs or adjectives. These are worth learning as fixed patterns.

Decision 3: Fixed Phrases & Quick Wins

The Strategy

Some gaps test fixed phrases that you either know or you do not. Learning common phrases gives you "free" marks.

High-Frequency Fixed Phrases

Linking Phrases
  • in spite of
  • as well as
  • in order to
  • as long as
  • in case (of)
Emphasis Phrases
  • what I like is...
  • it was ... who/that
  • not only ... but also
  • such a + adjective + noun
  • so + adjective + that
Comparison Phrases
  • the same as
  • different from
  • similar to
  • compared to/with
  • rather than
Quantity Phrases
  • a great deal of
  • a number of
  • hardly any
  • few / a few
  • little / a little

Quick Win Practice

Complete these fixed phrases:

1. "In ___ of the rain, the match continued."

SPITE - in spite of = despite

2. "___ sooner had I arrived than the phone rang."

NO - No sooner... than (inversion structure)

3. "It was such ___ interesting book that I read it twice."

AN - such + a/an + adjective + noun

4. "She went to the library in ___ to study."

ORDER - in order to = purpose

5. "___ only did she win, but she broke the record."

NOT - Not only... but (also)

6. "I would rather stay home ___ go out tonight."

THAN - would rather X than Y

Full Practice Text

Read the text and fill in each gap with ONE word only.

The Benefits of Walking

Walking is one of the simplest forms of exercise, yet it offers 1________ great deal of health benefits. Unlike running, walking puts less stress on your joints, 2________ makes it suitable for people of all ages.

Research has shown that people 3________ walk regularly have lower rates of heart disease. In 4________ to improving physical health, walking can also boost mental well-being. Many people find that going for a walk helps them clear 5________ minds and reduce stress.

You do not need any special equipment 6________ than a comfortable pair of shoes. 7________ of whether you live in a city or the countryside, you can usually find somewhere pleasant to walk.

Experts recommend walking for at 8________ 30 minutes a day, five days a week. However, even short walks can make a difference if done consistently.

Gap Answer Why?
1 A a great deal of (fixed phrase)
2 WHICH relative pronoun referring to whole clause
3 WHO relative pronoun for people
4 ADDITION in addition to (fixed phrase)
5 THEIR possessive pronoun (their minds)
6 OTHER other than (= except)
7 REGARDLESS regardless of (fixed phrase)
8 LEAST at least (fixed phrase)

Analysis

Notice how this text tested:

  • Fixed phrases (a great deal, in addition to, at least)
  • Relative pronouns (which, who)
  • Possessive pronouns (their)
  • Linking phrases (other than, regardless of)

Key Takeaways

The 3 Decisions

  • Decision 1: What CATEGORY? (article, preposition, pronoun, auxiliary, linker)
  • Decision 2: What comes BEFORE and AFTER the gap?
  • Decision 3: Is this a FIXED PHRASE I should recognize?

Most Tested Word Types

Learn These Categories Well

  • Relative pronouns: who, which, that, whose, where, when
  • Dependent prepositions: interested IN, good AT, capable OF
  • Fixed phrases: in spite of, in order to, as well as, rather than
  • Articles: a, an, the (especially "the" for superlatives)
  • Auxiliaries: have/has/had (perfect), be (passive/continuous)

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Watch Out For

  • Writing TWO words (only ONE word allowed)
  • Spelling mistakes (especally vs especially)
  • Using the wrong preposition (interested FOR instead of IN)
  • Forgetting articles before superlatives (THE best)
  • Confusing which/who (which = things, who = people)

Homework Suggestion

Make a list of 20 dependent preposition patterns (verb + preposition + -ing). Test yourself by covering the preposition. This is the fastest way to improve Part 2 scores.
