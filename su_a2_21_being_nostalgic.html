Being Nostalgic

Childhood Memories & Talking About Past Habits

Warm-Up: Childhood Memories

Think about your childhood. What comes to mind?

🏫 School
What was your primary or secondary school like? Did you have a favourite subject or teacher?
🧸 Toys
What toys did you play with? Was there a toy you especially loved?
🎮 Games
What games did you play with friends? Were they indoors or outdoors?
🧹 Chores
What chores did you have to do at home? Did you enjoy them?

Now: As you think about these memories, notice how they involve things you did regularly (habits) or special single moments. This lesson will help you talk about both.

"Used to" or Past Simple?

Classify each sentence. Is it a single past action (past simple) or a past habit (used to)?

1. I made a promise to my parents.
✓ Past simple — single action in the past
2. We didn't use to have a lot of money.
✓ Past habit — "used to" — a situation over time
3. I used to dream of the end of the school year.
✓ Past habit — "used to" — a repeated action or feeling
4. I used to go and just look at Sandy in the shop window.
✓ Past habit — "used to" — something done regularly
5. One Sunday, my uncle came for Sunday lunch.
✓ Past simple — a specific event ("One Sunday")
6. I was so happy I couldn't sleep.
✓ Past simple — a specific situation at a moment in time
7. My parents used to work very hard to give my brothers and me a good life.
✓ Past habit — "used to" — something they did over a period of time

Key rule:

  • Past simple: One action, one moment ("I went to the park", "He arrived on Sunday")
  • "Used to": Repeated habit or state in the past ("I used to visit every week", "We didn't use to have money")

Pronunciation: Weak Forms of "Used to"

In natural speech, "used to" has a weak form. Listen and repeat.

Standard pronunciation: /ˈjuːst tə/ or /ˈjuːzd tə/
Weak form: /ˈjuːsˌtə/ (sounds like "yooss-tuh")
Example: "I used to play football" → sounds like "I yooss-tuh play football"

Practice Sentences

1. "I used to live in London."
The "used to" is quick and unstressed. Focus on "London" at the end.
2. "We didn't use to have much money."
"use to" (not "used to") in negatives — still quick and weak: /juːs tə/
3. "Did you use to study hard?"
In questions, "use to" (no d) — same weak form: /juːs tə/
4. "I used to dream of holidays."
Stress the verb after "used to": "I USED-to DREAM of holidays."
5. "They used to visit every summer."
"used to" is the weak link — stress falls on "VISIT" and "SUMmer."

Tip: Don't over-pronounce "used to" — make it quick and smooth. Let it connect naturally to the verb after it.

Discussion: Talking About Childhood

Answer these questions about your childhood. Take your time — these are conversation starters.

1. What toys did you use to play with as a child?
Think about specific toys or types of toys you enjoyed.
2. Who did you use to play with?
Friends, siblings, cousins? What did you play together?
3. Did you use to study hard at primary school?
What was school like for you? Did you enjoy it?
4. Did you use to enjoy going to secondary school?
What changed? Did you have favourite subjects or friends?
5. What did you use to do in your free time when you were a teenager?
Sports, hobbies, hanging out with friends, reading — anything?

Remember: You're practising the grammar AND sharing real memories. Both are important.

Use complete sentences when possible. Don't worry about making mistakes — that's how we learn.

Your Turn: Tell a Childhood Story

Choose a childhood memory and tell the story. Use both "used to" (for habits) and past simple (for specific events).

Story ideas:

  • A game with friends (who? where? how often?)
  • A special holiday or family event
  • A teacher or school memory (did you like school? what did you use to do?)

Using "Used to" for Past Habits

I used to + verb (base form)
"I used to play football every afternoon."
"She used to visit her grandmother on Sundays."

Negatives with "Used to"

I didn't use to + verb
"We didn't use to have a TV at home."
"He didn't use to like school."

Questions with "Used to"

Did you use to + verb?
"Did you use to play outside?"
"Did they use to live near the beach?"

Past Simple for Single Actions

I went, I played, I lived (for a specific moment)
"When I was seven, I lost my favourite toy."
"Last summer, I visited my cousins."

Combining Both

"I used to play with Lego every day, and one day I lost an important piece."
"My family used to live in a small town. We moved to the city in 1995."

What to include in your story:

  • At least two "used to" sentences (habits or things you did regularly)
  • At least one past simple sentence (a specific moment or event)
  • Details — names, places, feelings, why it matters to you

Wrap-Up: Reflection

Take a moment to think about what you learned and practised.

What's the difference between "used to" and past simple?
One talks about habits or repeated actions; the other is for specific moments. Can you think of an example from your own life?
Which is harder to use naturally: "used to" or past simple?
Why? (Maybe it's the pronunciation? The grammar? Remembering when to use it?)
What's one childhood memory you found yourself thinking about today?
And how would you tell that story using these grammar patterns?
How confident do you feel about using "used to" in your next conversation?
Not confident? That's normal. With practice, it becomes automatic.

Next steps:

  • Keep practising "used to" in sentences about your own life and habits
  • Listen to how native speakers use it — you'll hear the weak form in real conversations
  • Tell your childhood stories to a friend or family member — it's real, meaningful practice

Well done! Talking about the past is one of the most practical skills in English. You're building it right now.
