Effective Study Strategies

Strategy 1: Spaced Repetition

Instead of studying for 8 hours once, study for 1 hour on Day 1, then repeat on Day 3, Day 7, and Day 14.

Why it works:

  • Your brain remembers information better when you revisit it multiple times
  • It prevents "cramming" which leads to quick forgetting
  • You learn long-term, not just short-term

Strategy 2: Active Recall vs. Passive Reading

Passive reading: Simply reading your notes and textbooks (less effective)

Active recall: Testing yourself without looking at notes (more effective)

How to do active recall:

  • Write practice questions and answer them from memory
  • Use flashcards and test yourself
  • Practice past papers under exam conditions
  • Explain topics aloud to someone (or yourself!)

Strategy 3: The Pomodoro Technique

Work in 25-minute focused blocks, then take a 5-minute break. After 4 blocks, take a longer 15-30 minute break.

The schedule:

  • 25 minutes: study (one "Pomodoro")
  • 5 minutes: break
  • 25 minutes: study
  • 5 minutes: break
  • 25 minutes: study
  • 5 minutes: break
  • 25 minutes: study
  • 15-30 minutes: longer break

Benefit: Prevents burnout and helps you stay focused with clear goals.

Exam Techniques: Doing Well on the Day

1. Read Questions Carefully

Do not rush! Read each question twice and underline key words. Many students make mistakes because they did not understand the question.

Example: If the question says "Compare and contrast", you must do both — compare AND contrast. Just comparing is not enough.

2. Underline Key Words

Circle or underline the important words in the question so you know exactly what is being asked.

Example: "Explain why urban heat islands are a problem in developing countries."

Key words: "explain", "why", "urban heat islands", "problem", "developing countries"

3. Plan Before Writing

Spend 2-3 minutes planning your answer (bullet points or a simple outline) before you write. This saves time and makes your answer better organised.

4. Check Your Work

If you have time at the end, read through your answers. Check for:

  • Spelling errors
  • Grammar mistakes
  • Unfinished sentences
  • Did you answer the question completely?

Speaking Task: Your Revision Strategy

Describe your revision strategy for an important exam or goal you have. Talk for about 5 minutes. What works for you? What would you change?

5:00

Structure Your Talk:

  • What exam/goal? Describe what you are preparing for
  • When? When will it take place?
  • Current strategy: How are you currently studying? What techniques do you use?
  • What works: Which strategies help you learn best?
  • Improvements: What would you like to change or improve in your revision?
  • Confidence: Do you feel prepared? Why or why not?

Speaking Tips:

  • Speak at a natural pace
  • Use complete sentences
  • Use some of the vocabulary from this lesson (revision, spaced repetition, active recall, etc.)
  • Give specific examples
  • Try to use the full 5 minutes
