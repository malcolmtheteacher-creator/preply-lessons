Latin America: Stereotype vs Reality

8 lessons on Latin American cultures and differences — each one opens with a stereotype and finds out what is really true.

Everyone speaks Spanish, nobody is on time, it's all tacos and carnival: Latin America is often seen through a handful of clichés. Start with the Background lesson, then work through the topics. Each lesson asks three questions — what does the stereotype say, what is the reality, and why does the idea exist? — and finishes with practical tips for working, travelling and making friends across cultures.

📖 Background1

Read this first: what “Latin America” means, and how varied its countries are.

BackgroundLatin America: One Name, Many Worlds
Background
"Latin America" sounds like one culture. In reality it is around twenty countries — with Indigenous, African, European and Asian roots — from Mexico to the tip of Patagonia.
The stereotype: "Latin America is basically one country where everyone is the same."

🧭 Stereotype vs Reality7

Culture, cultural differences and the stories we tell about each other.

1Everyone Speaks Spanish?
Language
People often think "Latin America = Spanish". But the region's biggest country speaks Portuguese, and millions speak Quechua, Guaraní, Maya languages, French or English.
The stereotype: "Everyone in Latin America speaks Spanish."
Reported SpeechOpen →
2Mañana, Mañana? Time in Latin America
Time & punctuality
"Mañana" — tomorrow — is the classic cliché: in Latin America, nobody is on time. The reality depends on the country, the city and, above all, the situation.
The stereotype: "Latin Americans are always late — everything happens 'mañana'."
Contrast — Although, Despite, HoweverOpen →
3Family Comes First?
Family & relationships
Latin Americans are said to live in huge families, eat with grandparents every Sunday and never leave home. What is true — and what has changed?
The stereotype: "Latin American families are huge, and everyone lives together for ever."
Generalising Carefully — Tend to, On the WholeOpen →
4Warm, Loud and Touchy?
Greetings & space
Latin Americans are imagined as warm, loud and very physical — kisses, hugs and standing close. The reality varies by country, by city and by situation.
The stereotype: "Latin Americans are all warm, loud and touchy."
Comparatives & SuperlativesOpen →
5Is It All Tacos and Chilli?
Food & daily life
Tacos, chilli and burritos — many people think Latin American food is all Mexican and very spicy. In reality it is one of the world's most varied food regions.
The stereotype: "Latin American food is all tacos, burritos and spicy chilli."
Used to & Would — Past HabitsOpen →
6Always Dancing? Carnival, Samba and Tango
Music & festivals
Latin Americans are imagined dancing all night — samba at carnival, tango in Buenos Aires. Music really is a powerful part of the culture. But not everyone dances, and the stories behind the dances are deeper than the stereotype.
The stereotype: "All Latin Americans love to party and are born dancers."
The Passive & Reporting PassivesOpen →
7Day of the Dead: Mexican Halloween?
Traditions
Skulls, costumes and 2 November — many outsiders call the Day of the Dead "Mexican Halloween". In reality it is a family celebration of remembering, with very different roots.
The stereotype: "The Day of the Dead is just Mexican Halloween."
