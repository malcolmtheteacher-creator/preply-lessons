Understanding Exam Types

Different exams require different preparation strategies. Understanding the format helps you study effectively.

Multiple Choice

Format: Questions with 3-5 possible answers; choose the best one
Tests: Recall and recognition of knowledge
Strategy: Identify the best answer (not just correct). Eliminate obviously wrong answers first.

Short Answer

Format: 1-3 sentence answers to specific questions
Tests: Understanding and ability to express ideas concisely
Strategy: Be specific and complete. One sentence often isn't enough; explain your reasoning.

Essay Exam

Format: Extended written responses, usually 30-60 minutes per question
Tests: Critical thinking, analysis, and sustained writing under pressure
Strategy: Plan before writing (5-10 minutes). Structure clearly. Use evidence.

Problem-Solving (STEM)

Format: Mathematical or technical problems requiring worked solutions
Tests: Application of concepts and procedures
Strategy: Show all steps. Partial credit is often available for correct method even if final answer is wrong.

Oral/Viva Exam

Format: Spoken examination, often with a professor
Tests: Deep understanding and ability to explain under pressure
Strategy: Prepare key points. Practice explaining concepts aloud. Expect follow-up questions.

Time Management in Exams

  • Read all questions first (2 minutes)
  • Allocate time per question based on marks (1 mark = 1 minute, etc.)
  • Start with questions you're confident about (builds momentum)
  • Leave difficult questions for the end
  • Never spend all your time on one question
  • Check your work if time permits

Revision and Exam Strategies

Effective Revision Techniques

Active recall: Test yourself rather than just rereading. Close the textbook and write down everything you remember. Check accuracy afterward.

Spaced repetition: Review material at increasing intervals (1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks). This is more effective than cramming.

Practice problems: Complete past exam questions or practice problems. This shows you what you don't understand before the real exam.

Study groups: Explain concepts to classmates. Teaching is the best way to identify gaps in your knowledge.

Create summaries: Condense key information into study guides. The act of summarizing deepens understanding.

The Day Before an Exam

  • Do light review only (you've already studied)
  • Don't try to learn new material
  • Get adequate sleep (not an all-nighter)
  • Organize materials you'll need (student ID, pens, calculator if allowed)
  • Review the exam location and time

The Day of the Exam

  • Eat a proper breakfast
  • Arrive 15 minutes early
  • Bring water
  • Use the bathroom before the exam starts
  • Keep yourself calm through deep breathing

During the Exam

  • Read instructions carefully
  • Plan your time
  • Answer the question asked (not a related question)
  • Show your thinking (especially for math/science)
  • For essays: plan before writing
  • If stuck, move to the next question; return later

Exam anxiety reality: Some nervousness is normal and even beneficial (slight stress improves performance). If anxiety is severe, talk to your university's disability/support office about accommodations.

Practice: Analyzing an Exam Question

Essay Exam Question: "Evaluate the impact of social media on mental health in adolescents. Consider both positive and negative effects, and discuss methodological challenges in this research."
What does the question ask?

Key command word: Evaluate (make judgments about, weigh evidence for and against)
Topic: Social media's impact on adolescent mental health
Scope: Both positive AND negative effects; research methodological challenges
Don't write about: Social media use among adults, positive/negative effects unrelated to mental health, only one perspective

How would you structure your answer?

Introduction: Define key terms (social media, mental health, adolescents). State your position: "While social media offers connection opportunities, evidence suggests significant mental health risks outweigh benefits for many adolescents."
Body paragraph 1: Negative effects with evidence (depression, anxiety, FOMO, cyberbullying)
Body paragraph 2: Positive effects with evidence (social support, self-expression, community)
Body paragraph 3: Methodological challenges (causality vs. correlation, self-report bias, evolving platforms, individual differences)
Conclusion: Overall judgment, implications, future research needs

What would strengthen your answer?

Using specific research (author/year). Acknowledging counterarguments ("Some argue social media helps shy adolescents; however, research shows..."). Distinguishing between correlation and causation. Discussing variables (amount of use, type of use, age of adolescent). Showing you understand methodology (selection bias, confounding variables).

Preparing for Your Exam

Exam Preparation Checklist

☐ Understand exam format and question types

☐ Know the exam date, time, and location

☐ Have a copy of the exam specification/syllabus

☐ Create a revision schedule (start 3-4 weeks before)

☐ Review past exam papers

☐ Practice answering timed questions

☐ Study with classmates to identify gaps

☐ Know what materials are allowed (notes, calculator, dictionary?)

☐ Plan transport and arrange backup if needed

☐ Identify what topic areas you find hardest; focus there

After the Exam

Once the exam is over:

  • Don't obsess over answers
  • Reflect on what you learned about exam technique
  • If you didn't do well, meet with your professor to understand why
  • Adjust your preparation strategy for the next exam
  • Remember that one exam doesn't define your ability

Most important reminder: Exams are one way of assessing learning, but they don't measure your intelligence, worth, or potential. Regardless of the result, you'll learn and improve for next time.
