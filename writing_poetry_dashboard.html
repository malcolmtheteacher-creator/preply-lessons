Writing Poetry

A six-lesson series — from the blank page to a finished, performable poem. By the end of the sequence you will have written multiple poems and developed the toolkit to keep writing them.
6 lessons · designed for upper-intermediate to advanced writers

What this series does

Each lesson is a single 50-minute session with the same shape: a warm-up, vocabulary cards, a reading, a drill, a scaffolded write, and a free write. You will leave every lesson with a finished poem.

The lessons build on each other but can also be taken individually. Lesson 1 is the foundation; lessons 2–4 deepen specific aspects of the craft; lessons 5 and 6 take you out into performance and revision.

Lesson 1

Foundations

What makes a poem a poem. Line, stanza, metaphor, simile, image, rhythm, free verse, repetition. Includes the famous Brendan Behan anecdote about prose and poetry.
8 tools · 1 reading · scaffolded write · free write
 Lesson 2

Sound — The Music-Making Toolkit

Alliteration, assonance, consonance, onomatopoeia, anaphora, parallelism, internal rhyme, slant rhyme. How to make words behave like the thing they describe.
8 sound devices · soundscape poem
 Lesson 3

Image & Metaphor

Concrete images, sensory imagery, extended metaphor, personification, symbol, conceit. Show, don't tell — what it actually means. Building a poem on one central image.
8 image tools · extended-metaphor poem
 Lesson 4

Forms

Limerick, haiku, sonnet, ballad, villanelle, ghazal, free verse, blank verse. When structure helps and when it hurts. Write your own haiku and limerick.
8 forms · haiku + limerick
 Lesson 5

Slam & Spoken Word

Poems written for the stage, not the page. The hook, personal stakes, direct address, anaphora, code-switching, the volta, the closing beat. Write and perform.
8 slam tools · scaffolded slam piece
 Lesson 6

Revision

The work after the first draft. Cut, compress, specify, kill your darlings, cold-read. Before-and-after analysis of a real revision. Revise a previous poem.
8 revision tools · draft + revise in one sitting
Suggested sequence: if you're new to writing poetry, take Lesson 1 first. Then any order works — though Lesson 6 (Revision) is most useful after you have a few poems to revise. The Slam lesson (5) is the most accessible for confident speakers; Forms (4) is the most challenging for first-time writers.
