Poetry: Read It, Write It

Learn to read real poems closely, talk about them in English, and write poems of your own. Each lesson teaches eight tools, analyses a real poem, and ends with you writing.

The six lessons

Lesson 1

Foundations

What makes a poem a poem: line, stanza, metaphor, simile, image, rhythm, free verse, repetition.

Real poem: Emily Dickinson, "Hope" is the thing with feathers

 Lesson 2

Sound: The Music-Making Toolkit

Alliteration, assonance, consonance, onomatopoeia, anaphora, parallelism, internal and slant rhyme.

Real poem: Tennyson, The Eagle

 Lesson 3

Image & Metaphor

Show don't tell: concrete and sensory imagery, extended metaphor, personification, symbol, conceit.

Real poem: Keats, To Autumn

 Lesson 4

Forms

Limerick, haiku, sonnet, ballad, villanelle, ghazal, free verse, blank verse, and when form helps.

Real poem: Shakespeare, Sonnet 18; Bashō's frog haiku

 Lesson 5

Slam & Spoken Word

Writing for the stage: the hook, personal stakes, direct address, anaphora, the volta, the closing beat.

Real poem: Blake, London

 Lesson 6

Revision

Turning a draft into a poem: cut, compress, be specific, kill your darlings, cold reading.

Real poem: Wordsworth, two versions of the daffodils poem
How every lesson works
  1. Warm-up: a short idea about poetry
  2. Vocabulary: eight tools to learn
  3. Reading: a poem written for the lesson
  4. Drill: test yourself on new lines
  5. A Real Poem: read, analyse and discuss a famous poem
  6. Your Turn: write with a frame to help you
  7. Free Write: write your own poem with no frame
Suggested order: start with Lesson 1, then take Lessons 2 to 5 in any order. Lesson 6 (Revision) works best when you already have a few poems to improve. Lesson 5 (Slam) is the most accessible if you enjoy speaking; Lesson 4 (Forms) is the most demanding for a first-time writer. Your writing is saved in your browser, and each lesson has a button to copy it all.
