🪞 Quick Fix: As vs Like

Like for comparisons, as for roles and functions

Level B2

What's the error?

Original sentence:

"Like I said before, this is important."

Like I said — this uses LIKE before a subject + verb clause. That's the error.

Corrected:

As I said before, this is important.

The Rule

LIKE = comparison, similar to (followed by a noun or pronoun)

  • "She sings like a professional."
  • "He looks like his father."
  • "It smells like roses."

AS = in the role/function of, or in the way that (followed by a clause with subject + verb, or a role)

  • "As I said..." (clause: S + V)
  • "She worked as a nurse." (role)
  • "Do as I say." (the way that)

Common error: Using LIKE before a subject+verb clause like "like I said" or "like you know" — these need AS.

Quick Test: Does a verb follow immediately after? → Use AS. Does only a noun follow? → Use LIKE.

How to Fix It

✓ LIKE (comparison)

🪞

Pattern: LIKE + noun/pronoun

She swims like a fish.

It smells like roses.

He dances like her.

✓ AS (role/function)

📌

Pattern: AS + clause (S+V) or role

As I mentioned...

She works as a manager.

Do as you're told.

Remember

LIKE compares things to something similar. It's for resemblance or similarity.

AS talks about someone's role, function, or the manner/way something happens. It often introduces a clause.
