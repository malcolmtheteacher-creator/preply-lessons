📍 As vs Like — Comparison or Role?

Choosing the right preposition | B1/B2 Level

What's wrong with these sentences?

Two students wrote about their jobs. One made a mistake. Which one?

She works like a nurse, so she helps people every day.

She works as a nurse, so she helps people every day.

Why is the first one wrong?

✓ The key difference

Like = comparison. You're saying two things are similar in some way, but NOT the same.

As = role or function. You're saying what someone IS or what role they have.

❌ "She works like a nurse." — This means she works in a similar WAY to a nurse, but she's NOT a nurse.

✓ "She works as a nurse." — This means she IS a nurse; nursing is her job.

More examples:

✓ He works as a chef in a restaurant. (he IS a chef)

✓ She plays like a professional. (she plays well, similar to a pro, but might not be one)

✓ Do as I do. (follow my example; perform the same action)

📚 The Pattern

LIKE (Comparison)

🔀

Similar, but NOT the same thing

Like + noun

"She runs like an athlete."

↳ She runs well, similar to how an athlete runs.

AS (Role / Function)

🎭

IS that thing; HAS that role

As + noun

"She works as an athlete."

↳ Her job/role IS athlete.

How to choose:

LIKE + noun = SIMILAR, but different things
AS + noun = IDENTITY or ROLE
AS + clause = ACTION or FACT ("as I said")
LIKE (Comparison) AS (Role / Function)
She swims like a fish. (natural, effortless — similar to a fish) She works as a lifeguard. (her job/role is lifeguard)
He sings like a bird. (beautiful voice — similar to bird song) He works as a singer. (his profession is singing)
Like my sister, I love art. (I am similar to my sister) As your friend, I care about you. (my role is friend to you)
She acts like a leader. (behaves similar to a leader) She works as a leader. (she IS the leader)
As I said, we need more time. (expressing what was said)
💡 Quick test: If you can replace the phrase with "in the role of" or "in the capacity of," use AS. If you're comparing two different things, use LIKE.
