Exploring AI and Responsibility

The Three Key Issues

Read through these three critical issues in AI ethics:

1. Accuracy

AI systems must be thoroughly tested and their decision-making processes must be explainable. When an AI system makes a mistake — whether in medical diagnosis, financial prediction, or hiring — the consequences can be serious. Users need to understand why an AI system made a particular decision.

2. Responsibility

When something goes wrong with an AI system, who is accountable? Is it the developers who created the system, the company using it, the regulators who should have overseen it, or the users who relied on it? This question becomes critical when decisions affect people"s lives.

3. Regulation

Should AI follow the same rules as medicine or aviation? These fields have strict regulatory frameworks because mistakes can harm people. The question is: should AI have similar oversight, and if so, how much regulation is enough?

Language for Discussing Responsibility

Expressing Cause and Responsibility

"It was caused by…" — Use this to explain what led to a situation.

Example: "The error in the diagnosis was caused by inadequate training data."

"The responsibility lies with…" — Use this to assign accountability.

Example: "The responsibility lies with the developers who failed to test the system properly."

"If X had been tested, Y wouldn"t have happened." — Use conditionals to discuss hypothetical situations.

Example: "If the system had been tested in real-world conditions, these errors would not have occurred."

"Unless companies…, users will…" — Use to express consequences.

Example: "Unless companies increase transparency, users will lose trust in AI."

Hedging and Speculating

"It might / may / could have been…" — Show uncertainty about what happened.

Example: "It could have been a lack of oversight that led to these problems."

"It seems / appears that…" — Express tentative observations.

Example: "It appears that regulation has not kept pace with AI development."

"Arguably, the problem was…" — Present a debatable point.

Example: "Arguably, the core problem was the absence of industry standards."

"From a compliance perspective…" — Introduce a specific viewpoint.

Example: "From a compliance perspective, the company had not met existing safety requirements."

Speaking Task: Ethics Committee Role-play

You are members of an AI Ethics Committee deciding whether an AI product should launch. Read the scenario below, then prepare your role-play.

Scenario

Your company has developed an AI system that screens job applications. Initial testing shows 94% accuracy. However, recent audits suggest it may be biased against certain demographic groups, though the evidence is not conclusive.

Your role: You will take one of these positions:

  • CEO: Wants to launch quickly. The market window is closing.
  • Developer: Worried about the accuracy concerns and wants more testing.
  • Lawyer: Concerned about legal liability and compliance.
  • User Advocate: Focused on fairness and potential harm to applicants.
