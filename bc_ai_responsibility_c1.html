Three Key Issues in AI Responsibility

1. Accuracy: AI must be tested and explainable

AI systems need rigorous testing before deployment. Users and regulators must understand how the AI makes its decisions.

2. Responsibility: Who is accountable for outcomes?

When AI fails, determining accountability is complex. Is it the developers, the company using the AI, or the regulators who approved it?

3. Regulation: Should AI follow the same rules as medicine or aviation?

High-risk sectors like medicine and aviation have strict safety regulations. Should AI in these fields be regulated the same way?

Cause and Responsibility Language

Use these phrases when discussing who caused a problem and who bears responsibility:

It was caused by...
The AI error was caused by insufficient testing.
The responsibility lies with...
The responsibility lies with the developers who failed to validate the system.
If X had been tested, Y wouldn't have happened
If the algorithm had been tested on diverse data, these errors wouldn't have occurred.
Unless companies..., users will...
Unless companies invest in AI safety, users will lose trust in the technology.

Case Study: AI in Healthcare

Scenario: An AI system designed to diagnose diseases from medical images misdiagnoses several patients, leading to delayed treatment.

The Question: Who is responsible — the developers, the hospital that implemented it, or the regulator?

Who bears responsibility and why?

Likely shared responsibility:

  • Developers: Failed to test the AI on diverse patient populations; did not communicate accuracy limits clearly
  • Hospital: Did not implement proper oversight; may not have had doctors verify AI recommendations
  • Regulator: May not have required sufficient pre-deployment testing or ongoing monitoring

What should have been done differently?

  • Rigorous testing on diverse patient data before launch
  • Clear communication about the AI's limitations and accuracy rates
  • A requirement that doctors verify AI-assisted diagnoses before treatment
  • Ongoing monitoring and auditing of the system in real-world use
  • Regulatory approval and oversight before deployment in healthcare

Hedging and Speculating

When discussing what might have gone wrong, use speculative and hedging language:

It might/may/could have been...
The system could have been more accurate if tested on larger datasets.
It seems/appears that...
It appears that oversight mechanisms were missing from the deployment process.
Arguably, the problem was...
Arguably, the problem was insufficient transparency about AI limitations.
From a compliance perspective...
From a compliance perspective, the deployment violated best practices.

Regulation Timeline Debate

Three possible futures for AI regulation. Which timeline is most realistic and why?

Timeline A: 2025 — No AI-Specific Regulation

AI systems continue to develop with minimal regulation. Responsibility is determined through court cases after harm occurs.

Timeline B: 2030 — Light-Touch Regulation

Industry guidelines and voluntary standards are established. Some high-risk sectors like healthcare require regulatory approval.

Timeline C: 2050 — Strict Global AI Law

Comprehensive international AI regulations similar to aviation or medicine. All high-impact AI systems require pre-deployment approval and ongoing monitoring.

Which timeline do you think is most realistic? Consider: technological development speed, public safety concerns, industry influence, and international cooperation challenges.

Speaking Task: AI Ethics Committee Role-Play

Your company is deciding whether to launch a new AI product. You will take a role on an ethics committee and negotiate responsibility, risks, and safeguards.

Roles in the Committee:

  • CEO: Wants to launch quickly; focuses on business benefits
  • Developer: Knows technical limitations; concerned about accuracy and transparency
  • Lawyer: Focused on legal liability and regulatory compliance
  • User Representative: Concerned about safety and fairness for end-users

Your task: Decide together: Should the AI product launch? Negotiate what safeguards must be in place, who bears responsibility for failures, and what testing is required.
