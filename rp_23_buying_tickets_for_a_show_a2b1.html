Buying Tickets for a Show

Tonight there is a big magic show in town. You did not buy tickets before — so you go to the theatre and ask at the desk. Time to be friendly and polite.

The Scene 🎬

It is Friday evening. There is a big magic show in town tonight. Everybody wants to see it. You did not buy your tickets before — you forgot! Now you are at the theatre.

You want two tickets. You want good seats, where you can see the stage. And you do not want to pay too much money. But there is one problem: a small red sign says "SOLD OUT".

Behind the desk is the clerk. They sell the tickets. Sometimes, when a show is "sold out", there are still one or two tickets left — people give tickets back. So do not go home yet. Ask nicely, and maybe you are lucky!

Warm up — talk for one minute

  1. Do you like going to shows — music, theatre, or the cinema? What was the last thing you saw?
  2. You want to see a show, but it is "sold out". Do you go home, or do you ask anyway? Why?

Who's who

You
The Ticket Buyer 🎩

Friendly and polite. You want two tickets, good seats, and a fair price — and you really want to see the show tonight.

The other person
The Clerk 🎟️

Kind and helpful. The show looks "sold out", but they can often find a seat or two — for people who ask nicely.

First: read the conversation in the next tab. Look at the polite words: please, thank you, I would like, can I. It is OK to speak slowly. If you do not understand, you can say "Sorry, can you say that again?"

The Conversation 💬

Read it out loud, one person each — then change. Listen for the polite words.

YouHello. I would like two tickets for the magic show tonight, please.
ClerkLet me see… Oh, I am sorry. Tonight is sold out. All the seats are gone.
YouOh, that's a pity. Is there anything you can do?

Useful Phrases 🧰

Eight phrases you can use to buy tickets — at a theatre, a cinema, or a museum. Say each one out loud BEFORE you look at the answer. Guessing first helps you remember.

Say it with a smile. A friendly voice and the words please and thank you help a lot. Speak slowly and clearly. Short and polite is perfect — you do not need long sentences.

🎟️ Asking for tickets

Phrase 1

You are at the desk. You want two tickets for tonight. Complete it: "I would like two ___ for tonight, please."

"I would like two tickets for tonight, please."
"I would like…" is the polite way to say "I want". Use it everywhere: "I would like a coffee", "I would like one ticket, please".
Phrase 2

You want to ask for the tickets. Which sentence is polite?

A. "Give me two tickets."
B. "Can I have two tickets, please?"
C. "Two tickets. Now."
B — "Can I have two tickets, please?"
"Can I have…?" and "please" make it soft and polite. A and C sound like an order. People help you more when you are polite.

💺 Seats, times and price

Phrase 3

You want to know when the show begins. Put the words in order:

does itWhat timestart?
"What time does it start?"
Use "What time does it…?" for many things: "What time does it open?", "What time does it finish?"
Phrase 4

You want to know the price. Complete it: "How much ___ the tickets?"

"How much are the tickets?"
Use "are" for more than one thing (tickets). For one thing, say "How much is the ticket?" You can also say "How much is it?"

🔴 When it's sold out

Phrase 5

You want to know if there are still some tickets. Complete it: "Are there any tickets ___?"

"Are there any tickets left?"
"…left" means "still there, not gone". You can use it a lot: "Is there any coffee left?", "Are there any seats left?"
Phrase 6

The clerk says: "Sorry, it's sold out." You do not want to go home. What do you say? Choose the polite one.

A. "That's not fair!"
B. "That's a pity. Is there anything you can do?"
C. "OK, bye."
B — "That's a pity. Is there anything you can do?"
"That's a pity" shows you are sad, but you are calm and kind. "Is there anything you can do?" asks the clerk to help you. A is angry; C stops too soon — you might miss a lucky ticket!

💛 Checking and saying thank you

Phrase 7

The clerk says the seats are "at the side". You want to know: is the view OK? Say your question out loud first. (What do you ask?)

"Can we see the stage from there?"
Before you say yes, it is good to check the seats. You can also ask: "Is it a good view?" or "Can we see well?"
Phrase 8

The clerk was very helpful. At the end, you want to say a big thank you. What do you say? (Say it out loud first.)

"Thank you so much! You are very kind."
Add "so much" to make your thank you bigger and warmer. "You are very kind" tells the person you saw their help. A smile helps too!

🔑 Make them yours

These phrases work at a theatre, a cinema, a museum — everywhere you buy a ticket. Write your own sentences. Say each one out loud first.

"I would like ___, please." — What do you want to buy or ask for? (two tickets, one coffee, a map…)

"What time does ___ start?" — Think of an event. When does it start?

"Are there any ___ left?" — Ask about something that can run out (seats, tickets, tables…).

Quick test: close your eyes and say the whole visit out loud — ask for tickets → hear "sold out" → ask nicely → check the seats and the price → say thank you. When it is easy, go to Your Turn.

Your Turn 🎭

Now it is your theatre. Say your answers out loud FIRST, then write them. Use the timer if you like.

Round 1 — you are the ticket buyer 🎩

The clerk speaks — you answer. Be polite: use please, thank you, and I would like.

ClerkI am sorry — tonight is sold out. All the seats are gone.
ClerkWait… yes! Two tickets just came back. But they are at the side.
ClerkYes, you can see the stage. You only miss a little bit.

Change places. You work at the theatre. Be friendly and helpful. Say "Welcome", "Let me check", "No problem", "I'm sorry".

The Twist

🎩 Only one ticket left

One more time — no help this round. You want two tickets, but the clerk says: "I'm sorry, there is only one ticket left." But you are with a friend! What do you do? Stay polite and friendly. Maybe you ask: is there a seat at the back too? Can you wait for more returns? Can you come back tomorrow? Try to find a way — and stay kind. Start the timer and speak!

Before you finish…

Think about what you said. Can you say "yes" to these?

  • I asked for tickets politely, with "please".
  • When it was "sold out", I stayed calm and asked for help.
  • I asked about the seats, the price, and the time.
  • I said "thank you" warmly at the end.
  • I stayed friendly the whole time.

Can't say yes to one? That is your practice — try Round 1 again.

The Echo 🕰️

A hundred years ago, one man made theatres sell out again and again. His name was Harry Houdini. He was a magician and an escape artist. He got out of chains, locked boxes, and tanks of water.

People waited for hours to see him. Everybody wanted a ticket. Shows were "sold out" all the time — because everyone wanted to be in the room, on the night, to see the impossible.

So when you ask at the desk for a "sold out" show, you are in a very old queue. People have wanted to see magic — and to get that last ticket — for more than a hundred years.
