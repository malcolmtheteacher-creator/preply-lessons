Warm up

Today you'll describe things precisely and give personal details clearly, so you can sort out a real problem at a service desk.

Have a go. There are no wrong answers.

  1. Have you ever lost something important while travelling? What happened, and did you get it back?
  2. Look at the bag or coat you use most. Describe it in one sentence, so a stranger could pick it out.
  3. Which is harder for you in English: spelling your name, or saying a long phone number? Why?
2

Phrases for the desk

Four kinds of thing you'll need to say. Read them aloud once.

Describing it

  • It's a large black leather holdall.
  • It's got two wheels and a long strap.
  • It's the one with a yellow sticker on the front.
  • There's a ribbon tied to the handle.
  • It's quite worn. There's a scratch on the side.

At the desk

  • Can you describe it for me?
  • Does it have any distinguishing features?
  • Could you spell that for me?
  • Let me just check.
  • Bear with me a moment.
  • I'm afraid that one isn't yours.

Checking

  • Sorry, was that M for Mike or N for November?
  • So that's double seven, double oh…
  • Could you say that again, a bit more slowly?
  • Just to confirm: …
  • Is that with one L or two?

Sorting it out

  • It should have been on the flight from …
  • Could you send it to my hotel?
  • How long will it take?
  • Is there a reference number I can use?
  • That's a relief. Thank you.

The spelling alphabet

Desks use it to check letters that sound alike: B / P / D / T, M / N, F / S.

Size, then colour, then pattern, then material, then the thing itself.

Key words

suitcase (noun) — a large case with a handle for carrying clothes when you travel. Example: "My suitcase was so full I had to sit on it to close it."
holdall (noun) — a large soft bag with handles, often used for sports kit or a weekend away. Example: "He threw his holdall into the back of the car."
cabin bag (noun) — a small bag you are allowed to take onto the plane with you. Example: "My cabin bag was too big, so I had to pay extra."
strap (noun) — a narrow band of material for carrying or fastening something. Example: "The strap on my backpack broke halfway up the hill."
zip (noun) — two rows of small metal or plastic teeth that close a bag or a jacket. Example: "The zip on my jacket keeps getting stuck."
scratched (adjective) — with a thin mark or line cut into the surface. Example: "The back of my phone is badly scratched."
striped (adjective) — with lines of a different colour. Example: "She was wearing a blue and white striped shirt."
baggage reclaim (noun) — the place at an airport where you collect your bags after a flight. Example: "We waited at baggage reclaim for forty minutes."
lost property (noun) — an office where things people have left behind are kept until someone collects them. Example: "Try lost property. Someone might have handed it in."
distinguishing feature (noun) — a detail that makes one thing easy to tell apart from similar ones. Example: "Does your bag have any distinguishing features, like a sticker or a ribbon?"
