Listen in on this conversation

Ana: Do you walk to your office every day?

Ben: It depends. If the weather is nice, I walk. If it rains, I take the bus.

Look at Ben's answer. He is not talking about one special day — he is talking about every day. Look at his verbs: rains, walk, take. Are they past, present or future? Say your idea out loud before you check.

Check your idea CLICK
All present simple — in both halves of each sentence. When something is true every time — rain, bus, walk, again and again — English keeps everything in the present. No future, no past. That's today's pattern.
Today: sentences with if + present, present — so you can talk about facts, describe your routines, and choose between if and when.

Always True: "If you heat ice, it melts"

For facts about the world — things that are true every single time.

Spot the pattern

Three facts:

  • "If you heat ice, it melts."
  • "If you don't water plants, they die."
  • "If you mix blue and yellow, you get green."

Two questions before you check: What tense is in both halves? And what do you see after the first half?

Check the pattern CLICK
Both halves are present simple — even the "result" half. Never "it will melt" here.
And there is a comma after the if-half when "if" comes first.
If + present simple , + present simple

You can turn it around

"If you heat ice, it melts."
→
"Ice melts if you heat it."

Same meaning. When "if" moves to the middle, the comma disappears.

This pattern also gives simple instructions: "If you feel sick, drink water." If + present, then the thing to do.

If or When?

Two small words — one small but important difference.

Spot the pattern

  • "When I get home, I make a cup of tea." (every day)
  • "If I see a spider, I scream." (not every day!)
  • "When the film ends, we go home." (films always end)

Why "when" in two sentences but "if" in the other? Say your idea before you check.

Check the difference CLICK
When = you are sure it happens — every day, every time. If = maybe. I get home every day (when), but a spider is a maybe (if). The grammar stays the same: present + present.
When + present simple , + present simple → sure, every time
If + present simple , + present simple → maybe
