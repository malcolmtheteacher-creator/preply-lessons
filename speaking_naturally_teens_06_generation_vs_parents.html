Your Generation vs. Your Parents' - "Everything Looks Easy Until You Have to Do It"

C1-C2 Level | 50 minutes | 5 Key Expressions

"Everything Looks Easy Until You Have to Do It"

Gen Z is the first generation in modern history expected to be POORER than their parents.

Your dad says: "When I was your age, I worked hard, bought a house, and didn't complain."

You think: "Yeah, but when you were my age, a house cost 3 years' salary. Now it costs 15 years' salary. And I have student debt you never had."

So who has it harder? Your generation or your parents' generation?

The answer is more complicated than either side admits. Let's look at the actual numbers.

The Economic Reality: 1980 vs. 2024

🏠 Your Parents' Generation (1980s)

Average House Price (UK):
£23,000 (about 3-4x average salary)
University:
FREE in UK. Zero student debt.
Entry-Level Jobs:
Factory jobs paid £8,000-£12,000/year with pension & stability
Social Contract:
Work hard → Buy house → Retire comfortably

📱 Your Generation (2024)

Average House Price (UK):
£290,000 (about 10-15x average salary)
University:
£9,250/year tuition. Graduate with £45,000+ debt.
Entry-Level Jobs:
Retail/service jobs pay £20,000-£25,000/year, often part-time, no pension
Social Contract:
Work hard → Still can't afford rent → Retirement? What's that?
In 1980, you could buy an average UK house with 3 years of average salary.
In 2024, it takes 10-15 years of average salary.

But Wait... The Counter-Argument

Your parents say: "But you have things we never had!"

They're not wrong:

  • ✅ Internet, smartphones, global connectivity
  • ✅ Access to information (entire libraries in your pocket)
  • ✅ Remote work opportunities worldwide
  • ✅ Online learning, freelancing, side hustles
  • ✅ Better technology, better medicine, longer life expectancy
  • ✅ More social freedom (LGBTQ+ rights, gender equality progressing)

So the question becomes: Would you rather have economic security (house, stable job, pension) OR technological/social freedom (internet, opportunities, rights)?

Can't you have both? That's the real question.

Think About It:

  • Is your generation actually worse off, or just different?
  • Would you trade your smartphone and internet for the ability to buy a house at 25?
  • When your parents say "work hard and you'll succeed," are they out of touch, or is there still truth in it?
  • What advantages do you have that your parents didn't?

The Truth: Both generations face/faced real challenges. Your parents dealt with different economic conditions, limited technology, and different social issues. You're dealing with housing crisis, student debt, and uncertain futures—but with more tools and information to navigate it.

The conversation shouldn't be "who has it worse." It should be: "How do we understand each other's realities and work together?"

Expressions for Generational Debates (Part 1)

Click each expression to see how it's used

1. "The goalposts have moved since..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: The standards or requirements for success have changed (usually become harder). What used to be achievable is now much more difficult. Like in football when you move the goals farther away.
Generational Context:
"The goalposts have moved since my parents' generation. They needed A-levels to get a good job. Now you need a degree, plus internships, plus work experience, plus a perfect online presence. The bar keeps getting higher."
NOW YOU TRY:
Complete this: "The goalposts have moved since my parents were young. They could , but now we have to ."
2. "That's comparing apples to oranges"
Formality: Informal/Neutral
Meaning: You're comparing two things that are so different that the comparison doesn't make sense. They seem similar but are fundamentally different.
Generational Context:
"My dad says 'I worked part-time and paid my way through university.' But that's comparing apples to oranges. In 1985, university was free. Now it costs £9,250 a year. You can't compare the two situations."
NOW YOU TRY:
Your parent says: "I bought my first house at 25, why can't you?" Respond using "comparing apples to oranges" and explain why housing then vs. now is different.

Expressions for Generational Debates (Part 2)

3. "The elephant in the room is..."
Formality: Neutral
Meaning: The obvious problem or truth that everyone knows but no one wants to talk about. Like having an elephant in your living room—impossible to miss, but everyone pretends it's not there.
Generational Context:
"When older people say 'just work harder,' the elephant in the room is that they bought houses when they cost 3x salary, not 15x salary. They don't want to admit that the economy was simply easier to navigate."
NOW YOU TRY:
Complete: "When discussing career success with my parents, the elephant in the room is..."
4. "To be fair..."
Formality: Neutral/Conversational
Meaning: Acknowledge the other side's valid point even when you're arguing against them. Shows you're being balanced and considering both perspectives.
Generational Context:
"My parents had cheaper housing, but to be fair, they didn't have the internet, remote work, or the global opportunities we have now. They were more limited in some ways."
NOW YOU TRY:
You're arguing your generation has it harder. But acknowledge something your parents' generation struggled with. Start with "To be fair..."
5. "Taking everything into account..."
Formality: Neutral/Formal
Meaning: After considering all factors and perspectives, here's my conclusion. Used when you want to summarize or give a balanced final opinion.
Generational Context:
"Taking everything into account—the economy, technology, opportunities, and challenges—I think both generations faced real difficulties, just different kinds. We can't say one is objectively harder."
NOW YOU TRY:
After considering all we've discussed, give your verdict: "Taking everything into account, I think my generation / my parents' generation has it harder because..."

All 5 Expressions:

  • "The goalposts have moved since..." — Standards changed/harder
  • "That's comparing apples to oranges" — Invalid comparison
  • "The elephant in the room is..." — Obvious truth no one mentions
  • "To be fair..." — Acknowledge other side's valid point
  • "Taking everything into account..." — Balanced conclusion

Deep Dive: Defend Your Position

Choose a challenge below and speak for the full time. Try to use at least 3 of the 5 expressions naturally.

Challenge 1: Your Generation Has It Harder
Argue that Gen Z has it objectively harder than previous generations. Use statistics, explain how the goalposts moved, and address counterarguments fairly.
Challenge 2: Your Parents Had It Harder
Play devil's advocate. Argue that your parents' generation faced greater challenges. What advantages do you have that they didn't? Be honest.
Challenge 3: Explain to Your Parents
You're talking to your dad who says "just work hard like I did." Explain why that's comparing apples to oranges, but do it respectfully. Help him understand.
Challenge 4: The Balanced View
Taking everything into account, give the most balanced, nuanced take you can. Both generations face real challenges—how can we bridge the understanding gap?

The Big Takeaway:

The goal isn't to prove who has it harder. Both generations face real challenges—they're just different challenges.

Your parents grew up with:

  • ✅ Affordable housing and university
  • ❌ Limited technology and opportunities
  • ❌ Less social freedom

You're growing up with:

  • ✅ Internet, global opportunities, social progress
  • ❌ Housing crisis, student debt, economic uncertainty
  • ❌ Mental health pressure from constant comparison

The conversation should be about understanding, not blame.
