Reading: Growth Mindset and Learning

Read the text carefully, then answer the questions below.

Carol Dweck, a famous psychologist, studied how people learn. She discovered two different mindsets: the fixed mindset and the growth mindset. People with a fixed mindset believe their abilities are permanent — if they fail at something, they think they cannot improve. People with a growth mindset believe they can develop their abilities through practice and effort. When they make mistakes, they see them as learning opportunities, not failures. Dweck"s research shows that students with a growth mindset learn more effectively, overcome challenges better, and achieve higher grades. They are not afraid to make mistakes because they understand that mistakes help them grow. Schools and teachers now use growth mindset principles to help students become more confident and successful learners.

Question 1: Who is Carol Dweck?

Carol Dweck is a psychologist who studied how people learn and discovered the concepts of fixed and growth mindsets.

Question 2: What is the difference between a fixed mindset and a growth mindset?

A fixed mindset is the belief that abilities are permanent and cannot change. A growth mindset is the belief that abilities can be developed through practice and effort.

Question 3: How do people with a growth mindset view mistakes?

People with a growth mindset see mistakes as learning opportunities, not failures. They understand that mistakes help them grow and improve.

Question 4: According to the text, what are three benefits of having a growth mindset?

Students with a growth mindset (1) learn more effectively, (2) overcome challenges better, and (3) achieve higher grades.

Fixed Mindset vs. Growth Mindset

Fixed Mindset Thoughts:

  • "I"m not good at languages. I never will be."
  • "I made a mistake. I must be stupid."
  • "I failed the test. I should give up."
  • "If it"s hard, I"m not capable."

Growth Mindset Thoughts:

  • "I"m not good at languages yet, but I can improve with practice."
  • "I made a mistake. What can I learn from it?"
  • "I failed the test. How can I study differently next time?"
  • "If it"s hard, it"s a good opportunity to grow."

Language for Talking About Mistakes and Learning

Useful Phrases:

"I made the mistake of..." — to describe an error or wrong decision

Example: "I made the mistake of not reading the instructions carefully."

"Looking back, I should have..." — to reflect on what you could have done differently

Example: "Looking back, I should have studied harder for the exam."

"Next time I"ll..." — to describe what you will do differently in the future

Example: "Next time I"ll ask for help when I don"t understand."

"What I learned from this is..." — to explain the lesson or understanding you gained

Example: "What I learned from this is that preparation is more important than talent."

Speaking Task: Describe a Learning Experience

Tell a story about a mistake you made and what you learned from it. Speak for about 5 minutes. Use at least 3 of the language phrases from the Language Focus tab.

5:00

Structure Your Story:

  • What happened? Describe the mistake or failure
  • How did you feel? Describe your emotions
  • Why did it happen? Reflect on the cause
  • What did you learn? Explain the lesson
  • How did you improve? What did you do differently next time?

Tips for Your Talk:

  • Use at least 3 language phrases from this lesson
  • Speak clearly and at a natural pace
  • Use complete sentences
  • Show a growth mindset — explain how the mistake helped you improve
  • Try to use the full 5 minutes

Example Opening:

"I made the mistake of not preparing for my first English presentation. I was very nervous and forgot what I wanted to say. Looking back, I should have practised more. What I learned from this is that preparation is really important. Next time I"ll write notes and practise before any presentation. This experience helped me become a more confident speaker."
