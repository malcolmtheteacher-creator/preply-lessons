Present Perfect: Simple vs Continuous

Instructions: Look at each sentence. Which verb form is used, and is the other form possible? Click to see the explanation.
1 I'm here with Chuck Larsson who's brought out / who's been bringing out a book.
Answer: "who's brought out" (simple) is correct. This is a single, completed event – the book has been published. The continuous form sounds unnatural here.
2 I've basically spent / I've basically been spending the last 30 years studying meteorites.
Answer: Both are possible! The continuous form emphasises the ongoing duration of the activity. The simple form presents it as a completed fact about his career.
3 NASA has up to now detected / has up to now been detecting the most lethal giant rocks.
Answer: Both possible, but continuous emphasises the ongoing process of detection. Simple states the achievement up to now.
4 How come nobody has ever been killed / has ever been being killed by one?
Answer: Only "has been killed" (simple passive) is correct. The continuous passive form is grammatically awkward and unnatural here.
5 Well, I certainly think you've given / you've been giving our listeners food for thought.
Answer: Both forms work! "You've given" sounds more natural in this idiomatic expression, but "you've been giving" emphasises the ongoing interview.
6 Chuck Larsson who's been campaigning / who has campaigned relentlessly to raise awareness.
Answer: Both work – it's a matter of emphasis. The continuous emphasises the ongoing nature of his campaign; the simple presents it as a fact about him.

Key Point:

Present Perfect Simple = completed action/result
Present Perfect Continuous = emphasis on duration/ongoing process

Asteroid Deflection Strategies

Click each card to expand and read the details. Take notes on pros and cons!
💣 Nuclear Weapons
Click to read about this drastic option...
This might seem a drastic option bringing out our most deadly armaments. But the intention here would not be to pulverise the asteroid but rather to deflect its collision course. Otherwise, the danger is that you could generate masses of deadly debris – still heading for Earth. So the radiation from a nearby blast alone ought to be enough to steer it clear of Earth.
🚀 Kinetic Interceptor
A milder alternative to nuclear options...
A milder alternative to 'nuking' the asteroid might be to give it a small nudge. Paradoxically, a one-mile-an-hour impact from a rocket could do the trick. That alone would be sufficient to divert the asteroid by 170,000 miles. Mind you, we would need to hit it 20 years before its collision has been predicted.
🎨 Painting It
A surprisingly creative solution...
Again, this is a surprising idea and has to do with the science of absorbed and reflected radiation. It is common sense that white reflects more solar radiation whereas dark colours absorb it. Logically, if you 'paint' a section of the asteroid white, then the remaining darker part will receive more of a push from solar radiation, gradually knocking it off course. The white 'paint' could be light coloured dust or chalk.
⛵ Solar Sail
Using the power of the sun...
This involves sending a spacecraft to land a giant solar sail on the incoming asteroid. Once unfurled, the fact that it reflects solar radiation would enable it to push the asteroid away from Earth. The sail could even be adjusted to give it an element of remote control. Some experts have doubts about the complexity of setting up a working sail on a tumbling rock in space.
🕸️ Netting
Wrapping the asteroid...
NASA has given serious thought to the notion of netting an asteroid in a carbon fibre mesh. This would need to weigh about 250 kilograms to divert the course of an earth-bound asteroid. The net material would alter the amount of solar radiation that is absorbed and emitted and thereby nudge it off course. Merely 18 years would be enough to deflect a giant rock.
🪞 Mirrors (Laser Sublimation)
Concentrating solar energy...
Mirrors positioned strategically could concentrate the sun's solar rays and thereby heat the surface of the asteroid. This concentrated heat would cause the asteroid to spew vapours into space. This would provide enough thrust to change the asteroid's path of destruction. The use of mirrors is also called laser sublimation.
🛸 Gravitational Tractor
Using gravity to tow it away...
This strategy is a little controversial. Every object with sufficient mass will exert a gravitational pull, however small. Theoretically, navigating a heavy spacecraft close to the asteroid would be enough to tow it away with its gravitational force. However, some scientists feel that some thrust might be needed to stop the craft crashing into the asteroid which would cancel out any towing action.
⛏️ MADMEN (Mining)
Mining the asteroid itself...
The NASA-funded Modular Asteroid Deflection Mission Ejector Node project involves sending nuclear-powered robots to land on and start 'mining' on the surface of the threatening asteroid. The mined fragments would then be ejected into space at high speed by means of electromagnets, again providing enough thrust to alter its course. This idea is in its initial stages of research and development.
