Giving and Supporting Opinions

Learn to express ideas and support them with reasons and examples

5:00

Introducing Your Opinion

Polite Ways to State Your View

In my opinion... — Most common and direct

I think that... — Informal but clear

I believe... — Formal and strong

It seems to me that... — Softer, more thoughtful

From my perspective... — Academic and respectful

I would argue that... — Strong academic position

Agreeing with Others

Show You Support Their Ideas

I agree. — Simple agreement

That's a good point. — Acknowledge their idea

I couldn't agree more. — Strong agreement

You're absolutely right. — Complete agreement

I see your point. — Understand and accept

Disagreeing Politely

Respectful Disagreement

I disagree, but... — Soft disagreement

That's interesting, however... — Add another perspective

I see what you mean, but... — Acknowledge, then disagree

I'm not sure I agree because... — Respectful disagreement with reason

On the other hand... — Introduce different viewpoint

Supporting Your Opinion

Give Reasons and Examples

For example... — Introduce specific instance

Because... — State a reason

This shows that... — Connect idea to evidence

First of all... Second... Finally... — List multiple reasons

Studies show that... — Use research or facts

Essential Vocabulary

Perspective A particular point of view or way of thinking
Justify Give reasons or evidence to support a claim
Stance The position or attitude you take on an issue
Contend Argue or claim something as a position
Substantiate Support with evidence or proof
Concede Admit that something is true or valid
Refute Prove something wrong with evidence
Assert State something firmly and confidently

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: State an Opinion

Rewrite this too bluntly: "Social media is bad." Use a more academic phrase.

"In my opinion, social media has negative effects on student well-being."

Exercise 2: Agree and Add

Someone says: "Technology has changed education." Agree and add a supporting reason.

"I completely agree. For example, students now have access to online learning resources that weren't available before."

Exercise 3: Disagree Politely

Someone says: "Exams are the best way to test knowledge." Write a respectful disagreement.

"I see your point, but I'm not entirely sure I agree. Portfolio assessments and projects can also show student knowledge effectively."

Sample Discussion

Dialogue Example

Student A: "In my opinion, students should study abroad because it improves language skills."

Student B: "I agree with that. Additionally, it provides cultural experience."

Student C: "On the other hand, studying abroad is expensive for many families."

Student B: "That's a valid point. However, scholarships and grants can help solve this problem."

Student A: "Exactly. I would argue that the benefits outweigh the costs."

Key Takeaways

✓ Use academic phrases to introduce opinions

✓ Always support your ideas with reasons or examples

✓ Disagreement is okay if expressed politely

✓ Listen to others' perspectives

✓ Acknowledge good points even if you disagree

✓ Use transition words to connect ideas
