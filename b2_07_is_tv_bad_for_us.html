Is TV Bad For Us?

B2 English Lesson | Debate & Persuasive Language

What Do You Think About TV?

Television has been part of our lives for decades. But recently, people are asking important questions: Is watching TV good for us or bad for us? What are the benefits and risks?

Think about your own TV watching:

How many hours of TV do you watch per week? What do you enjoy watching?

People watch TV for different reasons: entertainment, news, sports, documentaries, dramas, or reality shows. Some people watch very little, while others watch several hours daily. What matters is being aware of how TV affects your health, relationships, and daily life.

What are the positives and negatives of TV?

Try to think of at least three benefits and three drawbacks.

Benefits: Educational content, entertainment, staying informed about world events, relaxation

Drawbacks: Too much screen time, passive activity, sleep disruption, eye strain, isolation from social interaction

Vocabulary for Discussing TV

Types of TV Programmes

Documentary
A programme about real events, people, or nature
Example: "I watched a fascinating documentary about climate change."
Drama series
A story-based show that continues over multiple episodes
Example: "The drama series has complex characters and interesting plot twists."
Reality show
A programme featuring real people in real situations (though often staged)
Example: "Reality shows can be entertaining but sometimes feel artificial."
News programme
A show that reports current events and world news
Example: "I watch the news programme every evening to stay informed."

Adjectives for Evaluating TV

Addictive
Causing people to want to watch more and more
Example: "This series is so addictive that I watched all five seasons in two weeks!"
Thought-provoking
Making people think deeply about important issues
Example: "The documentary was thought-provoking and changed my perspective."
Superficial
Not dealing with serious topics; lacking depth
Example: "Many reality shows are criticized for being superficial and mindless."
Engaging
Interesting and holding your attention
Example: "The plot is so engaging that you can't predict what happens next."
Intellectually stimulating
Making you think and use your mind actively
Example: "Quality documentaries are intellectually stimulating and educational."

Health and Impact Related Words

Sedentary
Involving a lot of sitting; not active or moving
Example: "Excessive TV watching promotes a sedentary lifestyle."
Screen time
The amount of time spent looking at screens (TV, phone, computer)
Example: "Experts recommend limiting children's screen time to two hours per day."
Distraction
Something that takes your attention away from something else
Example: "TV can be a distraction from work and social interaction."

Functional Language: Debate Skills

Stating Your Opinion Persuasively

In my opinion...
Use this to: Give your personal view on the topic
"In my opinion, the benefits of television outweigh the drawbacks because it provides education and entertainment."
It could be argued that...
Use this to: Present an argument in a more formal way
"It could be argued that excessive screen time damages children's development."
Research shows that...
Use this to: Support your argument with evidence or data
"Research shows that watching news programmes helps people stay informed about important events."

Agreeing with Someone

I completely agree with you.
Use this to: Express strong agreement
"I completely agree with you that documentaries are educational and valuable."
That's a valid point.
Use this to: Acknowledge someone's good argument
"That's a valid point about TV affecting people's sleep patterns."
You're absolutely right to mention...
Use this to: Agree and highlight why something is important
"You're absolutely right to mention the impact on children's health."

Disagreeing Respectfully

I see your point, but...
Use this to: Disagree politely while acknowledging their view
"I see your point about reality shows being entertaining, but many are also quite shallow."
I'd have to disagree with that because...
Use this to: Politely present your disagreement with reasons
"I'd have to disagree with that because educational programmes offer significant benefits."
That may be true, however...
Use this to: Acknowledge their point but offer a counter-argument
"That may be true, however, the problem isn't TV itself but how much we watch."

Asking for Opinions

What do you think about...?
Use this to: Ask directly for someone's opinion
"What do you think about the impact of TV on family relationships?"
Don't you think that...?
Use this to: Ask a question that suggests you already have an opinion
"Don't you think that documentaries are one of the best things on television?"
How do you feel about...?
Use this to: Ask about someone's feelings or views
"How do you feel about the amount of advertising on TV channels?"

Arguments: Is TV Good or Bad?

Arguments That TV Is Good For Us

Education & Learning
Main point: Television provides access to high-quality educational content.

Details: Documentaries teach history, science, geography, and culture. Children can learn from educational programmes. Adults can improve their knowledge about current events through news programmes.

Example: "A documentary about climate change can teach you more in an hour than a textbook chapter."
Entertainment & Relaxation
Main point: Television provides affordable entertainment and helps people relax.

Details: After a long, stressful day, watching TV helps you unwind. Quality dramas and comedies bring joy and laughter. Entertainment is important for mental health and happiness.

Example: "Coming home and watching your favourite comedy series is a great way to reduce stress."
Staying Informed
Main point: Television helps people stay aware of important world events.

Details: News programmes inform citizens about politics, disasters, and social issues. This is essential for making informed decisions and participating in democracy. Without television news, many people wouldn't know what's happening in the world.

Example: "Watching the news helps you understand important political and social issues."
Quality Productions
Main point: Modern television has exceptional artistic and narrative quality.

Details: Today's drama series feature complex characters, sophisticated storytelling, and high production values. Television has become a serious artistic medium, comparable to film and literature.

Example: "Award-winning drama series demonstrate that television can be intellectually stimulating and artistically valuable."

Arguments That TV Is Bad For Us

Health Problems
Main point: Excessive television watching causes serious health problems.

Details: TV promotes a sedentary lifestyle, leading to obesity and weight gain. Screen time damages eyesight and causes headaches. Blue light from screens disrupts sleep patterns. People who watch excessive TV have higher rates of heart disease and diabetes.

Example: "Researchers have found that watching TV for more than three hours daily increases the risk of heart disease."
Wasted Time & Reduced Productivity
Main point: Television is a massive time-waster that could be spent on productive activities.

Details: People spend hours watching TV passively instead of pursuing hobbies, exercise, reading, or learning. This "lost time" could be used for personal development. Binge-watching keeps people from achieving their goals.

Example: "The average person watches 20+ hours of TV per week—imagine what they could achieve with that time!"
Negative Impact on Children
Main point: TV has particularly damaging effects on children's development.

Details: Excessive screen time harms children's concentration, reading ability, and social skills. Violent content on TV can make children aggressive. Advertising targets children with unhealthy food products. Screen time replaces physical play, which is essential for healthy development.

Example: "Experts recommend that children watch less than two hours of quality programming daily."
Reduced Social Interaction
Main point: Television isolates people and damages relationships.

Details: People spend time watching TV instead of interacting with family and friends. Binge-watching keeps families from talking and connecting. This can lead to loneliness, depression, and weakened relationships. Face-to-face interaction is essential for human wellbeing.

Example: "Families who watch TV together often don't have real conversations—they just sit in silence."
Superficial Content & Advertising
Main point: Much of television content is superficial and designed to manipulate viewers.

Details: Reality shows feature artificial drama with no educational value. Advertising manipulates viewers into buying unnecessary products. TV promotes consumerism and unrealistic lifestyles. Much content is designed to keep viewers watching, not to enrich their lives.

Example: "Reality shows are criticized for their lack of substance and focus on celebrity gossip rather than meaningful content."

Speaking Guidelines

✓ DO:

  • Use phrases from the Functional Language tab
  • Support your arguments with evidence and examples
  • Listen to the other person's arguments
  • Acknowledge valid points from the opposing view
  • Speak clearly and at a natural pace

✗ DON'T:

  • Be aggressive or rude to the other person
  • Just say "I agree" or "I disagree" without explanation
  • Make statements without examples or evidence
  • Interrupt while the other person is speaking
  • Make it personal—focus on the topic, not the person
