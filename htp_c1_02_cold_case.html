Thinking about the past

Detectives rarely say "I know". They say how sure they are. Read these aloud once.

Sure it happened

must have + past participle

  • She must have known the code.
  • It must have been someone inside the house.
  • He must have left before midnight.

Sure it didn't

can't have / couldn't have + past participle

  • He can't have done it: he was on the phone.
  • She couldn't have reached it without a key.
  • It can't have been a stranger.

Possible

might have / may have / could have + past participle

  • He might have slipped out unseen.
  • She may have heard something.
  • Someone could have used the garden door.

Building the case

  • That rules out …
  • Which means that …
  • Unless, of course, …
  • So the only person left is …
  • Nobody can confirm where … was.

Listen to how it sounds

AThe laptop was on the desk at six and gone by seven. So it must have been taken in that hour. BThen Dev can't have taken it. He was in a meeting until half past seven. AThat rules him out. What about Lena? BNobody can confirm where Lena was. She might have gone back to the office. AUnless, of course, someone else had a key. Is there anything in your notes about keys?

Key words

alibi (noun) — proof that a person was somewhere else when something happened. Example: "His story held up: three people saw him in the kitchen all evening."
culprit (noun) — the person who did something wrong. Example: "The police are still looking for the culprit."
motive (noun) — the reason someone has for doing something, especially something wrong. Example: "Money is the most common motive for theft."
rule out (phrasal verb) — decide that someone or something is not possible. Example: "We can rule out the gardener: he was in town all evening."
deduce (verb) — reach a conclusion by thinking carefully about the facts you have. Example: "From the muddy boots, she deduced that he'd been outside."
red herring (noun) — a clue that seems important but leads you in the wrong direction. Example: "The broken window turned out to be a red herring."
witness (noun) — someone who saw something happen. Example: "A witness saw a man leaving the building at midnight."
corroborate (verb) — support someone's story by giving more evidence that it is true. Example: "Two other guests corroborated her story."
