Studying Abroad: Discussion & Language Skills

⏱ Lesson Time: 0:00

Starting the Conversation

Let's explore the topic of studying abroad. Think about your own experience, or imagine what it might be like. These questions will help us explore different perspectives on international education.

Your Experience & Reflections

Have you studied abroad, or do you know someone who has?
Think about what drew them to study internationally. What was their experience like? What stood out as memorable or challenging?
If you've studied abroad, where did you go and what was it like?
Consider: How did it feel to arrive? What surprised you? How long did it take to settle in? What do you miss about it now?
Would you like to study abroad in the future?
What appeals to you about it? What concerns or worries do you have? What kind of place would you choose?
If a friend wanted to study in your country, what advice would you give them?
What should they know before they arrive? What might be easy for them? What challenges might they face? What would surprise them about your culture or education system?

Benefits & Drawbacks

What do you think are the main benefits of studying abroad?
Think beyond just education—consider personal growth, relationships, career opportunities, and cultural understanding.
What drawbacks or challenges might someone face when studying abroad?
Consider: cost, homesickness, language barriers, cultural differences, visa difficulties, adapting to a new education system, isolation, or missing important events back home.

Education Vocabulary

Click on each term to explore its meaning and context in educational discussions.

Transferable skills
Abilities you develop that can be applied across different subjects or careers. Examples: communication, teamwork, problem-solving, time management.

Question: What transferable skills does studying a degree give you?
Prestigious universities
Institutions with high academic reputation and recognition worldwide. Known for quality teaching, research, and alumni success.

Question: What are the most prestigious universities in your country? Would you want to attend one? Why or why not?
Integrating into culture
Becoming part of a new community by understanding and participating in its customs, traditions, social norms, and everyday life.

Question: What challenges might you face when integrating into a different culture?
Character-building experience
An event or period that develops your resilience, strength, confidence, or personal maturity through challenge or hardship.

Question: Have you ever had a character-building experience? What did it teach you?
Revision techniques
Methods and strategies for reviewing and preparing material for exams. Examples: flashcards, mind maps, group study, past papers, summarizing.

Question: What revision techniques do you find most effective?
Cliché
A phrase or idea that has been used so often it loses impact and feels unoriginal. Often assumed to be true without question.

Question: The cliché about education is that "hard work is necessary to succeed." Do you agree with this? Are there counterexamples?
Standardised tests
Formal exams taken by many people following the same format and criteria, designed to measure knowledge and ability fairly across populations. Examples: SAT, IELTS, GRE.

Question: At what age do you take standardised tests in your country? How much weight do they carry in your education system?

Discussion Management Phrases

These phrases help you guide a conversation, keep it on track, and make sure everyone is included. In a live lesson, you'll use these to manage the flow naturally.

The Phrases

"OK... anything else to add?" Check if anyone has more ideas before moving on
"Are you happy to (take notes)?" Ask someone to volunteer for a task in the discussion
"OK, we're going off on a tangent." Gently point out you've strayed from the topic
"Any other (benefits)?" Invite more ideas on a specific subtopic
"What about...?" Bring in a quieter person or introduce a new angle
"Let's move on." Decide it's time to shift to the next point
"We're getting a bit side-tracked again." Remind the group you're drifting from the main discussion
"OK, let's summarise." Pull together the key points you've covered

Group Discussion Task

You'll work with your partner on a discussion. One person leads the conversation; the other takes notes. Afterwards, you'll summarise and share with another pair.

Choose Your Topic

Select one of the three topics below to discuss:

Topic 1: Studying in a Multinational Group

What are the pros and cons of studying in a group of students from different countries?

Focus areas: Friendship & support, language practice, cultural misunderstandings, different study habits, homesickness, shared experiences

Topic 2: Subject Language—English vs Your Native Language

What are the benefits and drawbacks of studying subjects in English versus your first language?

Focus areas: Deep understanding vs language challenge, job prospects, accessibility, connection to your culture, cost of programmes

Topic 3: Foreign Degree & Local Employment

Is a degree from abroad an advantage or disadvantage when looking for work in your home country?

Focus areas: Prestige, visa sponsorship, local experience, employer preferences, cost vs return, brain drain

How It Works

  1. Assign roles: One person leads the discussion (asks questions, keeps it on track, uses management phrases). One person takes notes (records key points and ideas mentioned).
  2. Discuss (8–10 minutes): Explore your chosen topic. The leader should use discussion management phrases naturally. Aim for 70–80% of the conversation to come from your mouth—this is your speaking practice.
  3. Summarise (2 minutes): Together, review what the note-taker wrote. Agree on the main points you covered.
  4. Present (2–3 minutes): Share your summary with another pair or with the group. Keep it clear and concise.

Reflection Questions for After the Discussion

How naturally did you use the discussion management phrases? Which ones felt easiest?
Did the note-taker capture what you meant? Were there any miscommunications?
What did you learn from your partner's perspective on the topic?
How could you improve if you did this task again?

Discussion Phrases Summary

You now know eight key phrases for managing group discussions. Use them to:

Include everyone: "What about...?" brings in quieter voices
Check progress: "OK... anything else to add?" makes sure ideas are exhausted
Redirect focus: "We're going off on a tangent" / "We're getting side-tracked" keep you on course
Move forward: "Let's move on" signals it's time for a new point
Recap: "OK, let's summarise" pulls together what you've discussed
