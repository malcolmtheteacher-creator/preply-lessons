📋 Intend vs Pretend

B1 • Portuguese/Spanish speakers • False friends: pretender ≠ pretend

🔍 Let's notice what we see

Look at this sentence. What's the problem?

"I pretend to travel next year."

(But they really mean they PLAN to travel, not that they're faking it!)

Do they really plan to travel? Or are they faking it? What's confusing?

⚠️ The False Friend:

Portuguese "pretender" LOOKS like English "pretend", but they mean DIFFERENT things!

In Portuguese: pretender = to intend, to plan, to aim to do something (pretendo viajar = I intend to travel)

In English: pretend = to act as if something is true when it isn't (pretend to be sick)

In English: intend or plan = to have a goal or plan

🛠️ Understand the difference

📊 Different intentions:

🇵🇹 Portuguese "pretender"

📋 INTEND / PLAN

Real goal, genuine plan

"Pretendo estudar" = I intend to study

🇬🇧 English "pretend"

🎭 ACT / FAKE

Not real, deceptive

"Pretend to be sick" = Act sick, not really

🎯 Key Rule:

Portuguese pretender → English INTEND / PLAN

English pretend → Portuguese fingir (act as if)

When you have a real plan: INTEND. When faking: PRETEND.

🚀 Now you try — no help!

Choose the correct word. Check your answers when you're done.

1. "We _____ to go on vacation in August." (intend / pretend)
Answer: "We intend to go on vacation."

→ Real plan = intend.

2. "Stop _____ to like something you hate."
Answer: "Stop pretending to like it."

→ Acting as if = pretend.

3. "I _____ to buy a new house next year."
Answer: "I intend to buy a new house."

→ A real goal = intend.

4. "Kids like to _____ they're superheroes."
Answer: "Kids like to pretend they're superheroes."

→ Make-believe = pretend.

✅ I can say 'intend' instead of 'pretend' when talking about plans
